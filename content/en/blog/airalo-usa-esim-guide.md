## Why You Should Solve Your US Data Problem Before You Fly

The United States surprises a lot of international travelers for one simple reason: **there are no cheap roaming agreements** like the ones that exist across Europe. Land in New York or Los Angeles with your home plan still active, and roaming charges can spiral quickly.

On top of that, since 2022, **every iPhone sold in the US (iPhone 14 and newer) ships without a physical SIM tray**. eSIM isn't an optional add-on in the US market — it's the default. If you're carrying a US-bought iPhone, it's an eSIM-only device: there's no SIM slot, so a digital eSIM is your only way online.

Put those together and the case for sorting out data ahead of time is hard to ignore:

- **Roaming is too expensive** to rely on.
- **Airport SIM cards are overpriced** (often 300–500% above online prices).
- **Your phone may not even have a SIM tray.**

### The "unlimited" truth: the Fair Use Policy (FUP)

"Unlimited" sounds great, but **every eSIM unlimited plan has a Fair Use Policy (FUP)**.

Under Airalo's policy, after **3 GB of high-speed data per day**, your speed drops to **1 Mbps** until the next reset. The daily allowance is counted from your **eSIM activation time** and resets every 24 hours.

**What does 3 GB/day actually cover?** Within that allowance you can:

- Watch about one Netflix episode in HD (~45 min)
- Watch 5–10 YouTube videos
- Browse Instagram/TikTok/X for 1–2 hours and upload photos
- Use Google Maps navigation all day
- Send unlimited LINE/WhatsApp messages plus ~30 minutes of video calls

Even a full sightseeing day — constant Google Maps, restaurant searches and bookings, photo uploads, and a Netflix episode back at the hotel — still fits comfortably inside 3 GB.

After the throttle to **1 Mbps**, you can still message, browse simple pages, navigate, and make low-quality video calls. What actually suffers is **video streaming, gaming, and large downloads** — those slow down until the next daily reset.

The real value of "unlimited" isn't "can't run out" — it's **"don't have to count."** Fixed plans make you watch your remaining data; unlimited removes that burden entirely. Airalo's unlimited plans also allow **hotspot/tethering with no device limit**, which is handy if you're sharing with travel companions.

### When should you actually buy unlimited?

**Buy unlimited if:**

1. **You're sharing a hotspot** — 2–3 people can burn 10 GB in 3–4 days.
2. **You video-call or stream daily** — a 30-minute daily call alone passes 1 GB.
3. **You're on a road trip** — one person navigating, another scrolling, a third streaming uses 2–3× city-trip data.
4. **You don't want to think about data** — travel is for relaxing, not watching "2.3 GB left."

**Skip unlimited if:**

1. You're doing a pure city trip with most of your time on hotel/café Wi-Fi — 5–10 GB fixed is plenty.
2. You travel solo with predictable usage — fixed plans are far better value.

## Airalo USA Network Coverage: T-Mobile vs Verizon

This is where a US eSIM differs most from a Europe or Asia eSIM. The US covers **3.8 million square miles** — you can get blazing 5G in Times Square and zero signal deep inside a Utah national park.

### Which network does Airalo use?

Airalo's USA eSIM runs primarily on **T-Mobile** and **Verizon**, with coverage across **all 50 states and Puerto Rico**.

### The catch: no automatic network switching

**Airalo does not switch networks automatically.** Your eSIM is assigned to T-Mobile or Verizon at activation and stays locked to that network for the whole trip:

- If you're on T-Mobile, you can't hop to Verizon even where Verizon's signal is better.
- If you're on Verizon, you can't switch to T-Mobile where it's faster.

This matters for planning. **City-focused trips are fine on T-Mobile** (fastest urban 5G). **National parks and rural areas favor Verizon's wider coverage.**

### How to choose by itinerary

- **Big cities only** (NYC, LA, SF, Chicago, Miami, Vegas, Boston, DC) → T-Mobile is plenty.
- **National parks** (Yellowstone, Grand Canyon, Yosemite, Zion) **or cross-state road trips** → Verizon's coverage edge shows up, especially in the rural West.
- **Not sure** → either network covers cities and major highways well.

### The honest truth about national-park signal

Let's be realistic: inside US national parks, **no carrier has strong signal**. The Grand Canyon's north rim, deep Yosemite trails — every network has large dead zones. But that doesn't make the eSIM useless:

- Gateway towns (Flagstaff, Jackson Hole, Moab) have solid signal.
- Main visitor centers and campgrounds usually get coverage.
- Interstate highways stay connected.

**Practical tip:** before entering a park, **download Google Maps offline maps on hotel Wi-Fi**.
