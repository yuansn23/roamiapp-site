## Why Long Stays Need a Different eSIM Strategy

A 7-day tourist eSIM and a 3-month stay eSIM are different products. If you pick a plan built for a week-long vacation and stay for a semester, you will overpay or run out of data at the worst time.

**The core differences:**

- **Cost per month beats cost per day.** A tourist plan priced at $29/7 days works out to about $116/month. A proper 30-day plan (Ubigi $59) costs nearly half that.
- **You usually need a Canadian number.** Students register for bank accounts, universities, and jobs. A data-only tourist eSIM cannot receive verification SMS. See our [Canada eSIM with a phone number guide](/blog/canada-esim-with-phone-number-guide/).
- **Data needs grow.** A tourist uses 3–10GB a week for maps and social. A nomad working from cafés uses 20GB+ a month on video calls alone.
- **Top-up and renewal matter.** You need a plan you can extend without swapping the eSIM, or you will reinstall every month. Canada's [Wireless Code](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/mobile/codes.htm) sets minimum standards for how carriers must handle service and billing.

**The roaming math for a month:** A US carrier's daily pass costs $10–$14/day. Over 30 days that is $300–$420 — more than a monthly eSIM plan by a wide margin.

## Unlimited Data Fine Print: What "Unlimited" Really Means

"Unlimited" is the most abused word in the Canada eSIM market, and long stays expose it faster than short trips. After two or three weeks of video calls, you hit the fine print a one-week tourist never sees.

**Fair Use Policies (FUP) are standard.** Most "unlimited" plans throttle or deprioritize your speed after a daily or monthly threshold:

- **Ubigi unlimited** has no hard data cap, but heavy hotspot use can trigger a fair-use slowdown during peak hours.
- **Holafly** is genuinely unthrottled for phone data, but hotspot is capped at around 500MB/day on many plans — a real limit for laptop work.
- **Nomad unlimited** applies a daily fair-use policy of roughly 2GB/day before speeds drop.

**What this means for long stays:** if your work depends on video calls and file uploads, do not assume "unlimited" means "unlimited hotspot". Read the provider's FUP, and if you need laptop tethering all month, Ubigi's unlimited-hotspot plan is the safer bet than a cheaper "unlimited" plan with a hidden hotspot cap.

**Deprioritization is not throttling.** Even on "unthrottled" plans, your traffic may be deprioritized behind the network's own postpaid customers during congestion in Toronto or Vancouver. For a long stay, this usually matters only during weekday rush hours.

## Top-Up, Renewal and Data Rollover

A monthly plan is only as good as your ability to extend it. Here is how renewal works for long stays — and one trap most people miss.

**Renewal without reinstalling.** Ubigi, Nomad, and PhoneBox let you top up or renew from the app without deleting and reinstalling the eSIM. That matters for long stays: reinstalling every month is a recurring source of activation failures and lost time. Roami's [Canada eSIM](/canada-esim/) includes **24/7 human support**, so a failed renewal can be fixed quickly rather than costing you a day of connectivity.

**Data rollover is rare.** Almost no travel eSIM rolls unused data into the next month. If you buy 50GB and use 20GB, the leftover 30GB usually expires at the end of the validity window. So do not buy a huge plan "just in case" — buy the size you will actually use, or choose a provider with smaller top-up increments.

**Auto-renew vs manual top-up.** Auto-renew is convenient but can surprise you if you forget to cancel when you leave Canada. Manual top-up gives you control but demands you remember the deadline. For a long stay, set a reminder 48 hours before expiry.

**Timing the top-up.** Top up at least 24 hours before your plan expires so you are never left without data between plans. If a payment fails, you want time to fix it before the connection drops mid-workday.

**Renewal policies at a glance:** Ubigi and Nomad support in-app top-up; PhoneBox and CanadianSIM offer longer-term plans; ByteSIM plans stop when the high-speed quota is used up, so plan your renewal around the quota, not just the calendar date.

## Do You Need a Canadian Number for a Long Stay?

**Almost always, yes.** The longer you stay, the more services require SMS verification:

- Bank accounts (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC)
- Universities and student portals
- Job applications and government services
- Uber, DoorDash, and Airbnb host contact

**The rule of thumb:** a stay under 10 days rarely needs a number; a stay over one month almost always does. Canadian wireless rules are set by the [CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/mobile/), which also governs number portability and carrier unlocking. ## Setup & Activation for Long Stays

The setup is the same as any eSIM, with two long-stay twists:

1. **Check compatibility first** — dial `*#06#` for EID. See our [eSIM-compatible phones in Canada guide](/blog/esim-compatible-phones-canada-guide/).
2. **Install before departure** on home Wi-Fi.
3. **Set the eSIM as your default data line**, keep your original number for voice.
4. **Verify the number works** — send a test SMS if your plan includes one.
5. **Set up top-up or renewal** so you do not lose service mid-month.

eSIM follows the [GSMA specification](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), so installation works the same across phone brands and carriers. For full step-by-step installation on iPhone and Android, see our [eSIM installation walkthrough](/blog/how-to-get-esim-in-canada-guide/).
