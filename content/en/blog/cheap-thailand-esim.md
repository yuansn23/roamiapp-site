### Pitfalls to Avoid

**Don't buy a physical SIM at the airport**

Airport physical SIMs are just as expensive as eSIMs, and you'll need a SIM ejector tool plus you risk losing the tiny card. If your phone supports eSIM, **skip the physical SIM entirely**.

**Avoid 7‑Eleven eSIMs if you value convenience**

7‑Eleven eSIMs require you to buy a physical card, scan a QR code, and register with your passport – much more hassle than online, and you have to do it after landing when you may not have a connection.

**Don't skip the compatibility check**

China‑market iPhones and some Samsung models sold in certain regions **do not support eSIM**. Dial `*#06#` before you go – if you don't see an EID number, your phone can't use eSIM, so buy a physical SIM instead.

**Don't activate AIS eSIM too early**

AIS eSIM registration must be completed **within 24 hours before departure**. Doing it too early may cause the eSIM to expire before you arrive.

**If AIS eSIM shows "Activating" or "Activation failed" outside Thailand – DON'T DELETE IT**

Operators usually won't reissue the QR code. Keep the profile and try again after you land.

**Don't forget to turn on Data Roaming after activation**

This is the most common mistake – about 35% of activation failures come from this. Go to Settings → Cellular → select the eSIM line → turn on Data Roaming.

## 8. FAQ

**Q1: Do I need to register with my passport for a Thailand eSIM?**

It depends. Thai local operators (AIS/True/DTAC) require passport registration as mandated by the [NBTC](https://www.nbtc.go.th/). International platforms (Airalo, Saily, Nomad, Travelsim Asia, etc.) do not.

You can install the profile, but don't activate it. The safer way: screenshot the QR code and save it, then scan and install after landing using airport Wi‑Fi. If an AIS eSIM shows "Activation failed" outside Thailand, don't delete it – try again after arrival.

**Q3: Can I top up or extend my eSIM?**

Yes. Most Thai local operator eSIMs support online top‑up or 7‑Eleven recharge cards. International platforms usually offer add‑on purchases or renewal within their app.

**Q8: Which is better – Thailand eSIM or physical SIM?**

For most tourists, eSIM is better – cheaper, more convenient, and lets you keep your home number active. Only choose physical SIM if your phone doesn't support eSIM.

**Q9: Does Thailand offer eSIM at 7‑Eleven?**

Yes, but 7‑Eleven primarily sells physical SIM cards that come with an eSIM QR code printed on the card packaging. You'll need to buy the card, scan the QR code, and register with your passport.

**Q10: Is eSIM available in Thailand?**
