## 2. The Japan eSIM Networks That Matter: Docomo vs SoftBank vs KDDI

There is a fourth operator, **Rakuten Mobile**, which is building its own network and roams onto KDDI where it has no coverage of its own. It is not yet a common partner for travel eSIMs, so treat **NTT Docomo, KDDI (au) and SoftBank** as the practical shortlist when you choose a plan.

### What each network is actually good at

Japan has three major mobile networks, and every eSIM provider resells access to one or more of them. Before comparing brands, understand what they mean for your trip.

| Network | Market Position | 5G Availability | Rural Coverage | Typical Speed |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **NTT Docomo** | Largest network | Highest | **Best** — the only reliable option in rural areas, Hokkaido, and the Alps | Strong, consistent |
| **SoftBank** | Strong in cities | High | Weak outside cities | **Fastest median** in independent measurements |
| **KDDI (au)** | Solid mid-tier | Moderate | Moderate | Good in cities |

**The practical takeaway:** if your itinerary is Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka, any network works. If you're headed to Hokkaido ski resorts, the Japanese Alps, Okinawa's outer islands, or rural Kyushu, you need a provider that rides **NTT Docomo**.

A provider that runs on **both** Docomo and KDDI (auto-switching) is the safest choice — you get rural reach plus urban speed without manual network selection.

## 6. Roaming-Type vs Local-Type eSIMs

This is the single most under-discussed difference in the Japan eSIM market, and it explains most "why is my eSIM slow?" complaints.

| | Roaming-Type (Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi, Saily) | Local-Type (CDJapan Rental, Sakura, Mobal) |
|---|---|---|
| **Data routing** | Through overseas servers (HK/Singapore) | Direct to Japan's network |
| **Latency** | 150–200ms | 20–50ms |
| **Japanese IP** | Usually not | Yes |
| **Price** | Cheaper, more flexible | Slightly higher |
| **Japan-only apps** | May be geo-blocked | Full access |

### When latency actually matters

For most tourists, roaming-type latency is fine — maps, messaging, and social media don't care about 150ms. It only matters if you need a Japanese IP for apps, gaming, or business video calls. In that case, choose a local-type provider.

## 8. eSIM Refunds, Backups and What Happens When It Fails

### The refund rules are not equivalent

Activation failures are rare but memorable, and the policies covering them differ more than the prices do. Holafly's approach is the most generous on paper: a refund window measured in months that also covers incompatible or locked handsets, provided the QR code has never been scanned. Airalo frames its refunds around technical faults, with change-of-mind requests returning as store credit rather than money. Japan Wireless takes the opposite position on its unlimited eSIM and lists it as non-refundable, but answers the risk differently by shipping a free pocket WiFi unit as a physical fallback if the eSIM will not connect after guided troubleshooting. Mobal's distinguishing feature is the absence of fees: no activation charge and no termination charge, where several Japanese providers add a setup fee in the thousands of yen.

### What to confirm before you pay

Four things, in this order. Whether you can still get a refund after the QR code has been scanned. Whether the policy covers a locked or incompatible handset. Whether there is a top-up route on the same eSIM, so running dry does not mean buying a new plan. And whether support staff a timezone that overlaps yours, because a failed activation at Narita at midnight is a support problem before it is a technical one.

### Why a backup beats a discount

The credible protection against a failed eSIM is a second route to the internet, not a five-dollar saving. A second small eSIM, a card bought at an airport counter, or a pocket WiFi rental each cost less than the value of one lost day. Where a provider offers a hardware fallback, that is worth more than the same money spent on a marginally cheaper plan.

## 11. Regional and Multi-Country eSIM Plans

### When one regional plan beats two country plans

If your itinerary crosses a border, a regional Asia plan can replace two or three country plans, and it installs once rather than at each stop. That is usually the cheaper and simpler answer for a Japan, Korea and Taiwan route. The trade-off is real, though: regional plans often run on fewer partner networks in each country than a dedicated country plan, so the rural coverage reasoning you did for Japan has to be redone for the second destination.

### What to check on a regional plan

Three details decide whether a regional plan is genuinely equivalent. Whether validity is counted across the whole plan rather than per country, which changes how a two-week trip divides. Whether every country on your route is included, since these plans are assembled from different sets of destinations. And whether the network partner changes at the border, because that is where a plan that worked perfectly in Tokyo can quietly become the weakest link in Seoul. Our [East Asia multi-country guide](/blog/japan-korea-taiwan-china-esim-multi-country-guide/) works through the routes where one plan genuinely replaces two or three.
