## Device Compatibility: Which Phones Work with France eSIM

Not every phone supports eSIM. Before purchasing a France eSIM, verify that your device is compatible. The [eSIM standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESIM) has been adopted across most flagship phones released since 2018, but budget and older models may lack support.

### iPhone Models

Apple introduced eSIM support with the iPhone XS series in 2018. All subsequent models include eSIM functionality:

- iPhone XS, XS Max, and XR (2018)
- iPhone 11 series (2019)
- iPhone SE (2020 and 2022)
- iPhone 12 series (2020)
- iPhone 13 series (2021)
- iPhone 14 series (2022) — US models are eSIM-only with no physical SIM tray
- iPhone 15 series (2023)
- iPhone 16 series (2024)
- iPhone SE (2025)

All iPhones from the XS onward support dual SIM with one physical SIM and one eSIM, or dual eSIM on newer models. The iPhone 14 and later US models support dual eSIM with no physical SIM slot.

For official details and the full compatibility list, refer to Apple's eSIM support documentation at [Apple Support — Using eSIM on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT212780).

Carrier-locked phones purchased from US carriers like Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile may have eSIM access restricted. Unlocked models work with any eSIM provider regardless of country.

Samsung maintains a device compatibility list on its support pages. For step-by-step eSIM activation on Samsung devices, visit [Samsung eSIM support](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS00085915/). ## Pre-Trip Setup: Buy Online and Install Before Departure

The single most important step in your France eSIM setup is completing the installation before you leave. Installing an eSIM requires an internet connection to download the profile. Doing this at home on Wi-Fi eliminates the risk of arriving in France without connectivity to install the eSIM in the first place.

### Step 1: Purchase the eSIM Online

Every major eSIM provider operates entirely online. Here is the standard purchase flow:

1. Go to the provider's website and select France as your destination
2. Choose a data plan based on your trip length and expected usage
3. Enter your email address and complete payment
4. Receive an email with your eSIM QR code and installation instructions

Payment is processed in USD or EUR depending on the provider. Most accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express, and PayPal. Plans are delivered within seconds to minutes after payment confirmation.

**Can I buy a France eSIM online before my trip?** Yes, and you should. Buying in advance locks in the price, gives you time to test the installation, and ensures you have connectivity immediately upon arrival. There is no penalty for installing before activation — the plan's validity clock starts when the eSIM connects to a French network, not when you install it.

### Step 2: Verify Your Installation Email

The email from your eSIM provider contains:

- A QR code image (scan this in your phone settings)
- An SM-DP+ address and activation code (alternative manual entry method)
- APN settings (save these for later)
- Your plan details: data allowance, validity period, and network partner

Do not delete this email until your trip is over. You may need the QR code again if you reset your phone or switch devices.

### Step 3: Connect to Wi-Fi

Before scanning the QR code, ensure you are connected to a stable Wi-Fi network. The eSIM profile downloads during installation. A dropped connection can cause the download to fail, requiring a new QR code from the provider.

### Step 4: Scan the QR Code and Complete Installation

Follow the generic steps below. Detailed instructions for iPhone and Android follow in later sections.

- Open your phone's cellular or mobile network settings
- Select "Add eSIM" or "Add Mobile Plan"
- Scan the QR code from your email
- Label the new plan as "France Data" for easy identification
- Complete the installation

If your phone does not recognize the QR code, most providers offer an alternative activation method using an SM-DP+ address and confirmation code that you enter manually.

### Step 5: Keep the France eSIM Disabled Until You Land

After installation, the eSIM profile sits on your phone in an inactive state. Turn it off until you arrive in France. On iPhone, go to Settings > Cellular and toggle the France eSIM off. On Android, go to Settings > Connections > SIM Manager and deactivate the France eSIM.

On the tarmac at Charles de Gaulle or Orly, enable the eSIM, turn on data roaming for that line, and your connection activates within seconds.

For travelers preferring a guided setup experience, the [France eSIM installation guide](/blog/france-esim-complete-guide/) on this site provides a visual walkthrough of the process.

## iPhone Setup: Step-by-Step France eSIM Installation

Apple's eSIM implementation is the most streamlined across all smartphone brands. The setup flow is consistent across iPhone models, with minor variations depending on iOS version.

### Installing the eSIM Profile

1. Open **Settings > Cellular** (labeled "Mobile Data" in some regions)
2. Tap **Add eSIM** (or "Add Cellular Plan")
3. A camera viewfinder appears — position the QR code from your email within the frame
4. iOS automatically detects the eSIM profile and displays plan details
5. Tap **Add Cellular Plan**
6. If prompted for a confirmation code, enter the one from your email (usually not required for standard eSIMs)
7. The eSIM profile downloads and installs — this takes 10-30 seconds on a stable Wi-Fi connection

### Naming Your eSIM

After installation, iOS asks you to label the plan. Choose "France Data" or "France eSIM" — something you will recognize when managing multiple plans. You can change this label later in Settings > Cellular > [plan name] > Cellular Plan Label.

### Setting Default Lines

iOS prompts you to configure which line to use by default:

- **Default Voice Line:** Choose your home SIM if you want to make and receive calls on your regular number. Choose the France eSIM if you plan to use a French phone number for local calls.
- **Default Data Line:** Choose the France eSIM. This routes all mobile data through the French network.
- **iMessage and FaceTime:** You can receive iMessages on both numbers if you enable both lines.

### Enabling Data Roaming

The France eSIM requires data roaming to be turned on at the eSIM level:

1. Go to **Settings > Cellular**
2. Tap the France eSIM plan
3. Toggle **Data Roaming** ON
4. Verify that **Enable 5G** is set to "Auto" or "5G On"

### Activating Upon Arrival

When your plane lands in France:

1. Turn off Airplane Mode
2. Swipe into Control Center and toggle Cellular Data ON
3. If you toggled the France eSIM off earlier, go to Settings > Cellular and toggle it back on
4. Wait 30-60 seconds for the phone to register on the French network
5. The carrier name (Orange, SFR, Bouygues, or Free, depending on your provider) appears at the top left

If no carrier name appears after 60 seconds, go to Settings > Cellular > Network Selection, turn off Automatic, and manually select Orange or the network your eSIM uses.

### Installing Without QR Code (Manual Entry)

If you cannot scan the QR code — for example, if you purchased the eSIM on a different device — iPhone supports manual entry:

1. Tap **Add eSIM** then **Use Details to Add Plan**
2. Enter the SM-DP+ address from your email (looks like "SM-DP+: rsp.airalo.com")
3. Enter the activation code if provided
4. Tap **Next** to download and install

This method is useful when you purchase an eSIM on your laptop and need to activate it on your phone without displaying a QR code on screen.

For official step-by-step guidance, see Apple's support page on [installing an eSIM on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT212780).

## Android Setup: Step-by-Step France eSIM Installation

Android eSIM installation varies slightly depending on whether you use a Samsung, Google Pixel, OnePlus, or other device. The underlying process is the same, but menu labels differ by manufacturer and Android version.

### Samsung Galaxy Setup

Samsung's eSIM implementation is accessed through the SIM Manager:

1. Open **Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager**
2. Tap **Add eSIM**
3. On the "Add eSIM" screen, tap **Scan QR code** (not "Download eSIM")
4. Scan the QR code from your email
5. Confirm the plan details and tap **Add**
6. The eSIM profile downloads — this takes 15-30 seconds
7. Once installed, tap the eSIM to configure it

After installation, go back to **SIM Card Manager** and:

- Set the France eSIM as **Primary for Mobile Data**
- Leave your home SIM as **Primary for Calls and SMS** (or switch if you have a French number)
- Enable **Data Roaming** for the France eSIM

Samsung phones running One UI 5.0 and later also support dual eSIM, allowing two eSIMs alongside the physical SIM.

### Google Pixel Setup

Pixel phones use the standard Android eSIM flow:

1. Open **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**
2. Tap **Add eSIM** (or the "+" icon)
3. Tap **Download a SIM instead?** when prompted about carrier activation
4. Tap **Next**, then scan the QR code
5. The eSIM installs — typically in under 20 seconds
6. The new SIM appears in the SIMs list, labeled as "Downloaded SIM"

To configure:

- Tap the France eSIM entry
- Toggle **Use SIM** ON
- Enable **Data Roaming**
- Set it as preferred for **Mobile Data**

Pixel phones running Android 13 and later show a notification when an eSIM is installed, allowing you to configure it directly from the notification shade.

### OnePlus Setup

OnePlus devices starting with the OnePlus 11 use this flow:

1. Open **Settings > Mobile Network > SIM Management**
2. Tap **Add eSIM**
3. Scan the QR code or enter details manually
4. The profile installs within 30 seconds
5. Name the profile and configure data routing

OnePlus OxygenOS 14 and later support dual eSIM with dual standby, meaning both eSIMs can receive calls and SMS simultaneously.

### Common Android Setup Issues

**"QR code not recognized":** Android camera apps sometimes fail to detect eSIM QR codes. Use the built-in scanner in SIM Manager instead of the standard camera app.

**"Can't download eSIM":** Ensure you are on Wi-Fi, not mobile data. Some Android versions require Wi-Fi for the initial eSIM profile download.

**"No option to add eSIM":** Your phone model or carrier variant may not support eSIM. Contact your carrier to verify, or check your IMEI against the [GSMA eSIM compatibility database](https://www.gsma.com/esim/).

If you are testing eSIM compatibility before your trip, a [free eSIM trial](/free-esim/) works on both iPhone and Android and confirms whether your specific device can install and activate eSIM profiles.

## Dual SIM Configuration: Home SIM and France eSIM Together

The advantage of eSIM technology is that you can keep your home SIM active for calls and SMS while using the France eSIM for mobile data. This dual SIM setup eliminates the need to remove your physical SIM or carry a second phone. Here is how to configure it correctly on both platforms.

### What Dual SIM with eSIM Means

In a dual SIM configuration with one physical SIM and one eSIM:

- **Home SIM (primary line):** Handles voice calls and SMS from your regular number. You remain reachable at your usual phone number.
- **France eSIM (secondary line):** Handles all mobile data traffic. Internet, app usage, maps, and streaming go through the French network.
- **Both lines active simultaneously:** Both SIMs remain on and connected to their respective networks. You can receive a call on your home SIM while using data on the France eSIM.

### iPhone Dual SIM Configuration

1. Install the France eSIM following the steps above
2. Go to **Settings > Cellular**
3. You see both lines listed: "Primary" (your home SIM) and "France Data" (the eSIM)
4. Tap **Cellular Data** and select the France eSIM
5. Tap **Default Voice Line** and select Primary (your home number)
6. Scroll down and tap each line to configure individual settings:

For your home SIM:
- Data Roaming: OFF (you want data on the France eSIM only)
- Enable 5G / VoLTE: As preferred

For the France eSIM:
- Data Roaming: ON (required for the eSIM to connect)
- Enable 5G: Set to Auto

iOS allows you to choose which line to use for iMessage and FaceTime. You can enable both lines, meaning iMessage works with your regular number and the France eSIM number (if it has one).

**Call forwarding between lines:** iPhone does not automatically forward calls between the home SIM and eSIM. If someone calls your home number, it rings on your home SIM. If you want calls forwarded, set up call forwarding with your home carrier before departure.

### Android Dual SIM Configuration

Android's dual SIM management varies by manufacturer, but the core settings are consistent.

**Samsung Galaxy:**

1. Open **Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager**
2. Under "SIM cards," you see SIM 1 (physical) and SIM 2 (eSIM)
3. Tap **Mobile Data** and select SIM 2 (the France eSIM)
4. Tap **Preferred SIM for Calls** — select SIM 1 (home) or "Ask every time"
5. Tap **Preferred SIM for SMS** — select SIM 1 (home)

For each SIM, tap its entry to toggle data roaming and network mode individually.

**Google Pixel:**

1. Open **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**
2. Tap the France eSIM entry
3. Toggle **Data Roaming** ON
4. Go back and tap your home SIM — ensure Data Roaming is OFF
5. Under "Preferred SIMs," configure:
   - **Calls:** Home SIM
   - **SMS:** Home SIM
   - **Mobile Data:** France eSIM

### Preventing Unexpected Roaming Charges

The most common issue with dual SIM travel is accidental data usage on the home SIM. Here is how to prevent it:

- **Turn off data roaming on your home SIM.** This is the single most important setting. Even if your phone shows the France eSIM as the default data line, some apps may route data through the home SIM under certain conditions (MMS downloads, carrier update checks).
- **Disable "Allow Cellular Data Switching" on iPhone.** Go to Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data > turn OFF "Allow Data Switching." This prevents iOS from automatically routing data through your home SIM if the eSIM signal weakens. Without this toggle off, your phone may switch to your home SIM in a tunnel or elevator and incur roaming charges.
- **On Android, set data to the eSIM only.** In SIM Manager, explicitly set the France eSIM as the exclusive data SIM. Do not set it to "Ask every time."
- **Disable MMS auto-download on your home SIM.** On iPhone: Settings > Messages > turn OFF "MMS Messaging." On Android: Messages app > Settings > Advanced > turn OFF "Auto-download MMS." Group messages containing images can trigger MMS downloads over your home SIM.

### Dual SIM Calling Behavior

When your home SIM is set as the default voice line:

- **Outgoing calls** use your home number by default. You can manually switch to the France eSIM for a specific call (on iPhone: tap the "Calls" button during dialing; on Samsung: tap the SIM selector before dialing).
- **Incoming calls** arrive on the line they were placed to. Both lines ring independently.
- **Voicemail** is handled per line. Your home number voicemail remains unchanged.

A [france esim](/france-esim/) configured as the data line with your home SIM handling calls is the optimal setup for most travelers. It keeps your regular number active for banking verification codes, two-factor authentication, and family calls while all internet traffic routes through the French network.

## APN Settings for Orange, SFR, Bouygues, and Free Networks

Most eSIM providers configure APN settings automatically during installation. When this works correctly, you never touch APN settings. However, certain providers — particularly those using Orange or Free Mobile networks — may require manual APN entry, especially on Android devices.

### When Manual APN Configuration Is Necessary

- Your eSIM is installed and shows signal but has no data connection
- Internet works on some apps but not others
- You see "4G" or "5G" in the status bar but cannot load web pages
- MMS (picture messages) do not send or receive
- Visual voicemail fails to connect

If any of these occur, manual APN configuration typically resolves the issue.

### How to Access APN Settings

**iPhone:**
1. Go to **Settings > Cellular**
2. Tap the France eSIM plan
3. Tap **Cellular Data Network**
4. Enter the APN in the "APN" field (username and password are typically blank)
5. Tap **Back** to save

The APN field appears only when you have selected the correct eSIM line. If you do not see "Cellular Data Network," the eSIM may not be active or properly installed.

**Android (Samsung):**
1. Go to **Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager**
2. Tap the France eSIM entry
3. Tap **Access Point Names**
4. Tap **Add** (or the "+" icon)
5. Enter the APN and tap the menu > **Save**
6. Select the new APN as active

**Android (Google Pixel):**
1. Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**
2. Tap the France eSIM
3. Tap **Access Point Names**
4. Tap **Add** and enter the APN details

### APN Settings Table by Carrier

The following table lists the APN values for each French carrier. These are network-level APNs, not provider-specific ones. International eSIM providers (Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi, etc.) typically use their own APN values, which are listed separately below.

### Orange Network APN in Detail

Orange is France's largest network and the most common partner for eSIM providers. The Orange APN (`orange`) works on both Orange and Orange Travel eSIMs for data. However, Orange Travel eSIMs (which include a French phone number) use a separate APN for MMS:

| Setting | Value |

Most international eSIMs on the Orange network use `orange` as the APN and do not require MMS configuration since they are data-only.

### Verifying APN Settings

After entering APN values, test the connection:

1. Toggle Airplane Mode ON for 10 seconds, then OFF
2. Open a browser and load any website
3. Open Google Maps and verify it loads tiles
4. Send a test WhatsApp message

If data works in the browser but not in specific apps, the APN is likely correct and the issue is app-specific. If nothing loads, double-check the APN spelling — APN values are case-sensitive and must be entered exactly as shown.

## Getting a French Phone Number with eSIM

International travel eSIMs are almost exclusively data-only. They provide mobile internet but do not include a voice number for calls or SMS. Getting a French phone number with an eSIM requires specific products that go beyond standard data plans.

### Orange Travel: The Only Carrier eSIM with a French Number

Orange Travel is the only eSIM product from a French carrier that includes a genuine French phone number (+33 number). This is not a virtual number or VoIP line — it is a fully registered French mobile number on the Orange network.

| Travel 15GB | 15GB | 14 days | Yes | €34.99 |
| Travel 25GB | 25GB | 21 days | Yes | €49.99 |

Each plan includes unlimited calls and SMS within France and to EU countries, plus roaming data within the EU.

**Purchase and activation process:**

1. Go to the Orange Travel website and create an account
2. Select your plan and complete payment
3. You receive a QR code by email (not instant — can take up to 2 hours)
4. Install the eSIM following the same QR code scanning process
5. Upon activation in France, you receive your French phone number via SMS
6. The number is assigned automatically — you cannot choose it

**Identity verification:** Orange Travel requires identity verification under French telecommunications law. You must upload a scan of your passport or EU national ID card during purchase. The verification process takes 15 minutes to 2 hours. Once approved, your eSIM activates on the Orange network.

### Why You Might Need a French Phone Number

A French phone number unlocks access to several services that are either difficult or impossible to use with a foreign number:

**SNCF (French Railways) App**

The SNCF Connect app requires a phone number for account creation and ticket purchase verification. Key use cases:

- **Account registration:** Creating an SNCF Connect account requires SMS verification. Foreign numbers may not receive the verification code. A French number guarantees this works.
- **Ticket purchase:** Some promotional fares (Ouigo, Prem's) require phone number verification during checkout.
- **Delay notifications:** The app sends SMS alerts for delays and platform changes. These are not available by email.
- **eTicket delivery:** While most tickets are digital within the app, some require SMS delivery of the ticket link.

**Navigo Pass (Paris Public Transport)**

The Navigo pass system for Paris metro, RER, and bus networks has specific registration requirements:

- **Navigo Easy card registration:** The card itself is anonymous and works without registration, but topping up online requires an account with a French phone number.
- **Navigo Découverte:** The weekly/monthly pass requires registration through the Île-de-France Mobilités website, which mandates a French phone number for SMS verification.
- **Bonjour RATP app:** The official Paris transport app uses SMS-based two-factor authentication for account creation.
- **Automatic top-up:** Setting up auto-reload on a Navigo card requires a French bank account and phone number.

**Restaurant Reservation Apps**

Several popular French dining platforms rely on phone verification:

- **La Fourchette (TheFork):** The leading restaurant reservation platform in France sends SMS confirmation for bookings. Foreign numbers receive confirmations, but some premium restaurants require a call-back verification that only works with a French number.
- **Allo Resto / Just Eat France:** Food delivery services use SMS for order confirmations and driver contact.
- **Michelin Guide app:** Account registration requires phone verification.

**Other Services Requiring a French Number**

- **French bank accounts:** Opening a bank account (even online like N26 or Revolut) requires a French phone number for verification.
- **CNIL complaints:** Registering complaints with the French data protection authority.
- **CAF (housing benefit):** Social welfare registration requires a French number.
- **Ameli (health insurance):** Health insurance registration uses SMS verification.
- **Parking apps:** PayByPhone France and other parking payment apps use French numbers for account creation.

For detailed guidance on which services require a French number and how to set them up, see our [France eSIM complete guide](/blog/france-esim-complete-guide/).

## Alternatives to a French Phone Number

Most travelers do not need a French phone number. For the majority of use cases, a data-only eSIM combined with existing communication tools is sufficient and more cost-effective than Orange Travel's premium pricing.

### Google Voice (US Residents Only)

Google Voice provides a free US phone number that works over Wi-Fi and mobile data. While it does not provide a French number, it can serve as a second number for calls and SMS while using a data-only eSIM.

**What Google Voice handles well:**
- Making and receiving calls over data (VoIP)

### Can I Use One France eSIM on Multiple Phones?

No. An eSIM profile is tied to the device it was first installed on. Once installed on Phone A, the QR code becomes invalid for Phone B. The eSIM provider's server registers the eSIM to Phone A's unique identifier (EID — eSIM ID).

**What this means in practice:**

- You cannot transfer an active eSIM between phones (with most providers)
- You cannot install the same plan on two phones simultaneously
- If you break your phone during the trip, you need to contact the provider to transfer the eSIM to a new device

**Exception:** Some providers allow eSIM transfer through their app or customer support. Airalo and Ubigi allow transfer requests via their respective apps. The process requires the old eSIM to be deactivated and a new QR code to be issued for the replacement device.

## Navigo Pass and SNCF App Activation Tips

The Navigo pass system for Paris public transport and the SNCF Connect app for French trains have specific eSIM-related requirements. This section covers setup steps and common issues.

### Navigo Pass: Digital Registration with an eSIM

The Navigo pass comes in three forms, each with different eSIM requirements:

**Navigo Easy Card (Anonymous):**
The Navigo Easy is a contactless smart card for metro, bus, tram, and RER travel within Paris and suburbs. The card itself is anonymous — you buy it for 5 EUR at any metro station vending machine, load tickets, and tap to ride. No phone number required for the card itself.

However, for online top-ups via the Île-de-France Mobilités app:

1. Download the **Île-de-France Mobilités** app (iOS and Android)
2. Create an account using your email
3. To register your Navigo Easy card to the app, tap "Add a card"
4. Hold the card to the back of your phone (NFC is required)
5. The app adds the card for digital management

**SMS verification issue:** The app does not require SMS verification for basic functionality. However, for purchasing weekly/monthly passes and loading them onto the card, the app redirects to the Île-de-France Mobilités website, which does require SMS verification with a French phone number.

**Solution for data-only eSIM users:**
- Use the ticket vending machines at any metro station (English language available)
- Machines accept credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) and cash
- Purchase Navigo weekly passes (Navigo Semaine) or carnet of 10 tickets directly at machines
- No account or phone number needed

**Navigo Découverte Pass:**
The Navigo Découverte is a weekly pass (Monday-Sunday) for unlimited travel in zones 1-5. It requires a passport photo and registration:

1. Purchase the Navigo Découverte card at any metro station ticket office (5 EUR)
2. Attach a passport photo to the card
3. Load the weekly pass at a ticket machine or online
4. Online loading requires a French phone number for account creation

**Workaround:** Load the weekly pass at any station's ticket machine using a credit card. This bypasses the online registration requirement entirely.

**Navigo Annual Pass:**
The annual subscription (Navigo Annuel) requires a French bank account, French address, and French phone number. This is not practical for travelers.

### SNCF Connect App: Account Setup

The SNCF Connect app is essential for booking TGV, Intercité, and regional train tickets. Here is how to set it up with a data-only eSIM:

**Step 1: Download and install**
- Download "SNCF Connect" from the App Store or Google Play
- The app works entirely over mobile data — no French phone number needed for basic browsing

**Step 2: Account creation**
- Tap "Create an account"
- Enter your email and a password
- The app asks for a phone number — this field is marked as optional during initial registration
- Complete the registration via email verification link

**Step 3: Purchasing tickets without a French number**
- Select your journey and choose your train
- Proceed to payment (credit card accepted)
- During checkout, the app may request a phone number for "delay notifications"
- Enter your home number with country code (e.g., +1 for US, +44 for UK)
- While some users report foreign numbers work for SMS alerts, the system may reject non-French numbers for certain promotional fares

**Step 4: Ouigo and Prem's fare restrictions**
- Budget TGV fares (Ouigo, Prem's) sometimes require a French phone number during booking
- If the system rejects your foreign number, switch to a standard fare instead
- The price difference is typically 10-20 EUR per ticket

**Step 5: Ticket storage**
- All tickets are stored digitally in the app
- No need to print anything
- Show the QR code to the conductor or scan at TGV platform gates
- Download tickets before the train departs in case the app has connectivity issues in tunnels

### Using the Bonjour RATP App

The Bonjour RATP app provides real-time metro and RER information:

1. Download the app and create an account
2. The app supports Google and Apple sign-in, bypassing phone number requirements
3. Real-time schedules, route planning, and disruption alerts work without a French number
4. Push notifications replace SMS alerts in most cases

### Practical Workflow: Navigating Paris Transport with Only Data

If you are using a data-only eSIM without a French phone number, here is the complete workflow for Paris public transport:

1. At CDG airport: Follow signs to the RER B station

## Activating Your France eSIM Before Travel

Most France eSIM plans allow you to install the eSIM any time after purchase, with the plan validity starting only upon first connection to a French network. This is called "activation on first use."

### How Pre-Trip Activation Works

1. Purchase the eSIM online (days or weeks before departure)
2. Install the eSIM profile immediately while on Wi-Fi at home
3. The eSIM sits inactive on your phone — no data consumed, no clock running
4. On arrival in France, enable the eSIM and turn on data roaming
5. The plan validity starts when the eSIM connects to Orange, SFR, Bouygues, or Free

This flexibility allows you to complete the technical installation before travel, eliminating any risk of arrival-day connectivity issues.

### Which Providers Support Pre-Installation

| Provider | Pre-Install? | Validity Starts | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| Airalo | Yes | On first connect | QR code works anytime |
| Holafly | Yes | On scheduled date | You choose activation date |
| Ubigi | Yes | On first connect | QR code works anytime |
| Orange Travel | Yes | After ID verification | Clock starts after verification |
| Roami | Yes | On first connect | Install anytime before trip |

### What Happens If You Install After Arrival

Installing the eSIM after landing in France is possible but carries risk:

- You need airport Wi-Fi or a pre-existing connection to download the profile
- CDG and Orly airports offer free Wi-Fi ("Paris Aeroport" network)
- The Wi-Fi connection process requires SMS or email verification (one-time code)
- If you cannot connect to Wi-Fi, you are stuck without data

**The safe approach:** Install the eSIM on home Wi-Fi before departure. The process takes less than five minutes and eliminates all arrival-day risk.

### Testing Your eSIM Before Travel

Some providers allow limited pre-travel testing:

1. Install the eSIM at home
2. Do not enable data roaming yet
3. Turn off your home SIM (Settings > Cellular > turn off "Primary")
4. Turn on the France eSIM with data roaming enabled
5. If the eSIM connects to a local partner network, you see the carrier name — but data will not pass until you reach France
6. Turn the France eSIM back off and re-enable your home SIM

This test confirms the eSIM profile is installed correctly and ready for activation upon arrival.

## Troubleshooting France eSIM Setup Issues

Even with careful preparation, issues can arise. This section covers the most common problems and their solutions.

### eSIM Profile Won't Install

**Symptom:** QR code scan fails or installation stops at 50%.

**Solutions:**
- Ensure you are on Wi-Fi, not mobile data
- Restart your phone and try again
- Clean your camera lens — fingerprints can interfere with QR scanning
- Request a new QR code from your provider (the original may have expired)
- Try manual entry using the SM-DP+ address instead of QR scan

### France eSIM Shows Signal but No Data

**Symptom:** Carrier name appears but browser and apps cannot load content.

**Solutions:**
- Check that Data Roaming is ON for the France eSIM (not your home SIM)
- Verify APN settings (see APN section above)
- Toggle Airplane Mode ON for 10 seconds, then OFF
- Go to Network Selection, turn OFF Automatic, manually select Orange
- If on Free network, try switching to Orange or SFR manually

### Data Works but Is Very Slow

**Symptom:** Connection works but pages load slowly or video buffers.

**Solutions:**
- Check which network you are connected to — Free is slower than Orange in many areas
- Manually switch to Orange or SFR in Network Selection
- Move to a different location (building interiors and basements have weaker signals)
- Check if your plan has a speed cap (some budget plans throttle to 5-10 Mbps)
- 5G is available in all major cities but may require specific phone bands

### Dual SIM: Home SIM Roaming Charges Appeared

**Symptom:** Your home carrier charged you for data roaming despite setting the France eSIM as default.

**Solutions:**
- Turn OFF data roaming on your home SIM explicitly (not just toggling it off in Control Center)
- On iPhone, disable "Allow Data Switching" under Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data
- On Android, ensure the France eSIM is set as "Mobile Data" exclusively, not "Ask every time"
- Remove the home carrier's data APN temporarily during your trip

### Navigo App Won't Register Without French Number

**Symptom:** Île-de-France Mobilités app asks for SMS verification with a French number.

**Solutions:**
- Use the Bonjour RATP app instead — it supports Google/Apple sign-in
- Purchase tickets at station vending machines (credit card accepted)
- Use the physical Navigo Easy card — no app required for daily use
- The SNCF Connect app accepts email-only registration

### eSIM Expired or Data Ran Out

**Symptom:** You receive a "plan expired" or "out of data" notification.

**Solutions:**
- Top up through the provider's app or website (most support instant top-up on the same eSIM)
- Purchase a new eSIM plan and install it alongside the existing one (phones support multiple eSIMs)
- Switch to a backup eSIM if you purchased a separate plan for extended coverage

### I Broke My Phone During the Trip

**Symptom:** Your phone with the installed eSIM is lost, broken, or stolen.

**Solutions:**
- Contact your eSIM provider's support team immediately
- Most providers can issue a new QR code for a replacement device
- Airalo and Ubigi support eSIM transfer via their apps
- Some providers charge a small fee for re-issuing eSIMs

For a complete list of error codes and fixes, reference the [France eSIM troubleshooting guide](/blog/france-esim-troubleshooting-guide/).
