An unlimited Turkey eSIM is not a speed guarantee — it is a data plan whose real shape is decided by the fair usage policy (FUP) printed in the provider's terms. In Turkey that matters more than almost anywhere else, because the two unlimited brands on the market document aggressive daily thresholds: Holafly throttles to 0.148 Mbps after 2–3 GB per day, and Saily throttles to 1 Mbps after 5 GB per day. This page does the arithmetic on those thresholds, shows what each throttle tier can and cannot do, compares unlimited against fixed-data plans at the 20 GB break-even point, and tells you which traveller profile genuinely comes out ahead. If you want the wider market picture first, the [Turkey eSIM ultimate guide](/blog/turkey-esim-ultimate-guide/) ties all thirteen cluster articles together.

## Turkey Unlimited Data eSIM: The Short Answer

**Direct answer:** an esim turkey unlimited data plan delivers full speed up to a documented daily threshold — 2–3 GB/day on Holafly, 5 GB/day on Saily — and then drops to a crawl (0.148 Mbps or 1 Mbps) until the daily window resets. For a tourist who uses 1–2 GB per day, unlimited is comfortable and often the simpler choice. For a heavy user, a hotspot user, or anyone planning to tether a laptop, the throttle and the hotspot caps make a 20 GB fixed-data plan the better buy.

The five facts that decide everything else:

- Holafly sells **only** unlimited plans, rides Turkcell plus Türk Telekom, and documents a 0.148 Mbps throttle tier after 2–3 GB per day.
- Saily's unlimited tier rides the strongest 4G/5G coverage, allows unlimited tethering, and throttles to 1 Mbps after 5 GB per day.
- Fixed-data plans from Nomad and Ubigi have **no FUP at all** — you get full speed until the plan's GB run out.
- The break-even point between unlimited and fixed-data sits around **20 GB of usage for a 7-day trip**.
- Top-ups from inside Turkey are blocked on the provider side, so whatever plan you land with is effectively the plan you keep.

Everything below expands those five facts with the numbers behind them.

## What FUP Actually Does After the Daily Threshold

**Direct answer:** a fair usage policy does not cut you off — it reclassifies you. After the daily threshold, the carrier shifts your traffic to a deprioritized class, which in Turkey's documented cases means a hard ceiling of either 1 Mbps (Saily) or 0.148 Mbps (Holafly) until the window resets.

The mechanics, in the order they hit you:

1. **Full speed until the threshold.** You consume your 2–3 GB (Holafly) or 5 GB (Saily) of the day at native 4G/5G speed. This part is genuinely good — Turkey's networks are fast in Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir and Antalya.
2. **The threshold trips.** Nothing dramatic happens on screen. The first symptom is usually a video that will not load, followed by everything else slowing down.
3. **The throttle tier applies.** Your traffic is capped at the documented rate. Uploads and downloads both, on every app.
4. **The window resets.** Providers define their own daily window; the reset hour is buried in the plan terms. Treat it as "sometime overnight" rather than a precise clock, and plan the next day's heavy use for the morning.

Three practical consequences follow from those mechanics. First, the throttle is **per day**, not per trip — a fresh full-speed allowance arrives every morning, so the pain is uneven rather than permanent. Second, the threshold counts **all traffic on the eSIM**, including background sync, app updates and maps tiles, which is why heavy-phone users hit it earlier than their own estimates predict. Third, the throttle applies **at the network level**, so no app setting, APN tweak or phone reboot lifts it — the [activation and troubleshooting guide](/blog/how-turkey-esim-works-activation/) lists FUP as a distinct cause of "very slow" symptoms precisely because travellers keep mistaking it for a fixable configuration problem.

At 0.148 Mbps, one gigabyte takes about 15 hours to transfer and a single 5 MB photo upload takes about four and a half minutes. At 1 Mbps, one gigabyte takes about 2.2 hours and standard-definition video just barely works.

| Holafly after 2–3 GB/day | 0.148 Mbps | ~15 hours | ~4.5 minutes | ~9 minutes | ~27 minutes |

What each tier can actually do:

|---|---|---|

Two conclusions fall out of the table. The Saily tier at 1 Mbps is a degraded-but-living experience: navigation, messaging and voice calls survive, video barely. The daily-budget view makes the same point. If you hit Holafly's threshold at 2 GB by early afternoon after a morning of navigation, uploads and streaming, the rest of your day runs at 65 MB per hour. If you spread the same 2 GB across a full day with hotel Wi-Fi at breakfast and in the evening, you may never trip the threshold at all. **The threshold rewards offloading**, and hotel Wi-Fi in Turkey is exactly where you do it — the [ultimate guide](/blog/turkey-esim-ultimate-guide/) documents that hotel Wi-Fi is also the reliable path around the BTK buying block, so the habit pays twice.

If you are the kind who estimates first and buys once, the fixed-data math wins.

## How to Verify an Unlimited Claim Before You Pay

**Direct answer:** four checks separate a real unlimited plan from a marketing one, and all four take less than five minutes on the provider's site — before payment, because after landing, the provider-block rules mean you cannot easily revisit the choice.

1. **Find the FUP number, not the word.** Search the plan page for "fair usage", "FUP", "throttle", "reduced speed" or "high-speed data". If the page never states a threshold or a throttle rate, the [provider failure-mode comparison](/blog/best-turkey-esim-providers/) treats that silence as a red flag, not a reassurance — the worst surprise in the market (0.148 Mbps) is documented in a terms page most buyers never open.
2. **Find the hotspot clause.** Search for "tethering" and "hotspot". One of three sentences will be there: allowed (best), allowed with a cap (do the cap arithmetic above), or not allowed (walk away if you share).
3. **Confirm the network partners.** Unlimited on Türk Telekom alone is worth less than unlimited on Turkcell plus Vodafone if your route includes Cappadocia, the Black Sea coast or the east. Coverage beats speed in the places tourists actually go.
4. **Check the refund window for failed activation.** The complaint record shows refund denial after activation is the third most common problem after hotspot caps and throttling — know the policy while you can still read it without a VPN.

## If You Already Bought Unlimited and Got Throttled

**Direct answer:** assume the cap will not lift before the daily window resets, and spend your effort moving traffic off the throttled line rather than trying to fix it. Five moves work; two popular ones do not.

**What works:**

1. **Confirm the cause in two minutes.** Run a speed test; if the result sits near 0.148 Mbps or 1 Mbps rather than varying with signal strength, it is the FUP, not coverage. A throttle is suspiciously stable — that stability is the signature. Free fixes for signal and APN problems are in the [troubleshooting table](/blog/how-turkey-esim-works-activation/), but they will not touch a throttle.
2. **Offload the queue.** Turn off photo backup, app updates and cloud sync on the eSIM line, then let the phone batch them over hotel Wi-Fi. Background sync is what turns a 2 GB allowance into a 1.5 GB one before lunch.
3. **Switch the data line if you carry a second profile.** A cheap fixed-data backup eSIM — installed before departure, because you cannot buy easily once inside the country — restores full speed in one settings change and costs less than a mid-trip panic purchase.
4. **Use the phone as the only hotspot consumer.** If you were tethering, stop: on Holafly the 1 GB/day tethering cap can bind before the general threshold, and tethered traffic is the first thing a provider's detection notices.
5. **Plan around the reset.** Heavy transfers belong in the first hours after the daily allowance returns; sightseeing and messaging belong in the throttle window. Once you accept the shape of the day, a throttled plan is annoying rather than trip-ruining.

**What does not work:** a VPN does not restore throttled speed — the cap is applied to your subscriber traffic before any tunnel, so the VPN adds overhead on top of a crawl. Nor does reinstalling the eSIM, resetting network settings, or switching APN: the limit lives on the account, not the device configuration, and a reinstall on a metered day can cost you the remaining allowance without returning any speed.

The one situation that justifies buying again mid-trip is a hard deadline — a work call, a flight rebooking, an emergency. In that case buy the cheapest fixed-data plan reachable over Wi-Fi and treat the unlimited plan as a sunk cost; the [refund reality](/blog/turkey-esim-reddit-reviews-legit/) is that activation usually ends the refund window.

## Turkey Unlimited eSIM Questions (FAQ)

**Is a Turkey unlimited data eSIM really unlimited?**
No. Every mainstream unlimited plan for Turkey carries a fair usage policy: Holafly throttles to 0.148 Mbps after 2–3 GB per day and Saily throttles to 1 Mbps after 5 GB per day. The plan never stops transmitting, but full speed is a daily allowance, not a property of the plan. Fixed-data plans are the opposite — a hard cap and no throttle.

**What is the best esim for turkey unlimited data?**
It depends on the threshold shape you prefer. For phone-only heavy use, Saily's 5 GB/day threshold and unlimited tethering make it the strongest unlimited value. For network coverage across the widest territory, Holafly's Turkcell plus Türk Telekom footprint wins, at the cost of the lowest documented throttle tier. For pure sharing, Roami's unrestricted hotspot on fixed-data plans beats both unlimited products.

**How much data do I need per day in Turkey?**
A realistic tourist day is 1–2 GB: maps and messaging in the morning, social and video in the evening, hotel Wi-Fi in between. Add 50–100 MB per hour of web browsing, 0.5–1.5 GB per hour of video calls, and 1–3 GB per hour of HD streaming when you are off Wi-Fi. If your honest total exceeds 2–3 GB per day, you are shopping in unlimited-or-bust territory.

**Why is my Turkey eSIM suddenly slow on the third day?**
The most likely cause is the FUP threshold, and the timing is the tell: a throttle that engages at the same time every afternoon is FUP, not congestion. Rule out the free fixes first — signal, network selection, data line — using the [troubleshooting table](/blog/how-turkey-esim-works-activation/), then check your usage against the plan's threshold.

**Does the throttle reset at midnight?**
The reset window is defined by each provider and stated, if at all, in the plan terms. Do not build your day around a specific hour: schedule heavy transfers for the morning after a reset you have observed, and offload to hotel Wi-Fi at night regardless.

**Is Holafly unlimited worth it for a 7-day Turkey trip?**
For 1–2 GB/day phone-only use on a route that needs Turkcell plus Türk Telekom coverage, yes — $24.50 for never thinking about data is a fair price. For video-heavy use, hotspot sharing, or laptop work, no: the 2–3 GB/day threshold and the 1 GB/day tethering cap both land on exactly the usage that made you choose unlimited.

**Can I top up an unlimited plan from inside Turkey?**
Usually not from the Turkish network itself — buying and managing from inside Turkey is blocked at the platform level, which is why the [rules page](/blog/turkey-esim-ban-availability-rules/) treats pre-departure purchase as the default strategy. Some provider apps remain reachable over hotel Wi-Fi, but you cannot rely on it. Buy the plan you need before you fly.

**Does unlimited data work in Cappadocia and rural Turkey?**
The FUP is rarely your problem there — coverage is. Cappadocia's valleys and the eastern provinces favour Turkcell, which is why the [network comparison](/blog/turkcell-vodafone-turk-telekom-esim/) puts a Turkcell-connected plan ahead of a cheaper one; a provider riding Türk Telekom alone is the weaker choice on exactly the routes where travellers most want to upload photos. Unlimited data on a network that cannot hold a signal delivers nothing.

**Is the throttle applied on 5G as well?**
Yes. The cap is set on the subscriber account, so it applies whatever radio you are on — 5G, 4G or a roaming partner's network. A 5G icon on the status bar after the threshold does not mean full speed; it means a fast pipe throttled to a slow rate.

**Can I buy an unlimited eSIM at Istanbul Airport on arrival?**
Not the travel eSIM you researched — buying from inside Turkey is blocked, so the profiles sold at the airport are local carrier packages, which start the local-device registration clock and carry airport markups the [cost guide](/blog/cheapest-turkey-esim/) counts as a hidden cost. Install and test before departure; the airport is the worst place to discover a problem.

## How This Turkey Unlimited Data Guide Was Compiled

Every threshold, price and policy on this page comes from the provider terms and traveller evidence already verified across the thirteen other Turkey cluster articles: plan documents (Holafly's duration pricing and throttle tier, Saily's 1 Mbps tier, Kudo's hotspot prohibition), the May 2026 Istanbul field test that confirmed more than fifteen pre-installed eSIMs connect normally, and the complaint patterns documented in provider review threads. The throughput tables are arithmetic on those published numbers — rate converted to transfer time — clearly labelled as such, because an honest FUP analysis does not need invented speed tests: the published tiers are extreme enough to plan around. Prices are listed in US dollars as published in the cluster's provider tables and can move; the thresholds and caps are the durable part of this page.
