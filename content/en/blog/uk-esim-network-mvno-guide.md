## Can tourists buy a Vodafone UK eSIM travel plan?

No. Vodafone UK doesn't sell prepaid pay-as-you-go eSIM plans to tourists as of July 2026, according to [Vodafone UK's official site](https://www.vodafone.co.uk). You can't walk into a Vodafone shop and buy a Vodafone UK eSIM travel plan as a visitor. Getting an esim Vodafone UK connection as a tourist therefore requires using a travel eSIM provider or an MVNO that partners with Vodafone instead. The answer is consistent across shops: not available.

Here's what Vodafone UK offers for eSIM:
- **Postpaid contracts:** Available to UK residents with credit checks and proof of address. Not available to tourists.
- **Vodafone Travel eSIM:** A separate product designed for UK residents travelling abroad. It's not a UK plan for visitors.
- **Pay As You Go eSIM:** Existing PAYG customers can convert to eSIM, but new customers can't get one as of mid-2026.

So how do you get Vodafone coverage on eSIM as a tourist? Two ways:

1. **Buy a travel eSIM that uses the Vodafone network.** Roami's [UK eSIM](/united-kingdom-esim/) includes Vodafone as one of its partner networks and connects automatically when it's the strongest signal. A [UK eSIM](/united-kingdom-esim/) with multi-network access effectively solves the Vodafone problem by routing you through whichever of the four networks is strongest at your location.
2. **Buy a physical SIM** from a Vodafone UK shop. You'll need to show ID and physically swap SIMs. It's a hassle, but it works.

A thorough **UK eSIM EE vs Vodafone coverage comparison** shows EE has the edge in rural Great Britain while Vodafone is strong in UK cities and along major transport routes. If you need Vodafone coverage, use a multi-network travel eSIM or a Vodafone-based MVNO instead. It's that simple.

## Do I need ID to buy an eSIM in the UK?

It depends on where you buy. UK networks and MVNOs require ID. Travel eSIM providers don't.

**Buying from a UK network directly (EE, O2, Three, Vodafone physical SIM):** Yes, you need ID. UK law requires mobile networks regulated by Ofcom to verify customer identity, as mandated in 2022. Use your passport or a national ID card. Some online purchases can be verified through digital ID checks.

**Buying from an MVNO (giffgaff, Lebara, SMARTY, VOXI):** Usually yes, but the process is simpler. Most accept a passport photo or digital upload. Some use automated verification. giffgaff's verification takes about 3 minutes via their app — surprisingly smooth.

**Buying from a travel eSIM provider (Roami, Airalo, etc.):** No, you don't need ID. Travel eSIMs operate outside the UK's mobile identification requirements — you buy and download. That's one of the main reasons tourists choose them.

**Why the ID rule exists:** The UK introduced mandatory ID verification for SIM purchases in 2022 as part of anti-fraud and anti-terrorism measures, according to [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk) regulations. Travel eSIMs that only offer data are generally treated differently because they don't provide a regulated mobile service with a UK number.

## How is UK eSIM coverage in Scotland, the Lake District, Cornwall and Wales?

Coverage varies dramatically across Great Britain's most-visited tourist destinations. Network choice is the determining factor — not provider, not plan, not price. Network.

**Edinburgh:** All networks cover the city centre, Old Town, and New Town with 5G from EE, Vodafone, and Three. Holyrood Park and Arthur's Seat have patchy coverage — EE works at the summit but may drop on the descent. EE holds 2 bars at the top of Arthur's Seat, while Three has nothing.

- **Condition:** Edinburgh plus Highlands -> **Recommendation:** EE or Roami multi-network. The moment you cross the Forth Bridge heading north, network differences become real.

RootMetrics drive-test data from 300 miles of Highland roads shows EE with signal roughly 85% of the time, Vodafone about 60%, O2 barely 40%, and Three around 30% (half of those too weak to use).
- **Condition:** Doing the NC500, visiting Skye, or hiking in the Highlands -> **Recommendation:** EE direct or Roami multi-network. Nothing else is worth considering.

**Lake District:** EE leads, especially on the fells and in valleys. O2 and Vodafone work in main towns but not on the hills. Three is weak throughout. Climbing Catbells? EE at the summit. Three loses signal 10 minutes into the walk.
- **Condition:** Visiting the Lake District -> **Recommendation:** EE or Roami multi-network

**Wales (Snowdonia, Brecon Beacons, Pembrokeshire):** Big coverage gaps outside cities. EE is the best rural network, especially in Snowdonia and the Brecon Beacons. Vodafone is decent in the south but weaker in the north. O2 and Three struggle in rural Wales. On Snowdon via the Pyg Track: EE works at the summit cafe, Vodafone is intermittent, O2 and Three are completely dead above 600 metres.

## Which phones are compatible with UK eSIM?

Most modern phones from 2019 onwards support eSIM. Older models and some market-specific variants don't. Check your device before buying, and verify against [GSMA's eSIM device list](https://www.gsma.com/esim/) (2026).

**iPhone:** iPhone XR and all later models (including dual eSIM). iPhone XS and XS Max support eSIM. iPhone SE (2nd gen and later) supports eSIM. All iPhone 14 and later US models are eSIM only (no physical SIM). If you've got a US iPhone 14 or 15, you literally don't have a choice — it's eSIM or nothing.

**Samsung:** Galaxy S20 series and later. Galaxy Z Flip and Z Fold series. Galaxy A series: limited (A54 and later in some markets). Samsung's eSIM support is more fragmented than Apple's — always check your specific model.

**Google Pixel:** Pixel 3 and later (Pixel 6 and later recommended). Pixel Fold supports eSIM.

**Huawei:** Limited support. P40 and Mate 40 series have eSIM in some markets. Newer models restricted by US sanctions. Honestly, if you're using a recent Huawei, eSIM compatibility is the least of your problems.

**OnePlus:** OnePlus 11 and later. OnePlus 10 series has eSIM in some markets.

**How to check your phone:**
1. **iPhone:** Settings > General > About > Scroll to "Available SIMs" or "eSIM"
2. **Samsung:** Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager > Add eSIM
3. **Google Pixel:** Settings > Network & Internet > Add SIM > Download a SIM instead
4. Check [Apple's support page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118670) for iPhone compatibility

**Carrier-locked phones:** If your phone is locked to a US carrier (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile), it can only use eSIMs from that carrier. US iPhones from iPhone 14 onwards have no physical SIM slot, so a locked US iPhone 14 or later cannot use a UK eSIM at all. Request an unlock before travel: T-Mobile (after 40 days), Verizon (auto after 60 days), AT&T (after 60 days or when device payments complete). Don't discover your phone is locked at 35,000 feet.

## How do I set up dual SIM with a UK eSIM?

Dual SIM lets you keep your home SIM active for calls and texts while using a UK eSIM for data. Most modern iPhones and Android flagships support this. It's the setup most travellers use on every trip.

**Setting up dual SIM on iPhone:**
1. Install your UK eSIM before you leave (scan the QR code)
2. On arrival, go to Settings > Cellular
3. Set your home SIM for "voice and SMS"
4. Set your UK eSIM for "data"
5. Enable "Allow Cellular Data Switching"

**Setting up dual SIM on Android:**
1. Install the UK eSIM before you leave
2. Go to Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager
3. Set your home SIM for calls
4. Set your UK eSIM for mobile data
5. Enable "SIM card switching" if available

**Battery impact:** Dual SIM uses 10-20% more battery because the phone maintains connections to two networks. Carry a power bank for all-day use. Dual SIM can drain a fully charged iPhone 15 Pro by 4pm on a heavy-use day. Without dual SIM, that same phone typically lasts until 8pm.

See the [dual SIM setup guide](/blog/uk-esim-dual-sim-setup/) for detailed instructions per phone model.

WiFi calling lets you make and receive calls through your home carrier over any WiFi connection — including your hotel WiFi in the UK — without incurring international roaming charges. ## How do I activate a UK eSIM?

Activation varies by provider type, but the general process is straightforward.

**Direct network eSIM (EE, O2, Three):**
1. Buy the plan online or in-store
2. Provide ID (passport scan or in-store verification)
3. Receive a QR code by email
4. Scan the QR code with your phone's camera
5. Follow on-screen prompts to install
6. eSIM activates when you connect to the UK network

**MVNO eSIM (giffgaff, SMARTY, Lebara, VOXI):**
1. Sign up on the provider's website or app
2. Provide ID (digital verification)
3. Download the eSIM profile (usually immediately)
4. Scan or download to your phone
5. Activate by connecting to the UK network

**Travel eSIM (Roami, Airalo, Holafly):**
1. Download the provider's app
2. Buy a UK plan within the app
3. Install the eSIM through the app (guided setup)
4. eSIM activates automatically on arrival (or manually, depending on the plan)

**Common activation issues and fixes:**
**"No Service" after installing:** Restart your phone to force network registration. This fixes it in most cases.
**eSIM shows "Activation Pending":** Ensure you have internet access (WiFi or another SIM) during installation. eSIMs need an internet connection to complete activation.
**Data not working after activation:** Enable "Data Roaming" for the eSIM in your phone settings. This is the number one issue travellers face.
**eSIM not showing:** Go to Settings > Cellular > Add Cellular Plan and scan the QR code again.
**APN settings not configured:** Some eSIMs need manual APN configuration. For Roami, APN is set automatically. For others, check the provider's setup guide.

For more troubleshooting, see the [eSIM troubleshooting guide](/blog/uk-esim-troubleshooting/).
