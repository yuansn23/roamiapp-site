## What does "unlimited" actually mean for UK eSIM plans?

In the UK travel eSIM market, "unlimited" is a marketing word. It doesn't mean what you think it means. What it actually means: your data connection continues after you exhaust your daily high-speed allowance — but at drastically reduced speeds, typically 128kbps to 512kbps. The term describes three distinct tiers of service that affect real-world usability in very different ways.

### Unlimited internet, unlimited data and data-only: same thing, different labels

Providers use "unlimited UK eSIM internet", "unlimited data" and "internet only" to describe overlapping products, and the label tells you less than the specification does. What matters is which of these three you are actually buying:

- **Unlimited mobile data with a daily high-speed cap.** The connection never stops, but after your daily allowance you drop to a throttled speed. Usable for maps and messaging, unusable for video. This is what most "unlimited" plans are.
- **Unlimited internet at full speed with no cap.** Rare and priced accordingly — Ubigi's 30-day unlimited is the clearest example in the UK market. Read the fair usage policy anyway; "no daily cap" does not mean "no fair use clause".
- **Internet only, with no allowance at all.** This is a data-only eSIM with a fixed bucket, sometimes labelled "internet only" to signal that there are no calls or SMS. It is not unlimited in any sense, and confusing the two labels is the most common buying mistake in this category.

One more label to decode: "**eSIM UK and Europe unlimited**". Regional unlimited plans exist, but the daily cap usually applies across the whole region rather than per country, so a heavy day in Paris consumes the same allowance as a heavy day in London. If your trip spans both, budget on the combined figure rather than assuming two separate caps.

**True unlimited (no throttling, no cap):** You pay for a plan and get unlimited high-speed data for the full duration. These plans are rare and don't exist in the UK travel eSIM space. Even [Three UK](https://www.three.co.uk/) — the network that pioneered truly unlimited data in Britain — applies traffic management policies during peak hours for its direct consumer unlimited plans, according to Three's published terms (2026).

**Soft-cap unlimited (throttled after a threshold):** This is the most common model. You get a set amount of high-speed data per day — typically 1GB to 5GB — after which your speed drops to 128kbps-1Mbps. You retain "unlimited" data at the reduced speed. At 128kbps, you can send WhatsApp messages but can't load modern web pages in under 30 seconds. At 1Mbps, web browsing, maps, and even audio calls remain functional. That gap — 128kbps vs 1Mbps — is the difference between a usable connection and a dead weight on your phone.

**Capped unlimited (hard limit per day):** Some providers give a fixed daily high-speed allowance — typically 500MB to 1GB — and throttle you to 256kbps until the next day resets the allowance. These are effectively limited-data plans wearing an "unlimited" costume.

A 1GB-per-day "unlimited" plan delivers roughly 30GB over a month. You could buy a straightforward 30GB fixed-data plan for less and have the exact same experience. The "unlimited" label is a marketing choice, not a technical specification. Don't fall for it.

## What is the Fair Usage Policy on UK unlimited eSIM plans?

The Fair Usage Policy (FUP) on UK unlimited eSIM plans is the daily or total high-speed data threshold after which your connection speed drops. Every unlimited plan has one — and it determines the real usable data you receive, not the "unlimited" marketing label on the checkout page.

The FUP typically works in one of two ways:

**Daily FUP:** You get a fixed amount of high-speed data per day (500MB to 5GB). Once you exceed it, your speed drops for the remainder of that day. The clock resets at midnight (UTC or UK time, depending on provider).

**Total FUP:** You get a fixed amount of high-speed data for the entire plan period (e.g., 30GB over 30 days). Once exceeded, you're throttled for the remaining days. This is less common in UK travel eSIMs.

|---|---|---|---|

| 2GB/day | Roami unlimited | 60GB | 512Kbps — usable for basics |
| 5GB/day | Saily unlimited | 150GB | 1Mbps — still functional |
| No published cap | Ubigi unlimited | N/A | N/A |

A 2GB daily FUP delivers roughly 60GB of high-speed data over a month — more than enough for almost any traveller. Very few people exceed 2GB of mobile data per day, even with heavy usage. The critical question isn't "how much high-speed data do I get?" — it's "what happens after I use it?" Saily's 1Mbps post-cap speed and Ubigi's no-cap policy are the most forgiving. Nomad's hard cap is the least — pass 2GB and your data pauses entirely. You're not comparing data caps at that point. You're comparing whether your phone remains functional after lunch.
