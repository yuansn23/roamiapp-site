## France's Four Mobile Networks: Overview

France is unusual in having four major mobile network operators. Most developed countries have three. This extra competition keeps prices lower than in the UK or Germany but means coverage varies more between providers.

| Network | Market Share | Population Coverage | 5G Cities | Prepaid eSIM for Tourists | Best Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orange | ~40% | ~95% | 200+ | Yes (Orange Travel) | Widest coverage |
| SFR | ~20% | ~90% | 150+ | No (MVNOs only) | Strong in Lyon/Marseille |
| Bouygues | ~20% | ~90% | 180+ | No (MVNOs only) | Fastest urban 5G |
| Free | ~15% | ~85% | 100+ | Yes (Free Mobile) | Cheapest plans |

Each network operates its own physical infrastructure — phone masts, fiber backhaul, spectrum licenses — and sells access to both direct customers and wholesale partners. When you buy a travel eSIM from Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi, or any other provider, you are buying wholesale access to one or more of these four networks. The quality of your eSIM experience depends directly on which of these networks your eSIM provider has partnered with.

The [Autorité de Régulation des Communications Électroniques (ARCEP)](https://www.arcep.fr/), France's telecom regulator, publishes annual coverage data and conducts independent speed tests across all four networks. The data in this guide draws from ARCEP's 2025-2026 reports and real-world testing on TGV routes and in major tourist destinations.

## Orange France: Deep Dive

Orange is France's dominant mobile network and the safest choice for travelers who want reliable coverage across the entire country.

### Orange Coverage

Orange's 4G network reaches approximately 95% of the French population. More importantly for tourists, Orange has the best rural coverage of any French carrier. In Provence, the Loire Valley, Normandy, and the Dordogne — regions that attract significant tourist traffic — Orange maintains signal in small villages and along country roads where SFR, Bouygues, and Free drop out.

In cities, Orange coverage is excellent. Paris has full 5G across all arrondissements. Lyon, Marseille, Nice, and Bordeaux all have comprehensive Orange 5G. The Paris Metro has Orange 4G/5G on all station platforms, with underground coverage on Lines 1 and 14. CDG and Orly airports have strong Orange 5G throughout all terminals.

Orange's 5G network covers more than 200 cities and towns across France, making it the largest 5G footprint of any French carrier.

### Orange Speed Performance

ARCEP's 2025 speed tests show Orange averaging 112 Mbps download on 4G and 210 Mbps on 5G in urban areas. In rural areas, Orange maintains 20-50 Mbps on 4G, which is significantly faster than competitors in the same locations.

Orange's network capacity is the highest in France, which means it handles congestion better than other networks. In tourist-heavy areas during peak season — central Paris in August, the Promenade des Anglais in July, Disneyland during Christmas — Orange maintains usable speeds while Free and SFR often slow to a crawl.

**Orange tradeoffs:** Travel eSIMs on Orange cost the same or slightly more than alternatives on other networks. Orange Travel eSIM is significantly more expensive than data-only eSIMs on the same network.

## Free Mobile France: Deep Dive

Free Mobile is France's price disruptor. Launched in 2012, it shook up the French mobile market by offering plans at roughly half the price of incumbents. For budget travelers staying in major cities, Free-connected eSIMs offer the best value. For anyone venturing outside urban areas, Free's coverage limitations become apparent.

### Free Coverage

Free's 4G network reaches approximately 85% of the French population. The gap with Orange (95%) is 10 percentage points, but in practical terms, Free's coverage is weaker in several important ways:

- **Rural gaps:** Free has significant coverage holes in rural France. Small villages in Provence, the Lot Valley, the Morvan, and the Massif Central often have no Free signal. If your itinerary includes any time outside major cities, Free's coverage is a risk.

- **Indoor performance:** Free's signal penetrates buildings less effectively than Orange or SFR. In hotels, restaurants, and underground spaces, Free often loses signal while other networks maintain connectivity. This is particularly noticeable in Paris metro stations and older stone buildings common in French city centers.

- **Tourist congestion:** Free deprioritizes traffic during network congestion. In tourist-heavy areas during peak season, Free users experience significant slowdowns. At Disneyland Paris, Free is noticeably slower than Orange or SFR during peak hours. On the Promenade des Anglais in Nice in August, Free can become nearly unusable in the afternoon.

Free's 5G network covers approximately 100 cities, fewer than Orange (200+) and Bouygues (180+).

### Free Speed Performance

In ideal conditions — uncongested urban areas with good signal — Free delivers competitive speeds. ARCEP tests show Free averaging 85 Mbps on 4G and 165 Mbps on 5G in central Paris. However, these averages drop significantly in congested conditions, especially during summer tourist season.

Free's speed degradation under load is more pronounced than any other French network. In ARCEP's 2025 congestion tests, Free speeds dropped by 60-70% during peak hours in tourist areas, compared to 20-30% for Orange.

## SFR France: Deep Dive

SFR, owned by Altice France, is the third-largest French mobile network by subscribers. It has strong coverage in major cities and along the Mediterranean coast, making it a solid choice for travelers visiting southern France.

### SFR Coverage

SFR's 4G network covers approximately 90% of the population. Its coverage is strongest in:

- **Lyon and the Rhone-Alpes region:** SFR's historical stronghold. Coverage in Lyon is excellent, often matching or exceeding Orange.
- **Marseille and the Mediterranean coast:** SFR has invested heavily in coastal coverage. From Marseille to Menton, SFR maintains strong 4G/5G.
- **Paris:** SFR coverage in Paris is good but slightly behind Orange and Bouygues in raw speed. Metro coverage is available at most stations.

SFR's weakest areas are rural northern France and parts of the Massif Central. In Normandy and Brittany, SFR coverage has gaps that Orange does not.

### SFR Speed Performance

SFR delivers competitive speeds in its coverage areas. ARCEP's 2025 tests show SFR averaging 95 Mbps on 4G and 175 Mbps on 5G in urban areas. In Lyon specifically, SFR was the fastest network tested.

SFR's network capacity is moderate. It handles congestion better than Free but not as well as Orange. In heavily touristed areas, SFR maintains usable speeds but can slow down during peak hours.

### SFR France eSIM Travel Plan

SFR does not sell a prepaid eSIM directly to tourists. Unlike Orange (Orange Travel) and Free (Free Mobile prepaid), SFR has not launched a tourist-focused eSIM product. Travelers who want SFR coverage must either:

1. **Buy a travel eSIM that uses the SFR network** — Holafly (Bouygues and SFR), Saily (Orange and SFR), and aloSIM (Orange and SFR) all include SFR access.
2. **Buy a physical SFR prepaid SIM at an SFR store** — Requires passport, in-person visit, and SIM swapping.

For most travelers, the first option is more practical. An SFR-connected travel eSIM provides SFR coverage without the need to find a store or swap SIMs.

### SFR for Southern France Travel

SFR is a particularly good choice for travelers visiting the French Riviera. SFR's Mediterranean coverage is second only to Orange, and in some coastal areas (Marseille's Old Port, the Corniche in Nice), SFR performance matches or exceeds Orange.

### SFR Verdict

SFR is a strong middle-ground option. It does not have Orange's rural coverage or Bouygues's urban speed, but it offers reliable connectivity in the places most tourists go — Paris, Lyon, Marseille, and the Riviera.

**SFR is best for:** Travelers visiting Lyon and the Rhone-Alpes region, travelers on the French Riviera, and anyone who wants a dual-network eSIM that pairs SFR with Orange.

**SFR tradeoffs:** No direct prepaid eSIM for tourists, weaker rural coverage than Orange, and moderate congestion handling.

## Bouygues Telecom: Deep Dive

Bouygues Telecom is the second-largest French mobile network by coverage quality, despite being the third-largest by subscriber count. It has invested heavily in urban 5G and delivers the fastest average speeds in French cities.

### Bouygues Coverage

Bouygues's 4G network covers approximately 90% of the population. Its coverage profile is similar to SFR — strong in cities, adequate in towns, weaker in rural areas — but with a few key differences:

- **Urban 5G leadership:** Bouygues has the most extensive urban 5G network in France. In Paris, Lyon, Nice, and Bordeaux, Bouygues 5G is available in more locations than any competitor.
- **TGV corridor coverage:** Bouygues has invested in coverage along major TGV routes. On the Paris-Lyon and Paris-Marseille lines, Bouygues performance is excellent.
- **Rural gaps:** Like SFR, Bouygues has coverage gaps in rural France, particularly in the Massif Central, parts of Normandy, and the Ardennes.

### Bouygues Speed Performance

Bouygues is the speed leader among French networks. ARCEP's 2025 tests show Bouygues averaging 135 Mbps on 4G and 245 Mbps on 5G in urban areas — the fastest of any French carrier. In central Paris, Bouygues 5G speeds regularly exceed 300 Mbps in low-congestion conditions.

This speed advantage makes Bouygues the best choice for travelers who need fast data for video calls, large uploads, or bandwidth-intensive applications.

### Bouygues France eSIM Prepaid Plan for Visitors

Bouygues does not sell a prepaid eSIM directly to tourists. Like SFR, there is no Bouygues-branded tourist eSIM product. However, several major travel eSIM providers use Bouygues as a network partner:

- **Ubigi:** Bouygues-only. The most popular Bouygues-connected travel eSIM.
- **Holafly:** Bouygues and SFR. Unlimited data on Bouygues network.
- **Multi-network eSIMs:** Switch between all four networks for optimal connectivity.

For travelers who want a Bouygues France eSIM prepaid plan for visitors, Ubigi is the most direct option. Ubigi customers get Bouygues's full 4G/5G network at competitive prices.

### Bouygues for Urban Travelers

Bouygues is the best network for travelers spending most of their time in French cities. Its 5G footprint in Paris, Lyon, Nice, Marseille, and Bordeaux is the largest of any carrier, and its speed advantage in these cities is significant.

For a week in Paris with heavy use of Google Maps, social media, and video calls, Bouygues through Ubigi or a multi-network eSIM provides the fastest experience.

### Bouygues Verdict

Bouygues is the speed king of French mobile networks. If raw performance in cities matters to you, Bouygues is the best choice.

**Bouygues is best for:** Speed-focused travelers in cities, Ubigi users who want fast connectivity, and anyone on TGV routes between major cities.

**Bouygues tradeoffs:** No direct prepaid eSIM for tourists, weaker rural coverage than Orange, and fewer travel eSIM partners compared to Orange.

## Network Speeds in Practice: What Tourists Actually Experience

Speed test averages from [ARCEP](https://www.arcep.fr/) and [Ookla](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/france) provide a useful benchmark, but real-world speeds for tourists depend on location, time of day, and network congestion. Here is what you can actually expect on each network in the places you will be.

### Paris City Center Speeds

In central Paris (1st-8th arrondissements), all four networks deliver strong performance, but the differences are measurable:

- **Bouygues:** 150-300 Mbps on 5G during off-peak hours, dropping to 60-100 Mbps during peak tourist hours (11 AM-3 PM) in high-traffic areas near the Louvre and Champs-Elysees.
- **Orange:** 100-200 Mbps on 5G during off-peak, 50-80 Mbps during peak hours. Orange's greater network capacity means less speed drop during congestion.
- **SFR:** 80-180 Mbps on 5G off-peak, 40-70 Mbps during peak hours. Performance is strongest in the Marais and the Left Bank.
- **Free:** 60-150 Mbps on 5G off-peak, dropping to 15-40 Mbps during peak hours. Free's speed degradation under load is the most significant.

### TGV Train Speeds

On high-speed trains, network performance varies by route segment:

- **Open countryside (80% of routes):** All networks deliver 20-60 Mbps on 4G. Enough for streaming, video calls, and browsing.
- **Urban approaches (15% of routes):** Speeds increase to 50-150 Mbps. Best for downloading content before entering tunnels.
- **Tunnels (5% of routes):** No signal on any network. The most common tunnel dead zones are on the Paris-Lyon route (TGV tunnel near Montbard, 8 minutes) and the Paris-Marseille route (various shorter tunnels in the south).

Orange reconnects fastest after tunnels, typically within 5-10 seconds. Bouygues and SFR reconnect within 10-20 seconds. Free can take 30-60 seconds to re-establish a connection.

### Disneyland Paris Speeds

Network performance at Disneyland Paris varies dramatically by season:

- **Low season (January-February, September-October):** All networks perform well. Bouygues and Orange both deliver 50-100 Mbps throughout the park.
- **Peak season (July-August, Christmas, school holidays):** Orange maintains 15-30 Mbps in most areas. Bouygues drops to 10-20 Mbps near crowded attractions. SFR drops to 5-15 Mbps. Free becomes nearly unusable near the main entrance and popular ride queues, with speeds dropping below 2 Mbps.

The practical effect: On Free at Disneyland during summer, expect Google Maps to load slowly or time out, Instagram to fail uploading, and WhatsApp calls to drop. On Orange, everything works but slower than normal.

## France Network MVNO Guide: Which Virtual Operators Use Which Network

Mobile Virtual Network Operators (MVNOs) are companies that do not own their own mobile infrastructure but rent access from one of the four main networks. Several MVNOs offer eSIMs and are worth knowing about for travelers who want cheaper access to a specific network.

### Orange MVNOs

Orange has the most extensive MVNO ecosystem in France. MVNOs on Orange include:

- **Sosh** (Orange-owned): Budget brand offering the same Orange coverage at lower prices. Sosh offers eSIM to French residents and long-term visitors.
- **NRJ Mobile:** Runs on Orange. Offers prepaid plans but eSIM availability is limited.
- **Cdiscount Mobile:** Runs on Orange. Competitive pricing. Limited eSIM support for short-term visitors.

Orange MVNOs provide Orange's full coverage but at lower prices. The trade-off is deprioritization during congestion — Orange MVNO users get slower speeds than Orange direct customers when the network is busy.

### SFR MVNOs

SFR's MVNO partners include:

- **RED by SFR** (SFR-owned): Offers competitive prepaid plans. eSIM available but requires in-store setup for prepaid.
- **Auchan Telecom:** Runs on SFR. Budget pricing, limited prepaid eSIM availability.
- **Coriolis Telecom:** Runs on SFR. Data-focused plans, limited eSIM for short-term tourists.

### Bouygues MVNOs

Bouygues has fewer MVNOs than Orange but some notable ones:

- **B&You** (Bouygues-owned): Bouygues's budget brand. Offers eSIM. Same Bouygues coverage at lower prices.
- **Euro-Information Telecom:** Runs on Bouygues. Niche provider.

### Free MVNOs

Free itself is already the cheapest main network. Its MVNOs include:

- **Free Mobile:** The main brand already competes on price. Its prepaid option is cheaper than most MVNOs on other networks.
- **Di mobile:** Runs on Free. Extremely low prices. eSIM availability for tourists is limited.

### MVNO Verdict for Tourists

For most tourists visiting France for 1-4 weeks, MVNOs are not the most practical option. The registration process typically requires a French address and bank account, and eSIM activation for short-term visitors is inconsistent across MVNOs. Travel eSIMs from Airalo, Ubigi, Holafly, and multi-network providers are simpler to buy and activate without French residency requirements.

For long-term visitors (3+ months), an MVNO like Sosh (Orange) or B&You (Bouygues) with a French bank account provides better value than repeated travel eSIM top-ups.

## Laws and Regulations Affecting eSIM in France

### No Internet Firewall

Unlike China, France has no internet firewall or content restrictions. Google Maps, WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and all major websites work without restriction on any French network. This is relevant for travelers coming from countries with internet restrictions who may be uncertain about what will work.

### No Passport Registration for Data-Only eSIMs

French law requires ID verification for physical SIM purchases and prepaid eSIMs that include a French phone number (like Orange Travel and Free Mobile). Data-only travel eSIMs are generally not subject to this requirement. You can buy, install, and use a data-only eSIM without providing any identification.

### ARCEP Regulatory Oversight

ARCEP, France's electronic communications regulator, publishes comprehensive coverage data and conducts independent network testing. Their reports are publicly available and provide authoritative coverage information for all four networks. The [ARCEP website](https://www.arcep.fr/) publishes coverage maps, speed test results, and quality of service reports for Orange, SFR, Bouygues, and Free.

## Emergency Services on French Networks

All French mobile networks provide free access to emergency services regardless of whether you have an active plan, credit, or even a SIM card installed. The key emergency numbers in France are:

- **112:** The pan-European emergency number. Works on all networks, all the time. Calls are answered in French and English.
- **15:** SAMU (medical emergencies)
- **17:** Police
- **18:** Fire brigade
- **114:** SMS emergency number for people with hearing or speech difficulties

Emergency calls on French networks are prioritized over regular traffic. If all network channels are congested, an emergency call will still connect. This applies equally to Orange, SFR, Bouygues, and Free.

### Network Coverage in Emergency Situations

ARCEP requires all French networks to maintain minimum coverage along major roads and in populated areas for emergency access. Orange has the most comprehensive emergency coverage due to its wider rural footprint. On remote hiking trails and in national parks, Orange is the network most likely to provide a usable signal for emergency calls.

## Practical Tips for Getting the Best Network Connection

Regardless of which network your eSIM uses, these tips will improve your connectivity in France.

### Manual Network Selection

If your eSIM is not connecting or seems slow, try manual network selection:

1. Go to Settings > Cellular > Network Selection (iPhone) or Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Network Operators (Android)
2. Turn off Automatic selection
3. Wait for the list of available networks to populate
4. Select Orange first (best coverage), then try Bouygues or SFR if Orange does not connect

Manual selection is particularly useful at airports, train stations, and other arrival points where automatic network selection can take 30-60 seconds to find the best network. For device-specific instructions, see [Apple's eSIM support guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118670).

### Airplane Mode Reset

If you arrive in France and your eSIM shows "No Service" or "SOS Only":
1. Turn on Airplane Mode
2. Wait 10 seconds
3. Turn off Airplane Mode
4. Wait 15-30 seconds for the phone to re-register on the network

This forces your phone to re-scan available networks and often resolves first-connection issues. If you continue to experience connection problems, our [France eSIM troubleshooting guide](/blog/france-esim-troubleshooting-guide/) covers common activation and connectivity issues.

### Data Roaming Must Be Enabled

This is the most common setup mistake. eSIMs for travel require data roaming to be enabled on the eSIM line specifically:

- **iPhone:** Settings > Cellular > [Select your France eSIM] > Data Roaming > ON
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager > [Select your France eSIM] > Data Roaming > ON

Data roaming on a travel eSIM is not the same as carrier roaming. It simply allows the eSIM to connect to a French network. It does not trigger home carrier charges.

### Best Times for Network Performance

Network congestion in France follows predictable patterns:

- **Best performance:** Early morning (7-9 AM) and late evening (after 9 PM)
- **Worst performance:** Midday (12-2 PM) and afternoon (3-5 PM) in tourist areas
- **Peak congestion seasons:** July-August (summer holidays), December (Christmas markets), school holiday weeks

If you need to make an important video call or download large files, schedule it for early morning when networks are least congested.

## Network Recommendations for Specific Destinations

### Best Network for Disneyland Paris

Disneyland Paris is the most network-intensive tourist destination in France. The combination of high visitor density, indoor queues, and heavy social media usage creates congestion that challenges all networks.

**Orange is the best network for Disneyland Paris by a wide margin.** Orange has invested in additional capacity at the park and maintains usable speeds even during peak summer and Christmas periods. ### Best Network for TGV Travel

For train travel, a multi-network eSIM provides the best experience because coverage varies by route segment. However, if you must pick one network:

**Orange is the best single network for TGV travel.** It maintains signal in more rural sections between cities, reconnects fastest after tunnels, and has the most consistent performance across different TGV lines.

Bouygues is the best alternative, particularly on the Paris-Lyon and Paris-Marseille high-speed lines where Bouygues has invested in corridor coverage.

### Best Network for Paris City Trip

For a purely urban Paris trip, the choice depends on your priority:

- **Fastest speeds:** Bouygues. The best 5G performance in central Paris.
- **Best metro coverage:** Orange. 4G/5G at all stations and in tunnels on Lines 1 and 14.
- **Best value:** Free. Adequate speeds for less money, but expect congestion in tourist areas.

For the French Riviera, coverage is excellent from all networks in city centers, but varies on coastal roads and in hill villages:

- **Best overall:** Orange. Covers the coastal corridor and hill villages most reliably.

## Final Verdict: Which France Network Should You Choose?

The France network comparison comes down to three scenarios.

### Scenario 1: You visit multiple regions including rural areas

**Choose Orange.** The coverage advantage in rural France — Provence villages, Normandy countryside, Loire Valley chateaux — is significant. Orange is the only network that reliably works in small French towns and along country roads. An Airalo eSIM on Orange or Orange Travel provides the widest coverage of any option.

### Scenario 2: You stay in cities only (Paris, Lyon, Nice)

**Choose Bouygues or a budget option.** If you never leave the main tourist zones of French cities, Bouygues offers the fastest speeds through Ubigi, and Free offers the lowest prices through budget eSIMs. Orange is still excellent in cities, but you are paying a premium for rural coverage you are not using.

**Choose a multi-network eSIM.** A [france esim](/france-esim/) that automatically switches between Orange, SFR, Bouygues, and Free eliminates the need to pick one network. The system adapts to whatever network is strongest at your location — Orange in rural areas, Bouygues in cities, SFR on the Riviera coast, and the best available network on TGV routes. A multi-network eSIM with automatic carrier switching provides this flexibility across all four French carriers.
