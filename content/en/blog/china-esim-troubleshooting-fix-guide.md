## 1. No Signal or No Service After Arrival

**Problem:** You land in Beijing, Shanghai, or Guangzhou, and your phone shows "No Service," "SOS Only," or "Searching..." Your China eSIM is not connecting to the network.

**Cause:** When your phone connects to a Chinese tower for the first time, automatic network selection often fails. The phone may try to register on a carrier your eSIM does not support, or the international roaming handshake between your eSIM provider and the local carrier stalls.

### Fix: Manual Network Selection (iOS)

1. Open **Settings** > **Cellular** > select your China eSIM line.
2. Tap **Network Selection**.
3. Turn **Automatic OFF**. Your phone scans for available networks.
4. Wait 10-30 seconds for the list to populate.
5. Select **China Mobile** (most compatible with international eSIM providers).

### Fix: Manual Network Selection (Android)

1. Open **Settings** > **Connections** > **SIM Manager** or **Network & Internet**.
2. Select your China eSIM.
3. Tap **Mobile Networks** > **Network Operators**.
4. Turn off **Select Automatically**.
5. Wait for the scan, then pick **China Mobile**.

**Why China Mobile first:** China Mobile owns the largest 4G/LTE and 5G infrastructure in the country. A quality **[china eSIM](/china-esim/)** like Roami with automatic carrier switching will try China Mobile first, then fall back to China Unicom or China Telecom depending on which tower has the strongest signal at your location. If China Mobile does not work, try **China Unicom** next. China Telecom is a distant third for roaming eSIM compatibility.

**If manual selection shows no networks:** Your phone may be in an area with weak coverage (airport parking structures, underground train stations). Move to an open area near a window and try again. If you just cleared customs, give the network 5-10 minutes to register your device.

**If the list is empty for 60+ seconds:** Toggle Airplane Mode on, wait 10 seconds, toggle it off, then repeat the manual network selection steps.

## 2. China eSIM Activation Failed or Stuck on Activating

**Problem:** You scan the QR code or enter the activation details, and the process hangs at "Activating..." for minutes. Or you get "China eSIM activation failed error" immediately. Some users see "Unable to activate on China Unicom" as the error message.

**Cause:** Activation requires an internet connection to download the eSIM profile. If you are trying to activate on airport Wi-Fi (which often blocks connection or requires SMS verification you cannot receive), the process stalls. The Great Firewall can also interrupt the profile download if the provider's activation server is outside China.

### Fix: Delete and Retry with Correct Steps

1. **Delete the stuck profile:**
   - **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > tap the eSIM that says "Activating..." > **Remove Cellular Plan**.
   - **Android:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > tap the eSIM > **Remove**.

2. **Connect to a reliable Wi-Fi network first.** The hotel Wi-Fi (Mariott, Hilton, Shangri-La) typically works. If you are at the airport, try the China Telecom or China Mobile free Wi-Fi (requires scanning your passport at a kiosk — ask at the information desk).

3. **Restart your phone.** This clears the stuck activation state.

4. **Activate with the provider's app instead of QR code.** Many providers (a China eSIM, Airalo, Holafly) let you install the eSIM directly from their app. This bypasses QR code scanner issues and usually has better error handling.

5. **Manually enter the activation details.** Every eSIM comes with an SM-DP+ address and activation code. Enter these manually instead of scanning:
   - **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM > Enter Details Manually.
   - **Android:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > Add Mobile Plan > Enter code instead.

6. **If still stuck: activate over VPN.** Download a VPN app on Wi-Fi before arriving, connect to a server outside China, then retry activation. The VPN provides a direct path to the activation server. If you did not pre-install a VPN, ask your hotel front desk — many business hotels maintain VPN access for international guests.

**Why this works:** The Great Firewall intermittently throttles or drops connections to eSIM provisioning servers located outside mainland China. A VPN bypasses this. Once the profile is downloaded and installed, the eSIM operates natively on China's networks — you do not need the VPN for the eSIM to work.

**Still failing?** Check that your phone is not [carrier locked](#10-carrier-locked-phone-issues). A carrier-locked phone cannot accept an eSIM from a different provider.

## 3. QR Code Not Scanning

**Problem:** The eSIM QR code email arrives on your phone, but the camera will not read it. You get "Invalid QR Code," "No cellular plan found," or nothing happens when you scan.

**Cause:** Common causes — screen brightness too low on the device displaying the QR code, the code is damaged or blurry in the email, or you are scanning from the same phone that needs the eSIM (you cannot scan a QR code displayed on the phone's own screen).

### Fix: Manual Entry Workaround

Every eSIM QR code encodes exactly three pieces of data:

- **SM-DP+ Address** (the server where the profile lives)
- **Activation Code** (your unique token)
- **Confirmation Code** (optional, sometimes blank)

### Step-by-step manual entry:

1. Open the eSIM confirmation email and look for the **SM-DP+ address** and **activation code**. These are usually labeled clearly. If you cannot find them, contact provider support.

2. **iOS:**
   - Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM.
   - Tap **Enter Details Manually** at the bottom.
   - Enter the SM-DP+ address and activation code exactly as shown.
   - Tap Next and follow the prompts.

3. **Android:** (varies by manufacturer)
   - **Samsung:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > Add Mobile Plan > Scan QR code from carrier > tap the menu (three dots) > **Enter activation code**.
   - **Google Pixel:** Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs > Add SIM > Download a SIM instead? > **Enter code**.
   - **Xiaomi/Huawei/Oppo:** Settings > Mobile Network > SIM Management > eSIM > Add eSIM > **Enter manually**.

4. **Pro tip for one-device setups:** If you received the QR code on the same phone that needs the eSIM, forward the email to a travel companion's phone or the hotel business center computer. Display the QR code there, scan it with your phone. Or use the manual entry method above — it is faster and more reliable.

### QR code scanning best practices:

- Increase screen brightness to 100% on the displaying device.
- Clean your phone's camera lens.
- Hold the phone 15-20 cm (6-8 inches) from the QR code.
- Avoid direct sunlight glare on the screen.

## 4. APN Settings Not Working

**Problem:** The eSIM activates and shows a signal, but data does not work. Apps show "No internet connection." You have tried APN settings from your provider's setup guide, but they do not stick or do not help.

**Cause:** Many China eSIMs auto-configure APN settings, but some require manual entry. The Great Firewall further complicates things by blocking certain APN protocols. Additionally, dual-SIM setups can confuse which line's APN is active.

### Fix: Manual APN Configuration Per Provider

### Common APN values by provider:

| Provider | APN | Username | Password |
|---|---|---|---|
| a China eSIM | `roami` | (blank) | (blank) |
| Airalo | `airalo.com` | (blank) | (blank) |
| Holafly | `holafly` | (blank) | (blank) |
| Nomad | `nomad` | (blank) | (blank) |

### iOS APN Setup:

The frustrating reality: iOS does not expose APN settings for eSIM lines in the main Settings menu unless the carrier profile allows it. Here is the workaround:

1. Settings > Cellular > select your China eSIM line.
2. Tap **Cellular Data Network** (appears only for some carriers — if you do not see it, skip to the profile method below).
3. Enter the APN exactly as your provider specifies.
4. Leave Username and Password blank unless specified.

### If "Cellular Data Network" does not appear:

- Download and install your provider's APN configuration profile (Airalo and a China eSIM both offer these on their help pages).
- **iOS:** Settings > General > VPN & Device Management > install the APN profile.
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Access Point Names > tap + or Add > enter the APN > Save > select it as active.

### Android APN Setup (detailed):

1. Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks.
2. Tap **Access Point Names**.
3. Tap the **+** (Add) button.
4. Enter **Name:** `China eSIM`, **APN:** your provider's APN value.
5. Scroll down — leave **Proxy, Port, Username, Password** blank unless your provider specifies them.
6. Tap the three-dot menu > **Save**.
7. Select the new APN from the list (a dot appears next to it).

**Important Android note:** If you have a dual-SIM setup, make sure the correct SIM is selected for mobile data before configuring the APN. Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > **Mobile Data** should show your China eSIM, not your home SIM.

### Troubleshooting APN problems:

- After saving the APN, toggle Airplane Mode ON then OFF to force the phone to reload network settings.
- Restart your phone after APN changes.
- If the APN resets automatically, your provider may push an OTA (over-the-air) configuration that conflicts with manual entries. In this case, delete the eSIM, reinstall, and let the auto-configuration complete fully before making manual changes.

## 5. Slow Data Speed

**Problem:** Your China eSIM works, but China eSIM slow data speed makes apps unusable. Web pages take 30+ seconds to load. Videos buffer endlessly. Speed tests show under 1 Mbps.

**Cause:** Three possible causes:
1. **Carrier congestion** — China Mobile towers in tourist-heavy areas get overloaded.
2. **Throttling by your eSIM provider** — some budget providers cap speeds after a data threshold.
3. **Great Firewall latency** — every international connection is inspected, adding 200-600ms of latency. This feels like slow data but is actually high latency.

### Fix 1: Switch Carriers

Not all Chinese carriers perform equally for eSIM roaming.

1. **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > select your eSIM > Network Selection > turn off Automatic > try **China Unicom** instead of China Mobile.
2. **Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Network Operators > search > select China Unicom.

China Unicom often provides better international routing speeds because its infrastructure has stronger peering with global carriers. In Shanghai and Guangzhou, China Unicom is frequently faster for eSIM users.

### Fix 2: Enable 5G (if available)

Some China eSIMs default to 4G/LTE to save battery.

- **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data Options > Voice & Data > select **5G On** (not "5G Auto").
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Network Mode > **5G/LTE/3G/2G**.

### Fix 3: Use a VPN for International Traffic

Counterintuitive but effective — a VPN often improves perceived speed for international browsing in China. The VPN compresses certain traffic and routes it through optimized servers instead of the Great Firewall's high-latency inspection path.

For specific VPN recommendations, see our [China eSIM VPN and Firewall Guide](/blog/china-esim-vpn-firewall-guide/).

### Fix 4: Change DNS

The Great Firewall also performs DNS poisoning — resolving google.com to a wrong IP that simply times out.

- **iOS:** Settings > Wi-Fi > tap the (i) next to your Wi-Fi network > Configure DNS > Manual > add `8.8.8.8` or `1.1.1.1`.
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > Wi-Fi > long-press your network > Manage Network Settings > DNS > Static > enter `8.8.8.8`.

Note: DNS changes on cellular data require a VPN or a DNS-over-HTTPS app like Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1 app.

## 6. Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook Blocked

**Problem:** Google Maps returns no data. WhatsApp messages show a single check mark (sent but not delivered). Instagram and Facebook feeds do not refresh. This is the single most common frustration for travelers using a China eSIM.

**Cause:** The Great Firewall of China blocks Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and hundreds of other foreign services at the network level. Your China eSIM connects to the internet through a Chinese carrier (China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom), which means all traffic routes through the Great Firewall. Your home carrier's roaming would face the same blocks — this is not an eSIM problem, it is China's internet policy.

For a deeper dive into how the firewall affects connectivity, see [Wikipedia's article on the Great Firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Firewall).

- **Install from provider website:** If the app store blocks the VPN app, download the APK (Android) or configuration profile (iOS) directly from the VPN provider's website.

### Fix 2: App-Specific Solutions

### Google Maps not working fix:
- Pre-download offline maps of your Chinese cities before arrival (Google Maps app > offline maps > select area).
- Use Apple Maps (works without VPN in China for basic navigation).
- Use Amap (Gaode Maps) or Baidu Maps — download from the Chinese App Store. These work perfectly without a VPN but are Chinese-language only. Point your phone camera at a Chinese menu in the app to use the English translation feature.

### WhatsApp blocked solution:

### Instagram and Facebook blocked fix:

## 7. VPN Keeps Disconnecting

**Problem:** Your VPN connects for 3-5 minutes, then drops. You reconnect, it drops again. The China eSIM VPN keeps disconnecting in a frustrating loop.

**Cause:** The Great Firewall actively detects and disrupts VPN connections using Deep Packet Inspection (DPI). When it identifies VPN traffic patterns (OpenVPN handshakes, WireGuard keepalives, IPSec packets), it injects RST packets to terminate the connection. Some VPN protocols resist this better than others, and your eSIM's carrier routing also affects stability.

### Fix 1: Switch VPN Protocol

Different VPN protocols have different resistance to DPI:

- **WireGuard** — fastest but increasingly detected in 2025-2026. Try it first.
- **OpenVPN over TCP 443** — disguises traffic as HTTPS. Slower but more stable.
- **OpenVPN over UDP** — faster but more detectable.
- **Stealth/Cloak protocols** — ExpressVPN's Lightway, NordVPN's NordLynx with obfuscation, Astrill's Stealth. These are designed specifically for restrictive networks.
- **Shadowsocks** — not a true VPN but a SOCKS5 proxy. Highly resistant to DPI. Many China-focused services like Astrill offer this as a protocol option.

### Fix 2: Change Server Location

Some VPN servers in specific countries (Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan) have congested or aggressively monitored routes into China.

- Try switching to a server in **Japan, South Korea, or the United States (West Coast)** .
- Avoid Hong Kong servers — traffic through Hong Kong often faces extra scrutiny from the firewall.
- Try 3-4 different server locations until you find one that stays connected.

### Fix 3: Enable Obfuscation

Most premium VPNs include obfuscation settings:

- **ExpressVPN:** Settings > Protocol > Automatic (obfuscation is built into Lightway protocol).
- **NordVPN:** Settings > Auto-connect > OpenVPN (TCP) with obfuscation enabled.
- **Astrill:** OpenVPN with Stealth or OpenWeb protocol — these are designed specifically for China and keep connections stable for hours.

### Fix 4: TCP Keepalive Configuration

On Android, you can adjust VPN keepalive settings (requires root, not recommended for most users). On iOS, this is not configurable.

### Fix 5: Switch eSIM Carrier

In some cases, unstable VPN connections are caused by aggressive packet inspection on one carrier's network:

- Switch from China Mobile to China Unicom via manual network selection (see section 1).
- China Unicom tends to be less aggressive with DPI than China Mobile.

### Fix 6: Use a Provider with Built-in Unblocking

This is the simplest solution. Rather than fighting with VPN disconnects on top of eSIM connectivity issues, use a [China eSIM](/china-esim/) provider that handles the unblocking layer for you. a China eSIM's service includes optimized routing that keeps Google and social apps accessible without a separate VPN maintaining a constant connection.

## 8. Dual SIM Conflict

**Problem:** Your home SIM is in slot 1 and your China eSIM is active, but data does not work. Or your home SIM loses signal completely. Or calls go to voicemail without ringing.

**Cause:** A China eSIM dual SIM conflict occurs when both lines compete for radio resources. Many travelers keep their home SIM active for SMS (bank 2FA, iMessage activation), and the phone struggles to maintain two active connections — especially when one is roaming on a Chinese network and the other is trying to connect to a home network 8,000 km away.

### Fix 1: Set the China eSIM as Primary for Data

- **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > **Cellular Data** > select your China eSIM. Then tap **Default Voice Line** > select your home SIM (if you want to receive calls on your home number).
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > **Mobile Data** > select China eSIM. **Voice calls** > select Home SIM or China eSIM as preferred.

### Fix 2: Disable Home SIM Roaming

Your home SIM, when kept active in China, will try to roam on Chinese networks. This can cause:

- Home SIM roaming charges (up to $10-20/MB on some carriers).
- Network registration conflicts where the phone resets connection when either SIM's carrier registration changes.

### iOS:
1. Settings > Cellular > tap your home SIM line.
2. Toggle **Data Roaming** OFF (keep it on only for your China eSIM).
3. Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data > select China eSIM.

### Android:
1. Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > tap your home SIM.
2. Toggle **Data Roaming** OFF.
3. Ensure **Mobile Data** switch is ON for the China eSIM only.

### Fix 3: Disable "Cellular Data Switching" (iOS)

iOS 17+ has a feature called **Cellular Data Switching** (also called "Allow Cellular Data Switching") that lets the phone automatically switch data between lines when one has a weak signal. This backfires in China because it tries to route data through your home SIM's roaming connection, which either does not work (no roaming plan) or costs a fortune.

- Settings > Cellular > **Cellular Data** > Toggle **Allow Cellular Data Switching** OFF.

### Fix 4: Adjust 5G/LTE Priority Per Line

Some phones let you set different network modes for each SIM:

- **Samsung:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > select a SIM > Network Mode > set home SIM to **4G/3G/2G** and China eSIM to **5G/4G/3G/2G** (or vice versa depending on which line needs more bandwidth).
- **iPhone:** iOS does not offer per-line network mode on dual SIM, but setting **Cellular Data Switching** to OFF (Fix 3 above) prevents the phone from shifting data between lines when one connection wobbles.

### Fix 5: Reset Network Settings (Radical but Effective)

If dual SIM conflicts persist after all the above, a full network settings reset clears out conflicting carrier profiles and registration states:

- **iOS:** Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > **Reset Network Settings**. This clears all Wi-Fi passwords and Bluetooth pairings, so only do this if you have those backed up or can re-enter them.
- **Android:** Settings > General Management > Reset > **Reset Network Settings**.

After the reset, re-add both SIMs fresh — home SIM first, then install the China eSIM. Configure data to the China eSIM before turning on data roaming on either line.

### Fix 6: If Nothing Else Works — Remove the Home SIM Physically

For Android phones particularly, radio contention between an active physical SIM and eSIM can cause persistent issues. Multiple travelers report that removing the home physical SIM completely resolves the "stuck on No Service for 30 seconds at a time" problem. Remove your home physical SIM, keep only the China eSIM active. You lose access to home SMS (including bank 2FA codes) though, so only do this as a last resort. Consider forwarding your home number to Google Voice or a VoIP service before traveling so you can still receive calls over data.

## 9. Signal Drops Frequently

**Problem:** Your signal goes from 3-4 bars to "No Service" repeatedly. The China eSIM signal drops every few minutes. You need to toggle Airplane Mode to reconnect.

**Cause:** Three explanations:
1. **Network handover failure** — moving between carrier towers triggers a registration re-check that fails.
2. **Carrier preference mismatch** — the eSIM is designed for China Mobile but your phone keeps trying China Unicom or China Telecom towers.
3. **SIM contention** — both lines periodically try to register on different carriers, causing the phone to drop one connection.

### Fix 1: Lock to a Single Carrier (Already covered in Section 1, but critical here)

This is the single most effective fix for signal drops:

- **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > Network Selection > OFF > **China Mobile**.
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Network Operators > **China Mobile**.

### Fix 2: Set Preferred Network Mode to 4G/LTE Only

5G in China uses different frequency bands than 4G (n41, n78, n79). If your phone supports some but not all 5G bands used by Chinese carriers, it can keep dropping 5G and reconnecting to 4G, creating the signal-drop loop.

- **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data Options > Voice & Data > **LTE** (not 5G Auto).
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Network Mode > **LTE/3G/2G** (skip 5G).

### Fix 3: Check for Carrier Settings Update (iOS)

Apple periodically pushes carrier setting updates that improve eSIM compatibility:

- Make sure your phone is connected to Wi-Fi (or has active data).
- Settings > General > About.
- Wait 10-15 seconds. If a carrier settings update is available, a popup appears asking you to update.
- Tap **Update**.

### Fix 4: Disable Wi-Fi Calling on Home SIM

When Wi-Fi Calling tries to activate over your China eSIM's data connection, it can trigger a network re-registration that drops both connections.

- **iOS:** Settings > Cellular > Home SIM > **Wi-Fi Calling** OFF.
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > Home SIM > **Wi-Fi Calling** OFF.

### Fix 5: Timing the Drop — Track the Pattern

Signal drops rarely happen randomly. Try to notice the pattern:

- **Drops every time you move between floors or enter an elevator** — this is normal for carrier handovers. The eSIM re-registers on each tower handshake. If drops last more than 30 seconds, it is a timing issue with the roaming registration. Lock the carrier manually (Fix 1).
- **Drops every 15-20 minutes exactly** — your home SIM may be doing periodic location updates to your home carrier, which forces both lines to re-register. Disable **Data Roaming** on the home SIM and set **Cellular Data Switching** OFF.
- **Drops only at certain times of day** — carrier tower maintenance or congestion. Nothing you can fix on your end; toggle Airplane Mode for 10 seconds to force reconnection.

### Fix 6: Try a Different eSIM Profile

If you have installed the eSIM on a phone you previously used in China with a different eSIM, residual carrier preferences from the old profile may interfere. Delete the current eSIM, restart the phone, and install it fresh. This clears any cached carrier data that might be pointing to the wrong network bands.

## 10. Carrier Locked Phone Issues

**Problem:** You insert or activate the China eSIM, but the phone immediately says "SIM Not Supported" or "Invalid SIM" or "This iPhone cannot be used with this carrier."

**Cause: China eSIM carrier locked phone issue.** If you bought your phone from a carrier like AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, Vodafone, or any carrier that locks devices to their network, the phone rejects any SIM or eSIM from a different provider. The lock is enforced at the hardware/firmware level — no amount of settings changes bypasses it.

### Fix 1: Check if Your Phone Is Carrier Locked

- **iOS:** Settings > General > About > look for **Carrier Lock**. If it says "No SIM restrictions," your phone is unlocked. If it says "SIM locked," it is locked.
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > SIM Manager. If adding a non-carrier eSIM fails with a lock error, the phone is locked. Alternatively, insert a different carrier's physical SIM — if it asks for an unlock code, the phone is locked.

### Fix 2: Request an Unlock from Your Carrier

Most carriers in the US and Europe provide free unlocks after certain conditions:

- **AT&T:** Device must be paid off, account in good standing. Free unlock at att.com/deviceunlock.
- **T-Mobile:** App-based unlock in the T-Mobile app. Most devices unlock automatically after 40 days on network.
- **Verizon:** Verizon phones sold after 2018 are automatically unlocked 60 days after purchase, no request needed.

## Preventing Problems Before You Arrive

Most China eSIM problems trace back to three things you can fix before your flight:

1. **Install the eSIM before you leave home.** Activation on your home Wi-Fi eliminates firewall-related profile download issues. The eSIM activates but does not start counting data until you connect to a Chinese network. This is the single best preventive step — it avoids 90% of activation failures.

2. **Install a VPN app before you arrive.** Download the app, create an account, and test it while on your home network. The Chinese app stores block VPN app searches, and you cannot download new apps without one. We recommend installing at least two VPN apps as backups in case one gets blocked between your download and your trip.

3. **Save offline maps.** Open Google Maps, select your Chinese cities, and download offline maps. These work without internet and continue working even while the live map struggles through the firewall. Download maps for Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and any other cities you plan to visit — each city map is 200-800 MB.

4. **Download WeChat.** Install WeChat and set up your account before arrival. You will need it to communicate with hotels, drivers, and restaurants regardless of your connectivity. ## Why China eSIM Problems Happen (The Technical Background)

Understanding why these issues occur helps you troubleshoot faster.

### The Great Firewall Effect

China's internet filtering system (the Great Firewall of China, or GFW) inspects all cross-border traffic. It controls which foreign servers Chinese network users can reach. When your China eSIM tries to:

- Download a profile from a server outside China → the firewall may throttle or block it.
- Connect to Google's servers → the firewall blocks them entirely.
- Establish a VPN tunnel → the firewall actively detects and disrupts VPN protocols.
- Connect to an SM-DP+ activation server located outside mainland China → the connection may timeout or reset mid-download.

The firewall uses several techniques to enforce these blocks:

| Technique | What It Does | Effect on Your Connection |
|---|---|---|
| DNS poisoning | Returns fake IP addresses for blocked domains | Blocked sites show timeout errors |
| IP blacklisting | Drops packets to known blocked server IPs | Cannot reach activation servers outside China |
| Deep Packet Inspection | Analyzes packet payloads to identify VPN traffic | VPN protocols detected and disrupted |
| TCP reset injection | Sends fake RST packets to terminate blocked connections | Active connections drop mid-session |

These techniques operate at different layers of the network stack, which is why a fix that works for one problem (e.g., manual APN configuration) does not help with another (e.g., Google access).

The firewall is not static — it evolves. New detection methods are deployed regularly, which is why a VPN that worked in 2024 may fail in 2026, and vice versa. For official technical reference, see the [GSMA's eSIM technical specification](https://www.gsma.com/esim/) for how eSIM provisioning works at the protocol level.

### Carrier Roaming Agreements

Your China eSIM works through a roaming agreement between your eSIM provider and one or more Chinese carriers. When this agreement has technical gaps (missing APN configuration, unsupported frequency bands, incomplete network registration parameters), the eSIM fails to connect properly.

Apple's [eSIM support documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT212780) and [Google's eSIM help page](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/9437173) provide device-specific compatibility information.

### Device Compatibility

Not all phones work equally well with China eSIMs. Here is a detailed compatibility breakdown:

### Apple iPhones:
- **iPhone XS, XR, 11, 12, 13 series** — excellent compatibility. These models support dual SIM (physical + eSIM) and work with all three Chinese carriers. Note that iPhone 13 and earlier support only one active eSIM at a time.
- **iPhone 14, 15, 16 series** — excellent compatibility. US models are eSIM-only (no physical SIM slot) but support dual active eSIMs. You can run your China eSIM alongside your home eSIM simultaneously.
- **China-market iPhones (sold in mainland China)** — do NOT have eSIM hardware. Chinese regulations require physical SIM slots for domestic phones, and Apple removes the eSIM chip from iPhones sold in China, Hong Kong, and Macau. If you bought your iPhone in China, you cannot use a China eSIM. You need a physical SIM from China Mobile or China Unicom instead.
- **Hong Kong/Macau-market iPhones** — these include both a physical SIM slot and eSIM capability, but dual-SIM functionality (physical + eSIM) is disabled on some Hong Kong models. Test before relying on it.

### Google Pixel Phones:
- **Pixel 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 series** — good compatibility. Pixel phones support eSIM natively and work with China Mobile and China Unicom. Some users report 5G band gaps on Pixel 7 and earlier — the phone may fall back to 4G in areas where Chinese phones maintain 5G.
- **Pixel 6a and older** — may not support all Chinese 4G LTE bands (particularly Band 40 used by China Mobile). If your phone drops to 3G frequently, this is likely the cause.

### Samsung Galaxy Phones:
- **Galaxy S20 and newer** — good compatibility with all Chinese carriers. Samsung's dual SIM implementation is mature and handles eSIM + physical SIM well.
- **Galaxy S23 FE, A series** — check band support. Samsung A-series phones sold outside Asia often lack Band 40 and Band 41, which China Mobile uses for 4G LTE.

### Chinese-Market Phones (Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, OnePlus China):
- These phones support eSIM inconsistently. Some models sold in mainland China have eSIM hardware disabled in software. If you have a Chinese-market phone, check the manufacturer's official eSIM support list before buying an eSIM.

**Important note on 5G bands:** Chinese carriers use unique 5G frequency bands:
- n41 (2500 MHz) — China Mobile's primary 5G band
- n78 (3500 MHz) — China Unicom and China Telecom 5G
- n79 (4500 MHz) — China Mobile's supplementary 5G band (not available on most international phones)

If your phone does not support n79, you will get 5G on China Unicom but may fall back to 4G on China Mobile in some areas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my China eSIM not working after arrival?

The most common reason is that your phone has not automatically selected the correct Chinese carrier. Go to Settings > Cellular (or Connections > SIM Manager on Android) > Network Selection > turn OFF Automatic > select **China Mobile** manually. If that does not fix it, your eSIM may have failed to activate properly — delete the profile and reinstall over a stable Wi-Fi connection.

### Why is my China eSIM data slow?

Two main causes: carrier congestion (China Mobile's towers are overloaded in tourist areas) and Great Firewall latency (international connections are inspected, adding 200-600ms). Try switching to China Unicom via manual network selection. A VPN also helps by routing traffic through optimized international gateways instead of the firewall's slow inspection path.

### China eSIM and VPN not working what to do?

If your VPN keeps disconnecting, switch protocols. OpenVPN over TCP port 443 (disguised as HTTPS traffic) is the most stable in China. Enable obfuscation if your VPN provider offers it. If no protocol stays connected, your eSIM provider's carrier routing may be the issue — try switching from China Mobile to China Unicom via manual network selection, as Unicom's DPI is typically less aggressive.

### Why does my eSIM show no service in China?

"No Service" means the phone cannot register on any Chinese network. This is almost always a carrier lock issue (phone locked to a specific carrier like AT&T or Verizon) or a manual network selection problem. Check Settings > General > About > Carrier Lock (iOS) or the equivalent on Android. If unlocked, perform manual network selection and pick China Mobile.

### Can I reinstall China eSIM if I delete it?

Yes, with some limits. Most providers allow 3-5 reinstallations per plan. You need your SM-DP+ address and activation code from the original purchase email, or you can use the provider's app to trigger a reinstallation. If you cannot find these details, contact support with your order number. The eSIM can only be active on one device at a time.

### How to fix Google not working with China eSIM?

Google is blocked by the Great Firewall at the network level. Your China eSIM routes through Chinese carriers, which enforce this block. A VPN is the only solution. Install a VPN app before arriving in China, connect to a server outside China, and all Google services (Maps, Search, Gmail, Drive) will work. Some eSIM providers like a China eSIM include built-in unblocking that bypasses the firewall without a separate VPN app.
