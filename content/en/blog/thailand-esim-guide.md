## What Is an eSIM and How Does It Work in Thailand?

**In short:** An eSIM is a digital SIM built into your phone. You install a network profile by scanning a QR code — no physical card needed. Thailand fully supports eSIM through AIS, TrueMove H, and DTAC. If your phone shows an EID number when you dial `*#06#`, it supports eSIM.

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a digital SIM soldered onto your phone’s motherboard. Instead of inserting a physical card into a tray, you download a network profile directly to your phone by scanning a QR code — the process usually takes under two minutes. The eSIM standard is maintained by the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/esim/esim-specification/), and Apple publishes a plain-language overview in its [eSIM support article](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118669).

Thailand fully supports eSIM. All three major operators — **AIS**, **TrueMove H**, and **DTAC** — offer eSIM for tourists and locals alike. Since the 2023 merger, DTAC and TrueMove H now form **True Corp**, so the market is effectively a duopoly of **AIS** and **True Corp**. Every travel eSIM you buy ultimately runs on one of these two networks.

If your phone shows an **EID number** when you dial `*#06#`, it supports eSIM.

**eSIM vs Physical SIM vs Pocket WiFi**

| Feature | Physical SIM | eSIM | Pocket WiFi |
|---------|--------------|------|-------------|
| Installation | Remove tray, insert card | Scan QR code, download profile | Separate device |
| Switching carriers | Need ejector tool, remove/insert/restart | Switch in settings | Not applicable |
| Loss risk | Small card, easy to lose | No physical card | Easy to leave behind |
| Keep home number | Original number inactive after removal | Home number stays active | Home number stays active |
| Battery | No extra battery | No extra battery | Needs daily charging |
| Best for | Older phones | Most modern phones | Groups with many devices |

You manage the eSIM in **Settings → Cellular / Mobile Data**. It lives inside your phone’s secure chip, not in the SIM tray.

**Key takeaway:** If your phone has an EID and is unlocked, you can use a Thailand eSIM. No physical SIM needed.

## Why Choose eSIM Over a Physical SIM in Thailand?

**Key takeaway:** An eSIM saves time at the airport, keeps your home number active, and eliminates the risk of losing a tiny card. For most travellers with a compatible phone, it’s the simplest option.

For most travellers, an eSIM is the better choice for three reasons.

**Save time.** At Bangkok’s Suvarnabhumi (BKK) and Don Mueang (DMK) airports, SIM counters can have queues of 20–40 minutes at peak times. With an eSIM you buy and install before you leave home, then activate with one tap after landing.

**Keep your home number.** Your physical SIM stays in the phone, so you still receive calls from home and bank SMS verification codes, while the Thai eSIM handles data. This is dual SIM dual standby.

**No card to lose.** There’s no tiny card to drop or lose. After your trip, delete the profile from settings.

If your phone supports eSIM, it’s usually the simpler option. Pocket WiFi still makes sense for groups with many devices, but it adds another device to carry and charge.

## How to Install a Thailand eSIM Before You Fly

**In short:** Confirm your phone supports eSIM and is unlocked, connect to Wi-Fi, then install the profile after purchase. Screenshot the QR code immediately. You can install before departure, but do not activate until you land.

Before you buy, confirm your phone supports eSIM (`*#06#`) and is unlocked, then connect to stable Wi-Fi. After purchase, screenshot the QR code immediately. You can install the profile before leaving, but **do not activate it until you land**.

### Thailand eSIM Pre-Installation Checklist

1. **Confirm your phone supports eSIM** — dial `*#06#` and look for an EID number.
2. **Confirm your phone is unlocked** — carrier-locked phones cannot use another carrier’s eSIM.
3. **Have stable Wi-Fi** — installation downloads the profile over the internet. Scan the QR code and download the profile at home.

**Key takeaway:** Install at home, activate in Thailand. This avoids failed activation and early countdown.

### Thailand eSIM QR Code: SM-DP+ and Activation Code

After purchase, the platform emails you a QR code or manual-entry details:

- **SM-DP+ address** — the server address for the eSIM profile
- **Activation code** — a unique code for your eSIM
- **QR code** — the easiest way to install

**Screenshot the QR code** and save it to your photos. If you can’t access email after landing, you can still install from your photos.

> Some plans, especially free trials, require you to wait until you arrive to install — otherwise the timer starts early. Read the provider’s instructions carefully.

### Thailand eSIM QR Code Won’t Scan? Quick Fixes

If your phone can’t scan the QR code, most platforms also provide **manual entry** details. Go to **Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → Enter Details Manually**, and enter the SM-DP+ address and activation code exactly as shown.

Common scan failures and fixes:
- Blurry QR image → ask the platform to resend, or use manual entry
- Low screen brightness → increase brightness and retry
- Dirty camera lens → clean it and retry

If manual entry also fails, contact your provider’s support.

**Bottom line:** Always screenshot the QR code and save the manual details. You may not have email access when you land.

## How to Activate a Thailand eSIM After Landing

**In short:** Thailand eSIMs must be activated in Thailand. After landing, connect to airport Wi-Fi, go to Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM, scan your saved QR code, turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line, and wait 2–5 minutes.

Thailand eSIMs must be activated in Thailand. After landing, connect to airport Wi-Fi, go to Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM, scan your saved QR code, turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line, and wait 2–5 minutes.

### When to Activate a Thailand eSIM (Avoid Early Activation)

Thai operator eSIMs need to connect to a Thai network to complete registration. Scanning too early, outside Thailand, causes activation to fail. This is the most common reason for a **Thailand eSIM not working** on day one.

**Step-by-step (iPhone and Android)**

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Buy the eSIM before departure — pay online, receive the QR code by email |
| 2 | **Screenshot the QR code** — in case you can’t access email after landing |
| 3 | After landing, connect to airport free Wi-Fi (available at BKK and DMK) |
| 4 | Settings → Mobile/Cellular → Add eSIM → Scan the QR code |
| 5 | Turn on **Data Roaming** for the eSIM line |
| 6 | Wait 2–5 minutes, then test with Google Maps or the browser |

**Important notes:**
- QR codes are typically **valid for 30 days**.
- Plan validity starts from **activation (installation)**, not purchase.
- The day you scan the QR code counts as day 1 (Thailand time, GMT+7).

**Key takeaway:** Do not activate outside Thailand. If activation fails, do not delete the eSIM — retry after landing.

### How to Activate a Thailand eSIM on iPhone

1. Connect to Wi-Fi.
2. **Settings → Cellular** (or Mobile Data).
3. Tap **Add eSIM**.
4. Scan the QR code you saved.
5. Label the line (e.g. “Thailand eSIM”).
6. Turn on **Data Roaming** for that line.
7. Set **Cellular Data** to the Thailand eSIM.
8. Keep your home SIM as **Default Voice Line**.

### How to Activate a Thailand eSIM on Android

1. Connect to Wi-Fi.
2. **Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs**.
3. Tap **Add eSIM** or **Download a SIM instead**.
4. Scan the QR code.
5. Enable **Data Roaming** for the eSIM.
6. Set mobile data to the eSIM.
7. Restart if needed.

**Bottom line:** Activation takes 2–5 minutes on a Thai network. Data Roaming is the step most people miss.

## Common eSIM Myths and Misunderstandings

**In short:** You don’t need Wi-Fi after installation. Installing does not activate. Not all eSIMs include a phone number. Unlimited is not always full speed. Never delete a failed eSIM.

**Myth 1: “I need Wi-Fi to use my eSIM.”**
You need Wi-Fi only for installation and QR scanning. Once installed and activated, the eSIM uses the local mobile network.

**Myth 2: “Installing the eSIM activates it immediately.”**
Installation and activation are separate. You can install before you fly; activation happens when the eSIM connects to a Thai network.

**Myth 3: “All eSIMs include a phone number.”**
Most international eSIMs are data-only. Thai local operator eSIMs include a Thai number, but they require passport registration.

**Myth 4: “Unlimited means full speed all the time.”**
Many unlimited plans have a fair-usage policy. Speeds drop after a daily high-speed allowance.

**Myth 5: “If activation fails, I should delete the eSIM.”**
Do not delete it. Most providers will not reissue the QR code. Keep the profile and retry after landing.

**Bottom line:** Understand these myths before you travel — they prevent most first-time eSIM mistakes.

**In short:** Turn off the eSIM line when not needed, download offline maps, and use a VPN on public Wi-Fi if you handle sensitive data. Saily is a privacy-focused option.

**Save battery:** Turn off the eSIM line when you do not need data, such as on a long flight or overnight. Use Wi-Fi when available.

## Thailand eSIM FAQ

**Q1: Can I install the eSIM before I arrive in Thailand?**
You can install the profile, but **don’t activate it**. You can scan the QR code before departure to download the profile, but leave that line turned off. The safer approach: screenshot the QR code, then scan and install after arriving using airport Wi-Fi.

**Q2: Can I activate a Thailand eSIM outside Thailand?**
**No.** Thai operator eSIMs must connect to a Thai network to complete registration. Activating early usually fails. If your phone shows “Activating” or “Activation failed” outside Thailand, don’t delete the eSIM — keep it and retry after landing.

**Q3: Can I use an eSIM without a physical SIM?**
Yes — if your phone supports eSIM, you can use it as your primary connection with no physical card inserted, as long as the device is eSIM-compatible and unlocked.

**Q4: Does Huawei support eSIM?**
Some Huawei models, such as Mate 40 and P40 series, support eSIM, but not all. Check your specific model before buying. If it doesn’t support eSIM, you’ll need a physical SIM.

**Q5: Can I use a Thailand eSIM and my home SIM at the same time?**
Yes — this is dual SIM dual standby. Set the Thailand eSIM as Cellular Data and keep your home SIM for calls and SMS. See the Dual SIM section above.

**Q6: What happens if I delete my Thailand eSIM by mistake?**
You usually **can’t reinstall it** — most providers don’t reissue QR codes. Contact support immediately. Some platforms, such as Roami, offer 24/7 live chat and may help with reissuance.

**Q7: My iPhone eSIM isn’t working — what do I do?**
Check in order: (1) Data Roaming is ON for the eSIM line; (2) the APN is correct; (3) Network Selection is Automatic or manually set to AIS/True; (4) restart your iPhone; (5) if it still fails, don’t delete the eSIM — contact your provider.

**Q8: Can I install a Thailand eSIM on a carrier-locked phone?**
No. Carrier-locked phones, such as US AT&T or T-Mobile contract phones, cannot use another carrier’s eSIM. Contact your carrier to unlock the phone before travelling.

**Q9: How do I activate a Thailand eSIM on iPhone?**
Install the profile before departure via **Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → scan the QR code**. After landing, connect to airport Wi-Fi, turn on **Data Roaming** for the eSIM line, and set Cellular Data to the Thailand eSIM. Activation takes 2–5 minutes. Full steps are in the iPhone section above.

**Q10: Does Apple Watch support a Thailand eSIM?**
Apple Watch eSIM support depends on the model and carrier. Most Thailand travel eSIMs do not support Apple Watch directly. If you need cellular on Apple Watch, check with the provider before buying. For iPad and Apple Watch eSIM details, see the **[iPad & Apple Watch eSIM guide](/faq/ipad-apple-watch-esim-support-guide/)**.

**Q11: How many eSIM profiles can I store?**
Most iPhones store 8 or more eSIM profiles, but only two lines can be active at once. Android devices vary by model. Delete unused profiles to free up space.

**Q12: Can I transfer my eSIM to a new phone?**
Some providers support transfer, but many Thailand travel eSIMs are tied to the first device. Check with your provider before deleting the old profile. For cross-platform transfer, see the **[eSIM transfer guide](/faq/how-to-transfer-esim-between-iphone-and-android/)**.
