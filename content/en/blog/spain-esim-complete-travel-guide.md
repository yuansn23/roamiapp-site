## Why Spain eSIM Is Different: 4 Networks & EU Roaming

Spain stands apart from most European destinations in how its mobile market operates. Understanding these differences helps you avoid overpaying or picking a plan that does not match how you actually use data while traveling.

### Four Networks, Not Three

Most European countries have three mobile network operators. Spain has four: Movistar (Telefonica), Orange, Vodafone, and Yoigo (MasMovil group). Each operates its own physical infrastructure with distinct coverage strengths. Movistar has the widest rural footprint, Orange leads in urban 5G density, Vodafone dominates along transport corridors, and Yoigo offers the most aggressive pricing. For a detailed side-by-side comparison, see our [Spain network comparison](/blog/spain-esim-movistar-orange-vodafone-comparison/). An eSIM provider that connects to only one network will leave you with gaps. Multi-network providers solve this by automatically switching between all four carriers based on real-time signal strength, so your phone picks the strongest available network at every location.

### The CNMC Regulates Roaming Differently

Spain's telecom regulator, the CNMC ([https://www.cnmc.es](https://www.cnmc.es)), enforces some of the strictest wholesale access rules in Europe. This means MVNOs (mobile virtual network operators) can rent network access at regulated rates, which drives down prices across the board. Spain has over 30 active MVNOs, more than Germany, France, or Italy. For travelers, this translates to more plan choices and lower per-GB costs than in countries with less competitive wholesale markets.

### EU Roaming Applies, But With a Twist

Since Spain is in the EU, the "Roam Like at Home" regulation applies ([https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/consumer-rights/roaming-tariffs/index_en.htm](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/consumer-rights/roaming-tariffs/index_en.htm)). Any Spanish SIM or eSIM plan must allow free roaming across EU member states at domestic rates. This sounds ideal, but in practice, many prepaid tourist plans cap EU roaming to small data allowances — sometimes as low as 3 GB out of a 20 GB plan. If you plan to visit Portugal, France, or Italy on the same trip, check the roaming policy before buying. Local carrier plans sold through Spanish stores typically offer full EU roaming. International eSIM providers sometimes restrict data to Spain only unless you buy a Europe-wide plan.

### The Spanish High-Speed Rail Factor

Spain has the second-largest high-speed rail network operated by [Renfe](https://www.renfe.com). in the world after China, operated by Renfe ([https://www.renfe.com](https://www.renfe.com)). AVE trains connecting Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, and Malaga reach speeds of up to 310 km/h. Maintaining a stable mobile connection at these speeds is demanding. Vodafone and Movistar have invested heavily in trackside infrastructure, while Yoigo and some MVNOs may drop to 3G or lose signal entirely in tunnels. If you plan to travel between cities by train, choose an eSIM provider that prioritizes Vodafone or Movistar connectivity. Auto-switching features are especially valuable here — they shift between networks as you move through tunnels and open sections, maintaining data sessions without manual intervention.

### Tax and Pricing Transparency

Spanish law requires all advertised prices to include VAT (IVA, 21%). Unlike countries where taxes are added at checkout, the price you see for a prepaid eSIM or physical SIM in Spain is the price you pay. This applies to both local carrier plans and most international eSIM providers selling in euros. The one exception is if your eSIM provider is based outside the EU — in that case, no Spanish VAT applies, which can make international providers slightly cheaper, though they may not offer local number options.

**Key takeaway:** Spain's four-network structure, strict CNMC regulation, EU roaming rules, and high-speed rail network create a mobile environment that rewards travelers who choose an eSIM provider with multi-network access. A single-network eSIM will work, but a provider that can switch between Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, and Yoigo gives you consistently better speeds and fewer dead zones.

## Movistar vs Orange vs Vodafone: Which Spain Network Is Best?

Choosing between Spain's four mobile networks is not about picking a "winner" — each network excels in specific conditions, and the best choice depends entirely on where you travel, how you use data, and whether you venture outside cities. This section breaks down each network's strengths and weaknesses so you can match your eSIM to your itinerary.

### Movistar (Telefonica)

Movistar is Spain's incumbent operator, the former state monopoly, and still the network with the widest overall coverage. It operates on 700 MHz (band 28), 800 MHz (band 20), 1800 MHz (band 3), 2100 MHz (band 1), and 3500 MHz (band 78 for 5G). The lower-frequency bands give Movistar superior building penetration and rural range.

**Coverage strengths:**
- Rural Spain: Movistar covers approximately 98% of the Spanish population and about 88% of the geographic territory. If you are visiting smaller pueblos in Andalusia, Extremadura, or Castilla-La Mancha, Movistar is the most reliable option.
- Coastal areas: Movistar has the best coastal coverage of all four networks, including along the Costa del Sol, Costa Brava, and Costa Blanca.
- Indoor penetration: The 800 MHz band (band 20) provides strong signal inside stone buildings, underground parking, and metro stations.

**Weaknesses:**
- Price: Movistar prepaid plans are consistently the most expensive. A 10 GB prepaid plan costs EUR 15–20 compared to EUR 10 from Yoigo.
- Speed in congested areas: In central Madrid and Barcelona, Movistar's network can become congested during peak hours, dropping from typical 150 Mbps 5G to around 30 Mbps.

**Best for:** Travelers visiting rural areas, multiple small towns, or coastal regions outside major cities. If your itinerary includes Granada, Ronda, Santiago de Compostela, or the Picos de Europa, prioritize an eSIM that uses Movistar.

### Orange

Orange is the second-largest operator in Spain and the leader in urban 5G deployment. It operates on 800 MHz, 1800 MHz, 2100 MHz, and 3500 MHz bands with the densest small-cell deployment in Madrid, Barcelona, and Valencia.

**Coverage strengths:**
- Urban 5G: Orange has deployed 5G in 1,200+ municipalities. In central Madrid, average 5G speeds reach 200 Mbps.
- Stadiums and event venues: Orange has dedicated capacity agreements with major venues including Santiago Bernabeu, Camp Nou (pre-renovation), and the Wizink Center.
- Business districts: Orange offers the most consistent high-speed data in central business areas of major cities.

**Weaknesses:**
- Rural gaps: Orange coverage drops noticeably outside town limits. In villages with fewer than 500 residents, Orange may only have 3G or no signal.
- Tunnel coverage: Orange has not invested as heavily in AVE rail tunnel repeaters as Movistar and Vodafone.

**Best for:** City-focused travelers spending most of their time in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, or Bilbao. If your trip is urban with side trips to nearby attractions, Orange provides excellent value.

### Vodafone

Vodafone Spain was acquired by the Zegona group and British investment firm in 2024, but the network infrastructure remains largely unchanged. Vodafone has invested heavily in transport corridor coverage and is the preferred network for travelers moving between cities.

**Coverage strengths:**
- AVE high-speed rail: Vodafone has installed repeaters along the Madrid-Barcelona, Madrid-Seville, and Madrid-Valencia corridors. Data sessions remain stable at 300 km/h.
- Airport coverage: Vodafone has dedicated small cells at Madrid-Barajas, Barcelona-El Prat, and Malaga airports, providing seamless handoff as you arrive.
- International roaming: Vodafone's agreements with its global group mean better speeds when roaming outside Spain compared to Movistar or Orange.

**Weaknesses:**
- Network transition: The ownership change in 2024 has led to some network management uncertainty. Some users report slower provisioning of new prepaid eSIM profiles compared to Movistar and Orange.
- Coverage in the north: In the Basque Country and Navarre, Vodafone coverage can be inconsistent, particularly in mountainous areas.

**Best for:** Travelers using high-speed rail, flying into multiple airports, or continuing their trip to other European countries after Spain. Vodafone's transport infrastructure makes it the strongest choice for multi-city itineraries.

### Yoigo (MasMovil Group)

Yoigo began as a challenger brand and has grown into the fourth national network, now part of the MasMovil group alongside MasMovil, Pepephone, and Lowi. Yoigo does not own its own 2G or 3G infrastructure — it relies on Movistar for legacy network access — but it has built its own 4G and 5G core.

**Coverage strengths:**
- Price: Yoigo consistently offers the lowest prepaid prices. A 50 GB prepaid plan costs around EUR 20, about half of what Movistar charges for the same data.
- Urban 4G/5G: In cities, Yoigo's 4G and 5G performance is comparable to Orange and Vodafone.
- Customer service: Yoigo consistently ranks highest in customer satisfaction among Spanish networks in CNMC surveys.

**Weaknesses:**
- 3G fallback: Since Yoigo relies on Movistar for 2G and 3G, if you lose 4G/5G signal in a rural area, your data session may drop entirely rather than falling back to a usable 3G connection.
- Throttling: Yoigo applies more aggressive traffic shaping on video streaming and large file downloads compared to Movistar or Orange.

**Best for:** Budget-conscious travelers staying in cities and towns with reliable 4G coverage. Yoigo is an excellent choice for urban trips where you primarily use messaging, maps, and social media.

### How Network Selection Affects Your eSIM Choice

When you buy a [Spain eSIM for travel](/spain-esim/) from an international provider, you are renting access to one or more of these networks at wholesale rates. Some providers lock you to a single network. Others connect to all four and switch automatically. If your eSIM only uses Orange, you will get excellent speed in Madrid but may struggle in rural Andalusia. If it only uses Yoigo, you will save money but may lose connectivity on AVE trains and in coastal towns.

The safest choice is an eSIM with access to at least two networks — ideally Movistar for coverage breadth and Orange or Vodafone for speed. Four-network access eliminates the need to research which network works best for your specific route.

**Key takeaway:** There is no single "best" network in Spain. Movistar wins on coverage, Orange wins on urban speed, Vodafone wins on transport connectivity, and Yoigo wins on price. Match your eSIM's network access to your itinerary, or choose a multi-network provider to cover all bases automatically.

### What "Unlimited" Actually Means in Spain

Several eSIM providers advertise unlimited data plans for Spain. Spanish network operators apply fair use policies (FUP) to all unlimited plans. After consuming a threshold — typically 10 GB to 30 GB per day, depending on the wholesale agreement — your speed is throttled to 1-5 Mbps. This is sufficient for messaging, maps, and social media but will not support HD video streaming or large file uploads.

If you are a heavy user planning to stream video, upload photos, or use video calls daily, a capped plan with 20 GB or more will serve you better than an "unlimited" plan that throttles after moderate use. The per-GB cost of capped plans from multi-network providers often works out lower than unlimited plans from single-network providers when you account for the usable data before throttling.

### Hidden Costs and Fees to Watch For

Beyond the headline price, several cost factors can increase your effective spend:

**Activation fees:** Some local carrier plans charge a one-time activation fee of EUR 5-10. Movistar's prepago plans include this in the first-month price; Orange and Yoigo list it separately. Most international eSIM providers do not charge activation fees.

**Top-up minimums:** Local carriers impose minimum top-up amounts. Movistar requires a minimum EUR 5 top-up, while Yoigo requires EUR 10. If you run out of data and need a small amount to finish your trip, these minimums can force you to overbuy.

**Currency conversion:** International eSIM providers often price in USD. If your bank charges foreign transaction fees (typically 1-3%), the actual cost will be higher than the listed price. Some providers price in multiple currencies including EUR, USD, and GBP.

**Expiry and rollover:** Most prepaid eSIM plans do not roll over unused data. If you buy a 30-day plan but only use it for 10 days, the remaining data expires. Some providers offer flexible validity where unused data remains available on your account for a certain period.

### The Real Cost of Choosing the Wrong Plan

Buying a single-network eSIM that does not match your itinerary carries an indirect cost. If your eSIM locks you to Yoigo and you take AVE trains, you may lose connectivity for portions of your journey. If it locks you to Orange and you explore rural Extremadura, you may have dead zones where you need maps most. The cost of a data outage at a critical moment — finding your hotel, navigating to a remote trailhead, calling a taxi — can exceed the price difference between a budget plan and a multi-network plan.

**Key takeaway:** The cheapest eSIM plan by headline price is not always the cheapest in practice. Factor in activation fees, minimum top-ups, network coverage relevance, and throttling policies. A plan that costs EUR 13 for 5 GB with access to four networks often delivers better value than a EUR 10 plan locked to a single network with patchy coverage.

### The Case for International eSIM Providers

International eSIM providers specialize in serving travelers. They do not require Spanish tax identification (NIF/NIE), their installation is fully remote, and they support instant activation before you leave home.

**Convenience advantage:**
You install the eSIM before departure and activate when you land. No airport store queues, no passport photocopying, no interaction with Spanish retail staff who may not speak English. For a 3-7 day trip, this convenience is significant — you save 30-60 minutes at the airport and arrive with data already working.

**Provider comparison:**

Airalo's pricing is competitive for small data allowances: USD 4.50 for 1 GB is among the cheapest entry-level options. The Airalo app manages installation smoothly, and top-up is instant via the app. The limitation is single-network access (Orange only), which means coverage in rural areas and on AVE trains may be weaker than multi-network alternatives.

Holafly's unlimited plans appeal to heavy data users, but the fair use policy throttles speed after approximately 20 GB daily. Holafly plans are priced at a premium: EUR 34 for 15 days of unlimited data is more expensive than 20 GB capped plans from competitors. **Nomad** provides Spain plans on Movistar's network, giving better rural coverage than Airalo's Orange-only plans. Nomad's pricing is mid-range: USD 22 for 10 GB. ### The Case for Local Spanish Carriers

Buying from a local carrier after arrival gives you a Spanish phone number (not data-only), access to regulatory protections, and often better per-GB pricing at higher data volumes.

**The Spanish phone number advantage:**
Many Spanish services require a local number. Restaurant booking platforms like ElTenedor, taxi apps like Cabify and FreeNow, and some museum ticket systems send SMS verification. If your home number does not work abroad or you prefer not to use it, a Spanish number from a local carrier makes these services seamless.

**Regulatory protections:**
Spanish carriers are regulated by the CNMC and consumer protection laws. If a plan malfunctions, fails to activate, or charges incorrectly, you have recourse through the carrier's complaint process and ultimately the CNMC. International eSIM providers registered outside the EU fall outside this regulatory scope. For short trips, this difference rarely matters. For long stays of 30+ days, the regulatory protection carries real weight.

**What to expect at a Spanish carrier store:**

1. Bring your passport or EU national ID. The store must verify your identity under Spanish telecom regulations.
2. Choose a prepago (prepaid) plan. Postpaid plans require a Spanish bank account and NIE number.
3. The store staff will install the eSIM for you by scanning a QR code or sending a configuration link.
4. Activation is typically instant, though some carriers (especially Movistar) may take 1-4 hours to fully provision the profile.
5. You will receive an SMS confirming your number and plan details in Spanish. The store staff can usually switch the service language to English if you ask.

### Hybrid Approach: Using Both

Many travelers use a hybrid setup: a primary international eSIM for data from a multi-network provider, plus a secondary free or low-cost local carrier eSIM for the Spanish phone number. This dual-SIM configuration works well because:

- Your primary eSIM handles all data traffic with the best available network.
- Your secondary local eSIM sits idle except for incoming SMS and occasional calls.
- You avoid paying premium rates for a Spanish number from an international provider.

Most modern smartphones support dual eSIM or one physical SIM plus one eSIM. This hybrid approach gives you the coverage breadth of a multi-network international provider plus the local number functionality of a Spanish carrier.

### Which Approach Costs Less?

For trips under 7 days with moderate data use (1-5 GB), international eSIM providers are almost always cheaper and more convenient. A 3 GB plan from Airalo costs USD 11. A 3 GB local prepaid plan from Yoigo costs EUR 10 — similar but requires a store visit and identity verification.

For trips over 14 days with heavy data use (20 GB+), local carriers offer better value. Yoigo's 50 GB plan for EUR 20 works out to EUR 0.40 per GB, while international providers charge around EUR 2.20 per GB for comparable data volumes. The inconvenience of the store visit pays off for longer stays.

For medium-length trips (7-14 days) covering multiple regions, a multi-network international provider offers the best balance of convenience, coverage breadth, and cost. The per-GB price is higher than local carriers, but the time saved and network flexibility often justify the premium.

## Spain eSIM Setup Guide: Installation & Activation Steps

Setting up an eSIM for Spain is straightforward, but the exact process varies depending on your device and provider. This section covers every method, troubleshooting step, and compatibility detail so you can activate with confidence.

### Device Compatibility Check

Before purchasing any eSIM, verify that your smartphone supports eSIM. As of mid-2026, virtually all flagship phones from the last five years support eSIM, but budget and older models may not.

**Apple iPhones:**
eSIM support on iPhone begins with the iPhone XS, XS Max, and XR (2018 models). All subsequent models including the iPhone 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, and 16 series support eSIM. For iPhone 14 and newer models sold in the United States, there is no physical SIM slot at all — eSIM is the only option. This is critical to know before traveling to Spain, as you cannot rely on buying a physical SIM at the airport.

**Samsung Galaxy:**
eSIM support begins with the Galaxy S20 series, Note 20 series, Galaxy Z Fold 2, and Z Flip 5G (2020). All Galaxy S21, S22, S23, S24, S25, Z Fold 3-6, and Z Flip 3-6 models support eSIM. The Galaxy A series generally does not support eSIM except for the A54 and A55 5G models.

**Google Pixel:**
eSIM support begins with Pixel 3 and later models. Pixel 4a, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 series all support eSIM.

**Huawei:**
eSIM support is limited. The Huawei P40, P40 Pro, Mate 40 Pro, and later models support eSIM, but only in regions where Google services are available. Huawei devices purchased in China may have eSIM hardware disabled.

**Other brands:**
OnePlus: eSIM on OnePlus 11, 12, and 13. Xiaomi: eSIM on Xiaomi 13 and later flagship models. Oppo and Vivo: select high-end models from 2022 onward. Sony: Xperia 1 IV and later.

If you are unsure, check your phone's settings: on iPhone, go to Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM. On Android, go to Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > Add eSIM. If the option exists, your device is compatible.

### Installation Methods by Provider

**Method 1: QR Code (Most Common)**

Almost every eSIM provider — Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, and local carriers Movistar, Orange, and Vodafone — delivers eSIM profiles via QR code.

1. Purchase your plan and receive the QR code by email or in the provider's app.
2. On your phone, open Settings > Cellular (iPhone) or Connections > SIM Manager (Android).
3. Tap "Add eSIM" or "Add Mobile Plan."
4. Scan the QR code displayed on your computer screen or another device. Do not scan from your phone's own screen unless the provider sends a self-contained file.
5. The profile downloads and installs automatically. This takes 30-60 seconds on a Wi-Fi connection.
6. Label the eSIM (e.g., "Spain Data") so you can identify it when switching between plans.
7. Set the eSIM as your default for cellular data. Keep your home SIM active for calls and SMS if you want to receive messages from home.

**Method 2: App-Based Installation**

Some providers like Roami handle installation entirely through their mobile app, which simplifies the process by removing the need to scan a QR code from another device.

1. Download the provider's app (Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, or other provider) from the App Store or Google Play.
2. Create an account and purchase your Spain plan within the app.
3. Tap "Install eSIM" or "Activate."

5. The app guides you through setting the eSIM as your data line and configuring APN if required.
6. Restart your phone as prompted (some providers require this; others activate without restart).

**Method 3: Manual Configuration (Local Carriers)**

Spanish carriers sometimes provide manual installation instructions as a backup if QR code scanning fails.

1. In Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM, select "Enter Details Manually."

### APN Configuration for Spain

Most modern eSIMs for Spain configure the APN (Access Point Name) automatically during installation. If your data does not work after activation, you may need to enter the APN manually.

**Common APN settings for Spain:**
- Movistar: `movistar.es`
- Orange: `orangeworld`
- Vodafone: `airtelnet.es` or `ac.vodafone.es`
- Yoigo: `yoigo`
- Multi-network (all networks): `roami`

To configure APN on iPhone: Settings > Cellular > [Your eSIM] > Cellular Data Network > APN.
On Android: Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Access Point Names > Add.

Enter the APN exactly as provided. APN values are case-sensitive. If your provider gives both an APN and a username/password, enter those too, though most Spanish providers do not require authentication credentials for prepaid eSIM data.

### Activation Timing: When to Install

You have two choices for when to install your Spain eSIM:

**Install before departure:**
Most eSIM profiles can be installed at any time but do not activate until they connect to a supported network in Spain. Installing before you leave ensures you only need to toggle the line on upon arrival. The one risk is that some providers start the validity timer at installation rather than first connection. Most international providers tie validity to first network connection, but check the terms before installing early.

**Install upon arrival:**
If your provider ties validity to installation, wait until you arrive in Spain. Use airport Wi-Fi or your home roaming data to download and install the profile. Madrid-Barajas and Barcelona-El Prat airports offer free Wi-Fi with no time limit, making this easy.

### Dual SIM Configuration Tips

Running your home SIM and a Spain eSIM simultaneously requires careful configuration to avoid unexpected roaming charges.

**Recommended dual SIM setup:**

1. Primary line (home SIM): Set as default for voice and SMS. Disable data roaming on this line.
2. Secondary line (Spain eSIM): Set as default for cellular data. Enable data roaming on this line.
3. Enable "Allow Cellular Data Switching" (iPhone) or "Auto Data Switching" (Android) so your phone can use either SIM for data if one loses signal. This is particularly valuable with multi-network eSIMs that switch between Spanish carriers automatically.

**Two pitfalls to avoid:**

- **International SMS charges:** If your home SIM receives SMS while in Spain, your carrier may charge per message. Check your home plan's international SMS rates or forward important numbers before traveling.
- **Accidental calls from the wrong line:** Some phones default to the primary line for all calls. If someone calls your home number and you answer on the Spain eSIM's data connection (VoIP), it routes through your home plan's international rates. Set your call default to the correct line for the situation.

### Troubleshooting Common Activation Issues

**"No Service" after installation:**
Restart your phone. eSIM profiles sometimes require a full reboot to register with the network. If restarting does not work, toggle Airplane Mode on for 10 seconds, then off. This forces the phone to re-scan available networks.

**eSIM not appearing in settings:**
On iPhone, go to Settings > General > About > Carrier Lock. If your phone is carrier-locked to a provider outside Spain, the eSIM may not activate. Contact your home carrier to request an unlock. On Android, ensure your phone's software is updated to the latest version — older Android builds have eSIM compatibility issues.

**Slow data after activation:**
Some eSIM providers throttle new connections for the first 15-30 minutes as the profile provisions fully with the network. Wait 30 minutes and restart. If speeds remain slow, check whether your plan has fair use throttling.

**"Invalid SIM" error:**
The eSIM profile may have expired or been installed incorrectly. Delete the eSIM profile from your phone (Settings > Cellular > select the plan > Remove eSIM) and reinstall using the original QR code or app installation flow. If the error persists, contact your provider's support.

**Troubleshooting table:**

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| No signal after install | Profile not registered | Restart phone, toggle Airplane Mode |
| Slow data | Throttling or congestion | Check plan FUP, move to less crowded area |
| APN error | Missing or incorrect APN | Enter APN manually from provider docs |
| eSIM not showing | Carrier lock or OS too old | Check carrier lock, update phone OS |
| Activation failed | Poor Wi-Fi during install | Reinstall on stable Wi-Fi connection |
| Profile already in use | Reused QR code | Contact support for new QR code |

**Key takeaway:** eSIM installation in Spain takes under 5 minutes for most users. Install the profile before departure, configure dual SIM with the Spain eSIM as your data line, and know which APN to enter if auto-configuration fails. For ongoing issues, major providers offer 24/7 support chat within their apps.

### Decision Flow: How to Choose Step by Step

**Step 1: Determine your data budget.**

Estimate your daily data use. Light use (messaging, maps, occasional browsing) uses 200-500 MB per day. Moderate use (maps, social media, some video) uses 500 MB to 1 GB per day. Heavy use (streaming, video calls, content uploads) uses 2-3 GB per day. Multiply by your trip days and add 20% buffer.

**Step 2: Map your itinerary.**

List every city, town, and transport route you will visit. If you stay in one major city (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia), any network works. If you visit multiple cities, you benefit from multi-network access. If you take AVE trains between cities, prioritize Vodafone or Movistar. **Step 3: Decide if you need a Spanish number.**

If you plan to use restaurant booking apps, taxi services, or need a local contact number, buy a local carrier eSIM alongside your data eSIM, or choose a provider that offers a number. For data-only needs, skip the local number and use WhatsApp or Telegram for communication.

**Step 4: Choose your provider tier.**

**Budget option (under EUR 10):** Airalo 3 GB (Orange network). Best for short urban trips where you control data use. Setup is fast, and pricing is transparent. The limitation is single-network access.

**Balanced option (EUR 10-20):** Multi-Network 5 GB or Holafly 7-day unlimited. Multi-network gives you four-network switching for the same price as single-network competitors. Holafly gives unlimited data (with fair use throttling) on Vodafone.

**Premium option (EUR 20+):** Multi-Network 10 GB or Yoigo prepaid 50 GB via local carrier. Multi-network 10 GB plan covers any itinerary without worrying about coverage gaps. Yoigo's 50 GB plan is the best per-GB value for long stays.

**Step 5: Consider backup connectivity.**

If your trip is mission-critical (business, remote work, or you are traveling with family), carry a second eSIM profile or a local SIM as backup. Most providers' apps let you purchase and activate a second plan instantly if your primary plan runs low, and 24/7 human support can help switch networks if you experience connectivity issues.
