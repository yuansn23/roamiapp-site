## Part 1: eSIM Compatibility — The Big Picture

Before we dive into specific brands, it's important to understand the three factors that determine whether your phone will work with a Japan eSIM.

### Factor 1: Hardware Support

Your phone must have an eSIM chip built in. Most flagship and many mid-range phones from 2018 onward have this hardware. However, some budget models and older devices don't.

### Factor 2: Country of Purchase

**This is the most common trap for travelers.** Even within the same model line, certain regional variants — particularly Chinese-market and some Hong Kong/Macau models — do not support eSIM at all.

For example:
- **China mainland iPhones** have eSIM physically disabled across all models.
- **Samsung phones sold in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and some Asian markets** lack the eSIM chip entirely.
- **Chinese-brand phones** (Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo) sold domestically typically lack eSIM due to local regulatory requirements.

**The rule of thumb:** If you bought your phone in mainland China, assume it does NOT support eSIM unless you've confirmed otherwise.

### Factor 3: Carrier Lock Status

Even if your phone has eSIM hardware, it won't work with a Japan eSIM if it's **carrier-locked** — tied to a specific mobile carrier. You can use a Japan eSIM on a **carrier-unlocked** phone only.

To check if your phone is unlocked:
- **iPhone:** Settings → General → About → look for "Carrier Lock"
- **Samsung:** Settings → Connections → SIM Card Manager
- **Google Pixel:** Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs

If it says "SIM locked" or "Carrier locked," contact your carrier to request an unlock before you travel.

### The Universal Check: The `*#06#` Method

Regardless of brand or model, there's one foolproof way to check eSIM compatibility:

1. Open your phone's dialer
2. Type `*#06#` and press call
3. Look for an **EID number** (32 digits) alongside your IMEI numbers

EID stands for **Embedded Identity Document** — a unique identifier for your phone's eSIM hardware.

**If you see an EID → your phone has eSIM hardware ✅**
**If you only see IMEI numbers → no eSIM hardware ❌**

This method works on all phones — iPhone, Samsung, Google Pixel, Xiaomi, OnePlus, and every other brand.

## Part 7: What If Your Phone Doesn't Support eSIM?

If your phone fails the `*#06#` test or is carrier-locked, don't worry — you still have options for staying connected in Japan. For a detailed comparison of all options, see our [Japan eSIM vs Pocket WiFi vs SIM Card guide](/blog/japan-esim-vs-pocket-wifi-vs-sim/).

### Option 1: Physical SIM Card (Recommended)

Buy a physical SIM card at the airport or an electronics store in Japan:
- **Where to buy:** Narita, Haneda, Kansai airports; BIC Camera, Yodobashi
- **Cost:** Similar to eSIM plans ($10-30 for 7-14 days)
- **Pros:** Works on any unlocked phone
- **Cons:** Requires physical card, must find a store

### Option 2: Pocket WiFi

Rent a portable WiFi hotspot:
- **Where to rent:** Japan Wireless, Sakura Mobile, airport counters
- **Cost:** $5-15/day
- **Pros:** Share with multiple devices, works on any phone
- **Cons:** Extra device to carry and charge

### Option 3: International Roaming

Use your home carrier's international roaming plan:
- **Pros:** No setup required, keep your number
- **Cons:** Expensive ($10-15/day), limited data

### Option 4: Upgrade Your Phone

If you travel frequently, consider upgrading to a newer phone with eSIM support. Many 2020+ phones are now affordable on the used market.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a Japan eSIM with an iPhone bought in China?

**No.** Most iPhone models sold in mainland China do NOT support eSIM at all. Apple physically removes the eSIM hardware from China-market devices.

### Can I use a Japan eSIM with a Samsung phone from Hong Kong?

**Probably not.** Hong Kong variants of Samsung phones typically lack eSIM hardware. Check with `*#06#` — if you don't see an EID, it won't work.

### Can I use a Japan eSIM with a Google Pixel bought in Japan?

**Only if it's a factory-unlocked international version.** Japanese carrier variants (SoftBank, au/KDDI, NTT Docomo) have eSIM disabled at the firmware level. This is a permanent restriction — even after SIM unlock, eSIM remains inaccessible.

### What's the safest phone for Japan eSIM?

**iPhone (international version, not China-market)** or **Google Pixel 6 or newer (non-Japanese carrier version)** have the most reliable eSIM support.

### Do I need a carrier-unlocked phone for Japan eSIM?

**Yes.** Your phone must be carrier-unlocked to use a Japan eSIM. Check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. "No SIM restrictions" means unlocked.

### Can I use a Japan eSIM with a Japanese carrier-locked phone?

**No.** Japanese carrier-locked phones (SoftBank, au, Docomo) typically have eSIM disabled at the firmware level — even if the phone model supports eSIM hardware.

### Does my phone need 5G for a Japan eSIM?

**No.** Japan eSIM works on 4G/LTE networks as well. 5G is an added benefit for faster speeds.

### Can I use a Japan eSIM with an iPad?

Yes! The following iPad models support eSIM: iPad Pro 11" & 13" (M4, M5), iPad Air 11" & 13" (M3, M4), iPad Mini (7th gen), and iPad (11th gen).

### What if I'm not sure about my phone?

The `*#06#` test is the most reliable method. If an EID number appears, your phone supports eSIM. If not, it doesn't.

### Can I use a Japan eSIM with a phone that has a physical SIM card slot?

**Yes.** Most eSIM-compatible phones support Dual SIM — one physical SIM and one eSIM active simultaneously. You can keep your home SIM for calls/SMS while using the eSIM for data.
