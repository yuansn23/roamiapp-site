# Japan SIM Card Guide 2026: Where to Buy, Prices, and Best Plans

**Last Updated:** August 8, 2026 | Reading time: 14 minutes


## Introduction: Why a Physical SIM Still Matters in 2026

In 2026, eSIM has become the default choice for most travelers with modern smartphones. It's faster, more convenient, and often cheaper. But what if your phone doesn't support eSIM? What if you're traveling with an older device, or you simply prefer the familiarity of a physical card?

**Physical SIM cards are still widely available across Japan** — and for many travelers, they remain the most practical option.

This guide covers everything you need to know about buying a **Japan SIM card** in 2026: where to find them, how much they cost, which providers offer the best value, and how to set one up when you arrive.

If your phone does support eSIM, you might want to check our [Japan eSIM Ultimate Guide](/japan-esim-ultimate-guide/) instead. But if you need a physical SIM — or just want to know your options — read on.

---

## Quick Answer: Should You Get a Physical SIM or eSIM?

| If you have... | Best option |
|----------------|-------------|
| **A modern phone (iPhone XR or newer, Samsung S20 or newer, Pixel 3 or newer)** | **eSIM** — buy before you fly, activate on arrival |
| **An older phone without eSIM support** | **Physical SIM** — buy at the airport or an electronics store |
| **A carrier-locked phone** | **Neither** — you need an unlocked phone first |
| **A group traveling together** | **Pocket WiFi** — share data across multiple devices |

For most travelers in 2026, eSIM is the easiest and most affordable option. But physical SIM cards remain a reliable fallback — and in some cases, they're still the best choice.

---

## Part 1: Types of Japan SIM Cards for Tourists

### Data-Only SIM Cards (Most Common)

Data-only SIM cards provide mobile internet access without a phone number. They're the most popular choice for tourists who plan to use messaging apps (WhatsApp, LINE, WeChat) and VoIP calls (Skype, Google Voice) rather than traditional calls.

**Best for:** Most tourists, short-term visitors, and anyone who doesn't need a Japanese phone number.

**Price range:** $10–$27 USD for 7–30 days

### Voice + Data SIM Cards

These SIM cards include a Japanese phone number, allowing you to make and receive traditional calls and SMS. You'll need one if you plan to make restaurant reservations, rent a car, or use services that require SMS verification.

**Best for:** Long-term travelers, expats, business travelers, and anyone who needs a Japanese phone number.

**Price range:** $20–$70+ USD, depending on data allowance

### Unlimited Data SIM Cards

Some providers offer "unlimited" data plans. However, as with eSIMs, "unlimited" often means a daily soft cap (typically 2–5 GB per day) after which speeds are throttled. Make sure to read the fine print before purchasing.

**Best for:** Heavy data users, streamers, and remote workers.

**Price range:** $30–$100+ USD

## Part 2: Where to Buy a Japan SIM Card

### 1. At the Airport (Most Convenient)

Major international airports in Japan have multiple SIM card counters in the arrival halls.

**Narita Airport (NRT):**
- SoftBank counters in Terminals 1 and 2
- BIC Camera in Terminal 2 arrivals
- JAL ABC counter (data SIMs from about $20 for 3 GB)

**Haneda Airport (HND):**
- BIC Camera in Terminal 3 (7:00–22:00)
- 24-hour JAL ABC counter in arrivals
- SoftBank counter in Terminal 2

**Kansai Airport (KIX):**
- Counters in Terminal 1, including Sakura Mobile with English-speaking staff

**Other airports:** Chubu, Fukuoka, and Naha airports also have SIM card counters

**Pros:** Instant activation, arrive already connected
**Cons:** Higher prices than electronics stores, queues after big arrivals

### 2. Electronics Stores (Best Selection and Prices)

BIC Camera and Yodobashi Camera are the two largest electronics chains in Japan, and they stock a wide variety of prepaid SIM cards.

**BIC Camera:**
- Located at major airports and in city centers
- Stocks multiple SIM brands including IIJmio (BIC SIM) and b-mobile
- Tourist data SIMs available

**Yodobashi Camera:**
- Locations across Japan, including Umeda (Osaka)
- Stock multiple SIM brands, often with more choice than airports
- Prices are generally lower than airport counters

**Pros:** Wider selection, lower prices than airports, English-speaking staff available
**Cons:** Requires finding a store after arrival, may need to take a train from the airport

### 3. Convenience Stores (Limited but Convenient)

Some convenience stores — 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson — sell prepaid data SIM cards. However, selection is limited and availability varies by location.

**Pros:** Open 24/7, everywhere
**Cons:** Limited selection, may not have English instructions

### 4. Online Before You Travel (Best for Planning)

Several providers allow you to order a SIM card online before you leave home and pick it up at the airport or have it delivered to your hotel.

**Providers offering this service:**
- Mobal — SIM cards with Japanese phone numbers
- Ninja WiFi — SIM and eSIM options
- Sakura Mobile — physical SIMs with English support
- NTT Communications — Prepaid SIM for Japan, available at designated airports

**Pros:** Plan ahead, guaranteed availability, often cheaper
**Cons:** Must plan in advance, cannot change your mind on arrival

### 5. Vending Machines (Emerging Option)

Some airports and hotels now have vending machines selling prepaid SIM cards. This is a quick option if you arrive outside normal store hours.

**Pros:** 24/7 availability, quick
**Cons:** Limited selection, potentially higher prices

## Part 4: How to Buy and Activate a Japan SIM Card

### Step 1: Check Phone Compatibility

Before buying any SIM card, confirm two things:

1. **Your phone is carrier-unlocked** — check with your home carrier
2. **Your phone supports the correct SIM size** — most modern phones use nano-SIM

### Step 2: Bring Your Passport

**Important new rule for 2026:** Since April 1, 2026, Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) has required a **passport or residence card ID for every SIM and eSIM purchase** — including data-only plans.

You **will not** be able to buy a SIM card in Japan without showing your passport. This applies to airport counters, electronics stores, and convenience stores alike.

**Pro tip:** Keep your passport on you when shopping for a SIM. Digital copies or photos are not accepted — you need the physical document.

### Step 3: Choose Your Plan

Decide on your data needs based on your trip length:
- **3-5 days:** 1-3 GB
- **7-10 days:** 3-7 GB
- **14+ days:** 7-15 GB or unlimited

### Step 4: Purchase and Activate

**At airport counters or electronics stores:**
1. Find the SIM card section or counter
2. Show your passport
3. Choose your plan
4. Pay (credit cards and cash accepted)
5. The staff will help you insert and activate the SIM card

**At convenience stores:**
1. Find the SIM card display (usually near the register)
2. Take the package to the counter
3. Show your passport
4. Pay and follow the instructions inside the package

### Step 5: Configure APN Settings

Most SIM cards require manual APN (Access Point Name) configuration. The APN settings are usually printed on the card or included in the package instructions.

**Common Japan SIM APN values:**

| Provider | APN | Username | Password | Auth Type |
|----------|-----|----------|----------|-----------|
| b-mobile | bmobile.ne.jp | (blank) | (blank) | None |
| IIJmio (BIC SIM) | iijmio.jp | mio@iij | iij | PAP or CHAP |
| Mobal | mobal | (blank) | (blank) | None |
| Sakura Mobile | sakura | (blank) | (blank) | None |
| NTT Com | prepaid.ezweb.ne.jp | (blank) | (blank) | None |

**On iPhone:**
1. Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Network
2. Enter the APN, username, and password from the instructions
3. Save and restart

**On Android:**
1. Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names
2. Add a new APN with the settings from the instructions
3. Save and select the new APN

### Step 6: Test Your Connection

Once activated, open a browser or app to confirm data is working. If not, double-check the APN settings or restart your phone.

## Part 5: When to Choose a Physical SIM Over eSIM or Pocket WiFi

Choosing between a physical SIM, eSIM, and pocket WiFi depends on your specific travel situation. Here's a detailed decision guide:

### Choose a Physical SIM if:

- **Your phone does NOT support eSIM** — the most common reason. Check with `*#06#` on your phone's dialer. If you see an EID number, your phone supports eSIM. If you only see IMEI numbers, it does not.
- **You're buying a voice-enabled plan** — eSIMs rarely include a Japanese phone number. If you need one for reservations or verification, a physical SIM from Mobal is often the simplest way.
- **You prefer the familiarity of a physical card** — some travelers simply prefer having a tangible product they can swap between devices.
- **You plan to use the SIM in multiple devices** — you can easily move a physical SIM between phones.
- **You want to keep your home SIM in the phone** — physical SIM + eSIM are both options, but if you want to keep the physical slot open for other travel SIMs, consider eSIM instead.

### Choose eSIM if:

- **Your phone supports eSIM** and you want the convenience of installing before travel.
- **You want to keep your home SIM active** for calls and SMS while using the eSIM for data.
- **You don't want to carry your passport while shopping** — eSIM can be bought online before travel.
- **You're a solo traveler or couple** — eSIM offers the best cost-to-convenience ratio.

### Choose Pocket WiFi if:

- **You're traveling with a group of 3 or more** — one device covers everyone, often cheaper than multiple SIMs.
- **You need to connect tablets and laptops** — a pocket WiFi router can connect multiple devices simultaneously.
- **You don't want to worry about phone compatibility** — pocket WiFi works with any WiFi-enabled device.

## Part 6: Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I buy a Japan SIM card at the airport?

Yes. Major airports including Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, and Naha all have SIM card counters. You can buy a prepaid SIM card on arrival.

### How much does a Japan SIM card cost?

**Japan SIM card price** ranges from:
- $10–$27 USD for 7-30 days
- $20–$70+ USD for voice + data plans
- $30–$100+ USD for unlimited data plans

### Do I need a passport to buy a SIM card in Japan?

**Yes.** Since April 2026, Japan requires a passport or residence card ID for every SIM and eSIM purchase — even data-only plans. You cannot buy a SIM without showing valid ID.

### Where can I buy a SIM card in Tokyo?

You can buy a Japan SIM card in Tokyo at:
- **Narita or Haneda airports** — counters in arrivals
- **BIC Camera** — multiple locations across Tokyo
- **Yodobashi Camera** — multiple locations
- **Convenience stores** — 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson

### What's the best SIM card for Japan?

The best SIM card depends on your needs:
- **Best budget:** b-mobile ($13 for 5GB/10 days)
- **Best value for moderate data:** IIJmio BIC SIM
- **Best with phone number:** Mobal
- **Best unlimited data:** Sakura Mobile
- **Best for light users:** NTT Communications Prepaid SIM

### Can I use a Japan SIM card with an iPhone?

Yes, as long as your iPhone is carrier-unlocked. Physical SIM cards work with any unlocked iPhone that supports the correct SIM size (nano-SIM).

### Do Japan SIM cards include a phone number?

Most data-only SIM cards do **not** include a phone number. If you need a Japanese phone number for calls or SMS, choose a provider like Mobal that offers voice-enabled plans.

### Can I buy a Japan SIM card online before I travel?

Yes. Providers like Mobal, Sakura Mobile, and NTT Communications allow you to order online and pick up at the airport or have it delivered to your hotel.

### Do I need a Japanese phone number for a short trip?

For most tourists, **no**. Messaging apps cover person-to-person calls, and hotels can make restaurant bookings for you. You only need a phone number for longer stays, deliveries, or app verifications.

### What's the difference between a tourist SIM and a regular Japanese SIM?

Tourist SIMs are prepaid, require no contract, and don't require a Japanese address. Regular Japanese SIMs typically require a contract, a Japanese address, and a residence card.
