## Brexit Changed UK eSIM – What Travellers Need to Know

Since 1 January 2021, the UK has sat outside the EU's "Roam Like at Home" rules. For most travellers, the practical impact seemed distant – until you board the Eurostar at London St Pancras, cross under the Channel Tunnel, arrive in Calais, and your phone screen shows "No Service".

That is the new reality of post-Brexit UK mobile networks: **UK eSIMs and physical SIMs no longer roam for free in EU countries.** Before Brexit, a UK SIM worked seamlessly in 27 EU countries at no extra cost. In 2026, if you buy a typical **UK-only eSIM** – whether a local EE or Vodafone bundle or a travel eSIM without EU coverage – when you cross the Channel into France, Belgium or the Netherlands, you will face daily roaming surcharges of £2-5, or more likely, lose data completely. This is the trap that catches most travellers who buy a UK-only plan before a multi-country trip.

This creates a unique situation: **the choice of eSIM must match the geographical itinerary, not personal preferences.** If you're visiting the UK and Europe, most providers force you into a **UK + Europe** regional plan rather than a UK-only one. The one exception we verified: Roami's UK eSIM includes EU roaming, so a single UK plan can cover a London-plus-Paris itinerary.

The independent [Opensignal UK mobile network experience report](https://www.opensignal.com/reports/2026/01/uk/mobile-network-experience) confirms how much the coverage varies between networks – an essential resource for trip planning.

## Refunds, Cancellations and Activation Windows for a UK eSIM

A UK eSIM is a digital purchase made at a distance, so it falls under the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 — the rules that give online buyers a 14-day cooling-off period. There is one exception you need to know about, and it is the reason travel eSIM refunds work differently from almost any other online purchase.

**Your 14-day right to cancel ends the moment the plan starts being supplied.** Under [regulation 37 of those Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37), a trader may only begin supplying digital content inside the cancellation window if you expressly consented to it and acknowledged that you would lose your right to cancel. Once both of those conditions are met and supply has begun, the cancellation right falls away. In practice this means the moment you scan the QR code and install the profile, the 14-day window closes — the purchase stops being a returnable order and becomes a delivered service.

| Stage | Cooling-off position | Practical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Paid, QR delivered, not installed | 14-day window open | You can normally cancel for a change of mind |
| Installed but not connected | Usually treated as supplied | Ask before you install if your plans are uncertain |
| Connected and in use | Cancellation right lost | Only quality remedies apply from here |
| Plan never worked at all | Not a change of mind | Buyer's rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 still apply |

Three habits follow from that table, and they save money far more often than any discount code.

**Buy the day before you fly, not a fortnight before.** The trap is buying early "to be organised" and then changing your plans. Because supply begins when you install the profile, a plan bought three weeks before departure and installed immediately has lost its cancellation right long before the trip. Leave the installation until the day before you go.

**Do not install a plan you might not use.** If there is any chance the trip moves, keep the QR code un-scanned. The email is not the clock; the installation is.

**If the plan genuinely fails, that is a different claim.** A plan that never connects on arrival is not a change of mind, and the Consumer Rights Act 2015 requires the seller to put it right — repair or replacement first, then a refund if that fails. Keep the evidence: screenshots of the error state, the time you tried, and the support conversation. That file is what turns a complaint into a remedy.

One practical note on fairness: providers that sell through an app sometimes offer a goodwill replacement rather than arguing the cooling-off point, especially for a plan that failed on day one. Ask for the fix you need rather than the refund you think you are owed — you will usually get further.

**Watch for the currency trap.** Paying in your own currency at checkout can trigger dynamic currency conversion, where the merchant or a payment processor chooses the exchange rate. **Expect 3-D Secure to ask for a code.** Most UK and EU card issuers now enforce Strong Customer Authentication, which means a large share of eSIM checkouts will bounce you to your bank's app or send a one-time code by SMS to your registered number. If you are buying from outside the UK for a trip you have not started, the provider's own site or a travel eSIM sold in your local currency will almost always be the smoother transaction.

**VAT is included in the displayed price.** UK consumer prices are quoted inclusive of VAT, so a plan listed at 20 GBP from a UK-based seller will not pick up a surprise tax line at the end. Providers selling from outside the UK handle tax differently, which is one more reason the totals across these channels do not line up neatly.

Search for a UK eSIM and you will meet the same product under half a dozen names: a UK eSIM card, a UK prepaid eSIM, a UK eSIM pay as you go plan, an instant eSIM, a travel plan. They are not all the same thing, and the differences decide whether the plan works for your trip.

| What it is called | What it is | Who it suits | Watch out for |

| **eSIM card** | A data eSIM installed as a digital profile — there is no plastic card | Almost every visitor | Some guides still sell a "card" that ships physically; not needed |

**The one distinction that matters most:** data-only versus full-service. A UK eSIM travel plan gives you internet and nothing else, which is fine for maps, messaging apps and bookings. If a site is asking you to apply, it is selling something else.


## Topping Up, Extending and What Happens When a UK eSIM Expires

How a UK eSIM behaves at the end of its validity is where cheap plans stop looking cheap, and it is the single most common surprise for travellers who assumed they could simply add data later.

**Validity is usually a wall, not a rolling balance.** Most travel eSIMs give you a fixed window — 3, 7, 15 or 30 days — and unused data does not carry over. Buy a 30 GB plan for a two-week trip, use 8 GB and fly home, and the remaining 22 GB simply stops existing. That is the arithmetic behind the fixed-versus-unlimited decision: an oversized fixed plan is not insurance, it is waste.

**Topping up a travel eSIM is possible but not always elegant.** Providers generally let you buy a second plan and install it alongside or after the first, but the new plan starts its own validity clock from its own activation. On a long trip that is fine. Mid-trip, it means a brief switch between profiles and a second QR code to scan. Check whether your provider supports top-ups on the same profile before you rely on it — networks selling their own prepaid eSIM have a clearer top-up path than most travel providers, and our [UK carrier eSIM guide](/blog/uk-carrier-esim-guide/) walks through those.

**Unlimited plans fail differently from metered ones.** A metered plan runs out; an unlimited plan slows down. Most "unlimited" UK eSIMs apply a daily high-speed allowance and then throttle to a low speed rather than cutting you off — the pattern, the thresholds and the tethering rules are compared in our [UK eSIM unlimited data guide](/blog/uk-esim-unlimited-data-guide/). What matters when you are topping up is knowing which of the two you bought: if you are on an unlimited plan and speeds have collapsed, you do not need more data, you need to wait for the daily reset.

| What ran out | What actually happens | What to buy |
|---|---|---|
| Metered data finished | Plan stops working for data | Top-up or a new plan on the same profile if supported |
| Validity period expired | Plan stops working, unused data lost | A new plan — the old allowance does not return |
| Daily high-speed allowance on an unlimited plan | Speed drops, typically to a few hundred Kbps | Nothing; it resets the next day |
| Monthly prepaid bundle on a network eSIM | Bundle ends, credit remains | Next bundle from credit, or a new bundle |
| The plan is fine but the number is not | Voice and SMS need a separate line | See the hybrid setup on our [UK eSIM with a phone number guide](/blog/uk-esim-with-phone-number/) |

**One thing to do before you buy a long plan.** Check the date your validity starts. If it starts at purchase rather than at first connection, buying a 30-day plan a week before departure costs you a week of the month you paid for. Where the provider starts the clock on first connection, buying early is safe. Where it starts at purchase, buy late.

If you are weighing a bigger fixed plan against an unlimited tier for a long trip, the break-even maths and the per-day costs of each are broken down in our [UK eSIM price guide](/blog/uk-esim-price-guide/).

## How to Activate Your UK eSIM – 4 Simple Steps

Activation takes less than 2 minutes. Do it **before you fly** – you only need Wi-Fi.

1. **Buy your plan** – Choose a plan from Roami or your preferred provider.
2. **Scan the QR code** – Go to Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM and scan the QR code sent to your email.
3. **Install the profile** – Follow the on-screen prompts to complete the download.
4. **Label your eSIM** – Name it "UK Travel" for easy identification.

**After arriving in the UK:** Go to Settings > Cellular > [your eSIM] > turn on Data Roaming. Set the plan as the Cellular Data line. Turn Data Roaming OFF on the home SIM. Turn OFF "Allow Cellular Data Switching".

**Important:** Install the eSIM before you fly – you need a stable Wi-Fi connection for the download. Do not delete an active eSIM profile during the trip. If "No Service" appears, toggle Airplane Mode or restart the phone – do not delete the profile.

For detailed iPhone instructions, check [device compatibility](/compatibility/) to confirm your phone supports eSIM.


## UK eSIM Not Working? Common Problems and Quick Fixes

Even with a good eSIM, issues can occur. Here's how to fix the most common **UK eSIM not working** problems:

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| **No signal after landing** | Data Roaming not enabled | Settings > Cellular > eSIM > toggle Data Roaming ON |
| **QR code not recognized** | QR code expired or damaged | Request new QR code from provider; try manual entry |
| **No data after activation** | APN settings missing | Configure APN manually (see table below) |
| **Slow speeds in rural areas** | Wrong network selected | Manually switch to EE (best rural coverage) |
| **Hotspot not working** | Plan restricts tethering | Check your plan's terms – some providers limit hotspot |
| **eSIM shows "Activating" for hours** | Wi-Fi connection unstable | Restart phone, toggle Airplane Mode, try different Wi-Fi |
| **"No Service" on London Underground** | Deep Tube has no cellular signal | Download offline Tube maps before you go |

### APN Settings for UK eSIMs

If you have no data after activation, configure APN manually. These are the values the networks publish — including the username and password fields most guides leave out:

| Provider | APN | Username | Password |
|----------|-----|----------|----------|
| **Roami** | roami | (blank) | (blank) |
| **EE** | everywhere | eesecure | secure |
| **O2** | mobile.o2.co.uk (contract) / payandgo.o2.co.uk (PAYG) | o2web | password |
| **Vodafone** | wap.vodafone.co.uk (PAYG) / internet (contract) | wap | wap |
| **Three** | three.co.uk | (blank) | (blank) |
| **giffgaff** | giffgaff.com | giffgaff | (blank) |

The provider column matters more than the network column here: a travel eSIM carries its own APN, so overwriting it with a UK network's value is the fastest way to break a plan that was already working.

## UK eSIM Buying: Frequently Asked Questions

**Will a UK eSIM work in Europe?**
Usually not. After Brexit, UK mobile networks no longer include free EU roaming, so most UK-only eSIMs either stop working in EU countries (e.g., France, Germany, Italy) or incur daily roaming charges of £2-5. The one exception we verified in September 2026 is Roami, whose UK eSIM includes EU roaming. If your provider doesn't offer the same and your itinerary includes the UK and Europe, a [Europe regional eSIM](/europe-esim/) is required.

**Can I buy a prepaid Vodafone eSIM for a two-week trip?**
Yes — this changed, and older guides have not caught up. Vodafone UK's Pay as you go Plus bundles support eSIM at online checkout with no contract and no credit check, and the plan is managed in the My Vodafone app after you land. What Vodafone still does not offer is a tourist eSIM over the counter in a shop, and its contract plans remain UK-residency only. If you want Vodafone coverage without the checkout and app friction, a travel eSIM that runs on Vodafone's network through a wholesale agreement gives you the same towers.

**Can I buy an eSIM at Heathrow?**
Yes, but the price is typically 100-200% higher than buying online in advance. Airport counters and vending machines charge £20-30 ($25-38) for a 5GB plan, while the same data bought online in advance starts at $4.79. Avoid the **Heathrow SIM trap**.

**Where can I buy an eSIM in the UK?**
Online, from anywhere, before you fly — that is the cheapest and fastest route by a wide margin. In the UK itself you can buy one from a network's own website or app, from a network store, or from the airport counters and vending machines that charge a 100-200% markup. For reference, a UK pay as you go eSIM bought in advance starts at $1.59 for 1GB, while the same data at Heathrow costs £12-18.

**Do I need a UK phone number?**
For the vast majority of travellers, no. Messaging apps (WhatsApp, iMessage, FaceTime, WeChat) work perfectly over a data connection. A UK number is only needed for: opening a UK bank account, registering with a GP (family doctor), or signing up for certain local delivery apps.

**What is the difference between a UK eSIM and a Europe eSIM?**
In most cases a UK-only eSIM only works in the UK (Roami's UK plan is the exception, with EU roaming included). A [regional eSIM for Europe](/europe-esim/) covers the UK plus 30+ EU countries with a single plan. If your itinerary includes both, the Europe plan is cheaper than buying two separate plans.

**Will my eSIM work on the London Underground?**
Mostly yes in 2026. The Boldyn Networks build-out has brought 4G and 5G to the majority of Tube stations and a growing share of tunnel sections, and all four networks ride on it – so a travel eSIM gets the same signal a local phone does. Coverage is still patchy on the deepest stretches, so screenshot your route and download offline Tube maps before you descend.

**What's the best UK eSIM for road trips in Scotland?**
Roami is the best choice because it automatically switches to EE – the only network with reliable coverage in the Scottish Highlands. Single-network eSIMs that use only O2 or Three will leave you with no signal in many remote areas.

**How much data do I need for a 7-day UK trip?**
Plan for 5-10GB total. On London city days you'll use 1-1.5GB, on travel days between cities you'll use 2-3GB. Choose a plan with extra capacity – you can always top up online.

**Which UK eSIM is cheapest?**
Roami's 1GB/3-day plan starts at $1.59 after discount. For more data, Roami's 5GB/3-day plan is $4.79 – significantly cheaper than Airalo ($13.00 for 5GB) and Holafly ($27.50 for 7-day unlimited).

**Can I use my UK eSIM as a personal hotspot?**
Yes – most travel eSIMs support hotspot sharing. Check your plan's terms to be sure. Roami supports full hotspot sharing on all plans.

**How do I transfer my UK eSIM to a new phone?**
Most travel eSIMs are one-time install and cannot be transferred. For postpaid carrier eSIMs (EE, Vodafone, etc.), contact your carrier. For detailed steps on transferring eSIMs between devices, see our [eSIM Cross-Platform Transfer 2026 guide](/faq/how-to-transfer-esim-between-iphone-and-android/).
