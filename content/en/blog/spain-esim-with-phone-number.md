## Do You Actually Need a Spanish Phone Number?

Let's be honest: **most travelers don't need a Spanish number.** Data-only eSIMs work perfectly for 90% of tourist activities – Google Maps, WhatsApp, iMessage, email, web browsing, and social media all function without a local number.

**Here's the real question:** will any of these apply to your trip?

| Scenario | Need a Number? | Workaround If You Don't Have One |
|---|---|---|
| Booking Renfe or AVE trains | ✅ Yes | Use station ticket machines or book via Trainline / Omio (email verification works) |
| Using Cabify, Uber Spain, or Bolt | ✅ Yes | Some apps accept email; but SMS to +34 is most reliable |
| Reserving restaurants via ElTenedor | ✅ Yes | Ask hotel reception to book, or call directly from your hotel phone |
| Ordering food via Glovo / Just Eat | ✅ Yes | Use the website instead of app; some accept WhatsApp verification |
| Receiving bank verification codes | ✅ Yes | Use your home bank's app with biometric login instead of SMS |
| Making local calls to hotels/tours | ⚠️ Maybe | Use WhatsApp or Skype calls over data |
| Messaging friends and family | ❌ No | WhatsApp, iMessage, Telegram all work over data |
| Navigation and maps | ❌ No | Google Maps works offline and over data |
| Social media and browsing | ❌ No | No number needed |

**The truth:** Most travelers can manage without a Spanish number. But if you're taking trains, using ride-hailing, or booking local restaurants regularly, the ~$30 for a tourist eSIM with a working number is money well spent.

## How to Book Renfe and AVE Trains with Your Spanish Number

This is the #1 reason travelers buy a Spain eSIM with a phone number. Here's the complete step-by-step process.

**Before You Start:**
- You need your Spain eSIM installed and active
- Your Spanish +34 number must be receiving SMS
- Have your passport ready (required for Renfe tickets)

**Step 1: Download the Renfe App or Use the Website**
- Download "Renfe Cercanías" (the app) from the App Store or Google Play
- Or go to [renfe.com](https://www.renfe.com) on your phone's browser

**Step 2: Create an Account**
- Tap "Registrarse" (Register)
- Enter your email address
- Enter your Spanish phone number (starting with +34)
- Create a password

**Step 3: Verify Your Account**
- Renfe sends a verification code by SMS to your Spanish number
- Wait up to 2 minutes (network delays are common)
- Enter the 6-digit code in the app/website
- Your account is now active

**Step 4: Search and Book**
- Enter your origin and destination stations
- Select travel date and time
- Choose your train type (AVE, Avlo, Alvia, Media Distancia)
- Select your seat preference

**Step 5: Payment**
- Enter your passport number (required for AVE trains)
- Pay by credit/debit card
- You can also use PayPal on the website version

**Step 6: Receive Your Ticket**
- You'll get a PDF ticket by email
- You can also save it in the Renfe app
- A QR code ticket is available in the app for station gate entry

**Pro Tip:** If you're using a discount (like the "Tarjeta Dorada" for seniors or "Tarjeta Joven" for youth), you'll need to register the discount card number in your profile before booking. Do this before you start the ticket search.

**Troubleshooting:**
- **SMS not arriving?** Wait 2-3 minutes – Spanish networks can be slow. Try requesting a new code after 3 minutes. If still nothing, see the "Why Your SMS Isn't Arriving" section below.
- **App stuck?** Use the website – it's often more reliable. Go to renfe.com in your browser.
- **Payment failing?** Try a different credit card. Renfe sometimes blocks non-European cards. PayPal usually works as an alternative.
- **Need to change your ticket?** You can modify or cancel tickets up to 24 hours before departure (fees apply). Always check the specific fare conditions.

## Why Your Spain eSIM SMS Isn't Arriving (And How to Fix It)

One of the most common frustrations: you've bought a Spain eSIM with a number, you're waiting for a verification code from Renfe or Cabify – and nothing arrives. Here's why and how to fix it.

**1. Wait – Sometimes It's Just Slow**

Spanish networks can be slow to deliver SMS. Wait 2-3 minutes before trying anything. Requesting multiple codes too quickly can slow things down further.

**2. Check Your Phone Settings**

Your phone might be blocking SMS from unknown numbers:
- **iPhone:** Settings > Messages > "Filter Unknown Senders" > Turn OFF
- **Samsung:** Settings > Messages > "Spam protection" > Disable temporarily
- **Google Pixel:** Settings > Messages > "Spam protection" > Turn OFF

**3. Your Number Isn't Fully Active**

Some tourist eSIMs take 10-15 minutes to fully provision after first connection. Wait 15 minutes, restart your phone, then try again.

**4. Network Registration Issue**

You're connected to data but SMS routing isn't working. Fix:

1. Toggle Airplane Mode ON for 30 seconds, then OFF
2. Go to Settings > Mobile Data > Network Selection > turn OFF Automatic
3. Manually select Movistar, Orange, or Vodafone
4. Try the SMS again

**5. The App Is Using the Wrong Phone Number**

You registered with your Spanish number, but the app is trying to send to your home number from a previous session. Clear the app's cache and cookies, restart the app, or reinstall it.

**6. SMS Center Number Missing**

This is rare but happens. Go to your phone's SMS settings – there should be an "SMS Center" field with a number. Spanish carriers normally preconfigure this automatically when the SIM provisions, so if the field is blank, don't guess: contact your provider's support (or check its official help pages) for the correct SMS center number, enter it, and restart.

**7. Contact Provider Support**

If none of the above works, the issue may be on the provider's side. Contact Orange, Vodafone, or Movistar support. They can check if SMS routing is enabled on your line. Have your phone number and ICCID ready.

**Pro Tip:** Some apps (like Renfe) also offer voice verification as an alternative. In the verification screen, look for "Receive a call instead" or "Call me" option. You'll receive an automated call that reads out the verification code – no SMS required.
