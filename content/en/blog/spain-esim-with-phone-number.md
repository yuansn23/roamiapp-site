Most Spain travel eSIMs are data-only. They connect your phone the moment you land, but they do not come with a Spanish phone number you can use for calls, text messages, or app verification. For many travelers that is perfectly fine — WhatsApp, iMessage, FaceTime, and Google Maps all work over a data connection. But if you plan to book Renfe trains, order a Cabify, reserve a table through ElTenedor, or receive a one-time verification code on a local number, a data-only eSIM will not be enough. This guide explains which Spain eSIM plans include a Spanish +34 number, how much they cost, and how to get one before or during your trip.

## Why You Might Need a Spanish Phone Number

For most tourists, a data-only eSIM is enough. A Spanish number becomes worth paying for when you need to:

- **Book Renfe and AVE trains.** Renfe's app and website often require a local number for account verification and ticket delivery.
- **Use ride-hailing and taxi apps.** Cabify, Uber Spain, and Bolt may need a local number to register or confirm a ride.
- **Reserve restaurants and activities.** Platforms like ElTenedor ask for a local phone number for bookings.
- **Order food or groceries.** Delivery apps such as Glovo and Just Eat send verification codes by SMS.
- **Make local calls.** Restaurant reservations, hotel enquiries, and contacting your accommodation are easier with a local number.
- **Receive verification codes.** Some banks and services send security codes that are easier to receive on a local number while traveling.

If none of these apply, a data-only eSIM is usually the simpler and cheaper choice.

## Orange Holiday Europe: The Easiest Tourist Option

Orange Holiday Europe is the most straightforward way to get a Spanish number as a visitor. It is sold by Orange directly, works as an eSIM or physical SIM, and includes a +34 number along with data, calls, and SMS.

- 25GB of data across 30 European countries
- 120 minutes of calls within Europe
- 1,000 SMS within Europe
- A Spanish (+34) phone number for local app verification
- EUR 29.99 for 14 days
- Full hotspot and tethering (up to 5 devices)
- Same network priority as Orange postpaid customers

The included number is the key advantage. You can register for Renfe, Cabify, Uber Spain, and ElTenedor without the workarounds that data-only travelers need. The 120 call minutes cover the short local calls — restaurant reservations, hotel enquiries, and taxi dispatches — that make up most travel use.

You can buy Orange Holiday Europe online before you leave, or at Orange stores and airport kiosks after arrival.

### In-store prepaid from all four carriers

When you buy a prepaid SIM or eSIM from Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, or Yoigo in a Spanish store, Spanish law requires you to show identification:

- Bring your passport (mandatory)
- Complete a short registration form
- The number is usually active within minutes to a few hours
- Recharge at stores, kiosks, or through the carrier app
- These plans are the cheapest per GB for stays longer than a few weeks

## MVNO Options for Long Stays

Spanish MVNOs — virtual operators that run on the major networks — offer some of the lowest prices, but they are aimed at residents rather than tourists.

- Pepephone runs on Movistar, Lowi on Vodafone, and DIGI on Movistar.
- Prices start around EUR 10-13 per month.
- Most plans require Spanish identification (DNI/NIE) or a Spanish bank account.
- Practical for long-stay visitors and students, not short trips.

For more on long stays, see our [families, students, and long-stay guide](/blog/spain-esim-families-students-long-stay/).

## Data-Only eSIMs and the Workarounds

Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, Saily, and Roamify sell data-only eSIMs. They are usually cheaper and easier to buy than plans with a number, but they cannot receive SMS or make calls on a Spanish number. If you only need data, that is not a problem.

If you use a data-only eSIM but still want a "number" for a few tasks, common workarounds include:

- Keep your home number active and use WhatsApp, iMessage, or Google Voice for messaging.
- Use email-based verification where apps offer it.
- Ask your accommodation to help with local bookings.
- Use a Wi-Fi calling app for the occasional local call.

Some travelers combine a cheap data-only eSIM for data with a low-cost prepaid number used only for verification — but for most short trips, a single plan with a number is simpler.

## How to Get a Spanish Number: Step by Step

1. Decide whether you actually need a number (see the list above).
2. If buying online, purchase Orange Holiday Europe, Vodafone Travel, or Movistar Turista before departure and receive the QR code by email.
3. If buying in store, visit a carrier store with your passport and ask for a prepaid eSIM.
4. Install the eSIM profile — see our [installation and activation guide](/blog/spain-esim-installation-activation-setup/).
5. Verify your number is active, then use it to register for Renfe, Cabify, and other local apps.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get a Spain eSIM with a phone number?

Yes. Orange Holiday Europe, Vodafone Travel, and Movistar Turista all include a Spanish +34 number and can be bought online. Direct prepaid plans from Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, and Yoigo also include a number but require passport registration in store.

### Do Airalo, Holafly, or Nomad include a Spanish number?

No. These are data-only eSIMs. You cannot receive SMS or make calls on a Spanish number with them. Use WhatsApp, iMessage, or your home number for messaging instead.

### How much does a Spain eSIM with a phone number cost?

From around EUR 20-30 for a 14-15 day tourist eSIM — Movistar Turista at EUR 20, Vodafone Travel at EUR 25, and Orange Holiday at EUR 29.99. In-store prepaid plans start lower but require registration.

### Which Spain eSIM with a number is best for tourists?

Orange Holiday Europe offers the most complete package — 25GB, a Spanish number, calls, and SMS across 30 countries — and can be bought online before you travel.

### Do I need a passport to get a Spanish number?

For online tourist eSIMs, no. For in-store prepaid plans from the four carriers, yes — Spanish law requires identification.

### Can I use a Spanish number to book Renfe trains?

Yes. A Spanish number makes Renfe and AVE app registration and ticket delivery easier, which is why travelers planning to take the train often choose a plan with a number.

### Can I get a Spanish number for a long stay?

Yes. For stays longer than a few weeks, a prepaid plan from Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, or an MVNO such as Pepephone, Lowi, or DIGI is usually the best value — but most require Spanish identification or a Spanish bank account.

### What if I only need data, not calls?

Then a data-only eSIM from Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, Saily, or Roamify is usually cheaper and simpler. See our [provider comparison](/blog/spain-esim-providers-airalo-holafly-nomad-comparison/) and [price guide](/blog/spain-esim-price-guide-cheapest-plans/).

## Choosing Between a Number and Data-Only

The choice between a data-only eSIM and one with a Spanish number comes down to how much you will rely on local apps and calls. If your trip includes Renfe trains, ride-hailing, or restaurant bookings, a plan with a number — Orange Holiday Europe, Vodafone Travel, or Movistar Turista — removes the most common friction. If you only need maps, messaging, and browsing, a data-only eSIM is the lighter, cheaper option.
