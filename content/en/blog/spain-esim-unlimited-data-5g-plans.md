## What 'Unlimited' Means in Practice: Fair Usage Policies

"Unlimited" data in the eSIM market rarely means truly infinite high-speed data. Every provider enforces a fair usage policy (FUP) that determines how much data you can use before speed reductions apply. Understanding **Spain eSIM fair usage** policies is the difference between a seamless trip and frustratingly slow connectivity.

### Fair usage policy structures across providers:

| Provider | Data Before Throttle | Throttled Speed | Reset Period | Typical Usable Data (30 days) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holafly | 1GB daily at full speed | 80 Mbps (varies by network) | Daily | 30GB+ at reduced speed |
| Multi-Network | 50GB monthly at high priority | Reduced during congestion | Monthly | 50GB+ at full speed |
| Orange Holiday | 25GB total (regional cap) | 128 kbps after cap | Never resets | 25GB at full speed |
| Movistar Prepaid | 10-20GB depending on plan | 256 kbps after cap | Billing cycle | 10-20GB at full speed |
| Airalo (high-cap) | Hard cap at plan limit | No overage possible | N/A | Exactly plan GB amount |
| Vodafone Travel | 10GB total | 128 kbps after cap | Never resets | 10GB at full speed |

Holafly's approach caps daily full-speed data at 1GB, then reduces speed to approximately 80 Mbps for the rest of the day. This remains usable for messaging, maps, and social media but struggles with HD video streaming. In practice, after the 1GB threshold, users can still browse, navigate, and message, but YouTube drops to 480p, Netflix buffers frequently, and video calls become pixelated.

Some providers apply a broader 50GB monthly threshold before potential deprioritization on congested towers. The key word is "deprioritization" rather than "throttling." Deprioritization means your data slows only when the tower is congested with higher-priority traffic. At 3 AM with no congestion, you receive full speed even after 50GB. During peak hours in central Madrid, you may experience slower speeds after crossing the threshold.

### Three types of "unlimited" data policies:

1. **Truly unlimited with soft cap**: Full speed up to a threshold, then potential slowdowns only during congestion (multi-network, premium carrier plans)
2. **Daily unlimited with hard throttle**: Full speed for a daily allowance (usually 1GB), then throttled to lower speed for the rest of the day (Holafly, some MVNOs)
3. **Regional unlimited with hard cap**: Full speed up to a regional data limit across multiple countries, then hard throttled to extremely slow speeds (Orange Holiday Europe, Vodafone Travel)

### Key limitations to watch for in **Spain unlimited data eSIM** plans:

- Daily data caps hidden behind "unlimited" marketing where providers advertise unlimited but throttle after 500MB-1GB daily
- Speed throttling that makes video streaming impractical after the daily or monthly threshold
- Hotspot/tethering restrictions on some unlimited plans that block laptop connectivity entirely
- 5G access limited to specific partner networks, with some budget resellers restricting to 4G
- Geographic restrictions preventing use outside Spain, which matters for multi-country trips
- Video streaming resolution caps (480p or 720p) enforced at the network level
- Background data limits that prevent app updates, cloud sync, and iOS/Android system updates

### How fair usage policies compare internationally:

Spanish carrier fair usage policies are regulated by [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/), which requires clear disclosure of speed caps, data limits, and throttling policies. International eSIM providers operating under home-country regulations (Holafly under Colombian regulation, Airalo under Singapore regulation) may not face the same transparency requirements. This creates an information asymmetry where Spanish carrier policies are easier to verify.

Orange Holiday Europe exemplifies the regional complexity: it offers 25GB across 30 European countries but switches to a hard throttle at 128 kbps after depletion. That speed is barely enough for text-based messaging and makes any video content impossible. The 128 kbps speed translates to approximately 0.016 MB/s, meaning loading a single website takes 30-60 seconds.

### Reading the fine print checklist:

- Check whether "unlimited" applies to Spain only or includes EU roaming
- Verify hotspot/tethering is explicitly mentioned as allowed
- Look for daily vs total caps in the terms of service
- Check if 5G access requires an additional fee or plan tier
- Confirm whether throttled speeds support video streaming
- Read recent user reviews from Spain specifically (not general reviews)
- Verify the provider's customer support availability in your time zone

For heavy users, the practical question shifts from "is it unlimited" to "at what speed and for how long." A 50GB roaming plan often delivers more usable data than a "truly unlimited" plan that throttles after 1GB daily over a two-week stay. The **Spain unlimited data eSIM** price 2026 varies significantly between providers, and paying more does not automatically mean getting more usable data.

### 5G frequency bands in Spain:

Spanish 5G operates across three primary frequency bands, each with different performance characteristics:

| Band | Frequency | Carrier Usage | Speed | Range | Penetration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n78 | 3.5 GHz | All carriers | 200-600 Mbps | Medium | Low (indoor weak) |
| n1 | 2100 MHz | All carriers | 100-300 Mbps | Wide | Good |
| n28 | 700 MHz | Movistar only | 50-150 Mbps | Very wide | Excellent (indoor/rural) |

The n78 band provides the fastest speeds but struggles with indoor penetration and long-range coverage. Travelers visiting Spain's interior villages, mountainous regions, or staying in stone-walled rural hotels will maintain 5G connectivity on Movistar when Orange and Vodafone users drop to 4G.

### 5G performance factors in Spain:

4. **Elevation**: Upper floors of buildings receive stronger 5G signals, lower floors and basements may drop to 4G
5. **Proximity to towers**: Optimal 5G range is 200-500 meters from a tower in dense urban areas

### How providers detect and block hotspot traffic:

Hotspot restrictions are typically enforced at the network level through TTL (Time To Live) value inspection. Every data packet has a TTL value that decreases by 1 each time it passes through a router or device. When a phone receives data for itself, the TTL decreases by 1 from the original value. When a tethered device receives forwarded data, the TTL decreases by 2 (phone + tethered device). Carriers identify this TTL delta and block packets with the "wrong" TTL signature.

Some providers use additional detection methods:

- User-Agent string inspection (checking if traffic comes from a non-phone browser)
- Deep packet inspection (DPI) to identify tethering protocol signatures
- Connection count monitoring (multiple simultaneous connections from different IP ranges)
- Traffic pattern analysis (tethered devices show different usage patterns than phones)

### Workarounds for hotspot-restricted plans:

- Use a dedicated travel router that connects to the eSIM phone and rebroadcasts WiFi (GL.iNet routers work well)
- Enable private hotspot via VPN on rooted or jailbroken devices that rewrite TTL values
- Carry a secondary device with a separate capped data eSIM for laptop work
- Use a phone-to-phone sharing app like NetShare (Android) that masks tethering traffic by rewriting TTL headers
- Purchase a separate low-cost tablet data eSIM for laptop use

None of these workarounds are guaranteed. Holafly actively blocks hotspot workaround techniques, and repeated attempts can result in account suspension. CNMC regulations do not currently require eSIM providers to support tethering, leaving it as a commercial decision.
