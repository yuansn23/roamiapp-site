## Orange France Prepaid eSIM: What the Carrier Sells Directly

Orange Travel is an eSIM product sold directly by Orange France through its website and physical stores. It is distinct from standard travel eSIMs because Orange is the carrier, not a reseller. The eSIM connects directly to Orange's network with the same priority as Orange postpaid subscribers.

Key specifications of Orange Travel eSIM:

- **Two product lines**: data-only plans (from 4.99 euro) and all-inclusive plans with unlimited calls, SMS, and a French phone number (from 19.99 euro)

- **Hotspot tethering** included on all plans

### Network differences

Both products now draw on multiple French networks: Holafly lists all four on its France page, while Orange Travel smart-selects among them. Orange still has the widest 4G/5G coverage in France at approximately 95% population coverage, so an Orange-anchored connection remains the safest bet in remote areas. In practice, this means:

- In Paris and other major cities: negligible difference between the two
- On TGV routes: both perform well, though Orange has slightly better coverage in tunnel sections

Holafly's unlimited data is attractive on paper, but there are limitations. As [Holafly's product page](https://esim.holafly.com/esim-france/) states, their France eSIM activates automatically upon arrival and connects to the strongest available partner network.

## When a French eSIM Number Really Matters

The primary advantage of Orange Travel over all travel eSIMs is the included French phone number. This is not a minor feature for certain scenarios. Travelers searching for a **France eSIM with French number** will find Orange Travel as the only carrier eSIM option. Tier-by-tier detail on the number plans sits in our [France eSIM number guide](/blog/france-esim-with-phone-number/).

### Services that require or strongly prefer a French phone number

**SNCF (French Railways):** Booking TGV and Intercité tickets through the SNCF Connect app often requires a phone number for ticket delivery and alerts. While some international numbers work, a French number provides the most reliable experience. E-ticket delivery via SMS to an international number sometimes fails.

**Navigo pass (Paris transit):** The Navigo Easy card and smartphone-based Navigo passes require a French phone number for registration and top-up through the [Île-de-France Mobilités](https://www.iledefrance-mobilites.fr/) app. Visitors using data-only eSIMs cannot register for digital Navigo passes and must use paper tickets or a physical Navigo card purchased at a ticket window.

**Restaurant reservations:** La Fourchette (The Fork), the dominant restaurant booking platform in France, sends SMS confirmations. International numbers can work for account creation, but French numbers receive confirmations more reliably. Many high-end Paris restaurants also call to confirm reservations, and a French number avoids international calling charges.

**Delivery services:** Uber Eats, Deliveroo, and food delivery platforms in France use SMS verification that works most reliably with French numbers.

**Banking and verification:** If you need to open a French bank account, register for a SIM-only plan, or verify identity with French services, a French phone number is mandatory.

### Alternatives to Orange Travel for a French number

- **Free Mobile prepaid SIM:** Available at Free stores in Paris and other cities. Costs approximately 10-20 euro for a starter pack with data. Requires in-person purchase with passport.
- **SFR prepaid:** Available at SFR stores. Similar pricing to Free but less accessible for tourists.
- **Google Voice:** Not officially available in France. Calls to French numbers from Google Voice may not work reliably.
- **Skype number:** A French Skype number costs approximately $5/month but requires internet for calls. Not useful for SMS verification.

## Why Travel eSIMs Usually Cost Less

The price difference between Orange Travel and travel eSIMs like Airalo, Holafly, and Ubigi is not arbitrary. It reflects structural differences in how these products are built and priced.

### Orange Travel's cost structure

Orange Travel is a retail product from a Tier 1 mobile network operator. It includes:

- Full MVNO infrastructure (HLR, billing, number allocation)
- French number management (number porting, regulatory compliance)
- Tier 1 customer support (phone, in-store, online)
- Marketing and distribution costs (physical stores, retail staff)
- Orange network infrastructure maintenance

These costs explain the historical price gap — and why the data-only line's recent repricing is notable. Orange has clearly decided to compete on entry-level data pricing while keeping the margin in the all-inclusive plans, where the French number infrastructure justifies the premium. The number is the product: at 19.99 euro for 20GB with calls, you are paying roughly 2 euro more than the data-only equivalent specifically for the line, not the data.

### Travel eSIM cost structure

Airalo, Holafly, and Ubigi operate as eSIM aggregators or MVNOs with different cost structures:

- No physical retail presence
- No French number infrastructure (in most cases)
- Automated digital support (chatbots, email, knowledge bases)
- Wholesale data rates negotiated with carriers like Orange and Bouygues
- Global platform costs spread across millions of users across 190+ countries

Travel eSIMs arbitrage the wholesale data market. They buy data in bulk from carriers at low rates and resell it with a markup that historically undercut carrier retail pricing — a gap Orange Travel's data-only line has now narrowed at the entry level, while travel eSIMs keep their edge in validity flexibility (30-day windows on small plans) and top-up economics.

For most tourists visiting France for 7-14 days and staying in cities, Orange Travel's data-only line and travel eSIMs now trade wins on price — the deciding factors are validity length, top-up policy, and whether the French number matters. ## When to Skip the Orange eSIM for France: Five Scenarios

Orange Travel is not automatically the right choice, even at its new prices. Here are five specific scenarios where choosing a travel eSIM is still objectively better. This section answers the common question "When should I **skip Orange Travel** and choose a travel eSIM instead?"

### Scenario 1: Paris-only city break of 3-5 days with minimal data needs.

Paris has excellent coverage on all four networks. Data needs for a light user are 1-2GB. Ubigi's 3GB/30-day plan at $4 or Saily's 1GB/7-day at $3.99 covers it for less than Orange Travel's 4.99 euro entry price with room to spare on validity. No French number is needed for a short Paris trip — restaurant bookings, museum tickets, and Uber rides all work with an international number. The saving is modest, but so is the effort: one purchase covers the trip plus a buffer.

### Scenario 2: Budget backpacking across multiple cities over multiple weeks.

For budget travelers spending 3-4 weeks in France, Orange Travel's fixed validity windows work against you — the 10GB plan dies at day 14. Ubigi's 10GB/30-day plan at $10 or Nomad's 20GB/30-day at $21 stretches across the whole stay. Backpackers also tend to top up in small increments, which Ubigi and Airalo handle more flexibly than a fixed Orange plan.

### Scenario 3: Multi-country European itinerary.

Orange Travel only covers France. If your trip includes France plus Italy, Spain, Switzerland, or the UK, you need either a separate eSIM for each country or a Europe regional plan.
