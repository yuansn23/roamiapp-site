Two things make Turkey unlike France, Germany, or Spain.

**1. Turkey is not in the EU roaming zone.** A "Europe" eSIM doesn't cover Turkey, and a Turkey eSIM doesn't roam into Greece. The "grab a Europe plan and go" advice falls apart at the border. ## What Is Turkey's 120-Day IMEI Registration Rule?

Most guides mention the IMEI rule in a single scare‑line and move on. Here's what's actually going on, so you can plan around it:

- **What it is.** Turkey keeps a whitelist of phone IMEIs. A foreign phone used on a **local SIM** (not a roaming eSIM) is allowed to work for about 120 days. After that, the network blocks the device's IMEI.
- **How to stay legal.** You register the phone and pay a fee. The fee has risen sharply with inflation — historically a few hundred dollars' worth of lira, and a moving target.
- **Who's affected.** Anyone on a local Turkcell, Vodafone, or Turk Telekom line for more than ~120 days. Short‑stay tourists on a physical SIM never hit the window.
- **Who's not affected.** Travel eSIMs. They operate as a roaming connection (through the provider's foreign carrier agreement), so the IMEI block aimed at local lines generally doesn't apply.
- **The practical upshot.** For a season or a long digital‑nomad stay, a [travel eSIM for Turkey](/turkey-esim/) skips the registration fee and the paperwork entirely — a saving that dwarfs any per‑gigabyte price difference.

Again, confirm the current rule and fee before a 120+ day stay; this is a fast‑moving area and we're relaying public reporting, not legal advice.
