**Short answer:** a data-only Japan eSIM costs roughly **$1.2–4.0 per GB**, and a full trip usually lands between **$4 and $25**. Unlimited plans run about **$2.5–4.1 per day**. Light travellers on a one-week trip typically spend under $15; heavy users who stream and hotspot should budget $25–70.

The numbers below are the tiers listed by the major providers in **September 2026**. Prices move often — treat every figure as "around", and confirm at the provider's checkout page before you buy.

## 1. How much does a Japan eSIM cost in 2026?

Three price bands cover almost every plan on the market:

- **Entry (about $3–5):** 1GB for a few days, or 500MB for a single day. Enough for maps and messaging on a short city break.
- **Mid (about $8–25):** 3–20GB valid for 7–30 days. This is where most travellers buy.
- **Unlimited (about $11–74):** priced by duration rather than data, roughly $2.5–4.1 per day, cheapest on 30-day plans.

### What actually drives the price differences

Two structural facts explain most of the price differences. First, **longer validity costs proportionally less** — a 5GB plan over 30 days is barely more expensive than the same 5GB over 7 days. Second, **unlimited is priced by the day**, so it only wins when your daily consumption is high enough to beat a fixed bundle.

A third factor is your handset. If your phone does not support eSIM — most iPhones from the XS (2018) onward and recent Android flagships do — you are pushed toward a physical tourist SIM, which generally costs more per gigabyte and has to be bought after you land.

### Fixed-data tiers: the per-gigabyte curve

Per-GB rates fall sharply as the bundle grows. At the entry tier you are paying **$3–4 per GB**; by 20–50GB the effective rate is closer to **$0.7–1.25 per GB**. If you are confident about your usage, buying the larger bundle up front is almost always better value than topping up twice.

One structural cost driver is easy to miss: **network generation**. A 4G LTE plan can be priced below a 5G-capable one in the same country, and a plan that routes through overseas servers costs its provider less to run than one that terminates locally. That is part of why two plans with identical data allowances can still be priced differently — and why the cheapest tier is not automatically the best fit.

### Unlimited plans are priced by the day

For unlimited plans, compare the **per-day** rate instead: a 30-day unlimited plan at around $69 works out near **$2.30 per day**, while a 3-day plan at $11.50 is closer to **$3.80 per day**. The longer you stay, the better unlimited looks.

### The days you pay for and the days you can use

There is a second clock running that the price table hides. A plan with 30 days of validity gives you 30 days from the moment it starts, and when it starts depends on the provider. Ubigi's Japan plans begin on arrival in Japan, so installing the profile at home weeks early costs you nothing. Airalo's rule varies by package: some plans start counting when you first connect to a network in Japan, while others start when you install the eSIM — which is exactly how people lose a week of a paid plan before their flight leaves.

The routine that is safe for both: buy early, install early, and leave the Japan line switched **off** in your phone's settings until you land. Installing a profile and enabling it are two different actions, and only the second one should happen in Japan. One more expiry quirk worth knowing: an Ubigi Japan plan that is never activated lapses six months after purchase, so buying in January for a July trip is fine, and buying in June for the following January is not.

## 6. Japan eSIM fixed bundles vs unlimited plans: which is cheaper?

Two worked examples, using the mid-tier rates above.

**Light user, 7 days, about 0.5GB per day (3.5GB total).**
A 5GB fixed plan at roughly $10 covers the week with headroom. Unlimited for the same 7 days starts around $25. **Fixed data saves about $15.**

**Heavy user, 14 days, about 3GB per day (42GB total).**
Fixed data means stacking bundles: 20GB at about $24 plus a second 20GB top-up lands near $45–50. A 14-day unlimited plan sits around $41–50 and removes the risk of running dry. Here **unlimited wins on convenience, and roughly ties on price**.

One more variable shapes that maths: what an "unlimited" plan actually allows before it throttles. The [Japan eSIM unlimited data guide](/blog/japan-esim-unlimited-data-guide/) sets out the daily caps and hotspot limits in detail.

### The crossover, worked through

The crossover is around **1.5–2GB per day**. Below it, buy data; above it, buy days.

## 7. Japan eSIM deals, promo codes and discounts

Discounts on Japan eSIMs come from three places, and only one of them is reliable.

- **Provider promotions.** Airalo and Nomad both run introductory and seasonal offers, and Nomad in particular has offered doubled-data promotions for new users. These are genuine but time-limited.
- **Marketplace coupons.** Travel platforms that resell eSIMs sometimes carry a first-order code. Worth a check, but stock and terms vary.
- **Bundle and multi-country plans.** If your itinerary includes Korea or Taiwan, a regional Asia plan is often cheaper than two country plans — the same logic that makes longer validity cheaper applies to wider coverage.

### Which discounts are worth chasing

Treat "discount code" listicles with caution: many are affiliate pages recycling expired codes. The reliable discount is the **structural** one — buy the longer validity or the larger bundle and the per-GB price drops on its own.

## 8. Hidden Japan eSIM costs the headline price leaves out

### The five costs that change the final bill

The advertised price is rarely the whole bill. Watch for:

- **Fair-use throttling on unlimited plans.** Airalo's unlimited drops to about 1 Mbps after 3GB in a day; Nomad's after 2GB. You still pay the same, but you get much less speed.
- **Hotspot restrictions.** Some unlimited plans cap tethering to around 500MB–1GB per day, which matters if you planned to work from a laptop.
- **Top-up pricing.** Buying more data later can cost more per GB than the original bundle. Estimate generously up front.
- **No refunds after activation.** Most providers refund only if the QR code has not been scanned, so a wrong purchase is hard to undo.
- **Currency conversion.** Prices shown in your local currency include a conversion margin; the USD price is usually the base.

## 9. What a Failed Activation Costs You

The cheapest plan is not the cheapest plan if it does not connect. Refund rules are the part of Japanese pricing that varies most between sellers, and they are worth reading before you pay rather than after.

Three patterns cover most of the market. Some sellers offer a generous window measured in months and cover incompatible or locked handsets, as long as the QR code has never been scanned — Holafly is the clearest example. Others treat refunds as a technical-fault remedy only, and route change-of-mind requests into store credit rather than money back, which is how Airalo's policy works. And at least one Japan-focused seller publishes a no-refund line on its unlimited product while shipping a free pocket WiFi unit as a physical fallback if the eSIM will not connect after troubleshooting, which is a different answer to the same problem.

The reason this matters is the cost of the alternative. A data SIM bought from an [airport SIM counter](/blog/japan-sim-card-guide/) after a failed eSIM runs to roughly $20–33 for a short allowance, and a roaming day pass from a US carrier runs $10–12 a day. A refund window of months is worth more than a two-dollar price difference.

Two mechanical points save most disputes. The QR code is one-time: deleting the eSIM profile and scanning again is not a supported route, so do not delete a profile while troubleshooting. And installing the profile is not the same as activating it, so an eSIM installed at home and never switched on has usually not consumed its validity — check the provider's activation policy before assuming a plan is spent.

## 11. How Japan eSIM Prices Moved Through 2026

Prices in this market move in steps rather than curves, and the step that mattered in 2026 was on the unlimited side.

### Unlimited got more expensive

The most visible change of the year was Airalo repricing its Japan unlimited tiers upward in early 2026. The effect was to close most of the gap between unlimited plans and fixed bundles, and to flip the value argument: a fixed 20–50 GB bundle now buys more usable high-speed data per dollar than a comparable run of unlimited days for anybody who is not genuinely streaming all day.

### Why some prices are quoted in yen and others in dollars

Sellers based in Japan — Mobal, Sakura Mobile, b-mobile, IIJmio — quote in yen, and Japanese consumer prices are displayed inclusive of consumption tax, so the number you see is the number you pay. Overseas sellers quote in US dollars and no Japanese consumption tax applies to a plan you buy from abroad, which is one structural reason an international eSIM can undercut a domestic counter product at the same data volume. The trade-off is currency: a yen-priced plan moves with the exchange rate between booking and billing, and a dollar-priced plan does not.

### Promotions are a permanent feature

Both of the large marketplaces run introductory offers, seasonal sales and occasional doubled-data promotions, and Japan-focused sellers periodically discount by a fifth. None of it is worth building a plan around, because the promotions rotate, but it does mean list price is the wrong number to compare at the checkout stage.

## 12. Japan eSIM Price FAQ: Cost per GB and per Day

**How much does a Japan eSIM cost?**
Around $4 for an entry 1GB plan, $15–25 for a typical 7–14 day bundle, and $26–74 for unlimited plans depending on duration. Per-GB rates fall to roughly $0.7–1.25 on larger bundles.

**What is the cheapest Japan eSIM?**
On entry pricing, Ubigi's small daily plan and Airalo's 1GB tier are among the lowest, at around $3–4. Ubigi and Nomad tend to lead on larger bundles, with 10GB for about $16–17.

**Is unlimited worth it in Japan?**
Only above roughly 1.5–2GB per day. Below that, a fixed bundle is cheaper. Above it, unlimited removes the risk of running out and is often priced similarly to stacked top-ups.

**Are Japan eSIM prices going up?**
They move with promotions rather than a single trend. Airalo's Japan unlimited tiers were repriced upward in early 2026, which closed much of the gap with Holafly and made fixed data relatively more attractive.

**Can I get a refund if I buy the wrong plan?**
Usually only before the QR code is scanned. Read the specific policy on the provider's checkout page before paying.

**Where these prices come from.**

**Do I pay Japanese tax on a Japan eSIM?**
Not usually. Plans sold by overseas providers are priced in US dollars with no Japanese consumption tax attached, while Japanese providers quote yen prices inclusive of tax because that is how consumer prices are displayed in the country.

**Does a Japan eSIM expire if I never use it?**
It depends on the provider. An Ubigi Japan plan that is never activated lapses six months after purchase, while some other providers do not state a shelf life on the Japan plan page at all. If your trip is more than a few months away, check before you buy early.

**Should I buy one large plan or two smaller ones?**
One larger plan, almost always. The per-gigabyte rate improves by four to five times between the smallest and largest tiers, and a top-up bought mid-trip can cost more per gigabyte than the bundle you started with.

**When should I install my Japan eSIM so I do not lose days?**
Install it at home on Wi-Fi, then leave the line switched off until you land. That works whether your provider counts validity from installation or from first connection in Japan, and it avoids the airport Wi-Fi scramble.

**Are Japan eSIM prices higher in 2026 than in 2025?**
Unlimited plans are, after the early-2026 repricing.
