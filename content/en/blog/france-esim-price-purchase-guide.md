## France eSIM Pricing Strategy: Why Providers Charge What They Do

Understanding why France eSIM prices vary so widely helps you avoid overpaying for what you actually need.

### Ubigi's aggressive pricing (lowest across most tiers)

Ubigi is the eSIM arm of Transatel, a French MVNO headquartered in Paris. Because Transatel is a French company with existing wholesale agreements with Bouygues Telecom, it can negotiate data rates that third-party resellers cannot match. Ubigi also operates its own eSIM platform without paying marketplace commissions. These structural advantages let Ubigi undercut Airalo, Holafly, and other international players by 20-40% on equivalent plans. Ubigi's 3GB at $4 and 10GB at $10 — both with 30-day validity — reflect these cost advantages.

### Airalo's Orange network premium

Airalo pays wholesale rates to Orange for access to France's premium network. Orange charges a premium to third-party MVNOs and resellers because its network is the most extensive in France. This wholesale premium is passed to Airalo customers. Airalo's 5GB at $11 on Orange versus Nomad's 5GB at $9 on Orange/Free reflects the single-network premium of roughly $2 per 5GB. For travelers who need Orange's wider rural coverage, this $2 premium is a reasonable investment.

### Orange Travel's repriced data line

Orange Travel historically ignored travel eSIM pricing — a retail product aimed at travelers who value the Orange brand, want a French phone number, and prefer buying at an airport store. The September 2026 price structure changes that story: at 4.99 euro for 5GB and 8.99 euro for 10GB, the data-only line now competes directly with wholesale-based travel eSIMs, while the margin has shifted to the all-inclusive plans (20GB with unlimited calls, SMS, and a French number at 19.99 euro). Orange Travel's pricing is still benchmarked against its domestic prepaid products — but the entry tiers now hold their own against anyone.

### Holafly's unlimited data pricing

Holafly charges a premium for unlimited data. The $27.50 for 7 days unlimited is more than double Ubigi's 10GB at $10, but for heavy data users who would consume 10-20GB in a week, Holafly's unlimited pricing is competitive. Holafly's cost structure includes wholesale payments to its French network partners, plus the risk that some users will consume 50-100GB. The unlimited pricing factors in this risk, making it more expensive per GB for light users but potentially cheaper per GB for heavy users.

## France eSIM Hidden Fees: What Providers Charge

The listed price of a France eSIM is rarely the complete cost. Understanding potential additional charges prevents surprises.

### Provider-specific fees and limits

- **Airalo:** No additional fees. The listed price is final. Some plans require top-up purchases in fixed increments ($5, $10, $25) that may leave unused credits.
- **Ubigi:** No additional fees. Plans are sold at the listed price. Top-ups are available in the exact amounts needed for specific data packages.
- **Holafly:** Hotspot/tethering is limited to 1GB per day on unlimited plans. Exceeding this cap results in hotspot speeds dropping to approximately 128 Kbps. The unlimited data claim applies only to on-device usage.
- **Orange Travel:** No additional fees. The listed price includes the French number and all taxes. Credit card payment at CDG stores may incur foreign transaction fees from your bank.
- **Nomad:** Some plans list prices exclusive of VAT. Nomad applies VAT at checkout based on your billing address. EU residents pay approximately 20% VAT on top of the listed price. Non-EU residents may have VAT removed upon verification.
- **Saily:** No additional fees. Plans are sold at the listed price with no hidden charges.
- Most providers: No additional fees beyond listed price. VAT may apply based on billing address.

### Fine print items to check before purchasing

1. **Validity period clock:** Most eSIMs count validity from the moment of activation, not from the moment of purchase. If you buy a 7-day plan but activate it 3 days before your trip, you lose 3 days of validity.
2. **Throttling after data cap:** All capped plans (non-unlimited) throttle speeds to 128-256 Kbps after the data allowance is exhausted. This is sufficient for messaging but not for any data-heavy tasks.
3. **Hotspot restrictions:** Many providers allow hotspot tethering, but some impose caps (Holafly: 1GB/day) or disable it entirely on certain plans.
4. **Carrier-locked phones:** eSIMs from all providers require an unlocked phone. US carrier-locked phones (AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon) may reject eSIM profiles from non-domestic carriers. Verify your phone's unlock status before purchasing.
5. **Multi-device restrictions:** Most eSIMs can be installed on only one device at a time. aloSIM is the exception with its data pooling feature.
6. **Refund policies:** Most eSIM providers do not offer refunds after the eSIM profile has been delivered or activated. Holafly advertises a 24-hour money-back window. Other providers handle refunds on a case-by-case basis.
7. **Network priority:** Travel eSIMs operate with MVNO priority on carrier networks. During congestion, carrier subscribers (including Orange Travel eSIMs) get priority over MVNO traffic. This affects speed during peak hours at tourist attractions.

### Transaction cost considerations

- **Currency conversion:** Providers listed in USD may charge in USD regardless of your card currency. Your bank's foreign transaction fee (typically 1-3%) applies if your card is in a different currency.
- **Payment method:** Credit cards, PayPal, and Apple Pay are accepted by most providers. Some providers (Ubigi, Nomad) also accept Google Pay.

- Data cap: 5GB high-speed, then throttled

## France Prepaid eSIM Card Options: Data-Only and Contract

The word "card" covers several different products, and the distinctions matter more than the label. A prepaid visitor plan needs no French address or bank account and is what almost every traveller should buy. A data-only plan drops voice and SMS to reach a lower price. ## France eSIM Seasonal Price Variations

France eSIM prices are relatively stable throughout the year, but some patterns affect availability and value.

### Summer peak season (June-August)

During France's peak tourist season, demand for eSIMs increases significantly. This does not typically change listed prices, but it can affect service quality:

- Network congestion reduces effective speeds during peak hours at tourist attractions
- Promo codes and discounts are less common during peak months
- Support response times may be longer during high-volume periods
- Top-up delivery may take longer during peak demand

### Shoulder season (April-May, September-October)

These months offer the best combination of good weather, moderate tourist crowds, and full promotional availability. Providers are more likely to offer promo codes during shoulder months to attract travelers.

### Low season (November-March)

Low season sees fewer travelers and more promotional offers. Some providers run seasonal sales during Black Friday (November), Christmas (December), and winter promotions (January-February). These sales can offer 15-30% off standard pricing.

### Last-minute purchase risk

Purchasing an eSIM on the day of travel carries a small risk of delayed delivery if your provider's automated systems experience issues. Purchase at least 24 hours before departure to ensure the QR code arrives in time.
