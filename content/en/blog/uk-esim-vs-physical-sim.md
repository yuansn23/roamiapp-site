## What are the three connectivity options for UK travel?

Travellers to Great Britain choose between eSIM (a digital SIM downloaded to your phone, GBP 5-25, no physical card), physical SIM (a plastic card bought from a UK shop, GBP 10-30, requires ID), and pocket WiFi (a rented portable hotspot device, GBP 25-45/week, shares data among multiple devices).

**eSIM**
A digital SIM you download onto your phone ?no plastic card, no swapping SIM trays. You buy a plan online, install it over WiFi before travel or upon landing, and your phone connects to UK mobile networks. Modern iPhones (XR and newer), Samsung Galaxy devices (S20 and newer), Google Pixels, and most recent Android phones support eSIM. The [GSMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSMA), the global body setting mobile standards, published the eSIM specification in 2016 ?the same technology powering every modern eSIM today.

**Physical SIM**
A traditional plastic SIM card you grab from a UK mobile shop, supermarket, or airport retailer. Since 2024, UK regulations require proof of identity (passport or valid photo ID) to buy and activate a pay-as-you-go SIM. All UK network SIMs include a real UK mobile number, and they work in any unlocked phone ?doesn't matter how old your handset is.

**Pocket WiFi**
A separate portable device, about credit-card-sized but thicker, that creates a personal WiFi hotspot. You pick it up at a UK airport or have it delivered to your accommodation, switch it on, and connect your phone and other devices to its WiFi network. Battery life typically lasts 8-12 hours, requiring daily recharging.

International roaming from your home carrier is a fourth option. Don't pick it. It's almost never the best choice financially. The **UK eSIM vs international roaming cost savings** are dramatic ?one week of roaming from a US carrier costs about USD 70 (AT&T International Day Pass at USD 10/day, 2026 pricing), while a UK eSIM with comparable data runs about USD 10-15. That's not a typo.

## How does coverage compare between eSIM, physical SIM, and pocket WiFi in the UK?

Coverage in Great Britain depends on which UK mobile network each option accesses. Multi-network eSIMs like Roami (switching across EE, Vodafone, O2, and Three) deliver the broadest coverage because they aren't tied to any single carrier's weak spots. Physical SIMs and pocket WiFi are locked to one network.

That's the whole game, really. Network access = coverage quality.

**Network Access**
**eSIMs** connect to one or more UK mobile networks. Single-network eSIMs (Airalo on EE, Nomad on O2) behave exactly like a physical SIM from that network. Multi-network eSIMs (Ubigi on EE and Three, Holafly on O2, Vodafone, and Three) can switch between carriers. Auto-switching eSIMs (Roami across EE, Vodafone, O2, and Three) provide the broadest coverage by dynamically picking the strongest available signal at any location, according to Ofcom coverage data (2025).

**Physical SIMs** lock you to the network you buy from. An EE SIM card connects only to EE's infrastructure. A Vodafone SIM connects only to Vodafone. If that network has weak signal at your location in rural Great Britain, you're stuck. No fallback. The upside is you can choose which network to buy based on your trip ?EE for the Scottish Highlands, for example.

**Pocket WiFi devices** run on a single UK network depending on the rental company. Most use EE or Three. The device creates a WiFi hotspot your phone connects to, so your phone's eSIM compatibility doesn't matter. But you're still tied to whichever network the rental company stuffed inside the box.

**The prices are genuinely good.** Plans start from roughly GBP 5 for a weekend's data. For a week of moderate use across UK cities, expect GBP 10-15 ?about half the cost of a physical SIM at the airport or home carrier roaming.
