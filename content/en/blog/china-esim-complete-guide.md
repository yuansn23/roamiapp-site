An international China eSIM routes your data through Hong Kong or Singapore, routing data outside mainland networks so Google Maps, WhatsApp, and Instagram work just as they do at home. The Great Firewall blocks Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, and thousands of other services on Chinese domestic networks — an international roaming eSIM sidesteps these blocks without a separate VPN.

This page is a quick overview of everything China eSIM. Each section links to a dedicated in-depth guide. **What Makes China eSIM Different from Other Destinations**

Using an eSIM in China is different from Europe or North America for one reason: the Great Firewall. In most countries, any eSIM or physical SIM works the same way. In China, the type of eSIM you choose determines whether Google, WhatsApp, and Instagram work at all. There are two categories: **international roaming eSIMs**, which use international routing through overseas routing, and **local Chinese eSIMs**, which operate inside the firewall. This single distinction is the most important decision you'll make.

**The 2026 China eSIM Landscape**

Built-in VPN functionality, Hong Kong routing, and automatic carrier switching are now standard features. Competition has pushed per-GB prices down roughly 15–20% versus 2025, and real-world reviews on Reddit and travel forums make it easier to compare providers.

## Does eSIM Actually Work in China?

Yes. All three state-owned carriers — China Mobile, China Unicom, and China Telecom — support eSIM technology, but foreign visitors can't easily buy a local eSIM (real-name registration and a Chinese ID are usually required). The practical solution is an international roaming eSIM, which routes data through Hong Kong or Singapore so Google Maps, WhatsApp, and Instagram work without a separate VPN. [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT212780) and the [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/technologies/esim/) publish the underlying compatibility and roaming standards.

Most travel eSIMs are data-only: you keep your home SIM active in dual-SIM mode for voice and SMS verification codes. For background on each carrier, see the official operator reports:

- [China Mobile's official reports](https://www.chinamobileltd.com)
- [China Unicom's official site](https://www.chinaunicom.com.cn)
- [China Telecom](https://www.chinatelecom.com.cn/)

### The Three Networks Behind Your China eSIM

China runs three state-owned networks, and the one your plan lands on decides how usable your signal is in any given place:

| Carrier | Scale | Where it wins | What that means for your trip |
|---|---|---|---|
| **China Mobile** | 980M+ subscribers | Widest national reach — rural villages, mountains and national parks; 5G in every prefecture-level city | The safest bet when your eSIM connects to only one network |
| **China Unicom** | ~320M subscribers | Fastest in dense city centres | Best real-world speeds in Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen CBDs |
| **China Telecom** | ~310M subscribers | Southern China and the eastern seaboard | Strong in Guangzhou and Shenzhen, weakest of the three outside cities |

Scale is not the constraint here: 4.84 million 5G base stations were in service nationwide by the end of 2025, the biggest such deployment anywhere. Inside Tier 1 cities all three networks perform well, so the ranking above matters most once you leave them; the [China eSIM coverage guide](/blog/china-esim-coverage-cities-trains-5g/) maps it city by city.

### What Routing Means for Which Apps Work

Two kinds of China eSIM exist, and the difference is where your data leaves the network:

| | International-routing eSIM | Local Chinese eSIM |
|---|---|---|
| Where your data exits | Provider gateway in Hong Kong or Singapore | Mainland Chinese network |
| How visitors buy it | Online, instant QR install, no local ID | Usually requires real-name registration with local ID |
| Google, WhatsApp, Instagram | Reachable on any connection | Not reachable |
| Best suited to | Short and mid-length trips | Residents and long-stay visitors with local ID |

Whichever plan you buy, internet use in mainland China is subject to local regulations — know the current rules and follow them. The mechanics of routing, and which providers use which gateway, are covered in the [China eSIM VPN and firewall guide](/blog/china-esim-vpn-firewall-guide/).

## How Do You Set Up an eSIM for China? Step-by-Step Guide

Install the eSIM profile before you leave home — provider websites and QR-code emails can be blocked once you're on a Chinese network. Scan the QR code, set the eSIM as your data line, enable data roaming, and keep your home SIM for calls and SMS.

→ Step-by-step for iPhone and Android: [China eSIM setup and installation guide](/blog/china-esim-setup-installation-dual-sim/).

### The Pre-Departure Setup Sequence

For a China eSIM, the order matters more than the steps. Doing these three stages out of sequence is the most common reason it fails on arrival:

| When | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 weeks before departure | Buy the plan, confirm your phone is unlocked and eSIM-capable, and start 12306 verification if you plan to book your own train tickets | Passport verification on 12306 takes a few working days online, so leaving it to the last minute can cost you a train |
| At home on Wi-Fi, the day before | Install the profile, set it as the data line, switch data roaming on, and run a test connection | Provider websites, QR-code emails and app stores all get harder to reach from a Chinese network once you land |
| On landing | Do nothing except turn off airplane mode — and select a network manually if it has not connected within a minute | The profile is already installed, so there is no airport counter queue and no QR code to hunt for while jet-lagged |

## China eSIM Practical Limits: Hotspot, Top-Ups and Validity

### Hotspot and Tethering Rules by Provider

| Provider | Shared hotspot | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Roami | Yes | No artificial throttling on shared data |
| Holafly | Yes | Shared hotspot can be capped after 500MB per day |
| Airalo, Saily, Ubigi, Nomad | Yes | Hotspot draws on the same plan allowance, so sharing shortens your own runway |

Hotspot always routes through the China eSIM data line even with your home SIM in place for calls and SMS. That is expected behaviour rather than a fault, and it means a laptop work session comes out of your plan, not your carrier's.

### Top-Ups and Validity Rules

Two rules keep a China eSIM from running dry mid-trip:

- **Top up before you need to.** Providers that support automatic top-up trigger it at roughly 10% remaining; if you top up manually, do it nearer 20% so you still have a working connection to reach the provider's app or website.
- **Check the validity model, not just the data size.** Fixed-date plans expire on a set day whether or not the data was used, while data-consumed plans stay valid until the allowance runs out. For a bursty trip — heavy one week, quiet the next — the second model wastes less.

### Where Local Rules and Geography Add Friction

| Situation | What applies |
|---|---|
| Buying a local Chinese SIM | Real-name registration applies to mobile services in China (MIIT Order No. 25, 2013), which is one practical reason visitors default to a travel eSIM |
| Tibet | Foreign independent travel is restricted — you travel on a guided tour with a Tibet Travel Permit — and coverage beyond Lhasa, Shigatse and the main corridor is thin. Carry a 20,000 mAh power bank; altitude drains batteries faster |
| Remote routes | The Yangtze gorges and some Great Wall sections have no usable signal at all, so download offline maps before leaving the city |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use an eSIM for travel to China?** Yes — a travel eSIM is now the standard way tourists stay connected in mainland China. You buy it online, install the QR profile before departure, and it connects to mainland networks the moment you land, with international routing for Google, WhatsApp, and Instagram on most providers.

**Can I use an eSIM for China if my phone is carrier-locked?** No — a carrier-locked phone only accepts SIM profiles from its home carrier. Unlock your phone first (usually free once your contract ends).

**Will an eSIM for China work immediately upon landing?** Yes, in most cases. Turn off airplane mode, enable data roaming, and it should connect to China Mobile within 30–60 seconds.

**Can I keep my home SIM in the phone while using eSIM?** Yes — dual-SIM mode is supported by modern iPhones (XS and later) and most Androids. The eSIM handles data; your home SIM handles voice and SMS.

**What happens if I run out of data mid-trip?** Most providers let you top up through their app. Top up while you still have data — around 20% remaining — so you can still reach the top-up site.

**Do I need to remove my China eSIM after the trip?** No — you can leave it installed and disabled. Set your home SIM back to the data line when you return.
