## 3. TOP 5 In-Depth Japan eSIM Reviews: Tested in Tokyo, Osaka, and the Shinkansen

### 🥇 #1 Ubigi — The Best Rated eSIM for Japan in All Aspects

**Ubigi** takes the top spot as the **best eSIM for Japan** for most travelers. Why does it outperform the competition? Ubigi is owned by Transatel, whose parent company NTT operates Japan's largest and most reliable network—**NTT Docomo**. This means Ubigi does not rely on third-party roaming aggregators; it rides natively on Docomo's infrastructure, offering the widest rural coverage from Hokkaido to Okinawa.

**Plans and pricing:** Plans start at $3.50 USD for 1GB of data valid for 3 days. The best-seller is the 25GB plan for 30 days at $32 USD. Unlimited data options run $39 USD for 15 days or $65 USD for 30 days. A 10GB/7-day plan is also available for $14 USD, and an 8-day unlimited option costs $25 USD.

**Performance:** In 2026 field tests across Tokyo and Osaka, 5G held up for streaming, gaming and video calls without lag on the move. On the Shinkansen, connectivity remained stable with only brief drops in long tunnels. Independent speed tests show Ubigi averaging approximately 12.2 Mbps download in Japan, with latency around 105ms.

**Fair Usage Policy (FUP):** Ubigi's unlimited plans include a generous high-speed allowance—roughly 30–60GB depending on plan length—after which speeds are reduced to around 2 Mbps. Most users will never hit this cap during a standard trip.

**Pros:**
- Native NTT Docomo connection—ensuring the **most reliable eSIM Japan** can provide outside major cities
- Unlimited hotspot/tethering with no daily caps—rare among travel eSIMs
- Extremely easy installation via QR code or app
- If you run out of data without WiFi, Ubigi sponsors a connection long enough to purchase a top-up
- Full MVNO with direct carrier partnerships, not a reseller

**Cons:**
- No Japanese phone number included (data-only plan)
- Email and chatbot support only—no phone support

**Best for:** Solo travelers, couples, and anyone venturing outside Tokyo, especially to rural areas, mountains, or smaller islands. As one verified reviewer on Trustpilot noted: *"I had excellent mobile data connectivity everywhere I went, and the price was reasonable."* If you are looking for the **best esim to use in Japan** without worrying about dead zones, Ubigi is your safest bet.

## 7. What's New in 2026 for Japan eSIM Users

- **5G Expansion:** Japan's 5G network now covers the vast majority of the population. All recommended eSIMs above support 5G on tier-one networks like Docomo, KDDI, and SoftBank.
- **Pocket WiFi is Becoming Obsolete:** The price-per-GB for portable WiFi has become worse than eSIMs, and you no longer have to carry or return another device.
- **JR Pass Goes Digital:** The official Japan Rail Pass app now requires a working data connection to activate and display your QR ticket—making a reliable eSIM mandatory for rail travelers.
- **Free WiFi is Still Unreliable:** While convenience stores and train stations offer free WiFi, the authentication is fiddly and often requires a Japanese phone number. A good eSIM remains the most practical solution.
- **Roaming vs. Local eSIMs:** The industry has clearly split into "roaming-type" (Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi—higher latency) and "local-type" (CDJapan Rental—lower latency). For 2026, choosing the right type depends on whether you need geo-local services or simply want the cheapest data.
- **New Entrants:** Budget aggregators like eSIM-Now and MobiMatter offer some of the lowest per-GB rates in the market, often around $0.70–$0.75 per GB on large bundles. Check the fine print on support and refunds before committing.
- **Device Compatibility Note:** Ubigi's official compatibility list includes the Pixel 9 and Pixel 10 families, though a few third-party travel blogs claim setup issues. Verify on the provider's site before purchasing if you use these devices.


## 8. Japan eSIM FAQs: Data, Phones and Networks

**Q: Which esim is best for Japan overall?**
**A:** For the vast majority of travelers, **Ubigi** offers the best overall experience with native NTT Docomo 5G coverage, reasonable prices, and unlimited hotspot tethering.

**Q: What is the best esim for Japan travel on a tight budget?**
**A:** **Airalo** is the best budget-friendly eSIM for Japan, with plans starting at $4.00 USD for 1GB/3 days. **eSIM-Now** often undercuts the big brands on small-bundle per-GB pricing.

**Q: What esim to use in Japan for truly unlimited data?**
**A:** **Holafly** offers genuinely unlimited data for 3, 7, 15, or 30 days starting at $11.90 USD (3 days), though hotspot tethering is capped at around 1GB per day.

**Q: Which esim for Japan iPhone should I pick?**
**A:** All listed providers work with iPhone XR, XS, and newer models. **Airalo** has the most polished iOS app. **Saily** offers the best security features for iPhone users.

**Q: Is eSIM better than pocket WiFi for Japan?**
**A:** Yes, for most travelers. eSIMs are cheaper, more convenient, and require no extra device to carry, charge, or return at the airport.

**Q: Can I use hotspot/tethering with my Japan eSIM?**
**A:** Yes, but restrictions vary. **Ubigi** allows unlimited tethering. **Holafly** caps hotspot at roughly 500MB–1GB/day. **Airalo**'s policy varies by plan—always check before buying.

**Q: Do I need a Japanese phone number for my trip?**
**A:** Usually not. Most restaurants use reservation apps like Tabelog, which work with email. For emergencies or car rentals, use WhatsApp, LINE, or Skype. If you absolutely need a number, choose **Mobal** or **CDJapan Rental**.

**Q: Which network is best for rural Japan—Docomo, SoftBank, or au?**
**A:** **NTT Docomo** has the widest coverage in rural areas, followed by au (KDDI). SoftBank is best in cities.

**Q: Does Ubigi work with Google Pixel 9 and Pixel 10?**
**A:** Ubigi's own compatibility list includes both models, though some travel blogs report setup problems. Verify with the provider—or test with a [free eSIM trial](/free-esim/)—before purchasing.

**Q: How much data do I need for a week in Japan?**
**A:** Light users (maps + messaging): 1–2GB. Moderate users (+social media): 3–5GB. Heavy users (+streaming): 7–10GB+.
