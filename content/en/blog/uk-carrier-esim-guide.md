## Which UK Networks and Providers Support eSIM in 2026?

Every UK mobile network operator (MNO) supports eSIM, and most virtual operators (MVNOs) do too. Which UK eSIM brands sit on which network is the first thing to pin down — an MVNO is a reseller of one host network, not a network of its own, so its coverage is decided before you ever look at its prices. Beyond that, the differences that matter to a visitor are prepaid availability, whether an eSIM is issued instantly or by email, and whether the plan can be managed without UK documentation.

| Provider | Runs on | eSIM available | Prepaid / PAYG eSIM | ID or UK address needed? | How the eSIM is delivered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EE** | Own network | Yes | Yes — prepaid travel and big-data passes | No for prepaid | App or emailed QR code |
| **Vodafone UK** | Own network | Yes | Yes — Pay as you go Plus | No for PAYG, yes for contracts | QR code, plan managed in My Vodafone |
| **O2** | Own network | Yes | Yes — O2 travel plans and Classic PAYG | No for prepaid | QR code or in-app activation |
| **Three UK** | Own network | Yes | Yes — 12 GB, 30 GB, 100 GB, Unlimited | No for prepaid | QR code, online checkout |
| **giffgaff** | O2 | Yes | Yes — goodybags | No | App or emailed QR code |
| **SMARTY** | Three | Yes | Yes — no-contract monthly plans | No | App |
| **VOXI** | Vodafone | Yes | Yes — 30-day rolling plans | No | App |
| **Lebara** | Vodafone | Yes | Yes — SIM-and-plan bundles | No | App or QR code |
| **Lycamobile** | O2 | Yes | Yes — from 5 GBP | No | App |
| **Tesco Mobile** | O2 | Yes, rolling out | Yes for shops, app eSIM is newer | No | App, slower rollout |
| **Virgin Mobile** | Retired into O2 | No — legacy brand | — | — | — |

Two practical notes on that table. First, "prepaid eSIM available" means a visitor can buy it without a contract, not that the checkout will accept your card — UK networks run fraud checks that occasionally decline foreign cards, and that is the single most common reason a direct purchase fails. Second, the delivery column matters more than the price column when you are booking from abroad: an emailed QR code you install at home is a very different experience from a QR code you generate after landing.

## How to Get an eSIM from EE

EE is the network most visitors ask about first, because it has the widest rural coverage in Great Britain. The good news is that EE prepaid eSIMs are available to visitors without a UK address or a credit check, so the process is genuinely open to tourists.

**The ordering route:**

1. Go to EE's prepaid page and pick a data pass — the Travel Data Pass (10 GB for 10 GBP) or the Big Data Pass (50 GB for 25 GBP) are the two that fit visitors. Both are data only and valid for 30 days.
2. Choose eSIM rather than physical SIM at checkout. If you select eSIM and choose delivery by email, you get a QR code rather than a postal SIM.
3. Enter a payment card. This is where foreign cards occasionally fail the fraud check; if it happens, retry with a different card rather than a different browser.
4. Install the profile before you travel — the QR code needs a working internet connection to download, and airport Wi-Fi is not where you want to be doing this. Our [UK eSIM setup guide](/blog/uk-esim-setup-guide/) covers the install step for both iPhone and Android.

**What you get:** data at 10p/MB after your allowance runs out on Classic PAYG, or a simple expiry on the data passes. Calls and texts on EE prepaid sit at around 25p per minute and 10p per text, which is fine for occasional use and expensive for anything longer. If you need a UK number for calls and SMS, plan for that separately — the trade-offs are in our [UK eSIM with a phone number guide](/blog/uk-esim-with-phone-number/).

**The catch:** EE's travel passes are data-only, so they solve connectivity and nothing else. Visitors who need to receive an SMS verification code should treat EE prepaid as half a solution.

## How to Activate a Vodafone UK eSIM and Find Your QR Code

Vodafone UK changed its position on prepaid eSIM, and a lot of older guides have not caught up. Its **Pay as you go Plus** bundles now support eSIM at online checkout with no contract and no credit check, and existing customers can also convert a physical SIM to an eSIM. So the honest answer to "can I get a Vodafone eSIM as a visitor?" is yes — with friction.

**Where the Vodafone eSIM QR code comes from:** order the plan on Vodafone's pay-as-you-go page, and the eSIM is delivered as a QR code during or immediately after checkout. If the QR code does not arrive, it is almost always sitting in the order-confirmation email rather than the marketing email, and Vodafone's own help pages route you to the order reference to re-request it.

**What Vodafone still does not offer:** a tourist eSIM over the counter. You cannot walk into a Vodafone shop on Oxford Street and leave with an eSIM on a prepaid plan in five minutes — eSIM orders go through the website, and the plan is then managed in the My Vodafone app, which you install after you land.

**Postpaid is a different product entirely.** Vodafone's contract plans require UK residency, a credit check and proof of address. If a comparison table tells you Vodafone eSIM is "contract only", it is describing the postpaid product and missing the Pay as you go Plus route.

**The smoother alternative, if you mainly want the coverage:** a travel eSIM that uses Vodafone's towers. Roami's [UK eSIM](/united-kingdom-esim/) includes Vodafone among its partner networks, so you get Vodafone signal where it is strongest without a UK-facing checkout or an app you can only use after landing. This is not a marketing point so much as a description of the friction difference: one route takes two minutes before you fly, the other takes a checkout, a QR code and an app install on arrival.

## O2 UK eSIM: Online, Prepaid and In Store

O2 sells prepaid eSIMs to visitors and pairs them with free EU roaming, which makes it the network of choice for anyone whose trip continues into Ireland or the EU. Coverage is strongest in cities and weakest in rural Scotland and Wales, so read the [UK coverage guide](/blog/uk-esim-coverage-guide/) before you commit to O2 for a road trip.

**How to get an O2 eSIM as a visitor:**

1. Buy an O2 travel plan online (typically 10 GB / 30 GB / 60 GB tiers) or an O2 Classic PAYG top-up.
2. Select eSIM at checkout and confirm the device is eSIM-capable — O2 publishes its own compatibility list, and our [device compatibility table](/compatibility/) covers the common iPhone, Samsung and Pixel models.
3. Receive the QR code by email and install it before departure.
4. After landing, activate and make sure roaming is enabled on the line.

O2 Classic Pay As You Go is the cheapest entry point but the least comfortable: data is charged at roughly 10p/MB, so it is a top-up-and-watch-it option rather than an all-you-can-use plan. The travel plans are the ones built for visitors.

**In-store:** O2 shops can issue an eSIM, and staff will do the profile transfer for you, but you will need photo ID and you may be asked for a UK address. For a tourist on a two-week trip, the online route is faster and cheaper.

## Three UK eSIM: Online Order and QR Code

Three is the cheapest of the four networks for large data allowances — 12 GB for 10 GBP, 30 GB for 15 GBP, 100 GB for 25 GBP and an unlimited plan at around 35 GBP — and it issues eSIMs online without requiring UK documentation. It also has the largest 5G footprint of the four by towns covered, which is part of why its value proposition holds up in cities and collapses in the countryside.

**Ordering a Three UK eSIM:**

1. Choose a prepaid plan on Three's website or in the Three app.
2. Select eSIM as the SIM type; the plan appears in the app, and the QR code is generated for installation.
3. Install before you travel. Three's eSIM is a standard profile install — no store visit, no postal SIM.
4. Top up from the app when your allowance runs out.

**Two things to know before you choose Three.** Its 4G is concentrated in urban areas and its rural reach is the weakest of the four networks, so it is a city-network choice rather than a touring one. And Three charges for EU roaming on new prepaid plans — around 2 GBP per day for the first slice of your allowance — which undoes part of the saving the moment you cross the Channel. If your itinerary includes Europe, that changes the maths, and our [UK and Europe multi-country guide](/blog/uk-esim-multi-country-europe/) runs the numbers properly.

## Lebara, Lyca, giffgaff, SMARTY, VOXI and Tesco: MVNO eSIM Rollout Status

MVNOs buy capacity from the four networks and resell it cheaper, but they roll out eSIM on their own schedules. Here is where each one stands, and what the rollout means for a visitor.

### Lebara UK eSIM rollout

Lebara runs on Vodafone and issues eSIMs to new and existing customers, with plans from 6 GB for 5 GBP up to 50 GB for 20 GBP and EU roaming included in the allowance. The rollout has been gradual — some Lebara resellers and retail bundles still ship a physical SIM by default — so the reliable route is to order through Lebara's own app or website and select eSIM explicitly. Note one historical wrinkle that still appears in search results: older Lebara SIM-only bundles genuinely could not be issued as eSIM, which is why guides written before the rollout tell you Lebara "does not have eSIM". It does now.

### Lycamobile UK eSIM

Lycamobile moved new customers onto O2's network and offers some of the lowest prepaid pricing in the UK, from around 5 GBP for 5 GB. eSIM is available through Lycamobile's app, and monthly plans can be taken without a contract. Coverage is O2's: strong in cities, weak in rural Scotland and Wales.

### giffgaff eSIM

giffgaff issues eSIMs to visitors with no UK address and no credit check — one of the easiest direct routes into a UK network plan. It runs on O2, so city performance is good and rural reach is limited. giffgaff's goodybags include EU roaming within the allowance, which makes it one of the better direct-network options for a UK-plus-Europe trip.

### SMARTY eSIM

SMARTY runs on Three and sells no-contract monthly plans with aggressive data pricing — around 9 GBP for 20 GB, 15 GBP for 100 GB and 20 GBP for unlimited with fair use. eSIM is issued in the app. Because it inherits Three's network, it is a city-and-data choice rather than a rural one.

### VOXI eSIM

VOXI is Vodafone's youth-focused brand and issues eSIMs through its app. Its selling point is zero-rated streaming — YouTube, Netflix and iPlayer do not count against your allowance — on top of Vodafone's network. EU roaming is charged at roughly 2 GBP per day, so it is a UK-only proposition in practice.

### Tesco Mobile eSIM

Tesco Mobile runs on O2 and has been the slowest of the O2-based brands to move. eSIM is available but the rollout has lagged the others, so availability can vary by plan type and by channel. If Tesco is your preferred brand because of O2 coverage plus Clubcard pricing, verify eSIM support for the specific plan before you buy rather than assuming it.

### What the rollout pattern tells you

The pattern across all six is consistent: the smaller and more app-first the brand, the faster the eSIM rollout. Legacy retail distribution is the brake, because a brand that still sells most of its SIMs through shops and bundles has to keep a physical product in the chain.

## What ID or UK Address Do You Need to Buy a UK Network eSIM?

This is the question that decides whether a direct network eSIM is realistic for you, so it is worth being precise.

| Situation | ID needed | UK address needed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying a prepaid eSIM online (EE, Vodafone, O2, Three) | No | No | Payment card is the only gate; foreign cards occasionally fail fraud checks |
| Buying a prepaid eSIM in a network shop | Yes — passport or ID | Sometimes requested | Staff may insist on an address for registration |
| Buying a contract / postpaid plan | Yes | Yes, plus credit check | Not available to visitors |
| Buying an MVNO eSIM in an app | No | No | giffgaff, SMARTY, VOXI, Lebara, Lycamobile |
| Buying a travel eSIM | No | No | No checkout in the destination country at all |

Notice what the table does not say: there is no legal requirement in the UK for a prepaid mobile customer to register identity, which is why online prepaid purchases work without documentation. The friction you read about is commercial and technical — card fraud screening, app-only plan management, and QR delivery — not regulatory.

## How to Top Up a UK Network eSIM

Top-ups are where direct network plans and travel eSIMs diverge most, because a network plan lives inside the network's own app.

| Provider | Top-up method | Card needed | Typical top-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| EE | EE app or My EE online | UK or international card | From 10 GBP |
| Vodafone UK | My Vodafone app | Card on file | Plan renewal or data add-on |
| O2 | My O2 app or online | Card on file | Top-up from 10 GBP, or travel plan renewal |
| Three | Three app | Card on file | Plan renewal or data add-on |
| giffgaff | giffgaff app or website | Card | Goodybag renewal |
| SMARTY / VOXI / Lebara / Lyca | Own app | Card | Monthly renewal |

Two practical warnings. First, if you paid with a card that later fails verification, the top-up will fail too and your data stops mid-trip — set up the card before you travel, not when you are standing in a queue. Second, several UK networks top up in whole-GBP increments only, so you cannot buy exactly the 2 GB you need. If topping up mid-trip is the scenario you want to avoid, choose a plan with headroom or a provider that lets you buy fractional add-ons.

## Can Tourists Buy a UK PAYG eSIM?

Yes. A UK pay-as-you-go eSIM is available to tourists from all four networks and from the major MVNOs, and none of the prepaid routes require UK residency. What differs is how much of the setup happens before you fly versus after you land.

The honest summary for a visitor:

- **EE prepaid:** widest coverage, data-only passes, easiest for a touring itinerary.
- **Vodafone Pay as you go Plus:** strong city coverage, PAYG eSIM at online checkout, plan managed in-app after landing.
- **O2 travel plans:** free EU roaming, best if your trip continues into Europe, weakest rural reach.
- **Three prepaid:** cheapest data, best for cities, EU roaming charged on top.
- **MVNOs:** cheapest overall, app-first, network coverage inherited from the host MNO.

If you are in the UK for under a month and your priority is being online the moment you land, the direct-network route has a real cost in setup time that has nothing to do with money. ## SIM Swap, eSIM Transfer and Keeping Your Number

An eSIM is not permanently welded to a plan, and knowing how a swap works saves a support call on the day you change phones.

**Converting a physical SIM to an eSIM.** All four networks support replacing a physical SIM with an eSIM on an existing prepaid or postpaid line. On most networks this is done from the app or account area, and a fresh QR code or in-app activation is issued. The physical SIM stops working when the eSIM activates, so do the swap before you travel rather than after.

**Moving an eSIM to a new phone.** This is where travellers get caught out. A network eSIM is tied to the line and the device profile, and most networks will reissue a profile rather than let you transfer the QR code itself. If you upgrade your phone mid-trip, expect to contact support and go through identity verification — which for a prepaid visitor can be awkward. Some networks support a self-service transfer on the newest iPhone and Android models, but it is not universal.

**Keeping your number.** A UK network eSIM comes with a UK number, and topping the line up keeps it alive. Prepaid lines in the UK are deactivated after a period of inactivity — commonly after several months without a top-up or a chargeable event — so a number you plan to keep needs the occasional top-up, not just a remaining balance.

**Travel eSIMs work differently.** A data-only travel eSIM has no number to port and no profile to reissue in your name; if you change phones, you install a new profile with a new QR code, and the old one is simply retired. That is more disposable, but it is also more forgiving when something goes wrong mid-trip — the trade-offs are set out in our [UK eSIM dual SIM setup guide](/blog/uk-esim-dual-sim-setup/) for the case where you keep a home line alongside a UK line.

## Official Network eSIM vs Travel eSIM: Which Should You Get?

Both routes put data on the same towers. They differ in where the admin happens, and that is the whole decision.

| Factor | Official network eSIM | Travel eSIM |
|---|---|---|
| Where you buy | Network's own site or app | Provider's site, from anywhere |
| When you can install | After payment; QR by email | Before you fly, immediately |
| UK number and SMS | Yes on most prepaid routes | Usually data-only |
| Contract or credit check | Prepaid: none. Postpaid: both | None |
| Network switching | One network per SIM | Multi-network switching available |
| Top-up | In the network's app | In the provider's app |
| Best for | Long stays, UK number needed, local calls | Trips under a month, arrival-day connectivity |
| Rural touring | Depends on the network you picked | Multi-network plans can switch to whatever is strongest |

A quick way to decide: **if you can name the specific UK network you want and you need a UK phone number, buy direct. If your requirement is "working data on arrival and coverage wherever I end up", buy a travel eSIM.** Neither is a compromise — they solve different problems, and plenty of long-stay visitors run both, with a data eSIM for connectivity and a cheap voice plan for the number. Direct purchases fail in predictable ways. None of them are mysterious once you know the pattern.

## APN, VoLTE and Wi-Fi Calling on a Network eSIM

A network-issued profile normally configures itself, so when a direct eSIM from EE, Vodafone, O2 or Three will not carry data, the cause is usually one of three device settings rather than the profile.

**APN.** The access point name tells the phone which gateway to use for data. If calls work but data does not, open Settings > Mobile Data > Mobile Data Network on iPhone, or Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Access Point Names on Android, and check that the APN field is populated. The published values are `everywhere` for EE (username `eesecure`, password `secure`); `wap.vodafone.co.uk` on Vodafone pay as you go, with `wap` for both credentials, and `internet` on contract lines; `mobile.o2.co.uk` on O2 contract lines and `payandgo.o2.co.uk` on O2 pay as you go (username `o2web`, password `password`); and `three.co.uk` for Three, with both credential fields left blank. If the network has already filled these in, leave them alone — a wrong manual entry breaks a working plan more often than a missing one, and that goes double for a travel eSIM, whose APN belongs to the provider rather than to the UK network.

**VoLTE.** The UK's 3G networks were switched off in 2024, so voice calls now travel over 4G. A handset without VoLTE can show full signal bars and still fail to place a call. iPhone models from the XS onward and most Android flagships from 2019 onward support it; a phone bought on a contract outside the UK is the case worth checking against the network's own device list before you buy.

**Wi-Fi calling.** This routes calls and SMS over Wi-Fi, which is the practical answer to a poor indoor signal and often the only way an SMS verification code reaches you. It is set per line, so enable it on both your home line and the UK line if you are running dual SIM — our [UK eSIM dual SIM setup guide](/blog/uk-esim-dual-sim-setup/) has the menu paths for each platform.

Network-side references worth bookmarking: [EE's eSIM help pages](https://ee.co.uk/help/mobile/getting-started/using-an-esim), [Three's eSIM support pages](https://www.three.co.uk/Support/sim-support/esim), [giffgaff's eSIM collection](https://help.giffgaff.com/en/collections/626993-esim), and [Apple's eSIM support article](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/118669) for the handset side of the install.

## Frequently Asked Questions

If you are checking a specific brand, the table at the top of this page is the short answer; anything else on the market is either a travel eSIM provider or a reseller of one of these networks.

**Does EE UK support eSIM for visitors?**
Yes. EE issues prepaid eSIMs without a UK address or credit check, and its travel and big-data passes are available to visitors. The passes are data-only, so they do not give you a UK number for calls or SMS verification.

**How do I get a Vodafone UK eSIM QR code?**
Order a Pay as you go Plus plan on Vodafone's pay-as-you-go page and select eSIM. The QR code is issued at checkout or in your order confirmation email. If it has not arrived, quote your order reference to Vodafone support and ask for a reissue — QR codes expire, so reissue is routine.

**Does Lebara UK have eSIM?**
Yes — Lebara issues eSIMs on Vodafone's network, with plans from 6 GB for 5 GBP including EU roaming. The rollout has been gradual, so older guides still say Lebara has no eSIM; order through Lebara directly and select eSIM rather than buying a retail bundle that ships a physical SIM.

**Which UK providers support eSIM on pay as you go?**
EE, Vodafone, O2 and Three all do, and so do the major MVNOs. The differences are in delivery (QR code versus app activation), top-up mechanics and whether EU roaming is included — not in whether PAYG eSIM exists.

**Can I buy a UK network eSIM without a UK address?**
For prepaid plans, yes. There is no identity-registration requirement for UK prepaid customers, and online purchases do not ask for an address beyond a billing address for your card. Contract plans are the exception: they require UK residency and a credit check.

**Can I use a US carrier eSIM in the UK?**
Roaming with a home carrier works, but it is a different product from a UK eSIM. Expect domestic-roaming rates rather than local rates, and check your own carrier's international terms — this page is about buying UK network plans, not about bringing a foreign plan to the UK.

**How long does activation take?**
Profile installation takes a couple of minutes with a stable connection. Network registration happens on first attach and is usually instant, but can take a few minutes on arrival. Install before you fly and activate on landing, so that any delay happens while you still have airport Wi-Fi.

**What if I need a UK number as well as data?**
Buy the two things separately: a data eSIM for connectivity and a low-cost UK voice plan for the number. The realistic options, and what each one will and will not do for SMS verification, are set out in our [UK eSIM with a phone number guide](/blog/uk-esim-with-phone-number/).
