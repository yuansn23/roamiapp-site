This guide ranks the **10 best eSIMs for Japan** across every category—unlimited data, budget plans, rural coverage, short trips, and long stays.

Whether you are asking **"what is the best eSIM for Japan"** or **"what esim to use in Japan"** for a two-week vacation, this comprehensive comparison will give you a clear, data-backed answer.

## 3. TOP 5 In-Depth Reviews: Tested in Tokyo, Osaka, and the Shinkansen

### 🥇 #1 Ubigi — The Best Rated eSIM for Japan in All Aspects

**Ubigi** takes the top spot as the **best eSIM for Japan** for most travelers. Why does it outperform the competition? Ubigi is owned by Transatel, whose parent company NTT operates Japan's largest and most reliable network—**NTT Docomo**. This means Ubigi does not rely on third-party roaming aggregators; it rides natively on Docomo's infrastructure, offering the widest rural coverage from Hokkaido to Okinawa.

**Plans and pricing:** Plans start at $3.50 USD for 1GB of data valid for 3 days. The best-seller is the 25GB plan for 30 days at $32 USD. Unlimited data options run $39 USD for 15 days or $66 USD for 30 days. A 10GB/7-day plan is also available for $14 USD. According to Japan Wireless's 2026 comparison, Ubigi's 7-day unlimited plan is priced at $25 USD, while the 30-day unlimited runs $65 USD.

**Performance:** In real-world tests across Tokyo and Osaka, 5G coverage was exemplary with top-tier speeds for all activities—streaming Netflix, playing online games, and video calls all worked without lag. On the Shinkansen, connectivity remained stable with only brief drops in long tunnels. Independent speed tests show Ubigi averaging approximately 12.2 Mbps download in Japan, with latency around 105ms.

**Fair Usage Policy (FUP):** Ubigi's unlimited plans are throttled to approximately 2 Mbps after roughly 30GB of usage within the validity period. This is significantly more generous than competitors' FUP thresholds—most users will never hit this cap during a standard trip.

**Pros:**
- Native NTT Docomo connection—ensuring the **most reliable eSIM Japan** can provide outside major cities
- Unlimited hotspot/tethering with no daily caps—rare among travel eSIMs
- Extremely easy installation via QR code or app
- If you run out of data without WiFi, Ubigi sponsors a connection long enough to purchase a top-up
- Full MVNO with direct carrier partnerships, not a reseller

**Cons:**
- No Japanese phone number included (data-only plan)
- Email and chatbot support only—no phone support

**Best for:** Solo travelers, couples, and anyone venturing outside Tokyo, especially to rural areas, mountains, or smaller islands. As one verified reviewer on Trustpilot noted: *"I had excellent mobile data connectivity everywhere I went, and the price was reasonable."* If you are looking for the **best esim to use in Japan** without worrying about dead zones, Ubigi is your safest bet.

**Plans and pricing:** Airalo Japan plans start at approximately $4.50 USD for 1GB valid for 7 days. The popular 7-day, 10GB Moshi Moshi plan runs approximately $15 USD and routes through SoftBank. For longer stays, 20GB for 30 days is available from around $22 USD—making it one of the most affordable long-term data options. Airalo also offers a 7-day unlimited plan on SoftBank at $27.00 USD. According to eSIM-Now's 2026 price comparison, Airalo's 1GB plan costs $4.00, 3GB costs $8.00, 5GB costs $11.00, and 20GB costs $25.00.

**Performance:** Airalo primarily uses KDDI and SoftBank networks, offering a well-balanced and stable experience. In Travel Arbitrage's 2026 Tokyo speed tests, Airalo on SoftBank delivered an impressive **85 Mbps average download speed** with 28ms latency—faster than Saily's 72 Mbps on Docomo. The connection handled the Shinkansen between Tokyo and Osaka without dropping below 3G—a critical test that Saily failed twice.

**Unlimited plan note:** Airalo's "unlimited" packages have a daily high-speed threshold of 3GB, after which speeds drop to 1Mbps for the rest of the day. This is an important distinction if you are comparing unlimited plans—Airalo's "unlimited" is not truly unlimited at full speed.

**Pros:**
- Massive 200+ destination catalog for multi-country travelers

**Best for:** City-focused trips (Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto), first-time eSIM users, and travelers who prefer app-based management. As one user noted on the App Store: *"Basically, you can't go wrong with Airalo for a week in Tokyo."*

Hotspot sharing is capped at approximately 500MB to 1GB per day depending on the specific plan, after which tethering speeds are heavily reduced. The FUP throttles speeds to roughly 1 Mbps daily ceiling for heavy users.

**Pros:**

## 7. What's New in 2026 for Japan eSIM Users

- **5G Expansion:** Japan's 5G network now covers the vast majority of the population. All recommended eSIMs above support 5G on tier-one networks like Docomo, KDDI, and SoftBank.
- **Pocket WiFi is Becoming Obsolete:** The price-per-GB for portable WiFi has become worse than eSIMs, and you no longer have to carry or return another device.
- **JR Pass Goes Digital:** The official Japan Rail Pass app now requires a working data connection to activate and display your QR ticket—making a reliable eSIM mandatory for rail travelers.
- **Free WiFi is Still Unreliable:** While convenience stores and train stations offer free WiFi, the authentication is fiddly and often requires a Japanese phone number. A good eSIM remains the most practical solution.
- **Roaming vs. Local eSIMs:** The industry has clearly split into "roaming-type" (Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi—higher latency) and "local-type" (CDJapan Rental—lower latency). For 2026, choosing the right type depends on whether you need geo-local services or simply want the cheapest data.
- **New Entrants:** eSIM-Now offers aggressively low pricing at $0.74 per GB for 20GB plans. MobiMatter offers 20GB for $13.99 USD ($0.70 per GB).
- **Device Compatibility Note:** Ubigi is reportedly incompatible with Google Pixel 9 and Pixel 10. Check device compatibility before purchasing.


## 8. Japan eSIM FAQ

**Q: Which esim is best for Japan overall?**
**A:** For the vast majority of travelers, **Ubigi** offers the best overall experience with native NTT Docomo 5G coverage, reasonable prices, and unlimited hotspot tethering.

**Q: What is the best esim for Japan travel on a tight budget?**
**A:** **Airalo** is the best budget-friendly eSIM for Japan, with plans starting around $4.50 USD for 1GB/7 days. **eSIM-Now** offers even cheaper per-GB rates at $3.17 for 1GB.

**Q: What esim to use in Japan for truly unlimited data?**
**A:** **Holafly** offers genuinely unlimited data for 7, 15, or 30 days starting at $27.30 USD, though hotspot tethering is capped at around 1GB per day.

**Q: Which esim for Japan iPhone should I pick?**
**A:** All listed providers work with iPhone XR, XS, and newer models. **Airalo** has the most polished iOS app. **Saily** offers the best security features for iPhone users.

**Q: Is eSIM better than pocket WiFi for Japan?**
**A:** Yes, for most travelers. eSIMs are cheaper, more convenient, and require no extra device to carry, charge, or return at the airport.

**Q: Can I use hotspot/tethering with my Japan eSIM?**
**A:** Yes, but restrictions vary. **Ubigi** allows unlimited tethering. **Holafly** caps hotspot at roughly 500MB–1GB/day. **Airalo**'s policy varies by plan—always check before buying.

**Q: Do I need a Japanese phone number for my trip?**
**A:** Usually not. Most restaurants use reservation apps like Tabelog, which work with email. For emergencies or car rentals, use WhatsApp, LINE, or Skype. If you absolutely need a number, choose **Mobal** or **CDJapan Rental**.

**Q: Which network is best for rural Japan—Docomo, SoftBank, or au?**
**A:** **NTT Docomo** has the widest coverage in rural areas, followed by au (KDDI). SoftBank is best in cities.

**Q: Is Ubigi compatible with Google Pixel 9?**
**A:** Reports indicate Ubigi is **incompatible with Pixel 9 and Pixel 10**. Check with your provider before purchasing if you use these devices.

**Q: How much data do I need for a week in Japan?**
**A:** Light users (maps + messaging): 1–2GB. Moderate users (+social media): 3–5GB. Heavy users (+streaming): 7–10GB+.
