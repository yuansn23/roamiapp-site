## Video Call Reliability: The Real Test of a Business eSIM

Let's talk about the thing that makes or breaks an **eSIM for UK digital nomad remote work**: video calls. Whether you're on Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, or FaceTime, video calling is the most demanding thing you'll do with your mobile data. And it's the place where cheap eSIMs fall apart.

### What Makes a Good Video Call Experience

For a smooth video call, you need three things:
- **Low latency**: Under 100ms round-trip time, ideally under 50ms
- **Consistent throughput**: At least 2-3 Mbps upload and download
- **Low jitter**: Minimal variation in packet arrival timing

Budget eSIMs that route your traffic through a central server (often in Hong Kong, Singapore, or the Netherlands) add significant latency. You might see 200-300ms round-trip times, which makes video calls feel laggy and unnatural. You end up talking over each other, there's a delay in facial expressions, and the whole experience becomes exhausting.

Roami's direct connection to UK networks means your traffic stays local. Round-trip times are typically **15-30ms** on a good 5G connection, and rarely above **60ms** even on 4G. That's indistinguishable from a wired broadband connection for video calling purposes.

## UK Network MVNOs vs eSIM: What's the Difference?

You might be wondering whether you should buy an eSIM from a travel specialist like Roami or go directly to a UK mobile operator. The UK has a thriving MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator) scene, with providers like giffgaff, Lebara, SMARTY, Talkmobile, and Voxi offering competitive prepaid plans.

But there's a critical distinction: most of these MVNOs are **physical SIM** providers, not eSIM providers. Some have started offering eSIM support, but it's often limited to specific plans or requires you to be a UK resident.

### When an MVNO Makes Sense

If you're staying in the UK for **more than six months** and you have a UK address, getting a local MVNO plan might work out cheaper. SMARTY, for example, offers unlimited data for around 20 pounds per month on a rolling contract. But you need to pass a credit check or pay upfront, and you generally need a UK billing address.

Students on year-long courses may find this a good option. But for stays under six months, a travel eSIM plan is almost always simpler and more flexible.

Travel eSIMs win on convenience. You can buy one before you leave home, install it while you're still in the airport lounge, and it activates automatically when you land. No credit checks, no UK address required, no waiting for a SIM card to arrive in the post.
