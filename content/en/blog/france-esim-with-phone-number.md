Most "France eSIM" products sold to travellers are data-only. They give you a fast connection on a French network, and that is where the story ends: no French phone number, no voice minutes, no ability to receive a verification text. That is fine for Google Maps, WhatsApp, and Instagram — and completely wrong if a hotel, train operator, or bank insists on texting a number you actually own.

This guide covers the other product class: a **France eSIM with a phone number**. It explains which plans include calls and SMS, what a French +33 number costs, how dual SIM keeps your home number alive while a French line handles bookings, and the cases where a travel eSIM without a number is genuinely the better buy.

> **Key takeaways:**
> - Most France eSIMs for tourists are **data-only** — Airalo, Ubigi, Holafly, Saily, and Nomad France plans do not include a phone number.
> - **Orange Travel** is the carrier-branded eSIM option that adds a genuine French +33 number with unlimited calls and SMS inside France. Number-included tiers start at 19.99 euro for 20 GB over 14 days.
> - You do not need the French number as your main line. Dual SIM lets your home SIM keep handling calls while the eSIM carries data, and you switch the voice line only when a booking needs it.
> - Voice on a French line now depends on **VoLTE**, because French operators are retiring their 2G and 3G networks between 2026 and 2029.
> - If you only need maps, messaging, and email, a data-only France eSIM is cheaper and simpler. Buy the number only when a specific task demands it.

## France eSIM with a Phone Number: What You Actually Get

An eSIM is a digital SIM profile installed on your phone rather than a plastic card. On its own, that tells you nothing about whether you get a phone number — the number comes from the *type of plan*, not from the eSIM technology.

There are two families of France eSIM, and they behave very differently:

| Plan type | Data | Voice calls | SMS | French number | Typical sellers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travel eSIM (data-only) | Yes | No | No | No | Airalo, Ubigi, Holafly, Saily, Nomad |
| Carrier eSIM (voice + data) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes (+33) | Orange Travel |

A data-only travel eSIM routes everything through apps. Calls happen over WhatsApp, FaceTime, or Google Meet. Texts happen over iMessage, RCS, or WhatsApp. None of that requires a phone number in the telecom sense, which is exactly why these plans are cheap and instant to deliver.

A carrier eSIM is a mobile subscription with a real number attached. Orange Travel sells one to visitors without a French address, contract, or bank account. The number is a genuine French mobile number, not a virtual number that some services will refuse.

The distinction matters most at the exact moment something needs to *reach you*: a two-factor code, a booking confirmation, a delivery driver, or a restaurant holding your table.

## Voice, SMS, and Data: Which France eSIM Plans Include Calling

### Data-only France eSIM plans

The mainstream travel eSIMs sold for France are all data-only. That is not a limitation of eSIM — it is a product decision. Selling voice minutes to a short-stay visitor requires carrier agreements, identity handling, and regulatory paperwork that most travel eSIM brands avoid. Their apps support data only, and their customer support will rightly tell you there is no number to give you.

If your plan is "stay online cheaply", this is the right category, and the [France eSIM provider ranking](/blog/france-esim-provider-ranking/) compares the options on price, network, and speed.

### Carrier eSIM plans with voice and SMS

Carrier eSIMs come from the network operator rather than a reseller. In France the practical option for visitors is the Orange Travel range, which is sold directly by Orange France and pairs data with unlimited calls and SMS inside France.

Note the wording: unlimited calls and SMS *inside France*. Calling a US or UK number from France is a different rate, and international calling is not the point of these plans. Read the tier description rather than assuming worldwide minutes.

### Why unlimited calls matter less than the number itself

For most travellers the value is not the minutes — it is the inbound capability. A plan with 200 minutes that can receive SMS is more useful than a plan with unlimited minutes that cannot, because verification codes and booking confirmations arrive by text.

That is why "does it include a French phone number" is the question to ask first, and "how many minutes" second.

### Before you buy: VoLTE and device checks

Voice on a French line is no longer a case of falling back to an old network. French operators began switching off 2G in March 2026 and have published 3G retirement dates running to 2029, which means calls now depend on VoLTE (voice over LTE). If your handset does not support VoLTE on the French network, data will work while voice may not. The regulator publishes the operator-by-operator timetable on the [ARCEP 2G and 3G sunset notice](https://en.arcep.fr/news/press-releases/view/n/2g-and-3g-mobile-network-sunsets-100626.html).

Two further checks:

- **Device must be eSIM-capable and carrier-unlocked.** iPhones from XS onward, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later, and Google Pixel 3a and later support eSIM. See [Apple's eSIM support documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118669) for the iPhone list, or run our [device compatibility check](/compatibility/).
- **Phones bought in mainland China or Hong Kong often have eSIM disabled in hardware**, even on models sold elsewhere with eSIM support. Verify before you buy a plan.

## France eSIM Calls and SMS Through Dual SIM

The reason a France eSIM with a phone number is practical rather than disruptive is dual SIM. Your phone can hold your home SIM and the French eSIM at the same time, and you decide which one does what.

### Using your home number for calls

This is the default for most travellers. The French eSIM is set as the line for mobile data; the home SIM stays active for calls and SMS. Your WhatsApp, iMessage, and banking apps keep working exactly as they do at home, because your home number never goes away. Nothing needs to be re-registered and no contact has to learn a new number.

The trade-off is that a French service calling your home number pays international rates, and some French booking systems will not accept a foreign number format at all.

### Using the French number as your voice line

The alternative is to make the French eSIM the line for calls and SMS during the trip. Then a restaurant calling to confirm a reservation reaches you on a local number, and SMS verification works anywhere it is needed.

The cost is that your home number stops receiving calls while it is switched out of the voice role — although incoming SMS to the home line usually still works if you keep that SIM active for messaging. Check your phone's dual-SIM settings before you decide, because the exact switch labels differ between iOS and Android.

### Which line handles data

Whichever voice arrangement you choose, point mobile data at the eSIM. Leaving data on the home SIM is the single most common way travellers accidentally trigger roaming charges, and it defeats the purpose of buying a local plan. Roami's multi-network [France eSIM plan](/france-esim/) is the data-only alternative for travellers who want automatic switching between French carriers without taking on a French number.

### What changes when your phone only takes one eSIM at a time

Some phones allow only one eSIM active alongside a physical SIM, and a few allow two eSIMs. If your model supports only one eSIM, you cannot run a French number eSIM and a second travel eSIM simultaneously — you would need to keep the home SIM in the physical slot and choose one eSIM as the active profile. Confirm your device's limits before buying two eSIM products for the same trip.

## Do You Need a French Number for SNCF, Navigo, and Banking SMS?

This is the section that decides whether you need to spend the extra money. The honest answer is that it depends entirely on which of these tasks is on your list.

### Transport bookings

Train and metro accounts increasingly ask for a mobile number somewhere in the sign-up or verification flow. A French number removes the ambiguity of a foreign number format. A data-only eSIM plus a booking through a ticketing website or app, or a purchase at a station machine, remains a perfectly workable route for most travellers — the number requirement usually appears in account creation and app-based flows rather than in every purchase.

### Restaurant and delivery reservations

Reservation platforms commonly send a confirmation text, and a delivery driver will call the number on the order. If you are booking tables in Paris during high season or ordering food to an apartment, a number that a French service can dial without an international prefix removes a real source of friction.

### Banking and two-factor codes

Two-factor codes are the strongest argument for a local number. If a bank, wallet, or marketplace has a French number on file, you need that number reachable — not a data-only eSIM that cannot receive SMS. Travellers who open a French bank account or a French mobile-money wallet during a long stay should treat the number as a requirement rather than a convenience.

If the account on file uses your home number, keep your home SIM active for SMS instead. That is cheaper than buying a French number purely for codes you could receive anyway.

### When a data-only eSIM is enough

Skip the number if your trip is navigation, messaging, social media, email, and streaming. Add it if at least one of these is true:

- A service you rely on has a French number on file, or requires one at sign-up.
- You expect calls from hotels, drivers, or tour operators.
- You are staying long enough to open local accounts.
- You want a number you can reuse on future trips by topping up.

Otherwise a data-only plan leaves real money on the table by comparison, and the cost gap widens the longer your trip runs.

## How to Get a France eSIM Number: Step by Step

1. **Confirm your phone is eSIM-capable and unlocked.** Install nothing until this is verified. A carrier-locked handset will reject the profile.
2. **Choose a number-included tier, not a data-only tier.** On Orange Travel, that means the all-inclusive plans rather than the cheaper data-only line. The price difference is the cost of the number.
3. **Check the validity window against your trip.** The 14-day and 30-day tiers cover typical holidays; longer stays should look at the 90-day tier.
4. **Buy before you fly if you want to test the setup on Wi-Fi.** Installing an eSIM needs a data connection. Doing it at home removes the dependency on airport Wi-Fi.
5. **Install the eSIM profile and label it.** Most phones let you name each line. Name this one "France" so the dual-SIM settings stay readable.
6. **Set the French eSIM for mobile data, and decide the voice line.** Home SIM for calls is the low-effort default; French eSIM for calls if you need to be reachable locally.
7. **Turn on data roaming on the eSIM line.** A local plan still requires the roaming toggle on most handsets. This is normal and is covered in our [France eSIM setup guide](/blog/france-esim-setup-dual-sim-number/).
8. **Test inbound SMS early.** Send yourself a message from another phone, or trigger a code from a low-stakes service, before you depend on it for a booking.

## France eSIM with a Local Number: Real Numbers vs VoIP

A "French number" can mean two very different things, and the difference decides whether verification codes arrive.

**A real French mobile number** is issued by a French operator and lives on the mobile network. SMS arrives as a normal network text. This is what Orange Travel's number-included tiers provide, and it is the only variant that behaves like a phone a French business expects to call.

**A VoIP or app-based number** is a number hosted by an internet service that forwards calls and texts over data. It costs less and takes minutes to set up, but a growing share of banks, ticketing platforms, and marketplaces screen out VoIP ranges as a fraud measure. When that happens, the code never arrives and there is nothing to troubleshoot.

### Why some verification codes reject VoIP numbers

Services that carry a fraud risk — banking, ticket resale, peer-to-peer marketplaces, and some delivery platforms — increasingly check whether a number belongs to a mobile range before sending a one-time code. A VoIP number fails that check silently. The practical rule: if the number exists to receive verification codes, use a real mobile number from a French operator, not a forwarding service.

For travellers who only need to place the occasional outbound call, a VoIP number can be adequate. It is the inbound-message path that breaks down.

## When a French SIM Card Beats a France eSIM with a Number

An eSIM with a number is not the only route to a French phone number. A prepaid physical SIM bought in France also gives you one, and for some trips it is the better instrument. The full trade-off is laid out in our [France eSIM versus physical SIM and roaming comparison](/blog/france-esim-vs-physical-sim-roaming/); the short version follows.

**Choose a physical prepaid SIM when:**

- You are staying long enough that a monthly local tariff beats a fixed travel plan.
- You need a number you can keep for months rather than days.
- You want the number tied to a physical card you can move between devices.

**Stay with the eSIM when:**

- You want the number working before you land, without visiting a shop.
- Your trip is short and the convenience outweighs the tariff difference.
- You want to keep your home SIM in the phone at the same time.

Two practical frictions apply to the physical route. Buying a French prepaid SIM in a store involves identity registration and, in busy periods, a queue; airport and city-centre outlets are the slowest. And a store-bought SIM means your home SIM must give up the physical slot unless your phone supports dual eSIM.

## France eSIM with Phone Number: Common Pitfalls

- **Assuming any France eSIM includes a number.** The mainstream travel eSIMs are data-only. Check the plan description, not the brand.
- **Buying the data-only tier by mistake.** Orange Travel's cheaper plans look identical in name and carry no number. The all-inclusive tier is the one with calls and SMS.
- **Ignoring VoLTE.** With 2G already switching off in France and 3G to follow, an older handset may hold data but drop voice.
- **Leaving mobile data on the home SIM.** This produces roaming charges and wastes the local plan.
- **Expecting the number to survive the plan.** The number is tied to an active plan; if it lapses, the number stops working. Top up before expiry if you want to keep it for a future trip.
- **Buying a handset with eSIM disabled.** Phones made for mainland China or Hong Kong commonly ship without eSIM support even on models that support it elsewhere.
- **Treating a virtual number as equivalent.** VoIP ranges are widely rejected for verification codes.
- **Waiting until you need the code to test inbound SMS.** Test the first day, not at the hotel check-in counter.

## France eSIM Number FAQ

### Does a France eSIM include a phone number?

Usually not. The France eSIMs sold by travel eSIM brands are data-only and come with no number, no voice, and no SMS. A carrier eSIM such as Orange Travel's all-inclusive tier does include a genuine French mobile number.

### Can I receive SMS on a France eSIM?

Yes — if the plan includes a number. Number-included tiers can receive normal network SMS, which is what makes them useful for verification codes. Data-only plans cannot receive SMS at all.

### Can I make calls with a France eSIM?

Yes, on a number-included tier, with unlimited calls inside France on the all-inclusive plans. Your phone must support VoLTE, because French operators are retiring the 2G and 3G networks that older handsets relied on for voice.

### How much does a France eSIM with a number cost?

Number-included tiers start at 19.99 euro for 20 GB over 14 days, rising with data and validity. Compare that with the data-only tiers on the same provider, which cost less but carry no number and no voice.

### Do I need a French address to get a French eSIM number?

No. The visitor-oriented carrier eSIM is prepaid and does not require a French address, contract, or bank account, which is precisely what separates it from a standard French mobile subscription.

### Will my French number still work after the plan expires?

No. The number is attached to an active plan. Once the plan lapses the number stops receiving calls and SMS. Prepaid visitor plans are rechargeable, so you can top up the same profile on a later trip instead of buying a new one.

### Can I keep my regular number at the same time?

Yes, and most travellers should. Dual SIM lets your home SIM stay in the phone for calls and messages while the French eSIM carries data, or you can switch the French eSIM into the voice role for the duration of the trip.
