        Will WhatsApp calling work in Egypt? I heard it's blocked.
      a: |
        <strong>Yes, WhatsApp calls are blocked on Egyptian local networks</strong> under Telecom Law Article 72. This means if you buy a Vodafone/Orange/Etisalat SIM, you <strong>cannot make WhatsApp or Facebook voice/video calls</strong>. Roami's eSIM routes data through international servers, <strong>bypassing this block</strong> — you can call family and friends on WhatsApp, FaceTime, and Skype just like at home.

In Luxor, Karnak Temple, and the Valley of the Kings, 4G coverage is generally good. The Egyptian Museum in Cairo has strong signal throughout. Roami auto-connects to the best carrier at each location.
    - q: |
        Do I need to show my passport or hotel address for an Egypt eSIM?

        What is the 30% top-up tax and why should I care?

        Vodafone Egypt charges a <strong>30% tax on all SIM recharges</strong>. Example: if you top up EGP 100, you only get EGP 70 of actual credit — EGP 30 goes to tax. This adds up quickly for longer stays. Roami has <strong>no hidden taxes or fees</strong> — the price you see is the price you pay. No 30% deduction, no surprises.
    - q: |
        Which carrier is best in Egypt — Orange, Vodafone, or Etisalat?
      a: |
        All three have good coverage in Cairo and major tourist cities. <strong>Orange</strong> has the most consistent 4G and is rolling out 5G in Cairo. <strong>Vodafone</strong> has strong city coverage but charges the 30% top-up tax. <strong>Etisalat</strong> is competitive on price locally but has less rural coverage. With Roami, you don't choose — your eSIM <strong>auto-switches</strong> to whichever carrier has the strongest signal at your location.
    - q: |
        Egypt has 30-day billing cycles for SIMs — does that matter for me?
      a: |
        Yes — it's a major waste of money. Vodafone, Orange, and Etisalat all use <strong>30-day billing cycles</strong>. A 7-day tourist buying Orange's EGP 200/5GB plan wastes over <strong>75% of the plan's value</strong>. Roami offers 3, 7, 15, and 30-day plans — you <strong>pay only for your actual trip length</strong>. No 30-day commitment, no wasted data.
    - q: |
        Can I use Google Maps for sightseeing in Cairo and Luxor?
      a: |
        Yes — Google Maps works well in all major cities. The Pyramids area, central Cairo, Luxor, and Aswan all have 4G coverage. For the Giza plateau, we recommend downloading <strong>offline maps of the area</strong> as a backup, as signal can be weaker near the desert edge. Google Maps also works offline once the map area is downloaded — no data needed for basic navigation.
    - q: |
        Is hotel WiFi in Egypt reliable? Do I really need an eSIM?
      a: |
        Hotel WiFi in Egypt can be <strong>unpredictable</strong>. Even in 4-star hotels in Cairo and Luxor, speeds slow down in the evenings when everyone is streaming, and connections can drop. Many hotels also block VoIP (WhatsApp calls) on their networks. An eSIM gives you <strong>reliable connectivity everywhere</strong> — at the Pyramids, in the museum, at a café, or on a Nile felucca ride. For the price of a coffee, you get data wherever you go.
