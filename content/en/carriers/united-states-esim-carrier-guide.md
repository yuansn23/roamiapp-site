## What your eSIM demands from your device

Three things decide whether your phone works on a US carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

### Will an iPhone connect in United States?

US carriers build their 5G on different bands, and the differences decide whether you see fast 5G or fall back to LTE. **T-Mobile** leans on **n71 (600 MHz)** for rural reach and **n41 (2.5 GHz)** for mid-band speed. **Verizon** uses **n77 (C-band)** for mid-band and **n260/n261 (mmWave)** in dense downtowns and stadiums. **AT&T** uses **n5 (850 MHz)** low-band and **n77** mid-band. Plenty of international handsets ship without n77, which is the band AT&T and Verizon depend on for mid-band speed.

You can skip the band tables entirely. The dependable check is to compare the exact model number against the [eSIM device checker](/compatibility/). For the mechanics ahead of the purchase, see [what happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/).

### Will United States carriers work with your phone?
A locked phone is the most common reason a US eSIM install simply fails, and the rules here are cleaner than many visitors expect.

Under the **CTIA Consumer Code**, US carriers must unlock devices, but the timing differs by plan type. Postpaid phones unlock once the device financing is paid off and the account is in good standing. Prepaid phones unlock no later than **one year** after activation. Carriers unlock "upon request" rather than automatically in most cases. The one exception travelers used to rely on — **Verizon's 60-day automatic unlock** — was waived by the FCC in January 2026, so a Verizon phone activated after that ruling may stay locked to Verizon's network for up to a year like other prepaid devices. That rollback matters if you buy a phone in the US and expect to roam its eSIM elsewhere quickly.

**Where:** iPhone, Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock. When the field reads "No SIM restrictions", the device is free and clear. A "SIM locked" reading stops any third-party profile from installing until the lock is cleared.

**Fix it:** call the carrier that sold you the phone and ask for the unlock code. Paid-off postpaid devices are normally cleared within a couple of business days, and there is no fee. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone from a US carrier on a payment plan, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. The FCC publishes a plain-language [guide to unlocking your mobile phone](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/unlocking-your-mobile-phone) that explains the commitments carriers made under the CTIA code.

## Tested APN values

Only a handful of values matter here, and you will need them only if automatic configuration fails. Below: the values for the three national carriers, when manual entry is genuinely required, and the exact menu paths.

### APN values for T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T eSIMs

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM **directly from a US carrier or its prepaid brand**. Travel eSIM profiles bring their own US APN and should be left as they are.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| T-Mobile | `fast.t-mobile.com` | blank | blank |
| AT&T | `phone` | blank | blank |
| Verizon | `vzwinternet` | blank | blank |

Budget brands inherit their parent's network values: Visible uses Verizon's `vzwinternet`, Cricket uses `ndo`, Metro by T-Mobile uses `fast.metropcs.com`, and Mint Mobile uses `wholesale`. Leave username and password empty unless the activation instructions that came with your profile state otherwise.

### APN configuration in United States

T-Mobile and the MVNOs usually provision the APN for you, so a manual entry is a fallback rather than a step in the setup. It is worth doing when:

- A device too dated to pick up carrier settings automatically
- A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
- A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
- Almost never on a travel eSIM — that is the point of a managed profile

### When a United States APN must be entered manually
- **iPhone:** Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → enter the APN
- **Android:** Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → add a new APN

After saving, restart the phone. If data is still dead, confirm the active data line is the US profile rather than your home SIM.

### Three fixes when your United States eSIM does not work

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our [eSIM troubleshooting resource](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The patterns below are the US-specific ones.

**A. The eSIM will not install**
1. Make sure no lock is present: About -> Carrier Lock shows "No SIM restrictions"
2. Look at the QR: legible, and never scanned before
3. For T-Mobile's tourist eSIM, confirm you are physically in the US, since activation is location-checked

**B. Installed, but no signal bars**
1. Re-check the lock status
2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → select T-Mobile, Verizon or AT&T manually instead of automatic
3. Full network settings reset, then another reboot

**C. Signal bars, but no internet**
1. Put the APN back in precisely as the table lists it (common on AT&T and Verizon prepaid)
2. Verify the US eSIM is the active data line and not your home SIM.
3. Check you have not exhausted your data allowance — budget brands deprioritize rather than cutting off

- **Ookla Speedtest Connectivity Report — United States 2H 2025** — [the per-carrier report](https://www.ookla.com/research/reports/united-states-speedtest-connectivity-report-h2-2025) carries each carrier-level figure used here: T-Mobile's 81.52 Connectivity Score and 91.2% 5G availability, Verizon's 30.0% Coverage Score and AT&T's 74.5 Connectivity Score. The same report names T-Mobile Best Mobile Network and Best 5G Network, and Verizon Best Mobile Coverage.
- **CTIA Consumer Code** — [the wireless service code](https://www.ctia.org/consumer-resources/bring-your-own-device/consumer-code-for-wireless-service) lays out the device-unlocking commitments cited above, prepaid and postpaid timing included.
- **FCC** — [unlocking your mobile phone](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/unlocking-your-mobile-phone) covers the consumer side of the unlock rules, and the January 2026 Verizon 60-day waiver is recorded in the FCC's order record.
- **Carrier pages** — [T-Mobile Prepaid eSIM](https://prepaid.t-mobile.com/esim-app), [Cricket Wireless](https://www.cricketwireless.com/) and [Visible](https://www.visible.com/) document tourist eSIM availability, plan prices, delivery methods and identification requirements.
- **Ookla Speedtest Global Index** — [the United States entry](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/united-states) publishes the national median mobile and fixed download speeds each month.

Third-party measurements only. Results vary by phone, by band and by how congested the local tower is.
