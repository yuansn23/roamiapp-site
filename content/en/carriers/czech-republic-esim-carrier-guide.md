## Your eSIM coverage: Prague is easy, the map decides the rest

### Prague coverage: what the Czech capital actually delivers

Every network is excellent in the capital. Ookla's measurement work credits O2 with the fastest 5G medians specifically in Prague, the metro runs signal end to end, and hotel-and-café congestion is the only real enemy — the Old Town square kilometre at midday tests every network's capacity, not its coverage. One Prague-specific caveat: the metro's deepest central sections and the stone cellars under the Old Town are the two places where bars drop on every network, and neither is a coverage problem you can configure your way out of. Surface streets recover within metres.

The airport is similarly frictionless: Vodafone staffs shops at Václav Havel Airport's terminals, and travellers report tourist SIMs around **599 CZK for 10 GB** — convenient, though roughly twice the per-gigabyte cost of the city-street starters.

Brno, Ostrava, Plzeň and Olomouc all sit inside dense 4G and active 5G footprints on all three networks, and the motorway and rail corridors connecting them to Prague stay usable at speed. ## Czech Republic eSIM compatibility: 5G bands and phone locks

### Czech eSIM bands, carrier locks and the two checks that matter

The three networks share a common European band foundation — **LTE bands 1, 3, 7, 8 and 20**, with band 20 (800 MHz) providing rural reach — and build 5G primarily on **n78 at 3500 MHz**. O2 additionally operates a 3700 MHz TDD block, and Vodafone deploys 5G on n3 (1800 MHz) as well, which extends mid-band 5G into more places. Practically every European and international flagship from the last several years handles all of it, and unlike some markets there is no exotic band a visitor is likely to miss.

The usual two checks still apply. Confirm the phone is not carrier-locked — Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock should read "No SIM restrictions" — and confirm eSIM support via the EID shown when you dial `*#06#` or directly through the [eSIM compatibility tool](/compatibility/). Mainland-China iPhones have eSIM disabled in hardware and will not install any profile. A locked phone is the single most common reason a Czech Republic eSIM fails to install, so settle it before purchase.
