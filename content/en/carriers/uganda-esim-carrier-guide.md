The honest line: **inside any national park, assume no signal** and download offline maps before you leave the last town. Read the table as a two-tier map: everything from Kampala to the park *gates* is a solvable coverage problem, and MTN solves most of it. The parks themselves — forest interiors, delta plains, the Karamoja dry season backroads — are a different problem that no carrier solves. Plan connectivity for the drives and the lodges; plan offline for the treks.

**Buying an MTN eSIM as a visitor:**

If eSIM specifically matters to you, register at Entebbe or a service centre, not a roadside kiosk in Kabale.

## SIM Registration Rules



| Step | What happens |

|:---|:---|

| 1 | Go to an authorized MTN/Airtel outlet or the **Entebbe International Airport** counter |

| 2 | Present **passport**; some retailers also ask for a **passport photo** and, occasionally, a **local address** |

| 3 | Fill the registration form; the SIM is activated immediately or within a few hours |

| 4 | For eSIM, scan the QR issued after registration; configure if data does not auto-connect |



Entebbe airport has both MTN and Airtel outlets, so arrival-day registration is straightforward. Keep the confirmation SMS or receipt — UCC can disconnect unregistered lines.



The whole process runs 10–20 minutes at the airport counters, faster at town service centres outside peak hours. Staff at the Entebbe booths handle international arrivals daily and will walk you through the bundle choice — decide roughly how many GB a week you need before you reach the counter, and don't let anyone register the line in a name other than the passport you hand over.



### Uganda SIM registration at Entebbe: what UCC counters want



Uganda's registration is typed by hand while you wait, so the queue is longest exactly when the widebody arrivals bank up — arrive with these ready and the whole stop takes ten minutes.



- **Unlocked, eSIM-capable phone** — confirm via `*#06#` (an EID should appear) and the [compatibility checker](/compatibility/)

- **Original passport** — the registration is done against it, on the spot

- **A passport photo** — some agents require one; carrying two spares costs nothing

- **A "local address"** — your first hotel's name covers it

- **Cash in Ugandan shillings** — SIM plus bundle rarely exceeds UGX 30,000 (~USD 8); small notes avoid the change problem

- **The profile installed before departure** — travel eSIMs install on home Wi-Fi; Entebbe arrivals is not where you want to debug a download

- **A note of which outlet registered the line** — the Airtel shop behind the arrivals-hall information desk and the MTN booth in the baggage-claim area are not interchangeable if a bundle dispute needs the agent who sold it



### Uganda registration rules: how tight?



1. **Accepting an unregistered "cheap" SIM from a street agent.** Informal agents sometimes skip the UCC registration to move faster; the line works for a day or two and then disconnects when the audit catches it. Use official counters and watch them scan your passport.

2. **Discarding the registration receipt or confirmation SMS.** Spot re-registration drives happen; the receipt is what proves your line is legitimately yours.

3. **Registering in your guide's or driver's name.** It seems easier until a bundle dispute or a QR reissue requires the account holder — which is not you. Register under your own passport.



## Why an Uganda eSIM number matters: mobile money (MoMo & Airtel Money)



Uganda runs on mobile money. **MTN Mobile Money (MoMo)** and **Airtel Money** are the dominant way Ugandans pay for food, transport, lodging and even park fees — far more than cash or cards in many places. Two implications for a visitor:



- **A data-only travel eSIM has no local number**, so you cannot open a MoMo or Airtel Money wallet yourself (registration needs a registered local line plus national ID). That is fine for most tourists who pay by card or cash.

- **If you are staying longer, doing business, or want to receive local OTPs and bookings**, a registered MTN or Airtel line (with an Ugandan number) is genuinely useful — tour operators, hotels and ride apps communicate over WhatsApp and local SMS. A travel eSIM keeps your home number for banking OTPs; a local SIM gives you an Ugandan one.



For a short safari, data is enough. For anything past two weeks, the local-number convenience is real — gorilla-permit agents, boda drivers and lodge managers all coordinate over WhatsApp on Ugandan numbers, and having one to receive calls on smooths the logistics.



### Getting a Uganda eSIM installed



The pattern that works: your home SIM stays physical and active for banking OTPs, a registered MTN line (UGX number) handles local calls, bookings and MoMo-adjacent logistics, and a data-only travel eSIM carries the internet. On a dual-eSIM phone all three coexist; on a single-eSIM phone, run MTN physical plus your travel eSIM. Whichever combination you pick, point **Mobile Data** at exactly one line and switch off data roaming on the others — accidental home-SIM roaming is the most common bill shock in East Africa.

## Uganda network speeds and what you pay per GB



- **Fixed broadband:** Ookla's [August 2026 Speedtest Global Index for Uganda](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/uganda) ranks the country ~130th globally, median **33.98 Mbps** download, 7 ms latency. This is the backhaul behind local mobile sites.

- **Mobile:** Uganda's public Global Index does not publish a separate mobile median; 4G LTE is the workhorse in cities, and 5G is **not widely available** to visitors (rollout is early and city-limited). Plan for solid 4G in Kampala/Entebbe, not 5G.

- **Real-world expectations:** video calls work in Kampala and Entebbe on both networks, WhatsApp messaging works in most towns, and uploads from a lodge near Bwindi happen at whatever speed the moment allows — often single-digit Mbps on a good day.

- **Price:** [Cable.co.uk's worldwide data pricing table](https://www.cable.co.uk/mobiles/worldwide-data-pricing/) prices 1 GB of Uganda mobile data at roughly **USD 1.11**, 103rd of 237 markets (global average USD 2.59).

- **Market:** [DataReportal's Digital 2025: Uganda report](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-uganda) reports 14.2 million internet users (28.0% penetration) and 38.6 million mobile connections at the start of 2025.

- **What this means practically:** Uganda's networks are affordable and good in towns, thin in parks, and slow to a crawl on the longest drives. Budget your data for the connected stretches and go offline deliberately for the rest.

## Uganda eSIM faults, and the ones that only happen here



The ordinary eSIM failures — a QR that comes back "already used", a profile that freezes while downloading, a line that installs but never registers — need no repeating here. They live in the [eSIM troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/), and the standard install order is in the [activation how-to guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). What follows are the faults specific to Ugandan networks, all four of them registration, APN, border or crowding problems.



### Is registration waived for visitors in Uganda?



Uganda ties every SIM to a registered identity, and an MTN or Airtel eSIM will not provision until the agent's UCC entry has posted. If the QR scanned at Entebbe but the line stays dead, the registration is the suspect, not the phone — ask the outlet to confirm it went through, and keep the confirmation SMS. A Roami travel eSIM skips UCC registration altogether, so this fault does not reach it.



### APN values for MTN and Airtel eSIMs



Both networks nominally use `internet`, but a locally issued profile that pushes `yellopix.mtn.co.ug` instead can leave an MTN line with bars and no data after a manual install. Re-enter `internet` with blank credentials, reboot, and confirm mobile data is pointed at the eSIM rather than the home SIM. Airtel rarely needs this; MTN occasionally does.



### Uganda coverage gaps: bars drop outside the last town



Inside Bwindi, Kidepo, the Murchison delta and the Ishasha sector the phone loses service on cue, because there is no tower in the forest. That is the arena, not the SIM — check the coverage table before blaming the eSIM, brief your guide, and carry everything offline. Lodge-level signal near the gate is the realistic best case.



### Abroad on a Uganda eSIM



Two crowding-shaped symptoms share a cause. In the parks, a lodge's satellite or microwave backhaul is shared across every guest, so throughput collapses at dinner even at full bars. At the Kenyan or Rwandan line the same pack stops being domestic — EAC "One Network Area" rates cut the shock but do not remove it — so buy a [Kenya eSIM](/kenya-esim/) or a [regional Africa eSIM](/africa-esim/) for the next leg rather than leaning on roaming.



### The carriers operating in Uganda



- **MTN direct:** the airport booth and flagship Kampala stores handle eSIM daily; small upcountry agents are physical-SIM shops, so register in Entebbe or the capital.

- **Airtel direct:** the Entebbe shop behind the information desk and the kiosks outside the terminal both issue eSIM QRs after passport registration.

- **Both:** bundles expire on fixed daily, weekly and monthly clocks — check the balance in the carrier app before assuming a fault.

### Why a registered Uganda number still cannot open a MoMo wallet



Even a registered MTN or Airtel line will not let a visitor open Mobile Money: MoMo and Airtel Money both require a registered local line plus a national ID, which most tourists do not hold. That is expected, not a fault, and it is why a travel eSIM is enough for a short safari — pay by card or cash instead. A local number earns its paperwork only for longer stays and business.

## Neighboring-Country Roaming



Uganda sits inside the East African Community, and the EAC's "One Network Area" rules have cut cross-border call and data charges across Uganda, Rwanda, Kenya, Tanzania and South Sudan — but they have not abolished them. Your Ugandan bundle still stops being a domestic bundle at the border, so a Kampala-bought SIM is not a substitute for local service in Kigali or Nairobi. The common itineraries where this bites: the Uganda–Rwanda gorilla double (trekking in Bwindi and Volcanoes National Park on one trip) and the Uganda–Kenya safari circuit via the Busia or Malaba crossings. For either, buy a [regional Africa eSIM](/africa-esim/) or a [Kenya eSIM](/kenya-esim/) before you travel; registration at each border town's carrier shop works too, but eats half a day. Note also that registration rules match Uganda's: both Rwanda and Kenya require passport-registered SIMs.



## Uganda eSIM questions: parks, mobile money and registration



### Which carrier should you pick: MTN vs Airtel?

MTN, for the simple reason that its sites reach further upcountry and along the park approach roads. Even so, expect little to no signal deep inside Bwindi — download offline maps and brief your guide by WhatsApp before you enter the forest.



### What documents does an Uganda SIM purchase require?

Yes. The UCC requires every SIM to be registered against an ID. Entebbe airport has MTN and Airtel counters for arrival-day registration. A travel eSIM from an international provider handles registration upstream, so you skip the counter.



### Signing up: Uganda SIM registration

Passport, sometimes a passport photo, and occasionally a local address — your first hotel's name is accepted. Activation is immediate at the airport counters and same-day in town shops.



### Can I use mobile money as a tourist?

Not directly. Opening an MTN MoMo or Airtel Money wallet needs a registered local line plus national ID, which visitors usually lack. You can still pay by card or cash almost everywhere; a local SIM only becomes useful for MoMo if you are staying long-term.



### Is 5G usable for visitors in Uganda yet?

Not in any visitor-useful way. 5G rollout is early and city-limited; plan for 4G LTE in Kampala and Entebbe, with 3G or nothing upcountry and inside parks.



### Uganda carrier APN values

Both use `internet` with blank username and password. Only set this on a carrier-issued eSIM; a travel eSIM configures itself.



### Can I rely on hotel Wi-Fi instead of a SIM?

Not alone. Kampala and Entebbe hotels have usable Wi-Fi, but lodges near the parks run on satellite or metered links that slow to a crawl at full occupancy, and boda-hailing, maps and permit logistics all assume you have your own data. A SIM or eSIM is the base layer; hotel Wi-Fi is the bonus.



### Uganda plans with the lowest prices

Airtel's weekly bundles (3 GB for UGX 10,000, 10 GB for UGX 23,000) edge out MTN's at the same price points. But if your route touches any national park, coverage should decide — a cheaper bundle with no signal in Bwindi is not a bargain.



### Can you buy an Uganda eSIM from MTN?

The Airtel shop sits behind the information desk in the arrivals hall, and MTN runs a booth in the baggage-claim area plus kiosks about 50 m outside the terminal. Both handle registration, eSIM QRs and bundle activation on the spot.



### Which firms run mobile service in Uganda?

Only barely, and only MTN. The far northeast has the thinnest coverage in the country — expect a signal near Apoka and long dead stretches on the drive up. Load all maps, permits and lodge contacts offline before leaving Gulu.



### Does your device pass Uganda's eSIM screening?

It must be unlocked and eSIM-capable. Check both in one pass with the [eSIM compatibility list](/compatibility/). Band support matters less than the unlock status for a clean install.



### Do I need cash at the SIM counter?

Cash in shillings is the safe assumption — SIM plus a first bundle is rarely more than UGX 30,000 (~USD 8). Airport kiosks sometimes take cards or dollars, but town agents deal in UGX cash, and small notes avoid the change problem.



### Home SIM plus Uganda eSIM at the same time?

Yes. Keep your home SIM active for calls/SMS verification and run data on the eSIM. Switch off data roaming on the home SIM so you are not billed for background traffic.



### Is Africell worth considering as a third network?

Africell exists, sells cheap bundles, and even has airport kiosks — but its footprint trails both majors where it counts for visitors, upcountry and around the parks. It is a reasonable Kampala-only backup line, not a safari carrier.
