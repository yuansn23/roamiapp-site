## Phones that can use eSIM

Three things decide whether your phone works on an Irish carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

### Is your phone eSIM-capable for Ireland?

Irish carriers build their 4G on specific bands and their 5G on a narrow slice. The rural backbone is Band 20 at 800 MHz, with newer low-band help from Band 28 at 700 MHz for 5G. Vodafone and Eir hold the heaviest Band 20 investment along secondary and regional roads, which is exactly why they reach the western coast better than raw speed numbers suggest. Cities and larger towns add Band 3 at 1800 MHz and Band 1 at 2100 MHz for capacity, and 5G sits almost entirely on n78 at 3500 MHz, a band whose signal rarely travels more than two or three kilometres from a mast.

| Band | Frequency | What it does for your trip |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Band 20 (LTE) | 800 MHz | The rural backbone; carries signal around coastal headlands and through western valleys |
| Band 28 (5G) | 700 MHz | Newer low-band 5G that reaches further than n78 in the countryside |
| Band 3 (LTE) | 1800 MHz | City and town capacity |
| Band 1 (LTE) | 2100 MHz | Capacity in suburban corridors |
| Band 7 (LTE) | 2600 MHz | Extra city capacity |
| n78 (5G) | 3500 MHz | Fast 5G in cities only; signal rarely reaches 3 km from a mast |

Nobody has to study band charts for a trip to Ireland. The safe route is matching your exact model number, not the shop label, against [eSIM device checker](/compatibility/). The behind-the-scenes version of that handshake lives in [what happens during eSIM activation](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/), if you want it before you buy.

### Does your handset do eSIM in Ireland?

A locked phone is the most common reason an Ireland eSIM install simply fails. Ireland has no national lock rule of the kind Canada or Japan enforces, so the lock status depends entirely on whichever operator originally sold the handset.

**Path:** Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock on iPhone. "No SIM restrictions" on screen means nothing stands in your way. "SIM locked" on the screen blocks every third-party profile until it is lifted.

**Fix it:** only the original seller can lift the lock, so start there rather than with a local shop. Handsets out of contract are typically released within a day and without a fee. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly.

### What is an eSIM and how does it behave inside your handset?

Under the hood, an eSIM is a chip fixed inside your handset that can hold several carrier profiles in place of one plastic card. In practice the network ships a profile, the handset stores it on the internal chip, and the line works like any other. For the full chain, SM-DP+ servers included and the reason the QR code dies after one scan, see [eSIM activation mechanics](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/).

## Documentation we used for the Ireland eSIM comparison

- **Ookla Speedtest Connectivity Report — Ireland, July to December 2025** — [the per-carrier report](https://www.ookla.com/research/reports/ireland-speedtest-connectivity-report-h2-2025) backs every carrier-level figure here: Three's 74.84 connectivity score and 60.22 speed score, Vodafone's 83.5 consistency, Eir's 82.6 5G availability, and the median download speeds of 82.44 Mbps for Three and 52.85 Mbps for Eir.
- **Ookla Speedtest Global Index** — [the Ireland entry](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/ireland) carries the national median mobile download and world ranking, refreshed monthly.
- **Commission for Communications Regulation (ComReg)** — [the official telecoms regulator](https://www.comreg.ie/) publishes the spectrum allocations and coverage maps referenced for the Irish band plan and the rural low-band explanation.
- **Cable.co.uk worldwide data pricing** — [the full country table](https://www.cable.co.uk/mobiles/worldwide-data-pricing/) carries Ireland's per-gigabyte price, about 1.50 US dollars, ranking it among the cheaper markets in Europe.
- **Three Ireland, Vodafone Ireland and Eir** — [Three's eSIM page](https://www.three.ie/esim.html), Vodafone's tourist eSIM page and Eir's support pages are the references for plan prices, delivery methods and the identity-free purchase rule quoted above.

Third-party measurements only. Expect variation by phone model, by the band in use, and by how busy the local site is.
