## Checking your eSIM compatibility

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Japanese carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

### Does my model qualify for Japan eSIM service?

Japanese carriers build their 5G on specific bands, and the split matters more than in most countries. Rakuten Mobile, au and SoftBank all share the global mid-band n77, so almost any foreign 5G phone connects there. NTT Docomo adds its own n79 layer and leans on LTE Band 19 at 800 MHz for low-band coverage, and many North American and European handsets ship without those two. Those phones still work on Docomo, but they fall back to Bands 1 and 3 and lose signal in rural areas, mountain tunnels and the Shinkansen.

The Japanese band list looks intimidating and is mostly not worth learning. The safe bet is to match your exact model, the model number rather than the shop name, against [eSIM compatibility list](/compatibility/). Want the theory before the practice? [what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) covers the load sequence.

### Docomo vs au: which is better in Japan?
A locked phone is the most common reason a Japan eSIM install simply fails, and Japan has unusually clear rules here.

Under guidelines from the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications that took effect in September 2019, Japanese carriers must offer SIM-lock removal, and the process is free when done through the carrier's online or phone channel. Phones sold since May 2015 are unlockable once any mandatory lock-in period, commonly around 100 to 180 days, has passed. MIC names eSIM on its own travel advice as a way to avoid roaming charges.

**How to check:** iPhone → Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. Where the field says "No SIM restrictions", the phone is free to take a profile. If the field shows "SIM locked", no outside profile will load until the handset is freed.

**Fix it:** the operator that sold the handset is the one to ask for an unlock. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and there is no fee through the carrier's self-serve channel. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a phone from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. Full detail: [MIC — travelling with your device](https://www.soumu.go.jp/english/).

### Handset compatibility in Japan

| Device | Symptom | What to do |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Carrier-locked phone, for example a US carrier lock | eSIM install fails entirely | Unlock through the original carrier first, then retry |
| Foreign phone missing Band 19 | Docomo drops to Bands 1 and 3 only; dead zones on trains and in mountains | Use SoftBank or au, or a multi-network eSIM |
| Phone without n77 or n78 | 4G only, no 5G | Expected; 4G is still fast in Japan |

Beyond those cases it is the model, not the network, that decides. Drop your exact handset into the [device compatibility checker](/compatibility/) before you pay for any plan.

### Sales channels for Japan eSIMs

Docomo's own consumer eSIM is for domestic users: it wants a Japanese address and a residence card, and it rides on plans like ahamo, Gigaho or Gigalight. International visitors are pointed at the Docomo network through budget brands instead.

**Reaching Docomo as a visitor, step by step:**

## The APN Values You Need

The APN screen matters most on plans bought inside Japan, where the profile does not always carry its own values. Here are the values per carrier, the narrow set of cases needing manual entry, and the exact menus.

### APN values for Docomo, au and SoftBank eSIMs

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM directly from a Japanese carrier or a budget brand on its network. A travel eSIM in Japan carries its own APN values and needs no manual entry.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| NTT Docomo | `spmode.ne.jp` | blank | blank |
| au (KDDI) | `au.au-net.ne.jp` | blank | blank |
| SoftBank | `smile.world` | blank | blank |
| Rakuten Mobile | `rmobile.jp` | `rm` | `0000` |

Leave username and password empty except on Rakuten Mobile, where the carrier specifies `rm` and `0000`. Should an operator insist on a value, the paperwork shipped with the profile will name it.

### APN configuration in Japan

Most Japanese profiles configure themselves; these are the narrow cases that do not.

- An aging handset that doesn't collect carrier settings automatically
- A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
- A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
- Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile

### Japan: when the APN won't auto-load
- **iPhone:** open Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → key in the APN
- **Android:** Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → add a new APN

After saving, restart the phone. When data still refuses to work, confirm the Japanese profile owns the data line before touching other settings.

### Four remedies for a stalling Japan eSIM

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our [eSIM troubleshooting resource](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). Japan has its own set of four patterns, listed below.

**A. The eSIM will not install**
1. Carrier Lock showing No SIM restrictions means the phone is unlocked
2. Check the QR code is clean and unscanned, most are single-use
3. Ask whether the profile is queued on the carrier side

**B. Installed, but no signal bars**
1. Re-check the lock status
2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → pick Docomo, au, SoftBank or Rakuten manually instead of automatic
3. Do a network reset followed by a full restart

**C. Signal bars, but no internet**
1. Re-read the APN row for your carrier in the table
2. Make sure the Japan eSIM is the line carrying data rather than your home SIM.
3. Check you have not hit a fair-usage throttle; unlimited travel plans often drop to a floor after a daily cap

**D. Dead zones on the train or in the mountains**
1. If you are on Docomo, confirm your phone supports Band 19; without it you fall back to Bands 1 and 3
2. Switch to SoftBank or au, which use globally aligned low bands, or use a multi-network eSIM
3. Download offline maps before the Shinkansen or a trailhead
