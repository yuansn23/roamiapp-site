## What phones accept Greece eSIMs

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Greek carrier: its frequency bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

### Is your model approved for Greece eSIMs?

Greek carriers build their 4G on bands 3 (1800 MHz), 7 (2600 MHz) and 20 (800 MHz), and their 5G on band n78 (3500 MHz) and band n1 (2100 MHz). Almost every flagship phone sold in Europe, North America or Asia since 2019 carries those bands, so most visitors connect without thinking about it. The phone you should worry about is an older model or a budget handset that lacks band 20, because that 800 MHz layer is what carries signal into rural valleys and across open water.

Nobody expects you to memorise band charts. What counts is the model number, not the retail name, so match it against [eSIM compatibility quiz](/compatibility/). If the technical side interests you before you board the ferry, [what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) covers it in plain terms.

### Who sells mobile service in Greece

A locked phone is the most common reason a Greece eSIM install simply fails, and the fix is the same everywhere. Greek carriers sell phones locked to their own network, and a handset locked to a carrier abroad will refuse any profile that is not from that carrier.

**Check it:** on iPhone, open Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock. A "No SIM restrictions" line means you are free to install anything. A "SIM locked" result means travel profiles will refuse to install until the phone is freed.

**Fix it:** the lock belongs to whoever sold the phone, so the unlock request goes to them, not to Cosmote. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and most carriers no longer charge for it. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone on a plan from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. The Greek shop staff cannot unlock a foreign handset for you.

The eSIM is the chip factory-fitted in your handset that holds carrier profiles in place of a plastic card. An operator issues the profile, your handset saves it onto that built-in chip, and from then on it acts like any SIM. The mechanics, SM-DP plus servers, activation codes, and why a QR code is single-use, are explained in [eSIM activation mechanics](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/).
