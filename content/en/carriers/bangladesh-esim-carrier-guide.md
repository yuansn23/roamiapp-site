### Rural coverage in Bangladesh: Grameenphone vs Robi

Grameenphone runs the country's largest network — its 2024 annual report counts nearly 23,000 towers — and holds spectrum at 900, 1800, 2100 and 2600 MHz. That low-band 900 MHz holding carries signal into delta villages, tea gardens and Sundarbans gateway towns where the others thin out. It was also the first Bangladeshi operator to support eSIM.

### Robi eSIM: the city performer

Robi's merger with Airtel gave it a strong urban grid, and it is widely regarded as the network to hold in Dhaka and Chittagong. Robi also describes itself as the first operator to switch on commercial 5G in Bangladesh, on 1 September 2025, in Dhaka, Chittagong and Sylhet; Grameenphone announced the same date with a wider footprint.

Banglalink, owned by VEON, wins on price, and the latest speed data puts it top of the 4G download table. The catch is process: eSIMs are sold only through its Banglalink Centres, with biometric verification completed in person, so the savings come with a shop visit. Its rural footprint is the thinnest of the three.

### Cirkle and Teletalk: the Bangladesh SIM brands at the counter

Two names in Bangladeshi shops confuse visitors. **Cirkle** is the brand Robi launched on 17 August 2026 to replace Airtel, its youth sub-brand since the 2016 merger. Airtel customers kept their 016 numbers and balances, and the network underneath is Robi's — so a Cirkle SIM uses Robi's APN. **Teletalk**, the state operator, is the cheapest of the four and the one to treat with most care.

## Which phones support eSIM

### Can my phone handle a Bangladesh eSIM?

Bangladeshi 4G runs on 900, 1800, 2100 and 2600 MHz — LTE bands 8, 3, 1 and 41 — with **band 3 (1800 MHz)** the workhorse and band 41 supplying urban capacity. Grameenphone's 5G is non-standalone on its 80 MHz holding at 2600 MHz. Almost every handset from the last five years covers band 3; older phones may lack band 41 and fall back to LTE in the cities.

Check your exact model — the model number, not the marketing name — against the [phone compatibility list](/compatibility/), and dial `*#06#`: if an EID appears, the device can hold a profile.

### Are Bangladesh carriers compatible with your device?

A locked phone is the most common reason a Bangladesh eSIM installs but never connects. Check under iPhone → Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. If it reads "SIM locked," no third-party profile will register until the original carrier unlocks it — usually free once it is paid off.

## BTRC registration rules for your eSIM or tourist SIM

Bangladesh requires every SIM to be registered to a verified identity, and since late 2015 that verification has been **biometric**: a live fingerprint scan checked against the government's Central Biometric Verification Monitoring Platform (CBVMP). There is no legal way around it for a locally issued SIM or eSIM.

For visitors, BTRC's tourist SIM guidelines add a specific framework, summarised here for anyone weighing a Bangladesh eSIM bought locally against one bought at home.

| Rule | What it means for a visitor |
|:---|:---|
| Passport-linked registration | A local SIM or eSIM is registered to your passport on the CBVMP, with a live fingerprint scan |
| Two SIMs per passport | You cannot replace a lost tourist SIM without leaving and re-entering the country |
| 7, 15 and 30-day blocks | Tourist validity comes in fixed blocks rather than custom durations |
| Automatic de-registration | The line unregisters itself after 30 days, so nothing needs cancelling |
| Longer stays | A regular SIM registered against a work permit is the route beyond 30 days |

### Does Bangladesh require SIM registration?

The sequence is short but unforgiving of missing documents. This is what happens at a Hazrat Shahjalal arrivals counter or an operator store.

1. **Join the operator's counter queue.** Grameenphone, Robi and Banglalink all staff arrivals at Dhaka. After a long-haul landing, budget 20–45 minutes.
2. **Ask for a tourist SIM or tourist eSIM** and request the full plan list. Staff sometimes lead with the biggest bundle; the 7-day and 15-day blocks are usually better value.
3. **Hand over your original passport and visa** (or the entry stamp, if you arrived on a visa-on-arrival arrangement). Photocopies are frequently refused.
4. **Watch the fingerprint capture.** Your finger goes on the operator's reader and the scan is matched against the CBVMP.
5. **Give a local contact address.** Your hotel is accepted. Some counters type this in without asking, but have the name ready.
6. **Pay in cash, in taka.** Airport counters are the least card-friendly point of sale in the country.
7. **If you chose an eSIM, scan the QR code immediately** over a stable connection, ideally the counter's own Wi-Fi. Most codes are single-use.
8. **Confirm the line is live before you walk away** by opening a page on mobile data, not on Wi-Fi.

### What are the registration rules in Bangladesh?

| Item | Why it matters | If you do not have it |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Original passport | The only identity document the CBVMP accepts from a foreigner | Nothing else substitutes — a copy will be refused |
| Valid visa or entry stamp | Proves your legal basis for a tourist SIM | Port-of-entry counters can work from the arrival record |
| Local address (hotel name) | Required as the registered contact address | Any booked accommodation works |
| Cash in taka | Airport counters take cash, and change may be scarce | Airport ATMs exist but can be empty at night |
| Your EID, from `*#06#` | Lets the counter confirm eSIM eligibility on the spot | Staff can check compatibility on the operator's system |
| A charged phone with a working screen | The QR code has to be scanned and the profile verified | Use a spare handset or buy a physical SIM instead |

### Bangladesh SIM registration: when your fingerprints will not read

The biometric reality deserves honest words. The scan happens at an operator counter, on their device. Travellers with worn or unclear fingerprints — common among manual workers, older visitors and anyone with dry skin in winter — have historically hit rejections, and BTRC has formalised a "special method" for those cases. Ask for it rather than retrying until the system locks you out.

## When a Bangladesh eSIM slows down: Eid, monsoon and peak hours

Congestion on a Bangladesh eSIM is predictable, and it is seasonal as much as hourly. Two national events move tens of millions of people at once, and one weather system does the rest.

### Eid travel: Bangladesh's biggest mobile network load of the year

The BTRC tracks subscriber movement by cell, and Eid-ul-Fitr 2025 shows why that week feels different: **10.7 million mobile users left Dhaka** in the seven days from 28 March to 3 April, peaking at 2.39 million departures on 30 March, while 4.44 million returned. Traffic inverts — urban cells empty out while rural sites absorb a surge, and Eid-ul-Azha repeats the pattern more quietly.

### Bangladesh monsoon and cyclone season: what it does to mobile data

The monsoon runs roughly June to September, and it is a network event as well as a travel inconvenience. Heavy rain degrades high-band signals, and standing water cuts power to sites that then run on backup batteries. Bangladesh also sits in the Bay of Bengal's cyclone belt, whose peaks fall around April–May and October–November; a cyclone can take towers down for days.

### Bangladesh evening data congestion, and what to do about it

| Situation | What you will notice | What actually helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Dhaka evening peak, roughly 8–11 pm | Downloads fall below the national median | Do heavy uploads in the morning; keep messaging for the evening |
| Friday afternoon and public holidays | More video traffic on every network in the cities | Nothing to fix — the network is simply busy |
| Eid travel week | Rural cells saturated, urban cells quiet | Let the eSIM reselect the strongest network and pre-download what you need |
| Monsoon downpour | Brief drops, especially on high bands | Move near a window or step outside; low-band signal recovers first |
| Cyclone warning in a coastal district | Outages can last days, not hours | Offline maps, a power bank and a written list of phone numbers |

## Bangladesh eSIM activation and the four failures to expect

The generic install sequence — add eSIM, scan the QR code, label the line, set it as your data line, enable data roaming — is covered step by step in [step-by-step eSIM activation guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). What follows are the four failure patterns a Bangladesh eSIM trip actually produces, in the order you are likely to meet them.

**A. Registration rejected at the counter.** Usually a fingerprint mismatch or a passport detail that will not validate. Move from a small agent shop to a corporate store — they have the escalation path — and bring the original passport. If your prints will not read, ask about the regulator's special-case process.

**B. Installed, but no signal bars.** Check the profile is enabled, then switch network selection from automatic to manual and pick Grameenphone, Robi or Banglalink explicitly. In remote districts your phone may camp on 2G — that is the network, not your eSIM.

**C. Signal bars, but no internet.** Cross-check the APN against the carrier table earlier in this section, confirm the eSIM is the active data line, and check data roaming is on for that line. Then check your balance: prepaid packs simply stop when the allowance ends, and a top-up from any corner shop fixes it.

**D. "SOS" or "Emergency calls only."** Confirm the eSIM line is switched on in the SIM manager, disable other active profiles, reset network settings and reboot. As a last resort, request a fresh QR code from the issuing counter — most codes are single-use.

If none of that lands, the wider catalogue in [eSIM error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/) covers profile-download errors and carrier-lock conflicts in detail.
