## Where your eSIM fits in a mobile-money economy



Ghana sits at the top of the GSMA Mobile Money Regulatory Index, a position the Bank of Ghana confirmed it still held in February 2026, and roughly 20 million Ghanaians use a wallet regularly. Traders take MoMo, landlords collect rent through it, and fuel stations display agent numbers by the pumps. Interoperability is mandatory: an MTN MoMo wallet can pay a Telecel Cash wallet directly.



That matters for one reason: **a wallet has to hang off a registered local line.** A data-only travel eSIM gets you online seconds after landing and runs maps, Bolt, WhatsApp and every booking app you need. What it will not give you is a +233 subscriber identity — the prerequisite for opening MoMo, Telecel Cash or AT Money in your own name.



So: buy a travel eSIM if your Ghana is a holiday — cities, coast, a national park, two or three weeks. Add a locally registered line if the trip involves paying people the way Ghanaians do: a driver quoting a wallet number, a guesthouse preferring a transfer.

## Ghana's eSIM registration law: what the NCA requires from visitors



Registration is governed by the Subscriber Identity Module Registration Regulations, 2011 (L.I. 2006), and the NCA is tightening the framework so every subscriber is verified biometrically against the National Identification Authority (NIA) database. A SIM activates only after that verification succeeds — an attempt to close off the anonymous registrations behind SIM-swap fraud and mobile-money scams.



The NCA's own guidance lists six subscriber categories, and visitors occupy a clearly defined one:



| Who you are | What you must present |

|:---|:---|

| Ghanaian citizen | Ghana Card |

| Foreign resident | Non-Citizen Ghana Card |

| Visitor or tourist | **Passport + proof of entry** |

| Refugee | Refugee Non-Citizen ID |

| Diplomat | Ministry of Foreign Affairs ID |

| Corporate body | Certificate of incorporation + signatory's Ghana Card |



Three consequences follow. You do not need a Ghana Card for a short visit: passport and proof of entry are the designated documents, and registration is free because the NCA has ruled operators absorb the cost. The platform supports self-registration for embedded SIMs as well as physical ones, though the counter agent still does the work. And foreign-national lines carry a **90-day validity rule** tied to your legal stay, after which they deactivate automatically.



One warning deserves its own paragraph: never buy a "pre-registered" SIM from a street vendor. It is registered to someone else's identity, it breaks the regulations, and it is the fastest way to lose both your money and your connectivity.



## SIM Registration Rules



If you plan to hold a local line, this is the sequence. It is not complicated, but the order matters — biometric verification comes before activation, and activation before any bundle works.



1. **Pick the store.** An MTN, Telecel or AT shop, or a staffed counter at Kotoka International Airport. Avoid street agents.

2. **Bring originals.** Passport, not a photocopy, plus the entry stamp or visa page showing your entry date.

3. **Give a reachable local address.** Your hotel or host's address is what the form expects; nothing is posted to it.

4. **Complete biometric capture.** The agent photographs you and takes fingerprints through the SIM Registration App, checked against NIA records.

5. **Nominate a primary number.** Subscribers pick one number to receive OTPs later — for a single-line visitor, your new line.

6. **Receive the eSIM profile.** The agent issues the QR code, usually printed. Keep it confidential; it provisions your line.

7. **Buy a bundle and test it.** Dial `*138#` on MTN, `*700#` on Telecel or `*111#` on AT, or pay cash at the counter, then load a page.



| Question | Ghana Card route | Passport route (visitors) |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Who it is for | Citizens and long-term residents | Anyone on a short visit |

| Document needed | Ghana Card, validated against the NIA database | Passport plus proof of entry |

| Cost | Free | Free; operators absorb registration costs |

| Typical time at the counter | 5–15 minutes when the system is up | 10–30 minutes, longer at airport peaks |

| Line validity | Open-ended | 90 days, or the end of your legal stay |

| Can it open a mobile wallet | Yes | Yes — a registered line is what the wallet attaches to |



The NCA has flagged that operators may move to **scheduled appointments** to control congestion, so it is worth checking the nearest store's practice first. Registration is free; the cost is your time.

Telecel handles eSIMs at its retail and community stores: identity verification, biometric capture if required, and a QR code sent to your email. Keep that code confidential — it provisions your line. Bundles run through `*700#` or Telecel's app, payable in cash or through Telecel Cash.

AT takes eSIM applications through the portal on its website or at a customer service centre: you supply your details, pass verification, and receive the QR code by printout or email. ## Ghana eSIM compatibility: bands, locks and quirks



### Will an iPhone connect in Ghana?



Ghana's 4G runs on LTE bands 1 (2100 MHz), 3 (1800 MHz), 7 (2600 MHz) and 20 (800 MHz), with 3G on bands 1 and 8 and 2G fallback on band 3. **MTN's 4G leans on bands 1, 7 and 20**; Telecel's on bands 3 and 20. Any international handset from the last several years covers bands 3 and 7, keeping you on fast 4G in every city. Phones without band 20 lose some rural reach but remain usable.



Ghana's 5G is a shared-infrastructure story moving slower than the marketing suggests. The Next-Gen Infrastructure Company (NGIC) runs a wholesale backbone on 100 MHz of 2600 MHz spectrum, live in Accra, Kumasi and Tamale, with operators leasing capacity from it. By March 2026 it had built only 49 of the 1,200 sites it targeted, and the NCA has since opened direct 5G licensing on 3.5 GHz and 26 GHz. A 4G phone will not miss much yet.



### Can Android devices use Ghana eSIMs?



A locked phone blocks a Ghana eSIM as surely as a local SIM. Check iPhone → Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock; "No SIM restrictions" is what you want. If it shows a lock, ask the original carrier to unlock it before you travel. Run your model through the [eSIM device checker](/compatibility/) to confirm eSIM support and bands in one pass.



### IMEI and EID screening for Ghana eSIMs

|:---|:---|:---|

| Mainland-China iPhone models | No "Add eSIM" option | Use a physical SIM or a different device |

| Dual-SIM setups | Data flows on the home SIM | Set the Ghana eSIM as the Mobile Data line |

| Carrier-locked Samsungs | eSIM menu greyed out | Unlock with the original carrier, then reboot |

## Taking a Ghana eSIM to the neighbours: ECOWAS roaming



Ghana wraps around Togo and borders Burkina Faso and Côte d'Ivoire. Because it belongs to the ECOWAS Free Roaming Initiative, cross-border connectivity is better than most travelers expect — and far better than the old per-megabyte rates suggested.



### Your Ghana eSIM beyond the border



Ghana has implemented the framework bilaterally with Côte d'Ivoire since June 2023 and with Togo and Benin since October 2024. A home line keeps working abroad at **local rates for calls, SMS and data**, with incoming calls free, for up to **30 consecutive days**. Permanent roaming is not allowed: past 30 days you need a local line. The catch for a data-hungry traveler is that you generally **cannot buy a new bundle while roaming**.



### Roaming charges on Ghana carriers



| Neighbour | What happens when you cross | Practical note |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Togo | Ghana–Togo bilateral roaming is live; local rates for voice, SMS and data | Lomé is close enough to Accra for a day trip |

| Burkina Faso | ECOWAS framework applies, but bilateral implementation varies by operator | Do not assume the free-roaming rate; confirm with your operator first |

| Côte d'Ivoire | Live since June 2023, the longest-running Ghanaian arrangement | Relevant if your route runs west along the coast |

| Benin | Ghana–Benin launched in July 2024; a trilateral with Togo followed that October | Matters only if the trip extends well east |

| Nigeria | Outside the implemented bilateral set; standard roaming charges apply | Buy a Nigerian line for anything beyond a short crossing |



A travel eSIM changes that arithmetic: a regional profile bills the crossed-into network at its own rate, so you never hit the "cannot buy a bundle abroad" dead end — worth the premium if your itinerary ends at a land border.

## Activating your eSIM

Often yes, at local rates. Ghana has implemented ECOWAS free roaming with Côte d'Ivoire since 2023 and with Togo and Benin since 2024: calls, SMS and data bill locally, with free incoming calls for up to 30 days. Top up first, though — you usually cannot buy a fresh bundle abroad.



### Will WhatsApp voice calls connect in Ghana?



Yes — the eSIM supplies data, and WhatsApp stays bound to your home number, which keeps taking calls and SMS on your physical SIM. Turn data roaming off on the home line so background traffic does not bill you.
