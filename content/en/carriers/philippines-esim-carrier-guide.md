## Will your handset cooperate?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Philippine carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

### Will an older phone accept a Philippines eSIM?

Philippine carriers build their 4G on bands including B1, B3, B5, B8 and B28, and their 5G on n78 and n41. Almost any recent international 5G phone connects on n78, which is the global mid-band, so 5G itself is rarely the problem. The harder gap is B28 at 700 MHz, which Smart and DITO use for exactly the rural and island reach that visitors care about. Phones sold only for North American or Chinese markets sometimes miss B28, and those handsets fall back to B1 and B3, which are city layers.

Skip the band tables. The safer move is to match your exact model — the model number, not the marketing name — against [compatibility list](/compatibility/). Want the mechanics first? [What your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) explains the process.

### Is your handset compatible with Philippines networks?

A locked handset is the single most common reason a Philippine eSIM install fails, and here the rule runs the other way. There is no blanket unlock mandate for foreign phones, so a handset locked to a US, European or Middle Eastern operator stays locked on arrival; the unlock has to happen with the operator back home before you fly.

**Check it:** on iPhone, open Settings → General → About and read the Carrier Lock field. If it reads "No SIM restrictions," you are clear. If it reads "SIM locked," no Philippine eSIM will install until that changes.

**Fix it:** request an unlock from the operator the phone is locked to. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and many operators do it for free through their app or website. Only then try installing your eSIM, because a locked baseband rejects every profile you scan.

If you bought a used or carrier-subsidised phone, assume it may be locked and check before you travel. The lock check takes ten seconds and saves a failed install on a Boracay beach.

### Do Philippines networks check IMEI or EID?

| Device | Symptom | What to do |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Carrier-locked phone from another country | eSIM install fails entirely | Unlock through the original carrier first, then retry |
| Phone missing B28 | Fast in Manila and Cebu, dead in the provinces | Use Smart or DITO where B28 matters, or a multi-network eSIM |
| Phone without n78 or n41 | 4G only, no 5G | Expected; 4G is still usable across the islands |
| Mainland-China iPhone models | No Add eSIM option at all, hardware disabled | Not fixable; use a physical SIM or another device |
| Dual-SIM users | eSIM installed but no data | Set the eSIM as the line used for mobile data |

Everything else is a handset quirk rather than a network rule. Check your model in the [phone compatibility list](/compatibility/) before paying for a plan.

## Data Plans: The per-GB Math

Three routes exist, and they differ more in registration than in price: Globe through the GlobeOne app, Smart through GigaLife or the Smart app, and DITO through its own app or a store. Each route, step by step.

### Where to source your Philippines eSIM

Globe is the most tourist-friendly of the three, because its app and registration flow are built for walk-up buyers.

**Globe prepaid eSIM, step by step:**

1. Download the GlobeOne app and open the eSIM purchase section.
2. Choose the Globe Prepaid eSIM at **Php 99**, or the Globe Traveler eSIM at **Php 1,750** if you want a tourist-focused bundle.
3. Pay with an international card, PayPal or Apple Pay, and the QR code arrives by email within minutes.
4. Scan the code in Settings, then finish SIM registration with your passport, hotel address and return ticket inside the same app.

Globe publishes eSIM support for iPhone XS and later, Pixel 3 and later, and Galaxy S20 and later. The 5-scan QR limit matters: Globe caps re-scans at five across all devices, so keep the delivery email safe and do not burn scans on test devices.

### Local SIM vs eSIM for tourists in Philippines

Smart is the fastest network in the country, and its eSIM is just as easy to buy as Globe's, through the GigaLife app or the newer Smart app.

**Smart prepaid eSIM, step by step:**

1. Open the GigaLife or Smart app and select the prepaid eSIM option.
2. Pay and receive the QR code by email, usually within minutes.
3. Add the plan in your phone settings, then register the line with your passport details.
4. Load a data promo such as GigaSurf 599 for 12 GB over 30 days, or GigaSurf 999 for 24 GB over 30 days, inside the app.

Smart's PowerAll and Unli 5G promos are popular with visitors who stream or share a hotspot, but watch the fair-use policy, because unlimited plans drop to a floor once a daily cap is reached. Smart's real edge is speed in the dense cities, which is why it tops Ookla's Speed Score table.

### Plans and prices: Philippines eSIMs

DITO is the youngest carrier and the value leader, with DITO 99 giving 10 GB over 30 days, DITO 199 giving 25 GB, and DITO 499 giving 50 GB. You can buy the eSIM in the DITO app after eKYC, or at a DITO store where staff register it on the spot.

**DITO prepaid eSIM, step by step:**

1. Install the DITO app and start the eSIM purchase with a photo of your ID.
2. Complete the registration with passport, address and return ticket.
3. Receive the profile by email or SMS and scan it in your settings.
4. Top up inside the app; DITO promos are the cheapest per gigabyte of the three.

The trade is coverage. DITO won Ookla's top consumer rating at 3.65 out of 5 and the best 5G consistency at 86.2%, but its footprint is concentrated on the main islands, so it is a city and resort play rather than a remote-island one.

## APN settings for Globe, Smart and DITO eSIMs

The APN screen is the last setting most travelers should touch and the first thing to rule out when data will not load. Below: the values for all three carriers, when manual entry is genuinely required, and the exact menu paths.

### APN values for Globe, Smart and DITO eSIMs

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM directly from a Philippine carrier. If you are on a travel eSIM, the profile supplies its own APN and you should not change anything.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| Smart | `internet.smart.com.ph` | blank | blank |
| DITO | `internet.dito.ph` | blank | blank |

Leave username and password empty. If a carrier requires values, the activation instructions that came with your profile will state them, and the exact string is confirmed in the carrier app.

### APN configuration in Philippines

A registered Philippine eSIM normally configures itself; these are the exceptions.

- An older handset where carrier settings never arrive on their own
- A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
- A carrier eSIM where automatic configuration did not run after registration
- Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile

### Manual APN: when Philippines needs it

The path is identical for all three carriers — only the values above change.

- **iPhone:** Settings → Cellular → select the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → type in the APN
- **Android:** Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → create a new entry

After saving, restart the phone. If data still will not flow, check that the eSIM — not your home SIM — holds the data-line role before changing anything else.

### A stalled Philippines eSIM: four fixes

Profiles that refuse to download, scans that fail, and eSIMs that install but never register are all catalogued in our [activation error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The list below narrows to four patterns that matter in the Philippines.

**A. The eSIM will not install**
1. Unlocked status check: Carrier Lock in About should show "No SIM restrictions"
2. make sure the QR code is clean and unused — nearly all are single-scan
3. check with the carrier that the profile is not still queued for registration

**B. Installed, but no signal bars**
1. Re-check the lock status
2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → pick Globe, Smart or DITO manually instead of automatic
3. Reset all network settings, then reboot again

**C. Signal bars, but no internet**
1. Match your APN once more to the table row for that carrier
2. make sure the eSIM, rather than your home SIM, is the active data line
3. Check you have finished SIM registration, because an unregistered Philippine eSIM shows bars but passes no data

**D. Signal dies on a boat or between islands**
1. This is coverage, not a fault; the gap is real on open water between islands
2. Switch to the carrier with the strongest coastal footprint, usually Smart, or use a multi-network eSIM
3. Download offline maps before the ferry, because the crossing may stay dark for the whole trip

### Which network is best in Philippines?

Because the gap is real, not a fault. The Philippines is an archipelago, and open water between islands has no mast, so every carrier drops to nothing for the crossing, especially the ferry runs to smaller isles off Palawan, Boracay and Siargaay. Download offline maps before you board, and expect the signal to return only as you approach the next port.

### The registration routine in Philippines

Exactly 30 days. Republic Act 11934 requires every SIM and eSIM to be registered, and a line registered under the tourist category is valid for only 30 days, then it is automatically and permanently deactivated. Loading more credit does not extend that clock, because the limit is tied to visa status, not to your balance. Plan a carrier eSIM only if your trip is shorter than a month.

**Should you expect an ID check when buying a SIM in Philippines?**

Tourists present a passport, proof of a Philippine address and a return ticket, and the line will not activate until that is done. A travel eSIM still asks for a passport at checkout, but only once, and it skips the 30 day tourist cliff that the carriers are bound by.

### What happens to my eSIM during a typhoon

Philippine typhoon season runs June to November, and severe weather can knock out coastal and island sites for 24 to 48 hours, longest on the remote islands. Smart and Globe run backup power at most sites, so cities recover fast, while smaller islands may stay dark until crews reach them. If you travel in season, keep a power bank charged, expect temporary drops, and do not rely on a single carrier in a storm path.
