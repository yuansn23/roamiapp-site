## How EU rules shape what a Bulgaria eSIM costs



One piece of context explains most of the price sheet. **Roam-like-at-home is a right for EU residents, not for visitors.** If you live in Germany or Italy and your domestic plan covers the EU, it works in Bulgaria under the EU roaming regulation at no extra cost. If you are flying in from the US, the UK or Australia, that right does not apply to you — you buy local.



What visitors inherit is the *downstream* effect of the EU wholesale caps. Under the recast Roaming Regulation the maximum one operator may charge another for 1 GB fell from **€2.00 in 2022 to €1.30 in 2025**, on a path to **€1.00 by 2027**, with the framework itself running to 30 June 2032. Because Bulgarian operators buy roaming data at those capped rates while competing hard at home, local prepaid prices sit far below the EU retail ceilings — which is why a Bulgaria eSIM competes against €7-per-15-GB packs and why travel providers price the country so aggressively too.



### Bulgaria's euro changeover: what it means for your eSIM bill



Bulgaria adopted the euro on **1 January 2026**, with the lev fixed at 1.95583 to the euro, and dual BGN/EUR display ended in August 2026. For a visitor that means quoted prices and charged prices now match, and a euro card meets no conversion step at the till.



A second EU consequence cuts the other way: **local plans give only small EU roaming allowances.** Vivacom's Free2Go includes roughly 9.2 GB usable across the EU, and Yettel's eSIM packages exclude roaming entirely — they work in Bulgaria and stop at the border.

## Checking your handset



Whether a Bulgaria eSIM runs on your handset comes down to three checks: its bands, its lock status, and an EID. Bulgarian networks build 4G on bands 3, 7 and 20 — the standard European LTE set — with 5G on the n78 (3.5 GHz) band in cities and resort areas. Any phone bought in Europe handles this without thinking. Handsets from North America or Asia usually work too, but a few regional models lack band 20 or n78, which shows up as fast service in central Sofia and a fall to 4G or 3G the moment you leave the highway.

## Activation: Step by Step



The generic install — Settings, Add eSIM, scan the QR code, label the line, set it as the data line, enable data roaming — is identical everywhere and covered in our [step-by-step activation walkthrough](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). What follows are the four failure patterns Bulgarian networks actually produce.



**A. The profile will not install.** Confirm Carrier Lock reads "No SIM restrictions," confirm the QR code is unscanned (they are single-use), and check the email did not bury the activation code in a PDF your scanner app cannot read — Yettel's flow includes both a QR image and a manual code, and the manual code solves most scan failures on Android.



**B. Installed, but no signal bars.** Wait two minutes at Sofia Airport — the arrival-hall cells are saturated in summer. Then set network selection to manual and pick A1, Yettel or Vivacom explicitly. A profile bought in store sometimes takes an hour to provision; a coffee is cheaper than a support call.



**C. Signal bars, but no internet.** Check the APN against the table below — this is the most common Bulgarian carrier failure for manually-installed profiles. Confirm data roaming is ON for the eSIM line, because the profile registers as a roaming line even at home. Then check your allowance: local packs cut off or throttle rather than billing overage.



**D. Works in Sofia, dead in the mountains.** This is not a failure but physics. Above the tree line in Rila, Pirin or the Rhodopes no carrier has continuous coverage, and a satellite messenger is the correct tool for serious hiking.

## Frequently Asked Questions



### Can I use a Bulgaria eSIM in Greece or Romania?



Not automatically. Data-only eSIMs like Yettel's 25 GB and 100 GB packages are valid in Bulgaria only and include no roaming. Vivacom bundles carry a small EU data allowance (roughly 9–17 GB depending on the pack), and voice bundles registered with ID may include EU minutes. For a real cross-border trip, buy a regional plan.



### Roaming charges on Bulgaria carriers



Only if you want more data or a local number. If you are an EU resident whose plan includes roam-like-at-home, your domestic allowance already works here and you pay nothing extra, subject to your operator's fair-use limit. Visitors from outside the EU have no such right, so a Bulgaria eSIM is almost always the cheaper route.



### Can I use a Bulgaria eSIM in Serbia or North Macedonia?



No, not on ordinary terms. Neither country is in the EU roaming area. The EU and the Western Balkans did agree a voluntary price reduction in 2023, and the Commission won authorisation to negotiate full free roaming in June 2026, but no deadline is set. Buy a dedicated plan for each country you cross into.



### Which companies run Bulgaria's networks?



All three cover the town well, and Yettel and A1 hold signal furthest up the gondola lines. On peak February Saturdays every network slows — the local cells are simply outnumbered. A multi-network eSIM that re-selects the strongest carrier is the most reliable fix.



### What ID does a Bulgaria SIM need?



Only if the product includes calls or SMS — those bundles must be registered in person against a passport or ID. Pure data packs and data-only eSIMs, including Yettel's online prepaid eSIM, require no registration at all. Keep your home SIM active for bank codes either way.
