The capital has fast, cheap, genuinely modern mobile internet; the Gobi, the Altai and the Khövsgöl shorelines operate on a different physics entirely, where coverage means the nearest ger camp and not the trailhead. This guide covers the Unitel vs Mobicom decision with the airport-tourist-eSIM detail other articles miss, the registration rules, and a region-by-region plan for the world's least densely populated country. ### Mongolia coverage: what the coverage map does not tell you



Three things worth internalizing before you plan connectivity:



- **Distance is the enemy, not terrain alone.** A ger camp 40 km from a provincial capital can be dark while the capital has five bars. Signal is a town service that leaks a little into the surrounding countryside.

- **Tourist camps cluster where coverage exists.** The camps that host foreigners are usually the ones that chose a site with signal, or installed Starlink. That is why "the middle of nowhere" often has Wi-Fi at dinner and nothing at breakfast on the road.

- **Your battery matters more than your bars.** Long days in a vehicle with the phone hunting for a network is the fastest way to a dead handset. Carry a power bank, and turn the eSIM line off in known dead zones rather than letting it search.



One modern fact changes the remote-travel calculus: **Starlink service works in Mongolia** and is increasingly used by tour operators and ger camps for backhaul Wi-Fi. Like in Kenya, it does not replace your eSIM — it explains why the middle-of-nowhere camp still has Wi-Fi at dinner. And arriving via the airport, note that UBN sits roughly 50 km from the city — if you land late with nothing installed, the free Mobicom tourist eSIM at arrivals is the fastest fix.

### Activating on Unitel, Mobicom and Gobi



1. **Manual network selection helps at the edges.** Outside the towns, forcing the phone to a specific operator (Settings → Network Selection) can attach where automatic search gives up.

2. **No signal is usually geography, not configuration.** Before troubleshooting, check whether you are simply outside every footprint — the region table above is the sanity check.

3. **The profile may take a minute to register after a long drive.** Coming out of a dead zone, give the phone time before assuming the eSIM has failed.

4. **Signal but no data:** data-line setting, roaming toggle, then APN — the full sequence is in our [eSIM problem-solving guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/).



### Four Mongolia eSIM failure fixes



**A. The eSIM will not install**

1. The handset must be lock-free; Carrier Lock under About proves it with "No SIM restrictions"

2. The QR needs to be sharp and unused -- rescans fail

3. Ask the provider to verify the profile was really pushed



**B. Installed, but no signal bars**

1. Confirm you are inside a coverage area — check the region table

2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose Unitel or Mobicom manually

3. Toggle the line off and on, then reboot



**C. Signal bars, but no internet**

1. Put the APN back in precisely as the table lists it

2. Verify data is routed to the eSIM, not your home SIM

3. Turn data roaming on for this specific line

4. Make sure the plan data bucket is not spent



**D. Worked yesterday, dead today**

1. You probably moved — recheck the coverage map for your new position

2. Toggle airplane mode to force a fresh network scan

3. Select the network manually rather than leaving it on automatic

## How to plan Mongolia eSIM data for an overland route



Mongolia's long-distance travel rewards a little planning in a way that city trips do not. Work through this before you leave Ulaanbaatar:



| Question | Why it matters |

|:---|:---|

| How many nights are outside a provincial capital? | Each one is a potential full offline day |

| Does your driver carry a satellite phone or radio? | Local operators often do — ask before you assume |

| Which operator does your tour company use? | Their routes are built around one network's coverage |

| Is there ger camp Wi-Fi anywhere on the route? | Increasingly yes, often Starlink-backed |

| What is the longest stretch with no settlement? | That number is your offline stretch, not your plan's data allowance |



### Buying local in Mongolia vs going travel eSIM



1. **Download before you leave the capital.** Every leg of the route, plus bookings and any tickets, as offline screenshots.

2. **Accept that connectivity is a town-level service.** Two days on the road will contain hours of no signal on any carrier.

3. **Charge at every opportunity.** A phone searching for a network in a moving vehicle drains faster than one sitting on a good signal.

4. **Set a daily check-in.** If someone at home expects to hear from you, tell them which days you will be dark.

5. **Switch the eSIM line off in confirmed dead zones.** Letting it hunt for a network wastes battery you cannot recharge.



### How many gigabytes does a week in Mongolia burn?



Mongolian phone plans are cheap, but rural bandwidth is not generous. Turn off automatic photo and video uploads for the duration of the trip and sync in towns instead. A single evening of backup-to-cloud over a congested provincial 4G cell can cost more time and data than the whole week's maps put together.

For a short trip, yes — it skips registration and works on landing. For a stay measured in months, a local Unitel or Mobicom line is cheaper per gigabyte and gives you a Mongolian number. The decision is about trip length, not network quality.



### What happens if my eSIM does not work in Ulaanbaatar?
