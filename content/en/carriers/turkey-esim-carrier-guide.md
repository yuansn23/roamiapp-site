## Device support for eSIM



Your handset has to clear three checks before a Turkish eSIM will work: the bands it supports, whether some operator still locks it, and a handful of model-specific quirks. All three are explained below, and the band issue weighs heavier here than in many countries because of how Turkey licensed 5G.



### Is my phone able to run a Turkey eSIM?



Turkey's 5G spectrum auction finished in October 2025 and commercial 5G switched on across all 81 provinces in 2026, so the networks you connect to today are a mix of new 5G and the mature 4.5G tier that ran before it. The 4.5G layer leans on Band 3 at 1800 MHz, Band 7 at 2600 MHz and Band 20 at 800 MHz for range, while 5G uses n78 at 3500 MHz. Almost every international phone sold in the last five years already supports those bands, so most visitors connect without thinking about it.



You do not need to learn the band table by heart. The safer play is to match your exact model number, not its shop name, against [compatibility checker](/compatibility/). For the theory first, [what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) explains the process.



### Which Turkey carrier should you pick: Turkcell vs Vodafone?



The single most common reason a Turkish eSIM refuses to install is a phone still locked to another operator. Turkey's own rules are clear: handsets sold on contract inside the country can be tied to the selling operator, and a phone locked abroad will not accept a Turkish profile until it is freed.



**Check it:** head to Settings → General → About on your iPhone and read the Carrier Lock entry. When it shows No SIM restrictions, the handset can take a profile; if it shows SIM locked, nothing else will install until that changes.



**Fix it:** go back to the operator that sold the phone and ask for an unlock. Handsets that are fully paid off are usually freed within hours at no charge through that operator's app or website, and only after that should you attempt the install.



If your handset came from a US or European operator, treat it as probably locked and verify before departure, because a Turkish prepaid line cannot override a foreign lock.

### Turkey: IMEI and EID screening



This is the rule that surprises people, and it is the single biggest Türkiye-specific trap in this whole guide. Turkey tracks every phone that connects to its networks by its IMEI number, and under rules set by the regulator BTK a foreign phone used on a Turkish SIM must be registered within **120 days** of your entry or it is blocked from all three networks. Calling is cut off; only emergency calls keep working.



| Part of the rule | What it says | What it means for you |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Grace period | 120 days from entry | A normal holiday is far inside this window |

| Registration fee | 45,614 TL in 2025, rising to 57,241 TL from 1 January 2026 | Expensive, and reviewed every year |

| Frequency | One device per passport every three years | You cannot keep re-registering phones |

| Who can register | Residents with a Turkish residence permit and e-Devlet access | Tourists normally cannot complete it |

| Exemption | Stays under 120 days need no device registration | Most visitors are simply exempt |



Here is the part travellers mix up. Buying a prepaid SIM registers the line to your passport; it does **not** register your phone. The 120-day clock is a separate BTK rule about the handset itself, and it only starts to matter if you stay longer than four months. If you use a travel eSIM supplied by a non-Turkish company, that profile is not a Turkish prepaid line tied to your passport, which is one reason longer-stay visitors lean on travel eSIMs to avoid the clock entirely. If you do settle in Turkey, register the device at a tax office or through e-Devlet, then take the receipt to a carrier store, and budget for the five-figure lira fee.

### Unstick a Turkey eSIM: four ways



The wider error catalogue is in our [complete eSIM troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/); the four patterns beneath are the ones you will actually meet in Turkey.



**A. The eSIM will not install**

1. Carrier Lock in Settings -> General -> About must show No SIM restrictions

1. This is the IMEI block, not a normal fault, and it only appears after 120 days on a Turkish SIM

2. Confirm the date you entered Turkey against your passport stamp

3. Either leave and re-enter, or register the device through e-Devlet with a residence permit and pay the fee
