## ID Requirements for SIM Purchases

Estonia changed the game on **1 January 2024**: every new prepaid SIM card — physical or eSIM — must be registered to a valid government-issued photo ID. Accepted documents are a **passport or an EU/EEA national ID card**. There are no exemptions for tourists, and the process is done in person at an official operator store (Telia Esindus, Elisa Esindus, or Tele2 Esindus), where staff scan the document and issue the eSIM QR code. A setup fee of roughly €2–5 may apply.

What this means in practice:

- **You cannot pre-order a Telia/Elisa/Tele2 eSIM from abroad and arrive with it ready.** The ID check happens at the store.
- **Airport and kiosk SIMs are limited.** Tallinn Airport has Elisa at R-Kiosk but no Telia counter inside the terminal; the nearest full Telia store is at Ülemiste, a short tram ride away. Weekend and evening arrivals often find shops closed.
- **A global travel eSIM bypasses the wall.** Providers such as Roami, Airalo and Holafly sell data-only Estonia eSIMs that install by QR code before you fly — no passport scan, no store visit. The trade-off is they are data-only (no +372 number) and cost more per gigabyte than a local prepaid.

Estonia's regulator, the **Consumer Protection and Technical Regulatory Authority (TTJA)**, maintains the national coverage map, and the ID rule is enforced uniformly across all three operators.

## Cross-border coverage

Estonia is inside the EU/EEA, so "Roam Like at Home" (EU Regulation [2022/612](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R0612), guaranteed through 2032) applies. But the details catch visitors:

- **EU visitors:** your home SIM works in Estonia at domestic rates, no surcharge. This is why many EU travellers never buy a local SIM at all.
- **Local prepaid plans are not automatically roaming-inclusive.** Elisa and Tele2 core prepaid plans do **not** include EU roaming by default; Telia's Super prepaid (€24/200 GB) includes an EU fair-use allowance of just 23 GB — an 88.5% reduction from the domestic bucket. If you buy a local prepaid and then cross into Finland or Latvia, you may hit a wall.
- **The wholesale cap ceiling:** the EU caps what visited networks can charge for roaming data — **€1.30/GB in 2025, dropping to €1.10/GB in 2026 and €1.00/GB in 2027** per the [European Commission's RLAH rules](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-single-market/en/faq/question-and-answers-roaming). That is the most a carrier can bill; many tourist plans simply cap or block roaming instead.
- **Tallinn–Helsinki ferry:** the 2-hour crossing is a real use case. Onboard you usually have ship Wi-Fi or Finnish coastal signal; in open-water gaps there is nothing. Once you dock in Helsinki you are back in the RLAH zone, so a travel eSIM that includes EU/EEA coverage keeps working without a second plan.

### Abroad on a Estonia eSIM

Estonia shares a land border with Russia along the Narva River (Narva and Ivangorod face each other across the water) and around the southeastern Setomaa area. Within a few kilometres of that frontier your phone can latch onto a Russian operator — Megafon, MTS, Beeline or Tele2 Russia — which is **outside the EU/EEA and outside Roam Like at Home entirely**. A local prepaid or even a travel eSIM can silently start billing Russian roaming rates, or simply drop, in those pockets. If your itinerary reaches Narva, Valga (the Latvia side is fine, but the border zone is busy) or the far southeast, switch Network Selection to **manual** and force Telia/Elisa/Tele2 so the phone cannot camp on a Russian tower. This is the one place in Estonia where carrier choice and manual selection genuinely protect your wallet.

## Manual APN entry

You only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM **directly from an Estonian carrier** and it did not auto-configure. Travel eSIMs supply their own APN — leave them alone.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Telia (EMT) | `internet.emt.ee` | blank | blank |
| Elisa | `internet` | blank | blank |
| Tele2 | `internet.tele2.ee` | blank | blank |

- **iPhone:** Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → enter the APN.
- **Android:** Settings → Network & Internet → Mobile Network → Access Point Names → add a new APN.

Save and reboot. If data still fails, confirm the eSIM — not your home SIM — is the selected data line before changing anything else.

## Estonia eSIM registration: passport, TTJA scan and payment before the counter

Because the 2024 rule puts a Telia, Elisa or Tele2 clerk between you and any local profile, the packing list starts with the documents that survive a TTJA scan — and with a realistic plan for the hours when no counter is open.

- **EID** — dial `*#06#` to surface it. A travel eSIM wants the number before it ships; a local profile is only issued to you once the TTJA scan is done, so you cannot collect one in advance.
- **Passport or EU ID card, the original** — the 2024 law reads the actual document in person. A photocopy, a phone photo and a driving licence are all turned away at the Esindus.
- **A card that clears outside its home region** — Estonian operator terminals sometimes decline non-EU billing. Apple Pay, Google Pay or euro cash at the counter saves you the errand.
- **Install any travel eSIM before you fly** — there is no arrive-and-buy path that skips the passport step, and Tallinn's counters keep closed hours at the weekend.
- **Leave your home line switched on** — Bolt, parking meters and guesthouse bookings text codes to your normal number, which a data-only eSIM cannot receive.
- **Switch Data Roaming on for the eSIM** — odd advice inside the EU, but without it the profile sits at "No Service"; see the setup list below.
- **A weekday arrival if a +372 number matters** — Telia's nearest full Esindus is at Ülemiste rather than the terminal, and Elisa's R-Kiosk counter inside the airport is the one landside option that reliably opens at weekends.
- **The exact spelling your passport prints** — the TTJA record is tied to that name, and a mismatch surfaces later as a failed identity check at the operator rather than at the till.
- **A note of which document the line went on** — losing the handset means a replacement SIM re-registered against the same passport, so keep the ID page details somewhere you can read them offline.

## Where an Estonian eSIM actually breaks

A QR code that will not scan, a profile stalled mid-install, a phone with no line at all — none of that is an Estonian fault. Those belong to the generic [eSIM error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/), and the ground-up walkthrough sits in [how to activate an eSIM](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). What follows are the four failures this country produces on its own.

### What forms come with a Estonia SIM?

If your Telia, Elisa or Tele2 profile came from a counter, it only goes live once the clerk finishes the TTJA registration scan against your passport. A registration that was started but never transmitted leaves the eSIM parked at "No Service", while the QR code itself looks perfectly healthy — which is why this fault is so easily misread as a handset problem.

The fix is administrative rather than a setting. Return to the same Esindus with the same document and ask staff to confirm the registration was sent; some branches complete it later the same working day.

### Who has the widest reach in Estonia?

Close to the Russian border a handset will happily camp on Megafon, MTS or Beeline, because those masts sit nearer and often transmit louder than Telia's. Data then either flatlines or, worse, starts billing non-EU roaming with no warning on screen.

Pin the network by hand: Settings → Cellular → Network Selection, switch off automatic and choose Telia, Elisa or Tele2. Telia holds best along the Narva river and through the southeastern villages, and manual selection stops the phone drifting across the water on its own.

### Does 10 GB last for Estonia?

Estonian prepaid plans are frugal with Roam Like at Home. Elisa and Tele2 core bundles exclude EU roaming outright, and even Telia's 200 GB pass concedes only 23 GB beyond the border. Sail to Finland on a local prepaid and you meet that ceiling, not a coverage gap.

Read the plan's EU allowance before you board the ferry, or run the crossing on a travel eSIM whose EU/EEA bucket is stated plainly at checkout.

### Is your device approved for Estonia eSIMs?

An install that loops near the end is frequently a VPN problem, and Estonia sees more than its share because so many visitors arrive tunnelled through a work profile. Kill the VPN, close its app, and restart the install on hotel Wi-Fi before you touch any other setting.

A travel eSIM leaves no record with any of them, so keep your confirmation email, your EID (`*#06#`) and a screenshot for your own provider instead.

Yes, and it is the sensible setup. Keep your home number on the physical SIM for calls and SMS verification — Estonia's Bolt, parking apps and many bookings send codes to it — and run data on the eSIM. Just switch Data Roaming off on the home line so you are not billed for background traffic.

Yes, specifically for billing. Within a few km of the Narva and Setomaa frontier your phone can camp on a Russian network outside the EU. Set Network Selection to manual and pick Telia/Elisa/Tele2, and never answer or dial on a connection that shows a Russian operator.

### Is Tallinn Airport Wi-Fi enough to get by?

### Which Estonia carrier should you pick: Telia or Elisa?

Ask the carrier it is locked to; many will unlock on request. On iPhone check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. A locked phone cannot install any third-party eSIM until it is unlocked.

### Which companies run Estonia's networks?
