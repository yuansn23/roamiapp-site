## What usually goes wrong with your eSIM

Start with the failure mode, because it is genuinely different from the rest of the world.

In most countries a travel eSIM works by roaming: your profile hops onto a local operator through a wholesale agreement, and that is the end of the story.

### Why do foreign eSIM profiles fail to attach in Iran

Iranian mobile networks have unusually limited international roaming arrangements.

The practical consequence — widely reported by travelers and resellers alike — is that eSIM profiles issued by many foreign brands either cannot find a partner network in Iran or connect so intermittently that they cannot be relied on for maps, translation or booking anything.

The failure looks like a coverage problem, but it is not one. Locals on the same street have full bars while your imported eSIM profile sits idle.

### Can you buy an Iran eSIM before you fly?

The second failure mode hits before you even land.

Some international storefronts happily sell an Iran eSIM plan to any compatible phone, then leave you to discover on arrival that the profile will not register on a local network.

There is no in-country support desk for a foreign brand's eSIM, and the activation help you would get in Turkey or the UAE does not exist here. If your itinerary depends on having data from the first hour, plan for the local option, not the imported one.

## Why international payment cards fail inside Iran

Whatever eSIM or physical tourist line you buy, you will eventually need to pay for it, and the payment rails are the part of Iranian travel that surprises people most.

### Which cards are refused at Iranian counters

Sanctions-related restrictions mean Visa, Mastercard and most foreign-issued cards are not processed by Iranian merchants, ATMs or top-up systems — this includes the card in your Apple Pay or Google Wallet.

There is no workaround at the point of sale. The entire card economy inside Iran runs on domestic networks, which is also why an eSIM you cannot top up is worse than no eSIM at all.

### MCI vs Irancell: which is better in Iran?

Cash is what works.

Travelers bring euros or US dollars in banknotes and exchange them for Iranian rials at licensed exchange offices (locally, "sarafi"), which are widespread in cities and at the airport.

The sums involved are modest by European standards — local mobile data is inexpensive, which is the one point in your favor — but every transaction you make with an operator settles in cash.

That includes the tourist eSIM itself and the recharge voucher that keeps it alive. In-app recharges that feel automatic elsewhere assume a local bank card, so plan for top-ups as cash purchases at a shop or counter.

Three money details are worth settling before departure:

- Exchange more than you think you need, because card-less travel punishes underestimation.
- Keep small-denomination notes: change is awkward and prices are quoted in both rials and tomans (1 toman = 10 rials), which trips up newcomers daily.
- Photograph the receipt for your SIM purchase — the registration details on it matter if the line needs attention later.

## The Iran eSIM carriers: MCI, Irancell and Rightel

Iran has three national mobile operators, all licensed and regulated by the country's Communications Regulatory Authority.

Their differences matter less in Tehran, where all three are strong, and more the farther your route strays from it. All three sell tourist products at the airport, and support for a direct carrier-issued eSIM to short-term visitors is newer and less uniform, so confirm the format at the counter or with your reseller before you commit.

### What does MCI (Hamrah-e Aval) offer a visitor

MCI is the oldest and largest operator, tracing back to 1992, and its network is the usual recommendation for itineraries that leave the big cities.

**What it offers:** national tourist packages sold at the airport and in city stores, with the widest reach of the three operators.

**Its network strength:** coverage is dense across Tehran, Mashhad, Isfahan, Shiraz and the towns between them. It is the operator most often cited for holding signal on intercity highways and in provincial centers.

If your trip is the classic Tehran–Isfahan–Shiraz loop, an MCI-attached data line is the conservative choice.

**Its weakness:** carrier-issued eSIM support for short stays is the least predictable part of its product range, so expect a physical card as the default at some counters.

### What does Irancell offer a visitor

Irancell — part of the MTN group — is the operator most visitors end up with, largely because its visitor eSIM and SIM product has existed the longest and its support handles English and tourist questions routinely.

**What it offers:** straightforward packages that are easy to understand and to top up, plus airport booths that are usually the busiest and best staffed of the three.

**Its network strength:** a strong 4G footprint in all major cities and most secondary ones, which covers the standard tourist circuit comfortably.

**Its weakness:** between the two giants the practical gap is small, so the convenience premium does not buy measurably better coverage on provincial roads.

### What does Rightel offer a visitor

Rightel is the smallest of the three, with a footprint that concentrates on major urban centers.

**What it offers:** an urban-first product range aimed at city users rather than road-trippers, and the least developed visitor eSIM offering of the three.

**Its network strength:** in a Tehran-centric stay it is a reasonable pick, because the capital is where its network is deepest.

**Its weakness:** on provincial roads and in smaller towns it is the network most likely to hand you to nothing — which, in a country where foreign eSIM profiles may not attach at all, is a real consideration rather than a nitpick.

| Including Mashhad or the northwest | MCI | Widest reach beyond the central corridor |

## Registering a SIM: What to Expect

Two registration systems operate in Iran, and they are different things that get confused constantly.

### Is ID required to buy a SIM in Iran?

Every Iranian SIM card must be registered to a person, and for visitors that means a passport, checked at the point of sale.

This has tightened over recent years, and the practical effect is simple: no legitimate counter will sell you a working line without taking your passport details.

The registration is done for you as part of the purchase — there is nothing extra to file. It applies equally whether you walk out with a physical card or a locally issued eSIM profile, so an eSIM carries exactly the same obligation as a plastic SIM.

### Is there SIM registration in Iran?

The second system covers devices rather than lines.

Since the National Mobile Registry scheme took effect, phones brought into the country are recorded by their IMEI — the 15-digit hardware identifier you can see by dialing `*#06#`.

Travelers are exempt from full registration, but the commonly applied rule is that a foreign phone can use an Iranian SIM for **around 30 days** before it is disconnected from local networks.

As travel resources such as [SURFIRAN's registration guide](https://surfiran.com/mag/do-you-need-to-register-your-phone-in-iran/) describe, longer stays require registering the device with Iranian customs and paying an import-related fee that scales with the phone's model and value — often a substantial sum for recent flagship phones.

Enforcement practice shifts, so verify the current requirement before a long stay. Three practical takeaways:

- Your phone is not modified or harmed: it works normally abroad, and the restriction applies only to local SIM service.
- A phone that goes silent on day 31 still works on Wi-Fi, and can still share a hotspot from another device.
- If your trip might stretch past a month, register the device early at the airport customs process rather than discovering the deadline mid-trip.

The exemption follows the device rather than the SIM card, so swapping lines does not reset the clock, and dual-SIM workarounds people suggest online are unreliable and change as the rules are enforced.

Iran's urban connectivity is better than most first-time visitors expect.

The country's national statistics explain why: DataReportal's [Digital 2025: Iran report](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-iran) counted **152 million mobile connections** — about **166% of the population** — with roughly 93% of those connections on 3G, 4G or 5G networks. This is a country built around mobile internet.

### Where to find a Iran eSIM

One band-level check applies regardless of operator: Iranian networks lean on LTE band 20 (800 MHz) for reach outside city cores, and some handsets sold in certain regional markets lack it.

A phone without band 20 still works in cities but thins out fast elsewhere. Verify your exact model against the [eSIM compatibility checker](/compatibility/) before you buy, whether your line arrives as a tourist SIM or an eSIM profile.

## Iran intercity coverage: the Tehran–Isfahan–Shiraz loop

The route most first-time visitors actually run — Tehran down to Isfahan and on to Shiraz, with Yazd as the classic side trip — is where connectivity stops being something you assume and becomes something you check.

### Iran coverage: who does it best?

Coverage follows the settlements: strong in Tehran, Isfahan, Shiraz and Yazd themselves, present in the towns strung along the highways, and absent for long stretches of open road between them.

Expect to be out of contact for stretches at a time on the emptier intercity sections.

Shared taxis and buses between the cities pass through covered towns regularly, so the reality is intermittent contact rather than isolation. The stretches of silence are still real and predictable, and no eSIM plan changes that.

### The intercity legs: what to load offline before you leave Tehran

Plan the drive the way you plan fuel.

Download the full route offline before leaving the capital, including the segments between towns, because navigation that loses its map tiles in an area with no signal cannot recover them.

Tell your hotel or host your planned arrival window — guesthouses on the tourist routes are used to it — and message people when you regain signal on the approach to a town.

The historic sights themselves — the squares and mosques of Isfahan, the gardens of Shiraz, the old town of Yazd — sit inside coverage. The gaps are on the open road between cities, not at the sights, and no travel eSIM or spare SIM card changes that math where there are no towers to attach to.
## Iran trip logistics: money, apps and connectivity before departure

Some widely used international apps and services may be unavailable or unreliable on Iranian networks.

Keep your home line installed with data roaming switched off. A tourist line in Iran handles data and local calls; your banking SMS codes arrive on your home number.

## Working APN entries

Access point settings rarely need attention, but in a market where device support is uneven they are worth understanding before you arrive.

Most travelers never open these menus at all, and the ones who do are usually reacting to a symptom rather than a cause.

### APN values for MCI, Irancell and Rightel eSIMs

An APN, or Access Point Name, is the address your phone gives the network when it opens a data session. It decides which service the operator connects you to and how that session is treated.

Get it wrong and the symptom is deceptive: full signal bars, no internet. A local line that configured itself correctly needs no edits at all.

### Iran tourist SIM: when to enter APN settings by hand

Rarely, and only in specific situations.

A locally issued tourist line in Iran should configure itself on a modern handset, but confirm that at the counter before you leave — device support is uneven here, and asking beats debugging in a hotel room. Profiles you install yourself from a travel eSIM come pre-configured.

Manual entry becomes relevant when an older handset refuses pushed operator settings, when a physical SIM was handed over without configuration, or when an eSIM installed from a typed activation code never received its settings.

In those cases, take the values from the operator's own material — the SIM pack leaflet, the operator's support pages, or the activation email from your reseller. Values differ between operators and product lines, so they must come from the source that issued your line, and our [connection troubleshooting steps](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/) cover what to check if the field is not the problem.

### Iran APN cheat sheet

Go to **Settings → Cellular**, select the Iranian eSIM line, then open **Cellular Data Network**. The APN field is the first entry on that screen.

If that menu does not appear at all, the eSIM profile has supplied its own settings and there is nothing to type.

### When a Iran APN must be entered manually

The route depends on the manufacturer, but it normally runs **Settings → Connections → Mobile networks → Access Point Names**, where you either edit the existing entry or add a new one and select it.

Save, restart the phone, and test the connection while the counter staff or your hotel host is still nearby to help.

## Iran carrier eSIM questions and answers

### Iran local SIM and travel eSIM compared

You can buy one, but you should not depend on it. Many internationally issued eSIM profiles fail to attach to Iranian networks, or attach too intermittently to rely on, because of the country's limited roaming arrangements.

The dependable option is a tourist product issued inside Iran by MCI, Irancell or Rightel, bought at IKA airport or through a reseller — and that applies whether the product arrives as a physical card or as a locally provisioned eSIM.

### Can you skip the passport at Iran SIM shops?

Yes. Every Iranian SIM card must be registered to an individual, and for visitors the passport is the identifying document.

The counter staff handle the registration as part of the purchase, which takes only minutes, and it covers the eSIM format the same way as a physical card.

### Are Android phones OK for Iran eSIMs?

Commonly, a foreign phone used with an Iranian SIM gets about 30 days of service without device registration; after that it is disconnected from local networks.

Staying longer means registering the device's IMEI with Iranian customs and paying a fee based on the phone's value. The phone itself is unaffected — it works normally once you leave the country, and an eSIM profile you used for the trip leaves nothing behind to deregister.

### Which Iran carrier should you pick: MCI or Irancell?

For the standard Tehran–Isfahan–Shiraz route, MCI and Irancell are both safe choices; MCI edges ahead for provincial sections, while Irancell has the most established visitor support. Rightel makes sense mainly for Tehran-centric trips.

Whichever counter you use, the product you walk away with is registered the same way and topped up the same way.

### How do I pay for my tourist SIM and top-ups?

In cash, in rials. Foreign-issued bank cards do not work with Iranian merchants or top-up systems, so bring euros or dollars to exchange on arrival.

Airport counters and city stores sell recharge vouchers, and the amounts involved are small because local data is inexpensive.

### Can I keep my home SIM active while I am there?

Yes, and you should, for banking codes and calls. Install the tourist line alongside your home SIM, point mobile data at the local eSIM, and switch data roaming off on your home SIM so it never bills you for background traffic.

A dual-eSIM phone can hold the tourist profile and your home line side by side with no tray swap. Check before flying whether your bank's app will operate during the trip at all, and set up alternatives where it will not.

### Your Iran eSIM, matched to the trip

Less than most visitors expect, because Iranian mobile data is inexpensive and city coverage is dense.

Maps, messaging and translation apps on a two-week city-to-city itinerary typically come in well under 2 GB a day, and a package sold at the airport usually covers about a month of validity — so the constraint is more often the package window than the volume. Hotspotting a laptop is the one thing that shifts the calculation upward.

### Is a locally issued eSIM better than a physical tourist SIM in Iran?

They are registered the same way and paid for the same way, so the choice is mechanical rather than commercial.

A physical card works in any unlocked phone, including one with no eSIM support. A locally provisioned eSIM suits phones without a SIM tray, or anyone who wants to leave a physical home SIM in place — provided the issuer supports your device, which is worth confirming at the counter.

### Does your model pass Iran's checks?

Yes, and it is the sensible setup. Keep your home SIM in the tray for calls and verification codes, and run data on the Iranian eSIM.

Two things to watch: point mobile data explicitly at the Iranian line, and leave data roaming off on the home line so background traffic does not trigger charges from your own operator.

### Locating your phone's APN screen for Iran

Normally no. A locally issued tourist line configures itself on a modern handset, and a travel eSIM you install arrives with its access point details already inside the profile.

Only older handsets, unconfigured physical SIMs or typed activation codes push you into manual entry, and the section above lists where the field sits on both platforms.

### What should I do if my Iranian line stops working?

Work through three checks in order: confirm the Iranian eSIM is the selected data line and that roaming is enabled for it; restart the phone so it re-attaches to the network; then, if the line has been active for roughly a month, treat it as the device registration deadline rather than a fault.

If none of that restores data, the counter or store that issued the line is the right place to take it, because the registration sits in your name there.
