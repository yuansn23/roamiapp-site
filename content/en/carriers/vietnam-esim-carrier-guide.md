### Viettel vs Vinaphone: which is better in Vietnam?



Viettel is the state-defence operator, and its advantage is geography rather than peak speed. It took Best Mobile Network, Fastest Mobile Network, Most Consistent Network and Best 5G Availability in Ookla's 1H 2025 Vietnam report in the same collection period.



- **Measured position:** median download of 165.03 Mbps across all technologies, against 78.69 Mbps for Vinaphone and 68.45 Mbps for Mobifone. Consistency of 97.4% — the share of samples clearing a 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up threshold. Speedtest Connectivity Score of 83.17.

- **Where it wins:** fastest provider in 59 of 63 regions and nine of the ten most populous cities, across the whole spine of the country — Lào Cai at 85.92 Mbps, Hà Giang at 85.88 Mbps and Cà Mau at 139.66 Mbps.

- **5G:** first to launch commercial 5G, on 15 October 2024. By November 2025 it had built about 7,000 5G base stations across all 34 provinces and cities, with a target of more than 20,000 by the end of that year. It reports 12.6 million active 5G users, the largest 5G base in Vietnam.

- **What goes wrong:** the strongest network still hands out the weakest 5G experience in the tourist package range — 50.4% 5G availability means roughly half your time on Viettel is spent on 4G, which in Vietnam is genuinely fast but not what the sticker promises.

- **Scenario:** the Hà Giang loop. Hà Giang province measured 85.88 Mbps with Viettel as the fastest provider, which covers the towns and the main viewpoints on the loop. The extreme passes between Đồng Văn and Mèo Vạc are where the bars disappear, so download offline maps before you leave Hà Giang city.



### Viettel vs Vinaphone 5G: which is better in Vietnam?



VNPT's mobile arm is the second network and the one that measures fastest on 5G. It won Fastest 5G Network in Ookla's 1H 2025 Vietnam report, and took Ookla's Best 5G Network award for Southeast Asia in 2H 2025 — ahead of Singtel, Unifi and AIS — judged on Speedtest Connectivity Score.



- **Measured position:** median 5G download of 594.16 Mbps, median 5G upload of 55.37 Mbps and 30 ms latency over 5G. Speedtest users also rated it the top provider in Vietnam, at 4.08 out of 5.

- **Where it wins:** dense urban cells and tourist zones. Vinaphone switched on commercial 5G in December 2024 and prioritised administrative centres, industrial parks, airports, seaports and tourism areas — which is close to a map of where visitors actually spend their days.

- **Where it loses:** the same Ookla period put its all-technology median download at 78.69 Mbps, less than half Viettel's, and Viettel was the fastest provider in 59 of 63 regions. Vinaphone's 5G lead evaporates the moment you leave the 5G footprint.

- **What goes wrong:** tourists buy Vinaphone for a mountain trip and discover the 5G they paid for is a city product. If your route is a loop rather than a stay, this is the wrong network.

- **Scenario:** Hanoi. The city recorded a 119.52 Mbps median mobile download, and Vinaphone's 5G covers the central districts and Nội Bài. Inside the Old Quarter the constraint is congestion from narrow streets packed with users, not distance to a mast — a slower speedtest there is network loading, not a fault.



### Rural coverage in Vietnam: Viettel vs Vinaphone



Mobifone is the third operator and the last to launch 5G, switching on commercial service on 23 June 2025 — eight months after Viettel. It concentrates on major urban centres, which is exactly where its strength shows.



- **Measured position:** the best mobile video experience in Vietnam, a Video Streaming Score of 74.46, plus a median 5G download of 306.5 Mbps and an all-technology median of 68.45 Mbps.

- **Where it wins:** streaming. If your evenings are spent on hotel Wi-Fi-free video, Mobifone's video score is the highest of the three, and Da Nang — 354.94 Mbps in Ookla's city table and the strongest beach-city network in the country — is where it has the most headroom.

- **Where it loses:** everything outside the cities. As the smallest of the three it has the least rural fallback, so the Mekong Delta, the far north and the islands are Viettel's ground.

- **What goes wrong:** the "Happy Tourist" plans are cheap and the registration is the same passport process as everyone else's, so the savings are real; the risk is coverage, not paperwork.

- **Scenario:** Hội An. Quảng Nam province measured 169.24 Mbps with Viettel fastest, and the old town and beach strip are well covered on all three. Mobifone is a reasonable choice for a Hội An-and-Da Nang stay; it is not a reasonable choice for the ride between them and the delta.

## 5G nationwide: Viettel, Vinaphone and Mobifone 5G coverage compared



Vietnam moved from pilot projects to full commercial 5G in under a year, and the sequence matters for travelers:



- **15 October 2024** — Viettel became the first operator to launch commercial 5G, and within weeks claimed 5G in the centers of all 63 provinces and cities.

- **December 2024** — VNPT switched on Vinaphone's commercial 5G, prioritizing administrative centers, industrial parks, airports, seaports and tourist zones.

- **23 June 2025** — Mobifone entered the market, initially concentrating on major urban centers.

- **By December 2025** — the Ministry of Science and Technology reported commercial 5G coverage at **91.2% of the population**, one of the fastest national rollouts on record.



Two caveats keep expectations honest. First, "coverage of provincial centers" is not the same as coverage of rural roads — in early 2025 Viettel operated about 6,500 5G base stations against a far larger 4G estate, so outside cities you will mostly ride 4G, which in Vietnam is very good. Second, Ookla's 1H 2025 data shows Viettel users spent a majority of time on 5G only **50.4%** of the time — plenty of 4G fallback even in the launch year.



The build-out has not stood still since. By November 2025 Viettel had completed roughly **7,000 5G base stations across all 34 provinces and cities**, with a stated target of more than **20,000** by the end of that year and 99% population coverage by 2030; it reports **12.6 million active 5G users**, the largest 5G base in the country. For a traveler, the summary is simple: 5G in the cities and tourist hubs on all three carriers, excellent 4G everywhere else, and Viettel's 5G footprint ahead of the other two in the provinces.



## How do you buy a local eSIM as a tourist?



Every SIM in Vietnam — physical or eSIM — must be registered to a verified identity. That rule comes from Decree 49/2017/ND-CP and subsequent cybersecurity regulations, and it is enforced, not optional: since 2018 carriers have been required to deactivate unregistered numbers. For a foreign visitor, this means your **physical passport** — copies and phone photos are not accepted — plus, for self-service eSIM registration, a live selfie matched to your passport page.

Viettel has the most tourist-friendly eSIM path of the three. Official red-and-white Viettel counters sit in the arrivals halls of Noi Bai (Hanoi), Tan Son Nhat (Ho Chi Minh City), Da Nang and Cam Ranh (Nha Trang) airports, and in Viettel stores nationwide. Registration works one of two ways: the counter staff photocopy your passport and register the line for you (5–15 minutes), or you self-register by scanning the QR code on the SIM packaging, photographing your passport and taking a live selfie, then electronically signing the digital contract. Viettel tourist eSIMs come with a real Vietnamese phone number, so you can receive SMS verification codes — genuinely useful, since Grab, food delivery and many hotel bookings are phone-number-driven in Vietnam.



### The Vietnam SIM counter visit, step by step



The airport route is the one most visitors take, so it is worth knowing the sequence before you join it:



1. Find the Viettel booth in the arrivals hall — Noi Bai, Tan Son Nhat, Da Nang or Cam Ranh. Red-and-white branding, staff in uniform, a queue.

2. Hand over your **physical passport**. Photocopies, screenshots and photos on your phone are refused; this is the whole point of Decree 49/2017.

3. Choose a tier from the table above. The 15-day 5G80T carries 5 GB per day, so it is 75 GB across the trip for 80,000 VND — the arithmetic is the appeal, not the headline price.

4. The staff register the line against your passport and issue an eSIM QR code, or a physical SIM if your phone is not eSIM-capable.

5. Scan the QR while you are still in the terminal, using the airport's Wi-Fi if your home roaming is already off.

6. Keep the receipt and the SIM packaging. If the line dies in the next two days you will need both to prove when and where it was registered.



Budget 5–15 minutes if the queue is short, and considerably longer when two or three wide-body arrivals land together in the late morning.



### Is my phone eSIM-compatible in Vietnam?



Vinaphone sells tourist SIMs and eSIMs through its airport counters and authorized resellers, typically from around 200,000 VND for multi-week packages with daily data allowances plus on-network calling. Mobifone's tourist products ("Happy Tourist" plans) sit in a similar range, roughly 220,000–280,000 VND for 30 days. All three carriers now sell eSIMs through their websites and apps, but international travelers are still routed through identity verification — some online purchases require completing verification at a store or kiosk after arrival.



The reseller layer — airport kiosks, city phone shops, guesthouse front desks — sells the same three networks, and it does not remove the paperwork. Someone still has to photograph your passport and complete the registration, because the carrier is legally liable for the line. This is why a reseller eSIM promising "registration included, no passport" is the single most likely product in Vietnam to go dead on day two.



### Vietnam SIM registration: Decree 49/2017 in practice



The regulation is easy to state and easy to underestimate. Three things follow from it that affect an ordinary two-week trip:



- **You will hand over your passport, not a copy.** Counter staff photocopy it, or the eSIM self-registration flow photographs the data page and takes a live selfie that is matched to it.

- **A failed registration is invisible at first.** The line activates, works for the evening, and is deactivated within 24–48 hours once the registration check fails — typically because a passport photo came back blurry, a name did not transcribe cleanly, or a street seller never submitted the paperwork at all.

- **The fix is at the counter, not on your phone.** If a local SIM stops working after a day and the phone still shows bars, the registration did not complete. Return to the issuing counter with your passport and the packaging rather than resetting network settings.

## Will your phone work on a Vietnamese network?



Vietnamese 4G networks use LTE bands 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 28, 40 and 41; 5G deployments center on n78 (3.5 GHz), with n28 (700 MHz) and n41 (2.6 GHz) also in use. Any mainstream smartphone sold in the last five years — iPhone XS and later, Pixel 3 and later, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later — covers these bands. The trap to check is not bands but two simpler things:



- **Carrier lock.** A phone locked to your home carrier will install the eSIM profile but refuse to connect to Viettel, Vinaphone or anyone else. Check at Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock (iPhone) or ask your home carrier; get it unlocked before you fly.

- **eSIM support.** Dial `*#06#` — if an EID appears, your phone has an eSIM. Mainland-China iPhone models have eSIM hardware disabled and cannot use any eSIM.



Vietnam's carriers switched off 2G and 3G, so voice calls run over VoLTE — enable VoLTE for your eSIM line if the option exists. For the model-by-model list, the [eSIM device checker](/compatibility/) settles it in one check.



## Setting the APN



If you buy from a carrier directly and data refuses to flow despite full signal bars, a manual APN is the fix. These are the settings in use on Vietnam's networks:



| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |

|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| Viettel | `v-internet` | blank | blank |

| Vinaphone | `m3-world` | mms | mms |

| Mobifone | `m-wap` | mms | mms |



Enter them at **iPhone:** Settings → Cellular → your eSIM line → Cellular Data Network, or **Android:** Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names. On Android, set the APN type to `default,supl,dun` — the `dun` entry is what makes the personal hotspot work on some handsets. Save, then toggle airplane mode once to force the phone to re-register.



If you are on a travel eSIM, do not touch any of this: the profile carries its own provisioning, and editing it will break the connection. The only common exception is a carrier-issued eSIM whose automatic configuration did not run because the registration was still pending — which is the same failure that kills the line 24–48 hours later, so check the registration before you start editing APN fields.



## Activating a Vietnam eSIM without the drama



Install the profile **before you fly**. Airport Wi-Fi in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City works, but you do not want to be troubleshooting a QR code while the arrivals hall crowd does the same thing. The full install sequence — scan the QR code, label the line, set it as your data line, enable data roaming — is in the [eSIM setup guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). Three Vietnam-specific points:



1. **Data roaming must be ON for the eSIM line.** Travel eSIMs roam onto the local network by design; turning roaming off kills the connection while your home SIM stays safe because data routing points at the eSIM.

2. **Check the data line, not just the signal.** With dual SIM active, Vietnam eSIM users' most common failure is the phone silently sending traffic over the home SIM. Settings → Cellular → Mobile Data → select the eSIM.

3. **A line that dies on day two usually means registration failed.** If a carrier-bought SIM stops working after 24–48 hours, the passport registration did not complete — return to the counter where you bought it rather than troubleshooting the phone.

### Five Vietnam eSIM failure patterns and how to clear them



**A. The profile will not download**

1. Re-scan on a stable Wi-Fi network, not on a moving bus.

2. Confirm the QR has not already been scanned — most are single-use.

3. Confirm the phone is unlocked and that the vendor's activation window has not expired.



**B. Installed, but no bars at all**

1. Check that the Vietnam profile is enabled in Settings → Cellular.

2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → pick Viettel, Vinaphone or Mobifone manually instead of automatic.

3. Reboot, then reset network settings if the bars stay empty.



**C. Bars, but no internet**

1. Check the APN against the table above — `v-internet` on Viettel, `m3-world` on Vinaphone, `m-wap` on Mobifone.

2. Confirm the eSIM, not your home SIM, is the selected data line.

3. On a carrier plan, check the daily allowance has not already run out.



**D. "SOS" or "No Service" only**

1. Disable the other SIM or eSIM temporarily so the phone stops trying to camp on the wrong network.

2. Re-check that data roaming is on for the eSIM line.

3. Network settings reset, power-cycle, then test again.

4. As a last resort, delete the profile and reinstall from a fresh QR code.



**E. It worked yesterday and died today**

1. This is almost always a failed passport registration, not a phone fault. Look at the phone first: if bars are present but no data flows, the line itself has been suspended.

2. Return to the counter that issued it with your passport and the SIM packaging.

3. If you bought a reseller eSIM, ask the seller directly whether the registration was submitted — and be ready to switch to a pre-installed travel plan.



Everything else sits in the [eSIM problem-solving guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/): a profile that will not download, a scan that keeps failing, or a line that installs but never registers.

## Vietnam coverage on trains, sleeper buses and the Hai Van Pass



Vietnam is 1,600 km long and the way you move through it decides how much of the trip you spend with signal. The three north–south modes behave differently.



**The North–South railway.** The Reunification Express — locally the *Thống Nhất* — runs the 1,726 km between Hanoi and Saigon, with end-to-end trips of 32 to 36 hours depending on service. The SE1/SE2 and SE3/SE4 pairs are the newest rolling stock; odd numbers run southbound, even numbers northbound. Soft sleeper (four berths per cabin) costs roughly 1,750,000–2,400,000 VND, hard sleeper (six berths) about 1,300,000–1,650,000 VND, and a soft seat around 950,000–1,200,000 VND for the full run. There is **no on-board Wi-Fi**, which is precisely why a working eSIM matters more on the train than in any hotel.



Rail signal is good in the populated stretches and absent in the terrain between them. The dead zones are the tunnels and the sparse corridor between Đồng Hới and Quảng Ngãi, plus the mountain tunnels north of Hue. Buy a berth, keep the phone charged, and expect two or three multi-minute gaps over a full day of travel rather than continuous coverage.



**Sleeper buses.** Open-tour and sleeper buses cover the same corridor for less — a Hanoi–Ho Chi Minh City sleeper runs roughly 600,000–1,200,000 VND, with operators including Futabus and Hoang Long. Buses follow National Route 1 and the Hồ Chí Minh Road rather than the rail alignment, so coverage tracks the highway towns. The gaps are the mountain passes, the Hồ Chí Minh Road's western branch through the Central Highlands, and the tunnels on the expressway sections. Overnight departures mean you sleep through the worst of the dead zones anyway.



**The Hai Van Pass.** The 21 km pass climbs to 496 metres on National Route 1 between Da Nang and Hue, and since 2005 most traffic has bypassed it through the 6.28 km Hai Van Tunnel, which cuts 20 km and 30–60 minutes off the drive. Motorcycles are not permitted inside the tunnel, so a self-ride means the pass road — bends, grades, fog and exposure. On the rail side, the Da Nang–Hue leg takes about 2.5 to 3 hours and is the scenic highlight of the whole line, hugging cliffs above Lang Co Lagoon; heading north from Da Nang, the sea is on your right, and it reverses southbound. The pass road stays open year-round but is frequently fog-bound in rain, and the tunnel is the reliable option when visibility collapses. Coverage tracks the towns at either end rather than the ridge: the 6.28 km tunnel is a gap on every carrier, and the summit climb carries less signal than Da Nang or Hue.

## Vietnam load events: Tet, typhoons and network strain



Two calendar events change what a working connection is worth in Vietnam, and both are predictable enough to plan around.



### The companies behind Vietnam's networks



Tet 2027 falls on **6 February 2027**, with the public holiday running across the surrounding days; many businesses close before it and reopen after. The railway's Tet peak runs from **23 January to 25 February 2027** — roughly **300,000** long-distance tickets across **57 train services** and more than **800 carriages**, released at 8 a.m. on 5 September 2026. To stop hoarding, the operator capped each passenger at 10 outbound and 10 return tickets and raised peak-period cancellation fees to 30%.



For data, the network itself is not the problem — Vietnamese carriers handle Tet traffic. What changes is that every train, bus, hotel and domestic flight is full, so urban cells carry holiday demand on top of tourist demand, and a phone that measured 120 Mbps in Hanoi in March may measure a fraction of that at Tet. Book transport and accommodation by November or December, and treat your eSIM as the one part of the trip that needs no booking.



### Typhoon season in central Vietnam's provinces



Central Vietnam's typhoon window peaks from **mid-September to mid-November**, with landfall typically between Quảng Bình and Quảng Ngãi — which puts Hue, Da Nang and Hội An directly in the path. The recent record is specific: Typhoon Trami in October 2024 dropped more than **600 mm** of rain on Hội An in 72 hours; Tropical Storm Bualoi in September 2025 closed Da Nang International Airport for close to 48 hours and cancelled more than **180 flights**; and the October–November 2025 floods put **825,638 customers** across Quảng Trị, Hue, Da Nang and Quảng Ngãi through a power outage at some point, with **475,874** off supply at the peak. Typhoon Kalmaegi made landfall on 6 November 2025 and left **330,506** customers without power, predominantly in Gia Lai and Đắk Lắk.



What that means for the phone in your pocket: a base station on backup power keeps serving when the local grid fails, but a flooded or landslid site is simply gone, and local outages typically last 24 to 72 hours. Plan the way the locals do — download offline maps, carry a power bank, keep cash because ATMs go down with the grid, and remember that the North–South railway keeps running when roads flood, which makes the train the most reliable way to move during a storm.

### Is passport verification mandatory for SIMs in Vietnam?



Yes, without exception. Under Decree 49/2017/ND-CP every SIM must be registered to verified identity, and carriers are legally required to deactivate unregistered lines. Bring your physical passport — scans and photos are rejected — and expect a live selfie during eSIM self-registration. Registration takes 5–15 minutes at an official counter.



### Where to source your Vietnam eSIM



Through resellers, yes — many sell Viettel-network tourist eSIMs that arrive by email as a QR code with no local paperwork. Buying from Viettel's own channels generally involves passport verification, either online or at a counter on arrival. ### Does your handset do eSIM in Vietnam?



Usually not — most travel eSIMs are data-only, so SMS verification codes (Grab, banking, hotels) go to your home number over your physical SIM, if roaming is enabled there for SMS. A carrier-bought Viettel or Vinaphone tourist eSIM does include a Vietnamese number. Keep your home SIM active for calls and codes, put data on the eSIM, and switch off data roaming on the home line to avoid surprise charges.



### The 5G picture in Vietnam



Yes — both have had commercial 5G since late 2024 from Viettel and Vinaphone, with Mobifone following in mid-2025. HCMC recorded the slowest median mobile speed (117.89 Mbps) of Vietnam's major cities in 1H 2025 — a congestion artifact of ~9 million people and 127 million national SIM connections.
