> **Four southern networks and the line that ends them**

>

> **Cyta, Epic and PrimeTel — and the line that splits the island**



Cyprus is the one European destination where the carrier question is not only "which network is fastest" but "**which side of the island are you on?**" The Republic of Cyprus in the south is served by Cyta, Epic (formerly MTN Cyprus), PrimeTel and Cablenet. The Turkish-administered north runs on completely separate Turkish operators — Turkcell KKTC and Telsim Vodafone — that a standard Cyprus eSIM does **not** include. Cross the Green Line and your southern eSIM simply stops, and your phone may quietly grab a northern signal. This guide answers which southern carrier your eSIM should ride, how to buy as a visitor, and how to handle the line.



**Quick answer:** Go with Cyta if coverage matters more than price, PrimeTel if you are staying around the main urban centres. First, confirm your phone actually supports eSIM — the [compatibility checker](/compatibility/) sorts it out in a minute.



A carrier is only as strong as the profile install behind it — if either part feels shaky, begin with [eSIM compatibility tool](/compatibility/) and [eSIM activation guide](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/).. Then come back for the network verdicts.



**Quick verdict:** A the local eSIM only covers the Republic. Staying in the south? **Cyta** has the widest reach into the Troodos mountains; **Epic** (the old MTN) is repeatedly rated fastest by Ookla; **PrimeTel** is the urban-coastal value pick; **Cablenet** bundles mobile with its cable TV/internet. All four cover the Republic only. Heading north to Kyrenia or Famagusta? Your southern eSIM goes dark — plan for it. A travel eSIM on the southern networks means no passport queue and automatic EU roam-like-home on some carriers.

## Before you buy a local eSIM: documents, euros, border reality

Cablenet is a cable operator that became a full mobile network. It is worth considering mainly if you already take its home internet, since bundled discounts appear. Mobile prepaid uses the APN `cablenet`.



**How a visitor buys Cablenet:**

1. Cablenet stores or online; physical ID required.

2. Choose a mobile line, often pitched alongside a home-broadband bundle.



**Quirk:** Coverage is best where its cable footprint runs, and EU roaming on Cablenet prepaid is more limited than on Cyta/Epic — fine if you stay in the Republic, weaker if you roam onward in the EU.

## The Green Line playbook for your your eSIM



This is the uniquely Cypriot trap for any Cyprus eSIM, and it bites near the buffer zone — most sharply in **central Nicosia**, where the line runs through the city.



- Your phone seeks the strongest signal. Near the line it may lock onto a **northern Turkish network** even while you stand in the south.

- If that happens: your southern eSIM data stops (it doesn't cover northern masts), **and** your home line, if its roaming is on, can bill Turkish roaming.

- **Fix:** glance at the network name when near the line. If it shows a Turkish operator, go to Settings → Cellular → Network Selection, switch from automatic to manual, and pick **Cyta, Epic or PrimeTel**. Toggling airplane mode also forces a re-scan.

- **Prevent:** keep data roaming **off** on your home line the whole trip. That alone blocks any surprise northern charge.

- Away from the line — the coast, the resorts, the Troodos interior — none of this applies; the phone stays cleanly on a southern network.



## Cyprus eSIM coverage and the Green Line crossing points



If you do go north, knowing the crossing points saves time — and explains why your eSIM dies the moment you pass.



- **Ledra Street** (Nicosia) — pedestrian only, the most central crossing, open limited daytime hours. Walk through the heart of the divided city.

- **Ledra Palace** (Nicosia) — pedestrian only, just west of the old town; the main foot crossing for NGO/UN traffic and visitors.

- **Metehan / Agios Dometios** — the principal **vehicle** crossing on the western edge of Nicosia; this is where most rental cars and buses go through.

- **Astromeritis / Zod** — the western vehicle crossing toward Morphou and the north-west; quieter than Metehan.

- **Pyla** — a village that sits **inside the buffer zone**, reachable from both sides without a full crossing; a useful reminder that the line is not a single wall.



All of these are in the Republic's buffer zone, so your southern eSIM still works up to the checkpoint — then cuts out on the northern side, where only **Turkcell KKTC** and **Telsim Vodafone** operate. Either carry a separate eSIM/regional plan for the north, or plan to use Wi-Fi in Kyrenia and Famagusta.

On price, [Cable.co.uk values 1 GB of Cypriot mobile data at about USD 2.41](https://www.cable.co.uk/mobiles/worldwide-data-pricing/) — 176th of 237 markets against a USD 2.59 global average, i.e. right around the world norm.



For context, [DataReportal reports 1.29 million internet users in Cyprus (94.7% penetration)](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-cyprus) and 2.53 million mobile connections — about 186% of the population — at the start of 2025.

When manual entry is required:



- **iPhone:** enter it under Settings → Cellular → your eSIM line → Cellular Data Network

### Before you fly: a Cyprus eSIM covers the Republic, not the north



Install at home rather than at Larnaca or Paphos, where counters keep limited hours and arrival Wi-Fi is thin. Then be honest about scope: a Cyprus eSIM covers the **Republic of Cyprus only**. Plan the north from the start — either a Turkish/regional eSIM or a decision to live on Wi-Fi in Kyrenia and Famagusta. If your home line is EU/EEA-issued, roam-like-home may already cover the south and you can skip the local SIM.



### What phones can run a Cyprus eSIM?



The signature Cypriot fault. A handset prefers the strongest tower, and around central Nicosia the line runs through the city, so a Turkcell KKTC or Telsim mast can win even while you stand in the south. Your Republic eSIM data then stops, and an unguarded home line can bill Turkish roaming. Go to Settings → Cellular → Network Selection, switch from automatic to **manual**, and pick Cyta, Epic or PrimeTel. Toggling airplane mode forces the same re-scan in a hurry.



### APN values for Cyta, Epic Cyprus and PrimeTel eSIMs



Southern APNs are mostly `internet` (Cablenet is the exception, at `cablenet`), so a fresh prepaid line usually works untouched; when it does not, re-enter the value from the table above and reboot. If bars are strong and the page still hangs, the plan is probably spent — PrimeTel and Epic prepaid are data-capped rather than throttled, so the fix is a top-up, not a setting.



### Region-hopping on a Cyprus eSIM



EU fair-use rules let Cyta and Epic prepaid lines roam at domestic rates only while you use the line more at home than abroad. Spend a fortnight in Limassol on a Cypriot SIM and the operator can apply a surcharge or trim speeds. If you are in Cyprus for the long haul, budget for that rather than assuming roam-like-home is unlimited.



### Is your phone eSIM-ready for Cyprus?



PrimeTel eSIMs are tied to the device that installed them. Change handsets mid-trip and you need a fresh QR from a store — there is no remote reinstall. A travel eSIM reinstalls from its own portal, which is why it suits anyone likely to swap phones.



### Short answers on Cyprus eSIMs

| What they ask | Where it lives |

## Your questions answered



### Can you buy a Cyprus eSIM from Cyta?



Yes. Cyta runs arrivals kiosks at both **Larnaca (LCA)** and **Paphos (PFO)**, and the other southern operators sell through city stores. Airport counters keep limited hours though — a Cyprus eSIM bought from a travel provider before you fly is live the moment you land, with no counter visit.



### Is "MTN Cyprus" still a thing?



No — MTN Cyprus was acquired by Monaco Telecom and rebranded to **Epic**. Any guide naming MTN means Epic today. Airport signage and some older kiosks still carry the old branding, so check the logo before paying at a counter.



### Cyta vs Epic Cyprus: coverage compared



Cyta has the widest footprint, especially into the Troodos mountains and rural areas; Epic reaches 99%+ of the population and is repeatedly rated fastest by Ookla. PrimeTel and Cablenet are strong in towns and along the coast.



### Will my Cyprus eSIM work in the north?



No. The Turkish-administered north runs on Turkcell KKTC and Telsim Vodafone — separate networks a Republic eSIM doesn't include. Use Wi-Fi or a separate eSIM up there, and keep home-line roaming off near the Green Line.



### Is SIM registration mandatory in Cyprus?



Yes, for a local prepaid SIM — registration to an ID is required (EU law). A travel eSIM needs no passport at point of sale.



### What Cyprus carriers charge for roaming



Epic and Cyta prepaid lines include EU/EEA + UK roaming at domestic rates under the roam-like-home principle (subject to fair use). PrimeTel and Cablenet prepaid roaming is more limited, and the north side is entirely outside the EU scheme.



### Is 10 GB of data enough in Cyprus?



PrimeTel is typically the value leader in towns and on the coast (e.g. 60 GB PAYG ~€10); Epic's Traveller 20GB (~€20) and Cyta's eSIM 20GB (~€10) are strong too. Cable.co.uk prices local data at ≈ USD 2.41/GB, near the global average.



### Where exactly can I cross the Green Line?



On foot through **Ledra Street** or **Ledra Palace** in central Nicosia, or by vehicle at **Metehan/Agios Dometios** (western Nicosia) or **Astromeritis/Zod** (north-west). Pyla village sits in the buffer zone itself. Your southern eSIM works to the checkpoint, then stops.



### Is my EU home SIM enough in Cyprus?



Usually yes — Cyprus is in the EU, so roam-like-home gives you domestic rates within fair-use limits. UK, US and most other visitors will want a local or travel eSIM instead.



### Can a Cyprus eSIM and my home number both stay active?



Yes — keep your home line for calls/SMS, run data on the eSIM, and switch off data roaming on the home line to avoid any border-zone charge.



### Cyprus eSIM rapid answers



PrimeTel prepaid eSIMs are tied to the device, so a reinstall after a phone change needs a fresh QR from a store. A travel eSIM reinstalls remotely, which is why it suits travellers who swap phones.
