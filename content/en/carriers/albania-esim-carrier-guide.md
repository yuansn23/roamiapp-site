## Roaming and Border Crossings



Your home plan's generosity is measured in zones, and Albania sits outside the zone you paid for. Two consequences follow, and both change how you should budget for connectivity.



### Using a Albania eSIM abroad



The single most expensive mistake on an Albanian holiday is assuming your European allowance travels with you. It does not.



A plan that roams free across the EU, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein treats Albania as a third country. Out-of-zone charges can run to several euros per gigabyte — sometimes more, depending on your home operator's small print.



The practical rule: the EU part of your allowance is worth nothing on this leg, so buy an Albania eSIM or a local prepaid line. Either option costs a small fraction of what your home operator bills per megabyte.



### Cheapest data plans in Albania



The second surprise is how cheap local data is.



Cable.co.uk's global pricing survey puts 1 GB in Albania at roughly **USD 1.55**, placing it **134th of 237 markets** against a worldwide average of USD 2.59. That is cheaper than France, Germany and the United States, and cheaper than every EU country except Italy and a handful of micro-states.



Albania is effectively Europe's budget data market hiding outside the roaming zone — the reason an eSIM on a local network beats international roaming by an order of magnitude.

### What happened to ALBtelecom and Telekom Albania



In 2023, **ALBtelecom and One Telecommunications merged**, forming today's **One Albania**, majority-owned by Hungary's 4iG Group.



If you find an older guide recommending "Telekom Albania" or an "ALBtelecom tourist SIM," that company no longer exists — its towers belong to One. The sector is overseen by [AKEP](https://akep.al/), the Electronic and Postal Communications Authority, which licenses both operators.



### What does Vodafone Albania offer a visitor



Vodafone is the market leader and the network most local businesses assume you are on.



Its commercial 5G went live in **late November 2024**, and by mid-2025 the operator listed Tirana, Durrës, Fier, Gjirokastër and the main tourist zones among its 5G coverage. In day-to-day terms that means full bars in the Blloku district, fast uploads from the Butrint ruins, and reliable video calls from a Saranda café.



**Where it is strong:** coverage density along the coast and through the cities, plus an arrivals-hall kiosk that sells an eSIM directly — unusual in the Balkans, and a legitimate fallback if you arrive unprepared.



**Where it is weaker:** its tourist packs bundle roaming across **Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Kosovo and North Macedonia**. If your trip is Albania-only, you are paying for coverage you will not use.



### What does One Albania offer a visitor



One Albania is the network travelers underestimate.



It launched 5G the day before Vodafone did, and claims the largest declared 5G footprint by city count — Tirana, Durrës, Elbasan, Vlorë, Shkodër plus the big tourist destinations. Its 4G layer reaches about 98% of the population, historically the widest of any Albanian operator.



**Where it is strong:** price. Its tiered prepaid range starts smaller and cheaper than Vodafone's tourist packs, which suits a one-week city break where 40 GB is more than you can spend.



**Where it is weaker:** One charged a small extra registration fee on some local prepaid plans, and **its Tirana airport counter sold physical SIMs only, not eSIMs**, per a February 2026 price check. If your device has no SIM tray, that pushes you toward buying an eSIM online before departure.

### Which network covers Albania best?



"Coastal coverage is good" hides real variation, so it is worth being specific.



The towns — Vlorë, Dhërmi, Himarë, Qeparo, Borsh, Sarandë — each hold a strong 4G footprint, and 5G now blankets the larger ones. Between them, the road pinches against cliffs and the signal drops into single bars for a few minutes at a time.



Beaches on south-facing peninsulas, the ones boats shuttle you to, sit outside the microwave paths and can be genuinely dark. If a day trip promises a "hidden beach," assume your eSIM will be offline there and tell someone where you are going.



None of this is a reason to carry a second device — it is a reason to load offline maps at breakfast, when the signal in town is at its best.



### Albania coverage by region



The Theth–Valbona pairing is the classic example of an intermittent zone.



Both village hubs have service, guesthouses offer Wi-Fi, and then the trail between them — or any detour into a side valley — goes dark. That is normal mountain behavior, not a defect of your eSIM: it connects to the same towers as a local SIM, and there are no towers mid-valley.



Send anything urgent while you are in the village, and treat the walk itself as offline time.



### Peaks of the Balkans coverage: what hikers should assume



The long-distance route deserves its own planning note, because it is the one Albania itinerary where connectivity is genuinely optional rather than assumed.



Hikers on the Peaks of the Balkans route should assume whole days without data between settlements and share itineraries with their guesthouse the night before.



The trail's own route information puts the loop at 192 km through Albania, Kosovo and Montenegro, normally walked over about ten stages, and crossing those borders on foot requires a cross-border permit arranged well in advance rather than at the trailhead. No eSIM changes that math — carry a charged power bank and a paper copy of your itinerary instead.

## The APNs Albania carriers use



Access point settings are the last thing most travelers need to touch and the first thing worth ruling out when data misbehaves. Here is what the setting does, when you would ever type it in, and where the field lives on each phone.



### APN settings for Albania carriers



An APN, or Access Point Name, is the gateway your phone hands to the network when it asks for a data session. It tells the operator which service to connect you to and how the session should be billed.



Without a correct APN, your phone can show full signal bars and still load nothing at all. That is why an APN mismatch looks like an eSIM coverage problem but is not one.



### When does a travel profile need manual entry



Almost never, and that is the point of a managed product.



A travel eSIM arrives pre-provisioned: the profile carries the access point details, the phone applies them automatically, and the menu you are looking for stays hidden or greyed out. If data works when you land, leave those settings alone.



Manual entry becomes relevant in three situations:



- You bought a **physical prepaid SIM** locally and the counter did not configure it for you.

- You are on an **older handset** that does not accept pushed operator settings.

- The eSIM installed from a **manually typed activation code** rather than a QR scan, and the operator settings never arrived.



In each case, look the values up from the operator itself — the leaflet that came with a local SIM, the operator's own support pages, or the activation email if you bought through a reseller. Do not copy access point strings from a forum post, because they change between operator and product line.



### When a Albania APN must be entered manually



Open **Settings → Cellular**, tap the eSIM line you want to configure, then **Cellular Data Network**. The APN field sits at the top of that screen.



Two things to check while you are there. Confirm you are editing the travel line and not your home line. And if the Cellular Data Network menu is missing entirely, the eSIM profile has supplied its own settings and there is nothing for you to enter.



### Where to find the APN screen on your phone in Albania



The path varies a little by manufacturer, but the shape is the same: **Settings → Connections → Mobile networks → Access Point Names**, then add a new entry or edit the existing one.



Save the entry, select it as the active access point, and restart the phone so the session re-establishes cleanly. If the menu is locked by your carrier, that is a sign the settings are managed and should not be changed.



Whatever you change, do it before you need the connection, not while you are standing outside trying to book a taxi.

1. Install the eSIM on Wi-Fi before departure and confirm the line is labeled and set as your **mobile data** line.

## Albania carrier eSIM FAQ



### Which network has the best 5G in Albania?



Casually, yes. Both networks launched commercial 5G in November 2024 and rolled it through the main cities and tourist zones during 2025, so in Tirana and along the Riviera you will often be on it by default. Outside the cities, 4G at Albanian speeds is already faster than most hotel Wi-Fi.



### Can you keep your home number active alongside an Albania eSIM?



Yes, and you should. A travel eSIM is a data line, so your physical SIM stays on for calls and SMS verification while the eSIM handles internet.



Set the Albania eSIM as your data line, and switch off data roaming on your home SIM so background traffic does not bill you at out-of-zone rates. If your phone holds two eSIM profiles at once, even better — the spare can sit dormant as backup.



### The companies behind Albania's networks



For a typical coastal-and-cities itinerary, Vodafone has the edge in coverage density and speed. One is cheaper on mid-size packs and its network claims the widest 4G population coverage.



An eSIM that attaches to whichever is strongest locally makes the question moot for most trips, which is why the operator comparison matters more for someone buying a local prepaid SIM than for someone arriving prepared.



### Do you have to register a SIM in Albania?



Only if you buy a physical SIM in Albania — the law requires ID registration at purchase, which is why the airport counters ask for a passport. An eSIM bought online before travel requires no in-person registration at all.



### Local SIM vs travel eSIM in Albania



Yes, with a caveat. Vodafone's arrivals-hall kiosk sells eSIMs directly; One sells physical SIMs to tourists. It works, but budget 15–30 minutes in the queue and bring cash in lek, because card payment at the counters is unreliable.



### What about the Corfu ferry and other island arrivals?



Ferries from Corfu dock in Sarandë, where the port has official carrier stores within a short walk. Skip anyone selling SIM cards on the pier itself — the standard advice from recent arrivals is that pier prices run far above the official tourist packs, and the walk to a branded store is five minutes.



### Will an Albania-only eSIM work in Greece or Montenegro?



No. The eSIM attaches to Albanian networks only, so a day trip to Corfu or a drive into Montenegro needs its own coverage. Travelers whose route loops over the border usually come out ahead with a multi-country eSIM rather than two separate ones — check the [Europe regional option](/europe-esim/) before buying single-country data.



### Albania data plans explained



Most visitors use far less than the tourist packs imply. For a data-heavy day of maps, messaging and social uploads, count on 1–2 GB daily, and a lot less for everyone else.



An Albania eSIM of 10–20 GB covers a week comfortably; the 40–100 GB airport packs only make sense if you hotspot a laptop or stay three weeks or longer.



### Is my phone eSIM-compatible in Albania?



It depends on eSIM support, not on price. Physical SIMs work in any unlocked handset, while an eSIM needs a device with an embedded chip — broadly iPhone XR and later, Pixel 3 and later, and most Galaxy S20-and-up models.



If your phone predates that, buy a physical tourist SIM at the airport instead; you lose the pre-flight convenience but keep the local pricing.



### What happens when your Albania eSIM runs out of data?



It stops rather than bills you, in almost every case. Prepaid local packs and travel eSIMs are sold as fixed allowances, so there is no out-of-zone shock — you simply lose data until you top up.



Plan the top-up before it happens: a local prepaid SIM needs a recharge voucher bought in cash, while an eSIM bought online can usually be topped up from the same account you purchased it in.



### Is Albania good value compared with Greece or Italy?



On price, yes — USD 1.55 per gigabyte against Greece at USD 2.79 and a global average of USD 2.59. On convenience, it depends on your route: crossing into Greece or Montenegro, a regional eSIM spreads the cost across borders instead of forcing a second purchase.
