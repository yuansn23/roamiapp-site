## Before you buy a Pakistan eSIM: five things that decide the outcome





### Does Pakistan support your device model?





Pakistan's 4G LTE runs on Band 3 (1800 MHz), Band 5 (850 MHz) and Band 40 (2300 MHz); 5G uses n78 (3500 MHz) and n41 (2500 MHz). Jazz and Zong have deployed 5G in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad and are still expanding. Flagship handsets sold in Europe, the Gulf and Asia normally cover all of these; a handset missing Band 40 will fall back to LTE more often than you would like.





### Which documents does a Pakistan SIM take?





This is the step that catches visitors out, and it is more than a form. Pakistan's regulator requires every connection to be biometrically verified, and foreign nationals register with a **passport and valid visa instead of a CNIC**. The [Pakistan Telecommunication Authority](https://www.pta.gov.pk/) caps foreign nationals at a small number of connections per passport — commonly three — and the line's validity is generally tied to the visa period, so a tourist SIM can stop working when your permission to be in the country does. As [one foreigner-registration guide](https://simsowner.net.pk/sim-card-foreigners-tourists-pakistan/) puts it, expect fingerprint capture, a scanned passport, and a local contact address, usually your hotel.





### 3. What "unlimited" means on a Pakistani prepaid bundle





Pakistani prepaid bundles are almost all capped, and the cap is usually a byte count rather than a speed throttle. Two consequences follow. First, a "monthly" pack is a fixed volume that expires with the calendar, not a subscription you can stretch. Second, when the volume runs out the data stops rather than slowing, so a hotspotted laptop can burn a week's allowance in an afternoon. Read the pack's data figure, not its name, and buy a bigger pack than you think you need if you are tethering.





### What's the top network in Pakistan?





Northern Gilgit-Baltistan and the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa valleys are where the national averages stop meaning anything — see the regional table below. Zong's consistency advantage shows up here, and SCO is the local operator in Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. Download offline maps for the Karakoram Highway regardless of which carrier you buy.





### Do visitors skip registration in Pakistan?





Pakistan runs a separate device-approval system. An imported handset using a Pakistani SIM may need approval through the **[Device Identification Registration and Blocking System](https://dirbs.pta.gov.pk/)**, and eligible visitors can normally register one personal device free for up to 120 days per visit. Find your IMEI by dialling `*#06#`, and note that the process needs a Pakistani number already issued in your name — a foreign number will not do.

## Getting your eSIM working: the failures that actually happen





The generic activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our [eSIM activation error guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The list below narrows to four patterns that matter in Pakistan.





**A. The local SIM works, then stops a few hours later.** Classic biometric-verification failure: the registration did not complete cleanly against NADRA's records and the operator's system blocked the line. Take the original passport, the visa and the verification receipt back to the same franchise and re-verify. Do not buy a replacement SIM — the new one will fail the same way.





**B. A working imported phone shows no service on a Pakistani SIM.** The device, not the SIM, is the problem: the handset's IMEI is not approved in the PTA device system. Dial `*#06#` for the IMEI, confirm its status, and register the device. This is separate from SIM registration and is the single most common cause of a phone that works at home and not in Pakistan.





**C. Installed, but no data on the Karakoram Highway or in a valley town.** The phone is hunting for 4G or 5G that is not there. Force manual network selection and allow 3G, then accept what the valley has rather than waiting for a band that is absent. In the settlements this usually recovers a usable signal; on the passes there is nothing to recover.





**D. Your travel eSIM camps on the weaker network.** A roaming profile attaches to whichever partner network it is provisioned for and will drift to another one that has no data agreement. Turn off automatic selection and lock the phone to the partner network the product page names, then re-enable automatic selection only when you leave that region.





### What a Jazz, Zong or Telenor helpdesk needs from you





Keep your EID (from `*#06#`), your order or account number, the name of the partner network your profile should attach to, a screenshot of the error, and a short list of what you have already tried. Pakistan's verification questions turn on the registration record, so having the passport and receipt to hand shortens the conversation considerably.





## Crossing out of Pakistan: India, Afghanistan, Iran and China





Pakistan's land borders each behave differently, and each one ends your Pakistani coverage in a different way.





**India — Wagah/Attari.** The Attari–Wagah crossing near Lahore is the only regularly functioning land route between the two countries, and it can close at short notice when diplomatic relations sour. Border formalities run from about 30 minutes to over two hours, and the crossing is the busiest leg for third-country nationals, since visas on both sides are hard to obtain. Your Pakistani SIM stops working at the last Pakistani post; a plan covering both countries is the only thing that keeps you online across the line. The Kartarpur Corridor is a separate, pilgrimage-only facility and is not a general entry point.





**Afghanistan — Torkham.** The main crossing on the Khyber route, roughly four hours from Islamabad, is busy with freight and requires a visa arranged well in advance. Coverage dies at the border; there is no roaming arrangement a travel profile can lean on in the immediate border zone.





**Iran — Taftan.** The border post on the Quetta–Zahedan corridor is remote — around 1,520 km from Islamabad via the N-40 — and the approach has long gaps in signal on the Pakistani side. Travellers using it should treat the whole corridor as offline-first, with offline maps, cash and a printed itinerary.





**China — Khunjerab Pass.** At 4,693 m, this is the highest paved international crossing in the world and the northern terminus of the Karakoram Highway. It is seasonal, generally open May to September, and there is no usable signal on the pass from either side. Whatever plan you carry, plan the gap.





In every case the pattern is the same: **a Pakistan-only profile dies at the border without a warning message**, and a multi-country plan is the only fix. Buy for the itinerary, not just for the first country you land in.

Fewer than a Pakistani citizen can.
