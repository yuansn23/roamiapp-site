> **Fastest 5G south, longest distances north — a Sweden eSIM guide that leads with your questions**

Sweden pairs two extremes that matter for your eSIM: a 5G network that is among Europe's best, and distances in the north that can swallow an afternoon of signal. Telenor took the Best Mobile Network and Best 5G Network titles in the [Ookla Speedtest Connectivity Report for Sweden, 1H 2025](https://www.ookla.com/research/reports/sweden-speedtest-connectivity-report-h1-2025) with a median 5G download of **234.94 Mbps**; Stockholm itself measured **263.63 Mbps** median mobile, the fastest Swedish city. Yet the same country runs a four-hour train between covered corridors in Norrland, and Swedish prepaid requires passport registration by law. Which carrier your Sweden eSIM should use — and how you buy one at all — is answered in the FAQ below, before anything else.

**Quick answer:** Sort it before you fly, install it before you land, and choose Telia or Tele2 based on whether your route is rural or urban — the full comparison below shows which is which. Tele2 is the solid second, Telia has the best 5G video experience (90.08) and the widest reach into the far north, and Tre (3) posts a credible 115.25 Mbps median 5G in cities. ## Your eSIM FAQ: the answers, up front

### Telia vs Tele2: which is better in Sweden?

Telenor on the evidence of 2025: Best Mobile Network, Best 5G Network, best 5G gaming, lowest latency (34 ms) in Ookla's 1H 2025 report, and 11 of 14 Opensignal awards including Time on 5G (21.4%) and Download Speed Experience (84.7 Mbps). Tele2 and Telia follow closely — Tele2 on 5G speed (145.54 Mbps median), Telia on video quality (90.08) and rural reach. Tre is strongest in dense urban areas. In Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö you will not feel much difference; in Norrland, you will.

### Can you buy a Sweden eSIM from Telia?

You can buy prepaid from all four — Telia, Tele2, Telenor and Tre — but Sweden requires **every prepaid SIM to be registered**, a rule in force since 2022 under PTS oversight: your passport is scanned at a staffed counter, often with a photo of you, which adds 5–15 minutes. Tourist starter kits typically begin around 249 SEK. Convenience chains sell starter kits, but staff cannot always complete tourist registration — use a carrier store or an airport counter. The friction-free alternative is a travel eSIM: no registration, QR delivery before you fly.

### Purchase options for Sweden eSIMs

For prepaid, no — a passport is accepted as identification at registration. For postpaid contracts, yes, along with a credit check. Nothing about a travel eSIM requires Swedish identity at all.

### Telia vs Tele2 5G: which is better in Sweden?

No, and this is the key nuance. South of roughly Sundsvall, 5G is mature in every city and along the main corridors — Telenor and Tele2 share a joint network company, Net4Mobility, and have been activating thousands of new sites. In Norrland, coverage follows the E4 and the few population centers; between them, 4G is the service and long stretches have neither. Plan the north as a 4G experience with gaps.

### Sweden carriers: who's who

Telia has the strongest rural and northern footprint of the four — the incumbent advantage above the Arctic Circle (Kiruna, Abisko, the Iron Ore Line corridor) is real and worth planning around. Telenor's far-north reach is good but does not match it; Tele2 and Tre are the weaker options in deep Norrland.

### Does a Sweden eSIM include calls and SMS?

Travel eSIMs are data-only — calls go through WhatsApp, FaceTime or Skype, and 112 works on any network in an emergency. Direct Swedish prepaid plans include voice and a Swedish number, which matters if you need to receive local calls or SMS verification for more than a week or two.

### APN values for Telia, Tele2 and Telenor Sweden eSIMs

Modern devices provision automatically, and travel eSIM profiles always bring their own APN. For manual entry on a direct-carrier SIM, the documented values are in [the APN table below](#manual-apn-setup-for-sweden) — Tele2, Telenor and Tre values are consistent across references; Telia's varies by SIM generation.

### Can I use a Swedish eSIM and my home SIM at the same time?

Yes — that is the standard dual-SIM setup. Keep your home line for calls and bank SMS, point Mobile Data at the eSIM, and switch data roaming OFF on the home line so it never bills background traffic. Data-only eSIMs cannot receive SMS, so verification codes still arrive on your physical number.

### Sweden eSIM installation steps

In order: check the eSIM line is enabled and selected for mobile data; confirm data roaming is ON for that line; try manual network selection (Telia, Tele2, Telenor, Tre or 3 will all appear); reboot. ### Does the eSIM work on the Stockholm–Copenhagen train?

Yes, for nearly the whole route. The Stockholm–Gothenburg and Gothenburg–Copenhagen corridors are among the best-covered rail lines in the country, holding 4G almost continuously and 5G around the cities. The Öresund crossing itself keeps signal on both the bridge and the tunnel side. Norrland trains are a different story — see the coverage split above.

### Does tethering work on a Sweden eSIM?

Yes, on travel eSIMs and on Swedish carrier plans alike — hotspot use draws from the same data allowance. The one caution is battery: a day of running a laptop off your phone in −10°C Lapland weather drains both faster than the spec sheet suggests, so carry a power bank in winter.

### Sweden plans: what you get

On a travel eSIM, the plan simply expires or tops up through the app — no surprise charges. On Swedish prepaid, you top up at a store or via the carrier app. On postpaid, overage rules vary by operator. Nothing in Sweden disconnects you mid-scroll; emergency data and 112 access continue regardless.

### Telia vs Tele2: coverage compared

Destination Gotland runs the state-contracted service from Nynäshamn, roughly 55 km south of Stockholm, in about 3 hours 15 minutes, several times a day all year; there is a shorter crossing from Oskarshamn. Passenger tickets start at 240 SEK and the car package at 1,250 SEK one way, on dynamic pricing that punishes late summer bookings. Signal holds approaching both coasts and thins mid-Baltic, so download what you need before boarding.

### Will my eSIM work at the Icehotel and on an aurora chase?

At the Icehotel in Jukkasjärvi, yes — the village, the hotel and the short road in from Kiruna airport all sit inside Telia's 4G footprint. The aurora is the harder part: the best viewing spots are chosen for darkness and distance from town, so signal fades exactly where the sky improves. Download the forecast and the map in Kiruna.

### Which carrier should you pick in Sweden?

Treat both as connected at the stops and not between them. The Inlandsbanan runs through the interior from Mora up to Gällivare, so signal returns at each station and disappears through the forested gaps. The E45 inland route behaves the same way — towns covered, forest between them not — which is why the E4 up the coast is the more predictable drive.

### If I take a Sweden eSIM into Norway or Finland, will it work?

Not on a Sweden-only plan — the profile stops at the border, often without warning. Sweden, Norway and Finland all sit inside one roaming framework, so an EU-issued SIM keeps working across all three, and Norway adds EEA membership on top. If the itinerary crosses a border more than once, one regional plan beats three country plans.

## The Sweden eSIM carriers: Telia, Tele2 and Telenor Sweden

Sweden has exactly four national networks — Telia, Tele2, Telenor and Tre (3) — plus a busy layer of MVNOs (Comviq on Tele2, Halebop on Telia, Hallon on Tre) that resell the same masts cheaper to residents. Every performance figure below is from Ookla's 1H 2025 Sweden report unless noted:

Two structural facts worth knowing before you choose. First, Telenor and Tele2 build their networks jointly through Net4Mobility and have been activating roughly 60% more sites in a rolling modernization — including new low-band 5G that reaches further into forest and wall interiors; that is why the two trade places depending on the metric. Second, Sweden's 3G networks are shut down (Tele2, Telia and Tre completed decommissioning by the end of 2025, per [PTS](https://www.pts.se/en/news-and-press-releases/controlled-shutdown-of-2g-and-3g-networks/)), so any handset that leans on 3G will not connect at all — a real trap for old backup phones.

### Which Sweden carrier should you pick: Telia vs Tele2?

**Telenor — the 2025 benchmark.** Fastest on every headline number and the most-awarded network in Opensignal's Swedish measurements: winner or joint winner in 11 of 14 categories, with users spending more time on 5G (21.4%) than on any other operator and enjoying the country's highest average download experience (84.7 Mbps). If your trip is southern and urban and you want the single strongest attach point, this is it. Its prepaid line is sold under the Telenor brand in its own stores, with passport registration at the counter — and Telenor is the operator that lets a visitor complete that registration online by uploading a passport photograph rather than queuing in a store. The misread to avoid is treating a strong city network as a national one: the profile that streams flawlessly in Stockholm is the same profile that looks thin above Sundsvall. Scenario: two weeks working remotely from Stockholm and Gothenburg, where the 84.7 Mbps Download Speed Experience figure is the number that keeps video calls steady.

**Tele2 — the quiet all-rounder.** Second on speed (123.92 Mbps overall, 145.54 Mbps on 5G) with the same Net4Mobility backbone as Telenor, and the network behind Comviq, Sweden's biggest budget brand. In practice Tele2 coverage and Telenor coverage track each other closely; the differences show at the margins in the far north, where Telia pulls ahead. Its practical advantage for a visitor is administrative rather than technical: Comviq SIMs are registered by the shop that sells them, which makes Tele2's budget brand the fastest route from shelf to working SIM in the country. Scenario: a Malmö and Copenhagen weekend, where the southern 5G layer and the Öresund crossing matter more than Norrland ever will.

**Telia — the incumbent, and the northern specialist.** The oldest and largest operator, with the widest rural footprint and the network you want above the Arctic Circle. Its 5G video score (90.08) leads the market, and its infrastructure carries Halebop, another major consumer brand. For a Kiruna–Abisko–Jokkmokk itinerary, Telia is the default recommendation among the four — the aurora villages and the Jukkasjärvi hotel road sit inside its footprint. The price of that reach is administrative: registering a Telia prepaid is the slowest route in the country, because the SIM is registered in a Telia store and the paperwork is then processed centrally, which can take hours before you can load a plan. Scenario: an Abisko aurora week, where the village itself is well connected and the fell trails above it are not.

**Tre (3) — the urban value play.** Ookla lists a 115.25 Mbps median 5G download for Tre, third among the operators that publish 5G medians, and the network performs best in dense city areas where its capacity shows. It runs the Hallon MVNO. Outside the southern cities and regional centers its footprint is the thinnest of the four — fine for Malmö and Stockholm, risky for Norrland. In Opensignal's December 2025 Sweden report, Tre appears among the award winners but only in shared categories, which is the quantitative version of the same point. Scenario: a long weekend split between Stockholm and Malmö, a trip that plays entirely to its strengths.

For the national picture: Sweden's median mobile download measured **133.32 Mbps** in August 2026 (27th globally) per the [Speedtest Global Index](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/sweden), with DataReportal counting 10.5 million internet users (98.3% penetration) and 14.8 million mobile connections — 139% of the population — at the start of 2025.

## Swedish winter coverage: the Icehotel, ski resorts and aurora belt

Swedish winter travel splits into three markets, and each one has a different connectivity shape.

| Destination | The draw | Connectivity reality |
|:---|:---|:---|

| Sälen | Sweden's largest alpine area, four interconnected ski areas in one, and the start of the Vasaloppet | Better than its reputation — the valley, the villages and the resorts work normally — but the weak point is capacity rather than coverage: in school holidays thousands of guests share the same masts and midday speeds drop |

The rules for all four are the same in a Swedish winter, and only one of them is about coverage. Cold is first: at −20°C a phone loses charge far faster than its indicator suggests, so keep it inside a jacket and carry a warm power bank. Capacity is second: at Sälen in half-term, or in an Åre lift queue, the mast is fine and the queue behind it is the problem. Darkness is third, and it is the one people underestimate — an aurora drive leaves the covered footprint and the lit road at the same moment, so the map, the forecast and the meeting point all have to be downloaded before you set off.

## SIM Registration Rules

Since August 2022 every Swedish prepaid SIM must be registered to a named person with photo ID before it will work, and cards bought earlier stopped working in February 2023. This is the rule that makes Swedish prepaid the most awkward in the Nordics for a visitor, and it is also the rule most travelers misread as "you cannot buy one". You can. You have to register it — and how painful that is depends entirely on whose card is in your hand:

| Operator or brand | How a visitor registers | Time to a working SIM |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Comviq (Tele2's brand) | The shop that sells you the SIM completes the registration on the spot | Minutes |
| Telenor | You register yourself online by uploading a passport photograph | Minutes, once uploaded |
| Telia and Halebop | Buy the SIM elsewhere, take it to a Telia store, complete a form that is then processed centrally | Can take hours before you can load a plan |
| Hallon (Tre's brand) | BankID only, which a visitor cannot obtain | Not an option |

Swedish residents register with a personnummer, or through BankID if they have mobile banking. A visitor registers with a passport, which is why the document you were carrying for identification turns out to be the whole purchase. The practical consequence is easy to miss: a prepaid SIM bought off a shelf does not work until registration is complete, and a till receipt is not an activation.

**Prices, in kronor.** Tourist starter kits commonly begin around 249 SEK, with the data allowance and validity varying by brand. For a month or longer, Swedish prepaid at local prices is genuinely good value — the market sits just below the global average at about USD 2.33 per gigabyte — and it brings a Swedish number, voice and EU roaming rights with it. For a holiday-length trip, the registration stop is precisely the cost an eSIM is bought to avoid.

## 5G in Sweden: Telia, Tele2 and Telenor Sweden 5G coverage compared

PTS, the Swedish Post and Telecom Authority, publishes both the market statistics and the timetable for the switch-off, and both matter to a visitor.

**The 5G turnover.** In PTS's *Svensk telekommarknad 2025* report, close to **9 million mobile subscriptions had used a 5G network** by the end of the year — around 60% of all Swedish mobile subscriptions — and 5G traffic more than doubled year on year to account for 30% of all mobile data. That is the number behind the advice throughout this guide: in populated Sweden, 5G is no longer a bonus, it is the default.

**The switch-off.** PTS describes the 2G and 3G shutdown as having been carried out "in a controlled manner without major incidents" in [its notice on the planned shutdown](https://www.pts.se/en/news-and-press-releases/controlled-shutdown-of-2g-and-3g-networks/), primarily during 2025:

| Operator | What it retired | Note |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Tele2 and Telenor | Both 2G and 3G, from 1 December 2025 | Delivered through their shared Net4Mobility network |
| Telia | 3G only | Keeps its 2G network running until the end of 2027 |
| Tre (3) | 3G only | Completed by the end of 2025 |

**What that means for your handset.** Voice calls and 112 now depend on **VoLTE** — 4G calling — because there is no 3G left to fall back on, and in most networks no 2G either. [Telenor's own shutdown page](https://www.telenor.se/om-telenor/digitalisering/varfor-stanger-vi-3g-natet/) tells customers to text "4G" or "5G" to **222** to check whether a handset is supported, and notes that PTS has ordered operators to block phones that cannot reach 112 over 4G. An older travel phone that seemed fine last year may simply not register. The other requirement is band support: Swedish 5G uses n78 for the fast urban layer and n28 for low-band reach, with 4G on bands 1/3/7/20 — a combination any unlocked phone from roughly 2019 onward handles.

## eSIM setup, start to finish

- **Telia:** a prepaid SIM bought elsewhere and registered at a Telia store, with the form processed centrally — allow hours before you can load a plan.
- **Telenor:** prepaid sold in Telenor's own stores with passport registration, and that registration can be completed online by uploading a passport photograph — the fastest of the big four.
- **Tele2:** the network runs Comviq, whose SIMs are registered by the shop at the point of sale, which is the quickest route from shelf to working line in the country.
- **Tre (3):** prepaid and eSIM through Tre and its Hallon brand, with the caveat that Hallon registration requires BankID and is closed to visitors.
- **Travel eSIMs:** a QR code by email, no registration, no store, attaching to whichever of the four networks is strongest where you are.
- **Home EU SIMs:** nothing to install — but confirm VoLTE is active on the handset, because there is no 3G left to carry a call.

## Will the eSIM be live when you land?

Four questions, in the order people ask them — with answers that are specific to Sweden rather than generic.

**Does your phone support eSIM at all?** Dial `*#06#`. An EID means yes. If nothing appears, run the model against the [device list](/compatibility/) before you spend anything.

**Will 4G calling work?** This is the question that matters most here, because Sweden has no 3G left to fall back on. Confirm Voice & Data is set to LTE or 5G; otherwise you will have data and no voice, which looks like a broken eSIM and is not one.

**Can you install it at home?** Yes, and you should. Scan the QR code while you are still on your own Wi-Fi, name the line something obvious, keep a second copy of the code in cloud storage, and let the profile sit dormant. Install it before Arlanda, not at Arlanda.

**What else is worth doing before you fly?** Put the eSIM on the data line, leave roaming ON for it and OFF for the home SIM — the item people most often forget to switch — and cache the maps and tickets for the first two days. Sweden runs close to cashless, so a dead phone here is a dead wallet as well as a dead map.

## Manual APN setup for Sweden

Only relevant if you bought a SIM or eSIM **directly from a Swedish carrier** — travel eSIM profiles carry their own APN and should be left alone:

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Tele2 / Comviq | `internet.tele2.se` | blank | blank |
| Telenor | `internet.telenor.se` | blank | blank |
| Tre (3) | `data.tre.se` | blank | blank |
| Telia | `online.telia.se` | blank | blank |

Telia's value varies by SIM generation, and some devices need `online5` instead — if the first value fails to carry data, try the alternative before anything else. iPhone route: Settings → Cellular → eSIM line → Cellular Data Network. Android: find Access Point Names under Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks. Save, reboot, retest.

## What to do when a Sweden eSIM refuses to attach

The install sequence — QR scan, label the line, set it as your data line, enable data roaming — is in the [QR-scan sequence](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). The Sweden-specific patterns:

1. **No service in Norrland.** Probably not a fault — check whether you are between towns before reconfiguring anything. Step toward an E-road or settlement and let the phone re-acquire.
2. **Service in cities only, never on 5G.** Normal outside the southern urban layer; the low-band rollout is closing that gap but has not finished. 4G here is fast — 95.6% of Telenor samples cleared the 5/1 Mbps consistency bar on all technologies combined.
3. **Signal but no data.** Confirm the eSIM is the selected data line and roaming is ON for it; then verify the APN against the table above if this was a direct-carrier purchase.
4. **Everything else.** Lock status, profile reinstall, network reset — the full sequence is in [activation error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/).

### Three Sweden eSIM cases that look like faults and are not

**The phone sits on a network but refuses to make calls**
1. Check VoLTE is enabled: iPhone → Settings → Mobile → Mobile Data Options → Voice & Data → LTE or 5G; Android → SIM settings → "4G calling"
2. Confirm the handset can do 4G calling at all — Telenor's own check is an SMS with "4G" to 222
3. Reboot and retest. Without VoLTE you get data and no voice, because there is no 3G left to fall back to

**Everything worked until you boarded a ferry or crossed a bridge**
1. On the Stockholm–Gothenburg corridor and the Öresund crossing, expect brief drops followed by automatic recovery
2. On the Gotland crossing, expect a long mid-Baltic gap — that is the sea, not the plan
3. If a Sweden-only profile dies at the Danish border, that is the plan ending, not a fault

**Installed, no bars, in the middle of a city**
1. Confirm the eSIM is enabled and selected for mobile data
2. Try manual network selection and pick any of Telia, Tele2, Telenor, Tre or 3
3. Wipe network settings and restart the phone

## Taking the eSIM across the border

Sweden's borders are all short hops, and each one changes something different about your connection.

| Border | The crossing | What it does to your connection |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Denmark | The Öresund bridge and tunnel between Malmö and Copenhagen | Denmark is in the EU, so roaming continues and signal holds across the bridge and the tunnel — but a Sweden-only plan stops dead at the coast |
| Norway | The E6 north of Gothenburg toward Oslo, and the E10 and inland crossings above the Arctic Circle | Norway is in the EEA but outside the EU, so EU roaming still applies while a customs border applies to what is in the car |
| Finland | Tornio–Haparanda at the head of the Gulf of Bothnia, plus the Torne valley crossings and Karesuvanto–Karesuando in the far north | Finland runs an hour ahead, uses the euro, and its mobile data is among the cheapest on earth — a sensible place to top up a physical SIM |
| The Baltic | The Stockholm–Helsinki and Stockholm–Turku services, some of which call at Mariehamn in Åland | Åland is a separate telecoms territory, so check that your plan names it before assuming it is covered |

The rule behind all four is the same: a country plan is a country plan. Sweden, Norway, Finland and Denmark sit inside one roaming framework, so a SIM from any of them works across the others, while a Sweden-only travel eSIM stops where the map does. If your trip touches three of the four — as almost every Nordic itinerary eventually does — one regional plan is cheaper, simpler and one support queue instead of several.
