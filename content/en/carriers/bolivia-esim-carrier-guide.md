> **Where Bolivia's networks differ: speed, coverage and the counter rules**

Bolivia is the one country in South America where the carrier question is not about speed at all — it is about **where the signal reaches**. The national median mobile download sits at just **20.65 Mbps** (Ookla, August 2026, 99th in the world), there is no commercial 5G yet, and the landscape does the rest: the altiplano sits at 3,600–4,000 m, the salt flats have no towers at all, and the Amazon basin eats every radio wave. Which Bolivia eSIM serves you well is therefore a coverage decision first and a price decision second.

**Quick answer:** the local networks is the network most visitors should shortlist first in Bolivia; the local networks is the value alternative to price against it. If you would sooner test than read, Roami's [free starter eSIM](/free-esim/) is the cost-free way to start in Bolivia.

This guide compares the three carriers an eSIM for Bolivia can ride on — state-owned **Entel**, Millicom-owned **Tigo** and the budget **Viva** network — then walks through what tourists can realistically buy locally, what a travel eSIM changes, and the two Bolivia-specific failure modes that catch visitors out. **Fast take:** Spend any real time outside the cities? Entel is the pick — its rural footprint is the widest in Bolivia, and it is the only operator with a usable signal in Uyuni town and much of the altiplano. Staying in La Paz, Santa Cruz or Cochabamba? Tigo usually posts the fastest urban 4G. Want the cheapest possible data for messaging and maps in the main cities? Viva does that job. And whoever you pick, remember the ground truth: the Salar de Uyuni itself has little to no signal for any carrier — download offline maps before you leave town.

## Entel, Tigo and Viva: the measured numbers

Ookla's Speedtest data puts Bolivia near the bottom of the global speed table, but the three carriers are not interchangeable — their footprints barely overlap outside the big cities.

### Which network covers rural Bolivia best?

Entel is state-owned and carries the widest 4G/LTE footprint in the country, reaching roughly 70% of the population and — more importantly for travellers — a disproportionate share of the small towns the highway network connects. It holds the largest 700 MHz allocation, which is exactly the band that travels well across open altiplano and thin mountain air. Speeds in remote zones are modest, and Ookla's consistency figures show Entel trading peak speed for presence: the signal is more likely to be there at all.

Where it matters: Uyuni town, Potosí, the Cordillera Real trailheads, the Rurrenabaque side of the Amazon gateway, and the long La Paz–Santa Cruz bus corridor.

### What's the fastest carrier in Bolivia?

Tigo, owned by Luxembourg's Millicom, is the largest private operator and concentrates its capacity where the people are: La Paz, El Alto, Santa Cruz and Cochabamba. In urban tests it typically posts the highest median download of the three, and its 1900 MHz and AWS spectrum is tuned for city capacity rather than rural reach. If your Bolivia time is split between hotels, offices and cafés in the big four cities, Tigo is the network your eSIM will enjoy most.

### The field: Bolivia mobile carriers

Viva (operated by NuevaTel, part of Trilogy International) runs 850 MHz and 1900 MHz, keeps costs low, and covers the main towns and trunk highways. It is entirely adequate for maps, messaging and the occasional video call, and it is the carrier local budget travellers default to. Its weakness is the same as its strength: it built the least, so the map thins out fastest once you leave the cities.

## Bolivia has no 5G — what that means for your eSIM

Entel has been running 5G trial "experience zones" in departmental capitals using 3.5 GHz spectrum allocated by the regulator ATT, and an official commercial rollout has been on the government's agenda since 2024. As of late 2026, though, no Bolivian operator sells commercial 5G service — the country's population at large is on 4G and 3G.

Practically, that means two things. First, ignore any marketing that promises 5G speeds on a Bolivia eSIM; a realistic urban experience is 15–50 Mbps on 4G, which handles navigation, WhatsApp video and HD streaming without drama. Second, band anxiety is mostly unnecessary here: Bolivia's 4G runs on bands (including 700 MHz and 850 MHz) that even modest international handsets support. The compatibility question that matters is whether your phone supports eSIM at all, not whether it has this year's flagship radio. The APN below concerns only those who bought a line **straight from a Bolivian carrier**. A travel eSIM arrives with its own profile and its own APN; changing anything in that case is how people break a working connection.

| Tigo | `internet.tigo.bo` | blank | blank |

Leave username and password empty unless the activation pack that came with the line states otherwise, and treat the carrier's own settings page as the authority if a value is ever rejected.

Save, then restart the handset. If data still fails, confirm the eSIM — not your home line — is the one selected for mobile data before changing anything else. On a dual-SIM phone a Bolivian profile that never activates is more often a data-line mistake than a network problem.

## When a Bolivia eSIM will not connect: four checks in order

Work down this list before you touch a support chat, because three of the four causes are local to your phone.

**1. Nothing installs.** Nine times out of ten the handset is carrier-locked, or the QR code has already been consumed. Check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock (iOS) or the SIM-lock status on Android, and confirm with your provider whether the profile is still pending.

**2. It installs but shows no bars.** The altiplano is a hard place to make a first attach. Run the radio through a full off-and-on cycle, then give the scan a minute — forcing a manual choice immediately often lands the phone on a rural cell that hands over badly. Two failed scans in a row justify a restart before anything more exotic.

**3. Bars, but no data.** Verify the eSIM is set as the mobile-data line, confirm data roaming is on for that line only, and check the APN against the table above if you bought locally. A prepaid line that has not been topped up looks exactly like a network fault.

**4. "SOS" or "Emergency calls only".** In practice this is a second live profile competing for the radio. Delete it, reboot, and reset network settings as the third step. Reinstalling should come last, because a single-use QR code cannot be issued twice.

Anything outside those four patterns belongs to the general catalogue in our [eSIM activation error guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). New customers can also test the whole flow at no cost with a [test at no charge](/free-esim/) before committing to a paid plan.

## Bolivia eSIMs: frequently asked questions

### Does any eSIM work on the Salar de Uyuni?

Realistically, no. Uyuni town has Entel-based 4G good enough for maps and messages, but once you are out on the salt crust, or up at the coloured lagoons and geysers of the Eduardo Avaroa reserve, the signal drops to nothing for every carrier. Guides on multi-day salt-flat tours expect you to be offline — download maps, accommodation details and entertainment in town before you depart.

### Is your device on the Bolivia eSIM list?

Yes, provided the phone is eSIM-capable and carrier-unlocked: iPhone XS and newer, Google Pixel 3 and newer, and recent Samsung Galaxy models all install a Bolivian profile without issue. Because Bolivia runs 4G, you do not need a 5G handset. The one hard exception is mainland-China-model iPhones, which have eSIM disabled at the hardware level. It is worth running your model against the [device compatibility list](/compatibility/) before buying.

### Which operator offers top 5G in Bolivia?

Not commercially. Entel operates trial 5G zones in some capitals, but no Bolivian operator sells 5G service to the public, so every Bolivia eSIM — ours included — runs on 4G and 3G. That is why the realistic speed ceiling is around 20 Mbps nationally rather than the three-digit figures you see for Chile or Brazil.

### Roaming with a Bolivia eSIM

No. The profile connects to Bolivian networks at local rates, so your home carrier never bills you for the data you use in Bolivia. Keep data roaming switched **off** on your home SIM and switched **on** for the eSIM line, and background traffic from your home number cannot run up a bill.

### Is hotspot allowed on Bolivia eSIMs?

Yes — most plans allow personal hotspot, which is useful when a laptop needs the connection and the hotel Wi-Fi does not. Check the allowance on the specific plan page, since a few unlimited-style products cap tethering separately.

### Is WhatsApp calling usable in Bolivia?

No. Bolivia's travel ecosystem — buses, hostels, tour operators, restaurants — runs on WhatsApp, and WhatsApp works fine on a data-only eSIM using your existing number. You will only miss a local number if you need to receive domestic Bolivian SMS verification codes, which almost no tourist does.

### What's the best network in Bolivia?

Confirm data roaming is enabled on the eSIM line, then manually select a network (Entel or Tigo) instead of leaving the phone on automatic — some handsets latch onto a weaker carrier and stay there. A reboot after the first registration solves most remaining cases. Still nothing after that? Work through the [activation error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/) or contact support with a screenshot of the error.

### Where coverage varies in Bolivia

Better than the altiplano average. Copacabana and the Isla del Sol approaches sit close enough to Entel and Tigo base stations that 4G holds in the town and thins out along the lake's northern shore and on the island trails. Entel is the safer of the two, and remember that a hop over to Puno puts you on Peruvian networks, which a Bolivia-only profile will not follow.

### Bolivia coverage: cities vs rural areas

Ookla's 1H 2026 report makes Entel the fastest on median download at 15.89 Mbps, with Tigo next at 11.1 Mbps, while VIVA takes the Best Mobile Network award on a Connectivity Score of 55.21. ### 5G coverage notes for Bolivia

No, and a 5G handset buys you nothing here, because Bolivia has no commercial 5G service at all. What decides your experience instead is low-band support: 850 MHz (B5) and 700 MHz (B28) are the frequencies that carry signal across the altiplano, and neither is guaranteed on an imported handset.

Three for city coverage — 1900 MHz (B2), 1700/2100 MHz AWS (B4) and 2600 MHz (B7) — and ideally 850 MHz (B5) plus 700 MHz (B28), which carry the rural and altiplano coverage. Most mainstream international handsets have all five. The [eSIM compatibility quiz](/compatibility/) confirms yours.

### Will a Bolivia eSIM work in Peru and Chile too?

A single-country profile will not; it goes dark at the border, usually without warning. If the itinerary strings Bolivia with Peru or Chile, buy a regional plan or a separate profile for each leg. ### Bolivia plans with the lowest prices

Cable.co.uk's worldwide pricing study puts Bolivia among the cheaper markets globally for a gigabyte of mobile data, and local prepaid is cheaper again — the trade is cash, a passport and a shop queue. A travel eSIM costs more per gigabyte and saves the afternoon.

### Do visitors skip registration in Bolivia?

Yes. Prepaid lines in Bolivia are registered against an identity document, and a passport is the standard document for visitors. The shop records your details against the line, so buying "off the books" is not an option at a legitimate counter.

### Tethering rules on Bolivia carriers

Yes on nearly every travel tier, and the tethered traffic comes out of the same pot. Bear in mind a tethered laptop eats several times what phone use does on a Bolivian 4G cell, so plan the allowance around the computer if you intend to work from the road.

### Bolivia plans: what you get

Comfortable. Both colonial cities have solid 4G on all three networks, with Tigo usually ahead on urban download and Entel ahead on reach into the surrounding valleys. Old-town stone does more damage to your signal than distance does — expect a bar or two to vanish inside thick-walled colonial buildings, and step outside for anything that matters.

### Which carrier should a Bolivia eSIM prefer on the altiplano?

Whichever tower is present — that is the point of a multi-network profile. Entel records the fastest median download in the country at 15.89 Mbps and VIVA takes the Best Mobile Network award on a 55.21 Connectivity Score, and their coverage is complementary outside the cities. ### Does a Bolivia eSIM work in the Yungas and the Amazon?

Workably in the towns and unreliably everywhere else. Coroico and Rurrenabaque have 4G; the road between them and the lodges along the rivers are where the connection stops. Download what you need in La Paz rather than expecting to fetch it on the way.

Only when the profile first registers on a Bolivian network. ### Can I keep my home number alive for bank codes while I am at altitude?

Yes — that is the whole point of running the eSIM beside your physical SIM. Your usual line stays on for calls and one-time passwords, the Bolivian profile carries data, and the two do not interfere. ### Can you buy a SIM in Bolivia without a passport?

On price alone, sometimes. On time, rarely. Entel, Tigo and VIVA all want a passport and a registration step for prepaid, the counters keep limited hours, and the queues in La Paz are not a small tax on a two-week trip. The travel profile costs more per gigabyte and saves the afternoon — and Bolivia has no commercial 5G yet, so the speed you gain by buying locally is close to nil.

### Topping up a Bolivia eSIM

From the provider's app, on whatever Wi-Fi you last passed through — a hotel in Uyuni, a café in Tupiza. Do not plan on finding a carrier shop between towns, because there is not one. Because the salt-flat crossing and the Amazon fringe both run for days without usable Wi-Fi, the honest advice is to size the plan for the whole trip before you leave the city rather than relying on a mid-trip top-up.

One profile covers both, and it will not behave the same way in either.
