This guide covers the Antel vs Movistar vs Claro decision, the Punta del Este January congestion effect, and the Carrasco airport kiosk that closes too early for late flights. ### Antel vs Claro: which is better in Uruguay?



Antel — Uruguay's state-owned carrier — is not just the domestic leader; it is one of Latin America's strongest mobile networks, an early 5G adopter with genuine coverage in Montevideo, Pocitos and Punta del Este's main avenues, and the widest rural footprint by a distance. Traveler reports converge on the same story: on Ruta 1 toward Colonia, on the coastal roads east, and in the interior towns, Antel holds while the rivals thin. [Ookla's Speedtest Global Index](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/uruguay) backs the infrastructure story — fixed medians around **208 Mbps** (top-40 globally) in recent editions — and [Cable.co.uk's survey](https://www.cable.co.uk/mobiles/worldwide-data-pricing/) prices local data at roughly **USD 0.28 per GB**, among the world's cheapest 20 markets.



The regulator is [URSEC](https://www.gub.uy/unidad-reguladora-servicios-comunicaciones), the communications services regulatory unit.



**Registration is the one hoop:** every SIM in Uruguay is tied to an identity document by law — the clerk scans or photographs your passport at an official outlet. Foreigners can register a limited number of lines per passport, and registration happens only at official shops, not at repair stalls or random resellers. For top-ups afterward, the ubiquitous Abitab and Redpagos kiosks reload credit in cash in under a minute.



### Movistar eSIM: the Uruguay urban-coast alternative



Movistar (Telefónica) is the credible number two: very good in Montevideo and along the coastal strip, competitive tourist bundles, growing 5G. Its documented weakness is the interior — traveler accounts of losing signal inland while a companion on Antel keeps streaming are consistent enough to be a pattern. City and resort trip: fine. Country-wide trip: the backup network.



### Claro: the budget third



Claro (América Móvil) is the smallest network and prices accordingly — decent urban coverage, cheap packs, noticeably thinner rural reach than either rival. The honest verdict from locals and long-stayers: the few dollars saved are not worth the dead zones unless you never leave the pavement.



### Can you buy an Uruguay eSIM from Antel?



**Yes for physical SIMs, with paperwork; less clear for carrier eSIMs.** Uruguay's operators sell prepaid lines to foreigners legally and cheaply, but every purchase requires in-person passport registration at an official outlet. That is a fixed cost in time rather than money — the process takes about ten minutes, and the clerk photographs your document.

## Uruguay coverage gaps: the Cabo Polonio exception and others



Most Uruguay coverage articles read like the whole country has signal. The reality has a short list of exceptions that matter:



| Region | Coverage reality | Best carrier | Watch out for |

|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| Montevideo | Dense 4G/5G; excellent everywhere | Any | Congestion downtown at peak |

| Punta del Este and José Ignacio | Full 4G/5G on the main drag | Antel | **January–February saturation** drops the peninsula and Playa Brava to 3G |

| Ruta 1 → Colonia | Unbroken 4G on Antel | Antel | The river road east of Colonia is patchier |

| Colonia del Sacramento old town | Good in town and at the port | Any | Historic walls weaken indoor signal |

| Rocha coast (La Paloma, Punta del Diablo) | Town coverage; gaps between | Antel | Movistar thins noticeably here |

| Cabo Polonio | **No signal from any carrier** — nearest mast ~7 km at the Valizas turnoff | None | Book your posada before you go; download offline maps in Montevideo |

| Interior and the north | Antel holds; rivals thin | Antel | Long gaps between towns on minor roads |

| Rivera and the Brazilian border | Covered, with Brazilian networks bleeding across | Antel | Your phone may lock onto a Brazilian network — check roaming |



### Why Cabo Polonio has no signal at all



Cabo Polonio is not a coverage gap in the ordinary sense — it is a village with **no electricity grid**, accessible only by 4x4 or on foot across the dunes, where the nearest confirmed signal is roughly 7 km away at the Valizas turnoff. Three practical consequences:



1. **You cannot book or rebook anything from there.** Confirm your posada before you travel.

2. **Download offline maps in Montevideo**, not in Rocha — the coast road has gaps before you even reach the village.

3. **Tell someone your plan.** This is the correct use case for an offline itinerary and, if you are travelling solo, a check-in arrangement.



### What the Uruguayan map will not tell you



- **The coast and the capital are covered by everyone.** The operator differences only register once you head inland or north.

- **January changes the physics.** Punta del Este's population multiplies, and cells built for a small town serve a seasonal city. Speeds fall; coverage does not disappear.

- **The Brazilian border is a roaming trap.** Around Rivera and Chuy, phones frequently attach to Brazilian networks. If your plan does not include Brazil, switch to manual network selection.



Market context: [DataReportal's Digital 2025: Uruguay](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-uruguay) counts 7.18 million mobile connections — 212% of the population — against 3.15 million internet users. That saturation figure is the statistical signature of a small, wealthy, thoroughly connected market where the networks are built for residents first and visitors inherit a good deal.

### Troubleshooting an Uruguay eSIM in four steps



**A. The eSIM will not install**

1. Carrier Lock (Settings -> General -> About) must read "No SIM restrictions" before anything else matters

2. Make sure the QR is unclaimed; most work exactly once

3. Have support resend the profile if it is stuck



**B. Installed, but no signal bars**

1. Toggle the line off, wait a beat, then on

2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose Antel manually

3. Reboot, and escalate to a network reset only if the fault stays



**C. Signal bars, but no internet**

1. Re-key the APN from the table without shortcuts

2. Assign mobile data to the eSIM

3. Line-level roaming needs to be on, not just the handset switch

4. The usage page will show if the allowance is finished



**D. Worked all week, dead at the coast or the border**

1. Cabo Polonio has no signal at all — check the region table first

2. At the Brazilian border, force manual network selection

3. In January at Punta del Este, congestion rather than coverage is the cause

### Will my eSIM work in Cabo Polonio?



No — and neither will anything else. The village has no cell signal from any carrier and no electricity grid; the nearest coverage is around 7 km away. Book accommodation in advance and download offline maps.



### Antel vs Claro 5G: which is better in Uruguay?



Yes — Antel's 5G covers central Montevideo, Pocitos and Punta del Este's main avenues, with Movistar and Claro expanding selectively. Rural Uruguay is a 4G experience, and it is a good one.

Once you cross into Uruguayan waters, yes — the profile attaches to a local network mid-crossing if data roaming is enabled. Argentine networks serve the Buenos Aires side; mid-River Plate is the switch zone.
