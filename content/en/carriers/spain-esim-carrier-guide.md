## Does your phone take your eSIM?



Three things decide whether your phone works on a Spanish carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.



### Do older phones take Spain eSIMs?



Spanish carriers build 4G on bands B1, B3, B7, B8, B20 (800 MHz) and B28a (700 MHz), and 5G on n1, n3, n7, n28a (700 MHz), n78 (3.5 GHz) and n258 (26 GHz). Most international handsets sold in Europe, Asia and the Americas already support these, so compatibility is rarely the problem it is in, say, Canada. The band that actually separates city from countryside is B20 (800 MHz) for 4G and n28a (700 MHz) for 5G: phones missing them still work in Madrid and Barcelona but lose the low-frequency reach that fills rural valleys.



No one needs to memorise these band numbers. The most dependable step is matching the exact model number to the [eSIM compatibility list](/compatibility/). Prefer the technical background first? [What happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) is the place to start.



### Does your handset do eSIM in Spain?



A locked phone is the most common reason a Spain eSIM install simply fails. Unlike some markets, most phones sold in Spain and the European Union arrive unlocked, because EU retail practice defaults to open devices. The phones that trip visitors up are contract handsets locked to a carrier in the United States, Canada, Japan or the Middle East.



**Check it:** iPhone, go to Settings, General, About, Carrier Lock. A "No SIM restrictions" line means no carrier is holding the device. Should it read "SIM locked", no third-party profile installs until the device is freed.



**Fix it:** request the unlock from whichever operator locked the device. Once the lock is removed, only then try installing your eSIM. If you bought a used or grey-market phone, assume it may be locked and check before you fly.

## What do visitors ask about Spain eSIM carriers?



### Can international visitors buy a prepaid eSIM from Movistar?



Yes. Movistar sells a prepaid eSIM through the Mi Movistar app or a store, with entry tiers around 10 EUR for 40 GB. The catch is Spanish registration law: you must verify a passport before the profile is released, and a physical-to-eSIM swap can carry a one-time fee unless you use iPhone Quick Transfer.



### Does Orange still exist after the MásMóvil merger?



Yes. Orange Spain merged with MásMóvil in 2024 to form MásOrange, but the Orange brand remains the main consumer face of the group, and Orange prepaid eSIMs are still sold under the Orange name through the My Orange app. The same group also runs Yoigo, Jazztel, Simyo, Pepephone, Lebara and Lycamobile.



### Does a Spain eSIM work elsewhere?



Yes, by law. Under the EU Roam Like at Home rules (Regulation EU 2022/612, in force through 2032), a Spanish prepaid eSIM works across the EU and EEA at no extra cost within fair-use limits. For example, Orange's Prepago 10 allows up to 26 GB of roaming out of its allowance. The United Kingdom is no longer covered automatically after Brexit, and Andorra and Gibraltar are outside the EU, so they may roam at a charge.



### Will a photocopy of my passport work in Spain?



For a Spanish carrier or budget brand, yes. Every Spanish SIM, whether physical or eSIM, must be tied to a verified identity under national law, and a passport is the document tourists use. International travel eSIMs register under the provider's home country instead, so they typically need no Spanish ID at activation. That registration waiver is one of the main reasons visitors choose a travel eSIM over a local one.



### Which Spain carrier should you pick: Movistar vs Orange?



Movistar, on the evidence. It led Ookla's 1H 2025 Spain report on 5G availability at 78.7% and on Consistency at 88.8%, the share of tests meeting a 5 Mbps down and 1 Mbps up threshold, which is the closest thing to a rural reliability measure. Yoigo borrows Movistar's rural network through a roaming agreement, so Yoigo prepaid users get similar interior reach.



### Do budget brands in Spain run their own networks?



No, they are brands that rent capacity from the four owners. Lowi runs on Vodafone, O2 on Movistar, and Simyo, Pepephone, Lebara and Lycamobile on the MásOrange group. Their coverage matches the parent network, but their registration rules can be stricter, and several ask for a Spanish ID and a Spanish card that a short-stay visitor may not have.



### What data quantity suits Spain?



Yoigo, on a per-gigabyte basis. Its prepaid tiers run about 7 EUR for 7 GB up to 20 EUR for 50 GB, and it inherits Movistar's rural roaming. Among the big three, Vodafone's Prepago S at around 10 EUR for 90 GB is the most data for the money, while Orange's Prepago 10 at around 10 EUR for 60 GB plus a bonus is the strongest all-round tourist tier.



### Movistar vs Orange 5G: which is better in Spain?



If your phone supports European bands it will. Spain's 5G uses n1, n3, n7, n28a (700 MHz), n78 (3.5 GHz) and n258 (26 GHz). The band that matters most outside cities is n28a, the low 700 MHz band that fills rural and indoor gaps; phones missing it still work in cities but lose the deepest coverage. Check your model on the [compatibility list](/compatibility/) before you fly.



### Handsets that struggle in Spain

Yes, and it is a sensible setup. Run calls and verification on your home line and Spanish data on the eSIM. Assign data to the Spanish eSIM line and leave the home line for voice. Two warnings apply: turn off roaming on the home line to avoid background charges, and expect verification texts to keep arriving on your home number rather than the data profile.



### What happens on the eSIM chip once a Spanish profile loads



The swap you would once perform at a Movistar or Orange counter is now a download, because Spain uses the same embedded-SIM standard as everywhere else. The carrier pushes a profile onto the handset's built-in chip, and the resulting line then behaves like any ordinary SIM in the phone.
