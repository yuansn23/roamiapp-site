## FAQ, briefly

The Maldives is not a country you drive across — it is a nation of coral islands scattered across 26 atolls, and your only links between them are speedboats and seaplanes. That geography decides everything about carrier choice.

- **Malé and Hulhumalé** sit on the most connected islands in the country. Both Dhiraagu and Ooredoo have dense 4G/5G here, and Ooredoo is frequently the faster of the two in the capital.
- **Resort atolls (South Ari, Baa, North/South Malé atolls)** are where Dhiraagu pulls ahead. Its network reaches more private resort islands and remote atolls, which is why most travel eSIMs and resort backhaul lean on it.
- **The transfers themselves have no coverage.** A seaplane or speedboat ride is a guaranteed dead zone — no bars, no data, no Wi-Fi. Plan to message your next hotel *before* you board, not after.
- **Local islands (Maafushi, Dhigurah, Thulusdhoo)** are served by both, but Dhiraagu again tends to reach the smaller guesthouse islands more reliably.

The honest summary: Ooredoo is the capital-city network; Dhiraagu is the everywhere-else network. Your itinerary — not a speed test in Malé — should pick the carrier.

## The local carriers: Dhiraagu and Ooredoo

### Dhiraagu — the wider footprint, the resort-island default

**Best for:** Anyone leaving the capital region — resort stays, local-island hopping, liveaboard trips, and anyone who wants the best odds of a signal at a remote atoll.

**Market position:** Dhiraagu is the older and larger of the two operators and historically the one with 4G+ reaching the highest share of inhabited islands — its own network reporting has put 4G+ coverage above 90% of inhabited islands. In Ookla's Maldives reporting it has consistently led the field on [video-call reliability and latency](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index) — the two metrics that matter when you are uploading GoPro footage from a dive boat or calling home from a water villa. It is licensed by the **Communications Authority of Maldives (CAM)** as a Tier-1 operator.

**How a visitor buys it, step by step:**
1. Go to the Dhiraagu tourist eSIM page and choose a plan.
2. **Upload your passport** and verify with a **selfie** — this registration is mandatory and strictly enforced.
3. Pay by an internationally working card (foreign-card declines are common; have a backup).
4. Receive a **QR code by email** and scan it under Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM.
5. Enable the eSIM as your data line and turn on data roaming before you board the seaplane.

**APN:** `internet` (the eSIM profile supplies this automatically; you should not need to touch it). Some Dhiraagu documentation also references `internet.dhimobile` — if a manual entry is ever required, `internet` is the standard data APN.

**Quirks:** no residence permit needed, but the foreign-passport registration step is non-negotiable; plan for a single-use QR; and remember CAM caps a foreign individual at **three mobile numbers per operator**.

### Dhiraagu vs Ooredoo: which Maldives carrier is faster?

**Best for:** Travellers basing themselves in Malé or Hulhumalé, and anyone who wants the strongest urban speeds for the first day or two.

**Market position:** Ooredoo is the second licensed Tier-1 operator and is genuinely faster than Dhiraagu in the capital region's dense 4G/5G. Its atoll reach is good but not as broad — it is the stronger pick *in town* and a reasonable pick on the popular resort islands near Malé, less so on the far atolls. Also licensed by **CAM**.

### Can you buy a Maldives eSIM from Dhiraagu?

1. **Passport is non-negotiable.** Under the SIM regulation CAM published in July 2023, [every mobile number must be registered to a verified individual](https://cam.gov.mv/sim_regulation.htm), a driver's licence or other ID is **not** accepted for foreigners, and CAM caps a foreign individual at **three mobile numbers per operator** — Dhiraagu notified customers of the same limit when the rule took effect in [August 2023](https://www.dhiraagu.com.mv/about-us/media-centre/announcements/changes-subject-to-sim-regulation-2023r-136). Fine for one traveller, worth knowing for groups.
2. **Tourist pricing is the only foreigner option.** Without a Maldivian residence permit you are steered to the pricier tourist tiers, not the local prepaid rates. Both carriers' tourist eSIMs run roughly US$30–45 for a 30-day bundle at the sizes above. A travel eSIM that pre-loads on Dhiraagu/Ooredoo is usually cheaper for short trips and skips the passport-upload step.

## Maldives-specific eSIM failures: four patterns

Very little of what goes wrong with a Maldives eSIM is a generic SIM error. The install sequence — adding the profile, scanning the code, labelling the line, switching on roaming — is set out step by step in our [activation guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/), and the ordinary error catalogue lives in the [activation error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). ### Maldives eSIM: full bars in Malé, no service on the atoll

This is the handover gap, not a defective profile. An eSIM pinned to Ooredoo that then travels out to a distant atoll can sit at "no service" simply because the handset has not reselected a tower. Open Settings → Cellular → Network Selection, leave automatic and pick Dhiraagu by hand — or carry a multi-network profile that reselects as you move. Nothing is broken; the phone is just attached to the wrong operator for where you are standing.

If a manual selection shows Dhiraagu with no data at all, the problem has moved from coverage to configuration — and on a tourist eSIM the profile should already have supplied the APN, so that is a carrier question rather than a settings one.

### Who asks for ID in Maldives SIM shops?

Dhiraagu and Ooredoo will not issue a tourist eSIM until a passport upload has been matched against a live selfie. Glare across the document, a cropped corner, or a name that does not line up with the booking hold the order at verification — which means no profile is generated, so there is genuinely nothing to scan. Chase the registration status with the carrier instead of assuming a lost email; both operators can see exactly where the check stalled.

The same rule catches groups: verification is per person, so four travellers need four passports and four selfies, not one upload copied across the booking.

### Is SIM registration mandatory in Maldives?

CAM allows a foreign individual three mobile numbers per operator, and a number left active from a previous trip counts against that ceiling. A traveller who kept an old Maldivian SIM alive, or who stacked a free trial on top of a purchased plan, can be turned away at registration with no obvious reason given. Check what is already registered to your passport before buying another number.

### Dhiraagu vs Ooredoo: coverage compared

Speedboat and seaplane legs are absolute dead zones: no tower watches open water, and resort Wi-Fi stays behind on the island. Losing the connection mid-transfer is expected behaviour, not a fault. Send messages, settle your next steps and download maps before you board; the profile reattaches by itself the moment the boat meets the far jetty.

## ID Rules for SIM Registration

The paperwork is where first-time visitors lose the most time, so it is worth knowing what the rules look like before you queue.

- **Every foreign number is tied to a passport.** CAM's SIM regulation requires in-person verification, and no driver's licence or other ID substitutes for a passport.
- **Three numbers per operator is a hard ceiling.** Fine for a solo traveller, awkward for a group buying several SIMs against one person's documents.
- **Tourist tiers only, unless you hold residence.** Without a permit you pay the tourist rate rather than the local prepaid rate, which is why a pre-loaded travel eSIM often works out cheaper for a short trip.
- **Airport counters need the same passport.** There is no walk-up anonymous purchase at Velana, so the queue saves you nothing if your documents are not ready.

### Where visitors buy Maldives eSIMs

- **Dhiraagu direct eSIM:** the passport-plus-selfie check is the choke point; once it clears, the QR arrives by email and scans cleanly.
- **Ooredoo direct eSIM or airport counter:** the same verification, plus a queue that builds after large arrivals — and the packs' bundled social data is wasted anyway once you are on a boat.
- **Travel eSIM:** nothing to upload, but it will attach to whichever of the two networks is nearest, so it inherits the atoll gaps described above.

## Maldives transfer days: seaplane and speedboat coverage gaps

Most connectivity problems in the Maldives happen on a transfer, not on an island. A speedboat from Malé to a nearby local island takes 20–90 minutes with zero signal; a seaplane to a far atoll runs 20–45 minutes of dead air; and a domestic flight to a remote island still leaves you offline over open water. The practical routine:

- **Before you board anything**, send your resort confirmation, transfer driver contact and next steps to your travel companion and to yourself.
- **Download offline maps** for the atolls you visit — offline map areas cover most inhabited islands.
- **Tell people at home you will drop out** for the crossing; do not promise a live call from the seaplane.
- **Time your messages to the islands, not the water.** Both ends of every transfer have signal; the middle has none. The moment the boat touches the jetty is when your eSIM reconnects.
- **Keep the eSIM on the strongest carrier at each end** — Dhiraagu at the resort, Ooredoo in town — or let a multi-network profile do it for you.

This is normal in the Maldives, not a fault. The eSIM is what keeps you reachable *between* the dead zones, not through them.
