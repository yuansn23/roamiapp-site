## eSIM-ready? Device checks for Singapore

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Singapore carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device specific quirks. All three are covered below.

### Singapore eSIMs on iPhone: supported?

Singapore's 5G is built on specific bands: **n78 (3500 MHz)** is the primary band all three carriers use for capacity, backed by **n1 (2100 MHz)** and **n3 (1800 MHz)**, with **n28 (700 MHz)** used to push coverage into buildings and underground. The LTE layer runs on B1, B3, B7, B8 and B28. Most international handsets sold in the last three years support these bands, but some regional variants miss n28, which is exactly the band that helps deep indoors.

You do not need to memorize the band tables. The reliable move is to check your exact model, model number not marketing name, against [compatibility lookup tool](/compatibility/). If you want the underlying mechanics first, [what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) explains the process.

### Which Singapore carrier should you pick: Singtel vs StarHub?
A locked phone is the most common reason a Singapore eSIM install simply fails, and Singapore's rule here is the opposite of what many visitors expect.

Singapore has prohibited SIM locking of devices sold locally since 1997, when the then telecoms regulator barred operators from locking phones, tablets and smartwatches imported and sold in the country. In practice that means a phone you buy from a Singapore carrier is unlocked by default. The catch is your own phone: a handset you bring from home may still be locked to its home carrier, and no Singapore operator can unlock it. That lock, not the local rule, is what blocks your eSIM.

**Check it:** on an iPhone open Settings, then General, About, and Carrier Lock. If it shows "No SIM restrictions" you are clear. If it shows "SIM locked" no third party eSIM will install until that changes.

**Fix it:** contact the carrier the phone is locked to and request an unlock. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and there is usually no fee. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. Full detail: [IMDA, Singapore's telecoms regulator](https://www.imda.gov.sg/).

## The APN step

APN settings are the last thing most travelers need to touch and the first thing worth ruling out when data fails. Below: the values for all three carriers, when manual entry is genuinely required, and the exact menu paths.

### APN values for Singtel, StarHub and M1 eSIMs

You will only need these if you bought an eSIM **directly from a Singapore carrier**. If you are roaming on a travel eSIM, the profile supplies its own APN and you should not change anything.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Singtel | `hicard` (or `internet`) | blank | blank |
| StarHub | `shwap` (or `shinternet`; prepaid `shppd`) | blank | blank |
| M1 | `sunsurf` (or `sunonet`) | blank | blank |

Leave username and password empty. These values are the commonly used ones for each network; the activation email or app that came with your profile will state the exact value if it differs.

### Carrier APN settings in Singapore

On a Singtel, StarHub or M1 tourist eSIM the profile configures its own APN, so treat manual entry as the exception rather than the setup step. It earns its keep when:

- A handset old enough not to fetch carrier settings on its own
- A profile installed from a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
- A carrier issued tourist eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
- Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile

### Singapore: when the APN won't auto-load
- **iPhone:** open Settings, Cellular, tap the eSIM line, Cellular Data Network, enter the APN
- **Android:** Settings, Connections, Mobile Networks, Access Point Names, add a new APN

Reboot the phone once you have saved. If data still will not flow, make sure it is the eSIM, not your home SIM, that is chosen as the data line before you touch anything else.

### Five fixes when your Singapore eSIM does not work

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our [eSIM troubleshooting resource](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The patterns below are the Singapore specific ones.

|:---|:---|:---|

1. Re check the lock status
2. Settings, Cellular, Network Selection, select Singtel, StarHub or M1 manually
3. Wipe network settings and restart the phone

**C. Signal bars, but no internet**
1. Hold the APN up against the table values
2. Set the Singapore eSIM as the data line rather than your home SIM.
3. Check you have not blown past a fair usage cap that throttles speed

**D. No data in Malaysia or Indonesia**
1. Confirm data roaming is on for the eSIM
2. Manually select the preferred partner network
3. Reboot and reset network settings if it still will not attach
