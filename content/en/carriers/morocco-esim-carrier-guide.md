## The Morocco eSIM carriers: Maroc Telecom, inwi and Orange Morocco

### Maroc Telecom vs inwi: which is better in Morocco?



**What it is best at.** Reach, and it is not close. Maroc Telecom took the highest Speed Score in Morocco at **68.20**, a median download of **53.72 Mbps**, a median upload of **13.28 Mbps** and the market's lowest latency at **43 ms**. Its consistency score of **89.2%** — the share of samples clearing 5 Mbps down and 1 Mbps up — is the single most useful number on this page, because it is a measure of how often the connection is usable rather than how fast it is at its best. 4. Label the line and set it as your mobile data line, with data roaming on for that line only.

## Registration: The Legal Side



Short answer for the overwhelming majority of travelers: no device-level registration. What Morocco enforces is SIM registration, not handset registration. Your phone's IMEI is not checked against a public visitor registry, and ANRT publishes no Turkey- or Indonesia-style IMEI whitelist that a tourist must join before using a Moroccan network. The device rules that do exist concern commerce and special equipment: handsets imported for resale need ANRT type approval (homologation), and **satellite phones and certain radio equipment are restricted and require ANRT authorization** — relevant only if you were planning to carry an Iridium or Thuraya unit into the desert.



Practical consequences:



- Bring your normal unlocked phone, register the SIM against your passport, and you are fully legal.

- Keep the registration receipt or the SIM packaging — you may need it for top-up help or a replacement.

- If your stay turns into residency, carrier-bought devices and longer-term contracts move onto ANRT-regulated territory where a Moroccan residence card substitutes for the passport.

- Rules evolve; if you are staying months rather than weeks, check the [ANRT website](https://www.anrt.ma/) before travel.



### Purchase options for Morocco eSIMs



The physical-SIM route is quick but not free of paperwork, and the paperwork is the part that catches people out.



1. **Carry the passport itself.** Registration is completed against the physical document; a photocopy or a photo on your phone will be refused at any compliant point of sale.

2. **Choose where you buy.** International terminals at Casablanca Mohammed V, Marrakech Menara, Tangier Ibn Battouta and Fes-Saïss all have carrier counters. So do carrier stores in the Ville Nouvelle districts of every city, mobile counters inside Marjane and Carrefour supermarkets, and the licensed teleboutiques and hanouts that display carrier logos.

3. **Expect five to ten minutes at the counter.** The vendor scans the passport biodata page and registers the line in your name, then inserts the SIM and checks for signal.

4. **Pick a plan at the same time.** Tourist packs bundle data with a few hours of national calls; standalone recharges come in 10, 20, 50 and 100 DH denominations.

5. **Test before you walk out.** Make a call, send an SMS, load a page — registration can take a few minutes to propagate.

6. **Keep the receipt.** You will need it for a duplicate or a number recovery, and without it a lost SIM is simply a lost line.



Registering a SIM is not optional. There is no legal anonymous prepaid line in Morocco: the identity check exists so that every number traces to an identified person, which is also why operators can and do cut off non-compliant lines without notice. That is the whole argument against the "ready to use, no passport needed" SIMs that circulate in tourist districts — the line is registered to somebody else, which means you have no right to a replacement, no claim to the number, and no protection if the number is misused.



### Do Morocco SIM sales always need a passport?



- **Street, riad and taxi-driver SIMs.** Pre-activated cards sold outside official channels are the classic shortcut and the classic problem: the registered owner is not you.

- **Airport kiosks versus city stores.** The airport is the fastest way to be online in the taxi, and the most expensive place to buy. The city store in the Ville Nouvelle is cheaper for the same product.

- **Language friction.** Counters operate in Arabic and French. "Passeport, s'il vous plaît" and a request for a "carte touristique" is usually enough; the carrier apps default to French.

- **Top-up mechanics.** Recharge works by buying a card or a printed receipt with a code, dialling the operator's USSD string, then converting that credit into a data bundle. If the menu is French-only, ask the hanout owner to do it — they do it a dozen times a day.



### Morocco's ANRT: what the regulator actually rules on



The Agence Nationale de Réglementation des Télécommunications is the licensing authority, the spectrum manager and the type-approval body, and it also runs the consumer complaint channel when an operator will not resolve a billing or quality dispute. It publishes quarterly sector observatories covering subscriptions, market share and penetration — the source of the subscription shares quoted earlier on this page. If you are staying long enough to care about a broadband installation or a contract dispute, [ANRT's published indicators](https://www.anrt.ma/) are the authoritative record.

## The Mobile Market, Mapped



This is the section that decides which Morocco eSIM carrier you should buy. The Atlas crossings and the southern roads are where the three networks genuinely diverge, because Maroc Telecom has spent decades building out the valleys and highway corridors that inwi and Orange treat as secondary markets.



What the coverage reality looks like on the classic routes:



- **Tizi n'Tichka pass (Marrakech to Ouarzazate):** usable coverage for most of the drive on Maroc Telecom, with predictable dead zones on the high stretches. inwi and Orange fade out earlier.

- **Dades and Todra valleys:** town coverage is solid on IAM, patchy to absent for the challengers between towns.

- **Merzouga and Erg Chebbi:** the village itself has signal — the remote camps beyond it generally do not. Plan your uploads for the evening back in town, and download offline maps for everything south of Ouarzazate.

- **High Atlas trekking (Toubkal, Azzaden valleys):** ridgelines occasionally catch signal; valleys between villages often do not. No carrier is a safety plan in the mountains.

- **Coastal detours (Essaouira, Agadir, the Anti-Atlas):** the Atlantic belt is well covered by all three; Agadir posts some of the fastest city speeds in the country.



If your Morocco trip continues overland, your eSIM stops at the border. Compare the [Algeria eSIM guide](/carriers/algeria-esim-carrier-guide/) for the eastern land route, the [Tunisia eSIM guide](/carriers/tunisia-esim-carrier-guide/) for the Mediterranean arc, and the [Egypt eSIM guide](/carriers/egypt-esim-carrier-guide/) if the journey continues to the Red Sea; a ferry to Europe means the [Spain eSIM guide](/carriers/spain-esim-carrier-guide/) is the one you want. Multi-country itineraries are exactly the case for a profile that can re-select the strongest local network automatically.



## Morocco on the road and rails: Al Boraq, taxis and offline maps



Morocco's rail network is the reason the classic north–south itinerary no longer needs a car. Al Boraq, Africa's first high-speed train, opened in 2018 and runs the Tangier–Kenitra high-speed section at up to **320 km/h** before continuing on upgraded classic track through Rabat to Casablanca — **Tangier to Casablanca in about 2 hours 10 minutes**, with departures through the day. ONCF's own figures for 2025 put the passenger total at **55.6 million journeys across a 2,295 km network**, of which **5.6 million were on Al Boraq**, running at 65% occupancy. The Kenitra–Marrakech high-speed extension is under construction, which is why ONCF periodically adjusts its timetable.



For data, what matters is that the rail corridor and the highway corridor are the same well-covered strip. A profile that holds Maroc Telecom from Tangier through Rabat and Casablanca is the profile that will hold when you transfer onto the Al Atlas service toward Marrakech — a further leg of roughly two and a half hours at conventional speeds.



Two caveats about on-board connectivity. Trains have power sockets in both classes, and first class buys you space rather than bandwidth, so treat the journey as a good moment to work offline. And because rail does not reach Chefchaouen, Essaouira, Merzouga, Agadir or Ouarzazate, every one of those legs is a road leg — CTM and Supratours coaches or a private transfer, both of which mean several hours of tunnel-free but signal-thin driving.



On the roads:



- **Petit taxis** are the metered city taxis. They are colour-coded by city, hold up to three passengers, and are restricted to the urban area. Ask for the meter before you set off; if the driver quotes a flat fare instead, decline and take the next car, which in any busy street is 30 seconds away.

- **Grand taxis** are the shared intercity cars, usually older Mercedes or Dacia Logans, that fill up at fixed ranks and depart when full. There is no meter, so agree the fare and whether you are buying extra seats for comfort before you get in.

- **Neither reaches into a medina.** A derb is narrower than a car, so a driver will drop you at the nearest gate. Pin the meeting point on the map before you book, especially for a riad pickup at night.

- **Offline maps are the Atlas insurance policy.** Download the regions for Ouarzazate, Tinghir, the Todra and Dades valleys, Merzouga and the Tizi n'Tichka corridor while you still have signal in Marrakech. A cached map works through every dead zone on the list above; a live one does not.



## Morocco ride-hailing, riad Wi-Fi and paying for things



Ride-hailing in Morocco has settled into a genuinely useful three-app market, and all of it runs on data.



- **Careem**, now an Uber subsidiary, is the most established: fixed price before you confirm, in-app card payment, and coverage that includes Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier, Agadir and Fes. It is the strongest choice in Casablanca.

- **inDrive** uses the bidding model Moroccans are comfortable with — you name a price, drivers accept or counter — and it is the most widely used app in the country, present in most major cities. Rides are generally paid in cash.

- **Uber** returned to Morocco in late 2025, starting with Casablanca and Marrakech, so coverage is real but thinner than the two incumbents, particularly at night and around the medina fringes.

- **Bolt, Heetch and Yango** also compete in the larger cities. Availability shifts, so check the store listing before you rely on one.



The practical consequence is that you need both cash and connectivity. inDrive expects cash; Careem expects a card; taxis expect cash; and every one of them needs a working data connection to show the driver where you are. In smaller towns, coastal villages and rural areas there is no ride-hailing at all, which is the point at which a petit or grand taxi becomes the only option.



**Accommodation Wi-Fi** is universal and uneven. Large international hotels generally deliver a stable line, often the incumbent's fibre — Maroc Telecom is the fastest fixed ISP in the country at 49.59 Mbps median. Riads are the interesting case: the same thick adobe and stone walls that keep a medina house cool in July are excellent at stopping Wi-Fi, so the strong signal in a riad lives in the courtyard and on the rooftop terrace and fades in the rooms at the back. Booking listings increasingly publish measured Wi-Fi speeds and guest ratings for it, and a guest score near 9 for Wi-Fi usually means the property has upgraded.



Three practical moves make this work:



1. Ask for a room adjacent to the courtyard rather than the far end of the building.

2. Do your call or your upload from the terrace, where you also get the best view.

3. Keep your own data line as the fallback, because the moment the riad's router drops you are in a stone box with no bars.



Cafés in Gueliz, Casablanca's business districts and central Rabat function as de facto workspaces, and Moroccan carriers sell dedicated domestic fibre and mobile plans for exactly this reason — Maroc Telecom's Jawal prepaid promos, for instance, run 3 GB for 59 DH a month up to 65 GB for 259 DH with 1 GB of roaming on the top tiers.



## Neighboring-Country Roaming



Morocco's European edge is 14 km away, and it changes your phone's behaviour in one step.



**Crossing to Spain.** The Strait of Gibraltar routes are short and frequent:



| Route | Crossing time | Notes |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Tarifa → Tangier Ville | Roughly 35–60 minutes | The shortest hop, and the only one that lands you in the city centre |

| Algeciras → Tangier Med | About 1h30 | Most departures per day; the vehicle-friendly route |

| Algeciras → Ceuta | About 1 hour | Ceuta is Spanish territory on the African side |

| Málaga → Melilla | About 5–6 hours | Melilla is also Spanish, reached from the eastern Andalusian coast |

| Almería → Nador | About 6 hours | The eastern gateway for the Rif |



Two details matter more than the timings. **Tangier Med is not Tangier** — it is a commercial port roughly 40 km east of the city, so allow a transfer if you are continuing by rail from Tangier Ville. And **Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish**, which means your phone hands over to an European network the moment you dock. A Morocco-only eSIM stops working at the gangway; that is the case for carrying a multi-country profile or a second Spanish plan, and the [carrier notes for Spain](/carriers/spain-esim-carrier-guide/) cover what the EU networks do there.



**The Algerian border stays shut.** The land frontier has been closed since August 1994, and Algeria severed diplomatic relations with Morocco in August 2021. The Oujda–Maghnia and Berkane–Talsint crossings are not open to passengers, so there is no overland route east from Oujda or Figuig. Travelers going on to Algeria fly, or route through a third country. If your plans do include Algeria, read the [carrier notes for Algeria](/carriers/algeria-esim-carrier-guide/) before you book anything.



## Start with your phone



Three checks, in order of how often they fail. First, **carrier lock**: a phone bought on contract in your home country must be unlocked before any Moroccan eSIM installs. Second, **bands**: Moroccan 4G runs on LTE bands 3 (1800 MHz), 7 (2600 MHz) and 20 (800 MHz), with 5G on n78 (3500 MHz) and n28 (700 MHz) — nearly every phone sold in the last five years covers these, so band problems are rare. Third, **eSIM hardware**: iPhone XS and later, Google Pixel 3 and later, and recent Samsung Galaxy models all work. Check your exact model in the [compatibility checker](/compatibility/) before you buy anything, and if you are curious what your phone is doing during install, [the activation explainer](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) covers it.



One Morocco-specific quirk: dual-SIM travelers should keep the home SIM active for SMS verification codes (banks love them) and set the Moroccan eSIM as the data line, with data roaming enabled on the eSIM only. Disable roaming on your home line before you land, or you will pay your home carrier's Moroccan roaming rates for background traffic.



## Activating your eSIM



Install before you fly. Airport Wi-Fi at Mohammed V works, but a profile you add in the arrivals hall competes with several hundred other tourists for the same access point. The generic sequence — scan QR, label the line, select it for mobile data, enable data roaming — is in the [eSIM activation walkthrough](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). The Morocco-specific additions:



- **Maroc Telecom eSIM:** place one outbound call after arrival; the line activates and the data allowance releases. If data still doesn't flow, confirm the eSIM is the selected data line and roaming is on for that line.

- **No manual APN needed:** Maroc Telecom provisions automatically. On a travel eSIM the same applies — the profile carries its own settings. Resist the urge to type APN values from a forum.

- **Network selection:** if the phone sits on "No service," force a manual network search and pick the carrier your plan uses (IAM, ORANGE or INWI appear under those names).

- **Signal but no data:** toggle airplane mode, then check that your home SIM isn't holding the data role. Between these, most first-day problems resolve without a support call.



Errors that survive those steps — a profile that won't download, a QR that's already been consumed, an eSIM that installs but never registers — belong to the general catalog in the [activation troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/).

### Which Morocco carrier should you pick: Maroc Telecom vs inwi?



- **Maroc Telecom Essentiel:** bought online or in an agency, delivered as a QR code, activated by one outbound call from Moroccan soil.

- **inwi:** issued at an official store or authorised channel after passport registration. No published online visitor flow.

- **Orange Morocco:** store-issued; traveller reports consistently describe the eSIM as counter-dependent, with airport kiosks mostly handing over physical SIMs.

- **Travel eSIM:** a QR code or a one-tap install link by email within minutes of checkout, self-service, and it registers on whichever Moroccan network your profile is built to ride.



### APN values for Maroc Telecom, inwi and Orange Morocco eSIMs



Morocco is unusual in how little APN work it asks of you. Maroc Telecom states plainly that its eSIM needs **no manual configuration** — the handset attaches to the internet automatically once the line is active — and travel eSIMs carry their own provisioning. So this article deliberately does not publish a table of Moroccan APN strings: the carrier's own support pages do not publish one for the visitor products, and a value copied from a forum is more likely to break a working profile than to fix a broken one. If a carrier does require values, the activation instructions that ship with your profile will state them.



Manual entry is still the right answer in three situations:



- An older handset that does not apply carrier settings automatically

- activation performed by typing a code rather than scanning the QR image

- A carrier-issued prepaid profile where automatic configuration did not run



Where the fields live:



- **iPhone:** Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network

- **Android:** Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → add a new APN



Save, then restart the handset. Before you change anything else, confirm that the eSIM — not your home SIM — is the selected data line.



### Five Morocco eSIM failure patterns, in the order you meet them



**A. The profile will not install**

1. Confirm the phone is carrier-unlocked.

2. Check the QR has not already been used — most are single-use.

3. Verify you have a data or Wi-Fi connection during the download; the phone fetches the profile from the carrier's server.

4. Ask the provider whether the profile is still in a pending state.



**B. Installed, but no signal bars**

1. Re-check lock status and that the eSIM is enabled in the SIM manager.

2. Force a manual network search and pick IAM, ORANGE or INWI by name.

3. Reset network settings, then reboot the phone.



**C. Bars, but no data**

1. The eSIM should be the default line for mobile data -- confirm it, not the home SIM.

2. Check that data roaming is enabled for the eSIM line.

3. On a Maroc Telecom Essentiel line, ask whether the first outbound activation call was actually placed.

4. Confirm the allowance has not run out — a suspended line looks identical to a misconfigured one.



**D. "SOS" or "Emergency calls only"**

1. Enable the eSIM in the SIM manager if it was switched off.

2. Disable or delete any other active profile that may be holding the radio.

3. Reboot and reset network settings.

4. Only then remove the profile and reinstall it — ask for a new QR first.



**E. Working, then suddenly slow**

1. Check the data balance before anything else — an exhausted bucket is the most common cause.

2. Move to an open square or a rooftop; medina stone and riad walls are the second most common cause.

3. Toggle airplane mode to force a fresh attach to a nearer cell.

4. Remember that Tangier and Oujda run high latency, so a video call can stutter on a line that speed-tests fine.

## Which route costs less



Morocco sits in the cheapest region in the world for mobile data, and it sits in the middle of that region.

Northern Africa as a whole is the cheapest region measured at **USD 0.86** average per gigabyte.

- On the visitor tiers, Maroc Telecom's 120 DH / 20 GB works out around **6 DH per gigabyte** and the 200 DH / 45 GB tier around **4.4 DH per gigabyte** — both at or below the national average, with no store visit and no queue.

- Heavy users should compare against Morocco's domestic prepaid pricing, which is a useful sanity check on what locals pay. Maroc Telecom's Jawal promos run 3 GB for **59 DH a month**, 29 GB for **119 DH**, 50 GB for **199 DH** and 65 GB plus 1 GB of roaming for **259 DH**.

- For the classic two-week, 10 GB trip, a local SIM or a carrier eSIM is cheaper per gigabyte, while a travel eSIM is cheaper in total time and hassle: no registration, no counter, no language barrier, and automatic re-selection when the network you bought is not the one standing next to you.



The honest comparison is not dirhams against dollars. It is the cost of the hour you spend finding a store, presenting a passport, choosing a plan in French and testing the line, weighed against a plan that is already on your phone when the wheels touch down.



## Carrier and eSIM questions for Morocco, settled



### Maroc Telecom vs inwi: coverage compared



Maroc Telecom, by a wide margin. It is the incumbent with the deepest rural build-out, and its 89.2% consistency score is the best in Morocco. In the remote camps around Merzouga no network is reliable; in the towns and valley roads IAM usually is.



### Morocco local SIM and travel eSIM compared



Yes, from Maroc Telecom: its e-boutique sells the tourist Essentiel eSIM offers (70–320 DH) online, and the profile activates with a first call once you are in Morocco. inwi and Orange are effectively store-purchase experiences for visitors.



### Buying a SIM in Morocco: ID rules



Yes. Moroccan law requires every SIM card to be registered to a named individual with valid ID, and a passport is the document travelers use. It applies at airport kiosks, carrier stores and resellers alike, for physical SIMs and eSIMs.



### Do you need to register your phone's IMEI in Morocco?



No visitor IMEI registration regime is published by the regulator as of this guide. The enforced controls are SIM registration and restrictions on special equipment such as satellite phones. Devices sold commercially in Morocco carry ANRT type approval, which is a matter for importers, not travelers with one personal handset.



### Maroc Telecom vs inwi: which Morocco carrier is faster?



Marrakech measured a median mobile download of 62.86 Mbps and Casablanca 58.31 Mbps in Ookla's 2H 2024 city data, with Maroc Telecom fastest in both. Consistency in both cities is above 91%, so the experience is stable even when it isn't spectacular.



### Morocco plans with the lowest prices



For data per dirham, inwi and Maroc Telecom's 120 DH / 20 GB tier are both competitive; Cable.co.uk puts the national average at about USD 0.63 per gigabyte. Note that data and calls are often separate products — buy a data pass explicitly.



### Morocco eSIM plans and prices



Roaming on your home plan works where your carrier has an agreement, but speeds and prices are usually far worse than a local or travel eSIM. A data eSIM that attaches to Maroc Telecom's network will beat almost any roaming arrangement on both.



### Maroc Telecom vs inwi 5G: which is better in Morocco?



Yes, commercially launched in the main cities, but 5G coverage is limited to urban pockets and most visitor time is spent on 4G, which is fast and consistent along the main axis. Don't buy a plan for its 5G label; buy it for coverage where you are going.



### Who provides mobile service in Morocco?



Not on a single-carrier profile — a Maroc Telecom eSIM attaches to IAM's network only. Multi-network travel eSIMs exist precisely for itineraries that cross between carriers' strong zones, which in Morocco means anywhere off the axis cities.



### Which Morocco eSIM suits your trip



Work the basics first: eSIM selected as data line, roaming on, airplane-mode toggle, manual network selection. A SIM that has been inactive without top-up can also be suspended — a recharge at any hanout or through the carrier app revives it. Anything else goes through the [activation error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/).



### Who leads 5G in Morocco?



All three operators launched commercial 5G on **7 November 2025**, about three months after ANRT awarded the licences. Orange Morocco said its launch covered more than 100 cities; the licence obligation is 45% population coverage by the end of 2026 and 85% by 2030.



### Maroc Telecom vs inwi prices compared



Yes. Maroc Telecom prices the eSIM identically to a physical SIM, and the QR code can be rescanned on multiple compatible devices at no extra cost. The only functional difference is the one extra step: the first outbound call that releases the line.



### Why is Tangier slower than Marrakech?



Latency, mostly. Tangier's median download of 45.29 Mbps is the slowest of the big cities, and its 66.3 ms latency is nearly double Marrakech's 38.8 ms. That gap shows up in video calls and gaming far more than in web browsing.



### Can I use a Morocco eSIM in Ceuta or Melilla?



No. Both are Spanish territory, so your phone attaches to an European network the moment you arrive. A Morocco-only profile stops there; you need a Spanish or multi-country plan for those days. The [Spain carrier guide](/carriers/spain-esim-carrier-guide/) covers the details.
