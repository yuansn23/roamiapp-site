## SIM and eSIM registration in Indonesia



Indonesia has required imported handsets to be known to its networks since 2020, and an unregistered phone can behave normally for a while and then stop connecting. Three things are worth separating, because travelers routinely confuse them:



- **Registering the SIM is not registering the phone.** They are two separate procedures. A registered prepaid line on an unregistered handset will still be cut off.

- **Tourists get a temporary route.** Customs guidance allows visitors staying **up to 90 days** to have the device registered through the mobile operator's own channel — which is exactly what an official airport counter or a GraPARI shop does when it asks for your IMEI alongside your passport. It adds two to five minutes.

- **Longer stays need the formal route.** Beyond 90 days the customs framework applies, with an allowance of about **USD 500** on the declared handset value and import duty above it.



None of this applies if you keep your home SIM on international roaming and use a travel eSIM for data — which is why an eSIM switch on a foreign handset bypasses the issue instead of solving it.



A second registration layer landed in 2026. **Permenkomdigi No. 7 of 2026**, in force from 19 January 2026, overhauled subscriber registration: from 1 July 2026 every new subscriber passes a **biometric facial recognition check** at the point of registration, and each identity is capped at **three prepaid numbers per operator**. Foreigners register with a passport, a KITAS or a KITAP. Telkomsel applies its own ceiling of three SIM cards and three eSIMs per passport on its tourist products. Two practical consequences: bring the passport you entered on, and do not expect to stockpile cards.



## Indonesia eSIM fundamentals: bands, biometrics and bundle windows



### Who has Indonesia's strongest 5G?



Indonesian 5G runs mainly on **n40 (2300 MHz)** and **n78 (3500 MHz)**, while 4G LTE sits on **B1 (2100 MHz), B3 (1800 MHz), B5 (850 MHz), B8 (900 MHz) and B40 (2300 MHz)**. Most recent international handsets cover those, but n40 is the band many Western models lack, so a phone that shows 5G in Europe may show 4G here. Check the model number against the [phone compatibility list](/compatibility/) rather than the marketing name.



### 2. Identity checks, biometrics and the three-number cap



Buying a local line means a registration, and since July 2026 that registration includes a biometric check. A passport is accepted for foreign visitors; a KITAS or KITAP is accepted for longer stays. The cap is three prepaid numbers per operator per identity, and Telkomsel applies the same ceiling to its tourist products. Cards sold by a random convenience store are often distributed inactive and only switch on after a verified identity check.



### 3. Fair-use thresholds and what happens after them



Most prepaid plans carry a fair-use policy, and after the threshold speeds drop to something between 256 kbps and 1 Mbps — enough for messaging, not for maps with live traffic. Note the shape of the allowance too: Smartfren's "30 GB" is half anytime and half midnight-only, and by.U's validity period changes between campaigns. Check both the threshold and the window before you buy.



### Purchase options for Indonesia eSIMs



The clock on an Indonesian prepaid package starts when the line first attaches to a local network, not when you pay for it. Some plans let you choose a start date. If you buy a card at the airport and do not insert it until the hotel two hours later, the countdown begins at the hotel. Buy the pack you will use on the day you will arrive.

## Indonesia eSIM by trip shape: seven islands, seven different networks



### Bali coverage: a southern beach stay



Seminyak, Canggu and Kuta sit in the strongest part of the island's map, where Telkomsel has the best average download and XL matches it on consistency. Beach clubs and villas are thick-walled, so expect to lose a bar indoors. Buy a generous allowance: at USD 0.28 a gigabyte there is no reason to ration, and the weather app is the most important thing on your phone during the wet season.



### Ubud and the northern hills: Bali coverage inland



Ubud itself is fine on every network; the trouble starts in the rice-field villas and the volcano-side guesthouses, where Telkomsel is the carrier most likely to hold signal. If your work depends on video calls, book accommodation that advertises fibre rather than relying on mobile data.



### Jakarta and the commuter belt: 5G and congestion



Jakarta has the country's best 5G and its worst congestion. Telkomsel's 47 ms latency is the figure that matters for calls in Sudirman, while XL often shows a stronger indoor signal in malls because of denser tower placement. Do not schedule a video call for 7 am on a Monday.



### Yogyakarta and the temple circuit coverage



Borobudur at sunrise and Prambanan in the afternoon are covered on all networks, and Telkomsel's wide coverage keeps ticketing apps and ride-hailing responsive in between. The pinch point is people rather than infrastructure: at sunrise the crowd on the temple terraces is competing for the same cells.



### Lombok and the Gili islands coverage



Mataram and the Lombok mainland are covered, and the transfer to the Gilis works until the boat is out of sight of land. On the islands themselves signal is patchy and often depends on which side of the island you booked. Treat the crossing as offline time and download what you need before boarding.



### Komodo, Flores and Labuan Bajo coverage



Labuan Bajo and the main Flores towns have usable 4G, and the dive-boat routes mostly do not. If you are diving Komodo, plan uploads for the evening; the marine park itself is one of the largest genuinely disconnected stretches on a standard Indonesian itinerary.



### Raja Ampat and Papua: Indonesia's remotest coverage



The most remote trip on this page. Sorong and Waisai have signal, and beyond them the practical internet is whatever your homestay or liveaboard provides. Take a satellite messenger if you are heading far from the towns, and treat mobile data as a bonus rather than a safety net.

## APN Configuration

## When an Indonesia eSIM misbehaves: four local failure modes



General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are catalogued in the [eSIM error troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The four patterns below are the Indonesian ones.



**A. A local SIM shows bars, then stops after a day.** The classic IMEI case. An unregistered foreign handset gets a temporary grace period on a local network and then loses access. Complete the operator-based IMEI registration with your passport and entry document, or move your data to a travel eSIM, which is not subject to the rule.



**B. Signal is there but mobile data never connects.** Almost always the APN. Confirm the correct value for the operator that issued the line, save it, restart, and give the phone a minute to reattach. This is the single most common silent failure on Indonesian prepaid.



**C. Data works in Denpasar and dies on the crossing to Lombok or Flores.** Coverage, not a fault. Open network selection and try each operator manually, then accept that the sea crossing is offline time and lean on offline maps.



**D. The package expires far earlier than expected.** Validity starts at first network attachment, not at purchase, and promotional bundles carry their own window. Check the remaining balance and expiry in the operator app — MyTelkomsel, myXL or myIM3 — rather than assuming the network has failed.

## Your eSIM myths, busted



### The registration routine in Indonesia



**✅ Fact:** You need the IMEI registered, but not necessarily at the airport: the operator-based route works at any official GraPARI or XL Center within your first 90 days, and it is free. Travelers using a data eSIM alongside a home SIM on roaming skip it altogether.



### ❌ Myth: A locked phone will work fine on an Indonesian eSIM.



**✅ Fact:** It will not. A carrier lock rejects third-party eSIM profiles whatever the hardware supports, so the lock has to be cleared with the original carrier before you buy anything.



### ❌ Myth: All Indonesian eSIMs use the same network at the same speed.



**✅ Fact:** Providers partner with different operators. Telkomsel leads on speed and reach, XL on consistency and video, and Indosat on price in Java's cities. In a country this size the network you land on matters more than the brand on the checkout page.
