## Yettel, Magyar Telekom and One: what the 1H 2025 measurements found

### Yettel vs Magyar Telekom: which Hungary carrier is faster?

Yettel's H1 2025 numbers lead the report on both speed tiers: median 5G download of **369.08 Mbps** with 37.18 Mbps up, a 4G median of 68.40 Mbps, and the best consistency in the country at **94.6%** of samples clearing 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up. In plain terms: the fastest network is also the one that holds up most reliably outside the capital — which is why it appears at the top of both the city and the countryside rows above. Yettel is majority-owned by PPF Group and runs the former Telenor Hungary network, with a long-established rural footprint to show for it.

### Magyar Telekom: the availability and video leader

Magyar Telekom, the country's Deutsche Telekom arm, wins the metrics that describe **how often you are on 5G at all**: 50.8% 5G availability, the highest in Hungary, plus the best mobile video experience score at 91.23. Its median 5G download (101.82 Mbps) trails Yettel's headline number, but availability and video quality are what you feel during an actual streaming session or a video call from a moving train. For remote workers spending a month in Budapest or Debrecen, Telekom's profile fits the workload best.

### One eSIM: Hungary's gaming and value challenger

One (backed by 4iG) is Hungary's third network and its Ookla story is latency: the best 5G gaming experience in the country, with a median 5G download of 66.84 Mbps in the report period. It is the network locals pick for price-sensitive plans, and for a traveller it is a perfectly good third choice — genuinely useful in towns like Szeged and Pécs, thinner between them.

## EU roaming rules, and what they change for your eSIM users

Hungary is an EU member, and that matters for who needs what. A SIM or eSIM issued in another member state roams here at domestic rates under the "roam like at home" regime — the European Commission's [roaming page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/internet-telecoms/mobile-roaming-costs/index_en.htm) sets out the rights and the fair-use limits. Travellers from the UK, the US and elsewhere enjoy no such automatic rights, and home-carrier roaming in Europe is where bills get ugly.

That is the niche the travel eSIM fills: a Hungarian or regional European profile, bought and installed before departure, that attaches to Yettel, Telekom or One on arrival at local rates. Two practical notes: data-only travel eSIMs do not come with a Hungarian phone number (Hungarian SMS verification is rarely needed by tourists), and the EU's roaming rights do not extend to a non-EU provider's plan — check whether your provider's regional plan covers only the EU bloc or includes your neighbours as well.

## How Much Data Do You Need?

Hungary's tourist infrastructure runs through a handful of apps, and they set the real data budget.

**Getting around.** Budapest's BKK tickets and passes are sold in the official app with QR validation at the gate — a live connection at the barrier is part of the routine. Inter-city rail to Vienna, Bratislava and Debrecen is covered well on all three networks, so mobile tickets and the MAV app behave on the move.

**Thermal baths and museums.** The big baths (Széchenyi, Rudas) and the main museums all push advance online booking with time slots, and walk-up capacity in high season is not something to bet an afternoon on. Booking over the hotel Wi-Fi and presenting the QR from your phone's offline copy is the safe pattern.

**Day trips.** The Danube Bend (Szentendre, Visegrád, Esztergom) and Lake Balaton are the classic escapes, and coverage along both routes is solid — Yettel's consistency numbers were earned largely on exactly these corridors. Between towns, brief 4G blips are the worst of it.

One quirk worth knowing: Hungarian carrier networks do not fall back to each other, so a single-network plan can sit on a weak signal in a valley while a stronger tower is visible across it. That is precisely the failure mode a multi-network [eSIM for Hungary](/hungary-esim/) removes.

## Fixing a Hungary eSIM that will not connect

**Install fails.** Carrier lock is the first suspect, then a QR code that has already been used. A handset bought on contract in another EU country is frequently the problem.

**Installs, no bars.** Put the phone into flight mode for fifteen seconds, then choose by hand from Yettel, Magyar Telekom or One. One cause is worth ruling out early: a handset that speaks only 3G shows this exact symptom in Hungary, and no setting will repair it.

**Signal but no throughput.** Three things to verify in order — that the eSIM rather than the home line is carrying mobile data, that roaming is switched on for that line, and, on a locally bought tariff, that the APN matches the operator's.

**Emergency calls only.** Start by removing the second active profile, then restart. Reset network settings only if that changes nothing, and reinstall as a very last step.
