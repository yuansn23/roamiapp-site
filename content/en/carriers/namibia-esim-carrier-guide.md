## The eSIM trap nobody mentions

Most "Namibia eSIM" products sold online are **TN Mobile roaming profiles**, not MTC. Here is the documented reality (checked against operator and provider pages, 2026):

- **Airalo's Namibia eSIM connects to the TN Mobile network** — [its own plan listings state it](https://esims.io/en/providers/airalo/10291662158133). Nomad's provider documentation likewise names TN Mobile, and several providers do not name a network at all.
- **MTC supports eSIM for its own customers** (a QR code issued in an MTC store, on iPhone 15+ / Galaxy S20+), but its tourist package is a physical SIM sold only at Hosea Kutako International Airport. There is no tourist eSIM from MTC. (MTC also sells a product called "Travel eSIM" — that one is for Namibians travelling *abroad*, not for use inside Namibia.)
- **A few niche providers do include MTC** — one budget provider's Namibia plans [ride the MTC network](https://traveltomtom.net/destinations/africa/namibia/best-esims-for-namibia) per its published plan details — but they are the exception, and speeds on most Namibia travel eSIMs cap at 3G regardless.
- **TN Mobile's rural coverage is limited** — fine in Windhoek, Swakopmund, Walvis Bay; thin to absent on the long gravel roads.

The consequence: if your plan is a self-drive loop, a standard travel eSIM gives you city signal and gaps everywhere your trip actually happens. A multi-network eSIM that includes **MTC** is the only way to get MTC's footprint through a downloaded profile.

Read that last paragraph twice before checkout. It is the difference between being online at the Etosha gate and being offline for the four days you actually came for.

### MTC vs TN Mobile 5G: which is better in Namibia?

Best for: Everything beyond the city limits — Etosha, Sossusvlei, the Caprivi/Zambezi strip, the Skeleton Coast approach.

Market position: Founded in 1994, MTC is the country's biggest mobile operator with [more than 2.5 million customers](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/mtc-launches-5g-commercial-services-in-namibia/) — roughly nine in ten mobile connections in the country — and its footprint is the one that follows the highways and towns into the hinterland. It launched 5G on 24 August 2025, [the first operator to do so in Namibia](https://thebrief.com.na/2025/08/mtc-launches-5g-network-in-namibia/) — but only in four towns: Windhoek, Swakopmund, Walvis Bay and Ongwediva. That is the entire 5G footprint. Everywhere you go on safari is 4G/LTE or less.

**Tourist plan table (from MTC's own tourist-package page):**

| Package | Data | Validity | Price (NAD) | Approx USD | Extras |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Leisure | 10.1 GB | 14 days | N$349 | ~US$19 | N$65 voice/SMS credit |
| Premium | 20.1 GB | 30 days | N$659 | ~US$35 | N$125 voice/SMS credit |

Details per [MTC's tourist packages page](https://www.mtc.com.na/tourist-package), which also notes the starter SIM is exclusively available at Hosea Kutako International Airport and is activated by dialling `*148#` or `*682#`. Outside the airport, MTC's cheaper everyday "Aweh" bundles are sold at city stores and petrol stations — but every SIM must be registered in person with a passport.

**Step by step: how a visitor buys one:**
1. Land at Hosea Kutako and find the MTC kiosk in baggage claim (a fuller shop sits in the entrance hall — the entrance hall carries the cheaper everyday bundles too).
2. Present your passport for in-person registration — mandatory under Namibian rules.
3. Choose Leisure (14 days) or Premium (30 days); pay cash or card.
4. Insert the SIM; the tourist bundle is activated by dialling `*148#`.
5. If you missed the airport counter, buy a starter SIM in town and complete registration at an MTC office — slower, and bring proof of accommodation.

APN: `internet` (auto-supplied on eSIM; `ppsinternet` also appears in some MTC documentation).

Quirks: the airport kiosk has been known to close before the last evening arrival; multiple bundles do not stack their validity — each runs its own 14 or 30 days.

Best for: Business travellers and long-stay renters in Windhoek/Swakopmund wanting fixed-wireless or 4G; not a roaming eSIM option.

## Can tourists buy a SIM or Namibia eSIM?

- Registration is mandatory and enforced. SIM-registration rules under the Communications Act were gazetted in March 2021, operators had to register every new SIM before sale from January 2023, and existing customers faced a 31 March 2024 deadline — Telecom Namibia [deactivated 191,598 unregistered prepaid SIMs on 1 April 2024](https://telecom.na/media-centre/776-telecom-namibia-deactivates-unregistered-prepaid-sim-cards-in-accordance-with-regulatory-requirements). Nationally, the regulator reported [702,246 SIMs at risk of disconnection and a 78.26% registration rate](https://thebrief.com.na?p=11290/) at the deadline. Bring your passport; registration is done in person at a licensed outlet.
- Foreign-card friction: airport and city counters take major cards, but some top-up flows are cash/local-card oriented. Carry some NAD.
- **MTC tourist SIM** is sold at Hosea Kutako airport (kiosk in baggage claim, fuller shop in the entrance hall — the entrance-hall shop also has the cheaper Aweh bundles). The baggage-claim kiosk sells only the tourist packs.
- Travel eSIM skips the passport counter and the queue, but — repeat — usually rides TN Mobile, not MTC.
- **Starlink is not a fallback.** CRAN **rejected Starlink's licence application in March 2026** (it failed the statutory requirement for at least 51% Namibian ownership, among other criteria). Do not plan a trip around lodge Starlink; if a property claims it, that is between them and the regulator.
- **The Starlink story is not over, but do not count on it either.** Starlink said it would appeal, and by mid-2026 the regulator had received [624 public reconsideration applications](https://observer24.com.na/cran-gets-624-starlink-reconsideration-applications) alongside a formal request from the company. Until CRAN decides otherwise, satellite internet is not a licensed consumer option in Namibia — plan on terrestrial networks only.

## Phone compatibility for a Namibia eSIM

Namibian networks run conservative, widely compatible bands, so most unlocked international phones work — the failure modes are the usual eSIM suspects rather than band mismatches:

| Device | Symptom | What to do |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Mainland-China iPhone models | No "Add eSIM" option at all — hardware disabled in that market | Not fixable; use a physical SIM or another device |
| Carrier-locked phones | eSIM installs but never registers, or install fails outright | Get the unlock from the original carrier before you fly |
| Dual-SIM users | eSIM installed but no data flowing | Set the eSIM as the mobile-data line; disable data roaming on your home SIM |
| Older 3G-only handsets | Signal but poor speeds; some travel eSIMs cap at 3G anyway | Expected on cheap travel eSIMs — a local MTC SIM will be faster |
| Rugged/older GPS units | No eSIM support | Consider a physical SIM at the airport counter instead |

When in doubt, run your exact model through the [compatibility page](/compatibility/) before buying anything — in Namibia the cost of discovering a problem is measured in hundreds of kilometres, not minutes.

One band note worth knowing: MTC holds 700/800 MHz spectrum, which is what carries its rural reach — that is why a signal can persist where towns are dozens of kilometres away, and why a phone that only supports higher bands will struggle long before the map says it should.

## Namibia eSIM coverage across the borders

Namibia's trip rarely ends at Namibia's edge, and your profile will not cross with you — coverage stops at the border post, and the neighbouring network only takes over if you have arranged for it. The crossings that matter most:

- Into South Africa: the Noordoewer/Vioolsdrif crossing on the Orange River (Cape route) and Nakop on the Trans-Kalahari/Aupass route. South African networks take over immediately; you need a [South Africa eSIM](/south-africa-esim/) or a regional plan.
- Into Botswana: the Buitepos/Mamuno crossing on the Trans-Kalahari Highway and the Ngoma gate near Kasane. Same story — a [Botswana eSIM](/botswana-esim/) is a separate purchase, and the corridor towns between are where you will get your last Namibian signal.
- Into Zambia (Katima Mulilo/Wanella bridge) and Zimbabwe: the Zambezi strip crossings change networks again; coverage on the far side is thinner still.

Practical pattern: buy per-country, or carry one multi-country eSIM across the whole route — a Namibia-only profile goes quiet at the border post, and it is the Fish River Canyon → Cape Town run that catches travellers out most often. ## APN configuration, done once

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM directly from a Namibian carrier. A downloaded travel eSIM carries its own APN settings — leave them as they are.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| TN Mobile | `internet` | blank | blank |
| Paratus | `pp.internet` | blank | blank |

On a local SIM, settings usually arrive automatically via SMS when you first connect; if not, MTC users can request them by texting GPRS to 1251. Leave username and password empty.

APN screens, by platform:
- iPhone: Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network
- Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names

Save the change, then reboot. If data has still not appeared, make sure the eSIM — not your home SIM — is the line selected for mobile data.

## Troubleshooting a Namibia eSIM: the failures that are actually local

Namibia's eSIM problems are less about the SIM than about which network the SIM happens to be standing on. The install mechanics — adding the profile, scanning, labelling the line, enabling roaming — are covered in our [step-by-step activation walkthrough](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/), and ordinary faults sit in the [eSIM troubleshooting resource](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). Four situations account for almost every Namibia-specific failure.

### Install guide for Namibia eSIMs

This is the roaming trap, not a fault. The travel eSIM you bought most likely rides TN Mobile, whose reach thins to nothing on the long gravel roads — exactly where the trip happens. Confirm which network your profile actually uses; if it is TN Mobile and your route is a self-drive loop, move to an MTC-inclusive multi-network plan instead of troubleshooting a phone that is behaving correctly. On a gravel C-road stretch, "no service" is also simply accurate: there is no tower, and that is normal in Namibia rather than something to repair.

### Can you buy a Namibia eSIM from MTC?

Namibia is sometimes excluded from "Africa-wide" regional packs, and a code consumed on another device is spent — activation QRs normally work once only. Before blaming the handset, confirm the plan genuinely lists Namibia and that the QR has not already been scanned. A profile for the wrong country installs perfectly and then never registers.

### APN values for MTC, TN Mobile and Paratus eSIMs

MTC and TN Mobile both use `internet`, but Paratus reports `pp.internet`, and a hand-edited profile can easily end up pointed at the wrong one — common when a local SIM and a travel eSIM share the same phone. Compare the APN screen against the table above, then confirm the eSIM, not the home SIM, is the line carrying mobile data. On a local MTC SIM the settings usually arrive by SMS on first connection; texting GPRS to 1251 re-requests them.

### What ID do Namibia SIM buyers need?

SIM registration has been mandatory since the March 2021 rules and enforced hard: Telecom Namibia deactivated 191,598 unregistered prepaid SIMs on 1 April 2024. Registration is completed in person with a passport at a licensed outlet, and a SIM that was never registered simply stops working — that is not a coverage problem, and no settings change brings it back. Bring the passport to the counter and do not defer it.

## The registration counter experience

- **A passport, presented in person.** Registration cannot be finished online or on someone else's behalf.
- **Proof of accommodation, when you register in town.** City MTC offices ask more often than the airport kiosk does.
- **Cash as a fallback.** Airport and city counters take major cards, but some top-up flows are cash or local-card only.
- **A note of the kiosk's closing time.** The Hosea Kutako kiosk has been known to shut before the last evening arrival, which makes a late landing a poor moment to depend on it.
- **Your bundle's own clock.** Leisure runs 14 days and Premium 30, and a second pack does not extend the first.
- **The starter SIM's location.** MTC sells its tourist starter at Hosea Kutako airport only, while the cheaper everyday "Aweh" bundles sit in city stores and petrol stations.

### Two handover points where a Namibian SIM stalls

- **MTC physical tourist SIM:** airport-only for the tourist packs, passport registration required, and activated by dialling `*148#` — miss the kiosk and it becomes a town errand.
- **Travel eSIM:** installs in seconds with no counter at all, but usually rides TN Mobile, which makes it a city-and-coast tool rather than a safari one.
- **Multi-network eSIM that includes MTC:** the only downloaded profile that reaches MTC's rural footprint; verify the provider's network list before checkout rather than after.

## The short version, question by question

### Rural coverage in Namibia: MTC vs TN Mobile

MTC, by a wide margin — it is the only operator with a real rural and highway footprint, holding roughly nine in ten mobile connections. TN Mobile is strong in cities; Paratus is urban/business only.

### Getting hold of a Namibia eSIM

Not directly. MTC supports eSIM for its own customers (QR in store) but sells its tourist bundle as a physical SIM at Hosea Kutako airport only. To get MTC's footprint through a downloaded profile, use a multi-network travel eSIM that includes MTC.

### Do travel eSIMs use MTC in Namibia?

Most do not. Airalo's plan listings name TN Mobile, and Nomad does the same; one niche budget provider rides MTC, but it is the exception. If MTC coverage is what you need for the self-drive, check the provider explicitly includes MTC.

### Local SIM vs travel eSIM: Namibia edition

Both, ideally. Install an eSIM before departure so you land with data for the arrival shuffle, then decide: the MTC tourist pack at Hosea Kutako is the simplest way to hold MTC's rural footprint, while city MTC offices can register a SIM if you missed the airport counter.

### What ID will a Namibian SIM purchase ask for?

Yes. SIM registration has been mandatory since the March 2021 rules and was enforced in April 2024, when nearly 192,000 unregistered Telecom Namibia SIMs were deactivated. Bring your passport; registration is done in person.

### Where is 5G strongest in Namibia?

Only in four towns — Windhoek, Swakopmund, Walvis Bay and Ongwediva — since MTC's August 2025 launch. Everywhere else, including every safari destination, is 4G/LTE or less. Any "5G Namibia eSIM" marketing claim should be read accordingly.

### Will my Namibia eSIM work in Etosha or Sossusvlei?

At the lodges and main gates, often yes (MTC). On game drives and the long gravel roads, frequently no. Download offline maps and plan to be unreachable for stretches — that is normal in Namibia.

### Is 20 GB overkill for Namibia?

The MTC Premium tourist pack (20.1 GB / 30 days) comfortably covers two weeks of maps, messaging, lodge Wi-Fi backup and photo uploads — provided you are on MTC. A 5 GB TN Mobile travel eSIM will feel tight and will vanish anyway once you leave the towns.

### Do I need a satellite messenger?

For the major tourist routes (Etosha, Sossusvlei, the B-roads), no — towns and lodges give you regular signal windows. For deep Kaokoland, the Skeleton Coast north of Torra Bay or the empty south, a satellite device is the honest backup, because there is no network to strengthen.

### Is Starlink available at lodges?

CRAN rejected Starlink's licence in March 2026, so it is not an authorised fallback. Some lodges may claim it; do not build your connectivity plan around it.

### Traveling onward with your Namibia eSIM

Technically yes — unlike Myanmar, Namibian networks accept international roamers — but roaming data on a safari route is brutally expensive and often metered at 2G-like speeds. A local SIM or travel eSIM is far cheaper; keep the home SIM for SMS verification with its data roaming switched off.

### How to top up a Namibia eSIM

Yes. Additional Leisure or Premium bundles can be bought by dialling `*148#` again, but each bundle runs its own validity — buying a second one does not extend the first. Top up at city MTC stores or partner kiosks if the USSD flow rejects a foreign card; cash is the reliable fallback.

### Does my Namibia eSIM work in Botswana or South Africa?

No — a Namibia-only profile stops at the border post. For the Trans-Kalahari run or a Fish River Canyon to Cape Town route, buy a plan for the next country or use one multi-country eSIM that covers the whole route.
