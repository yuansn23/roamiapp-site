> **Inside Finland's mobile networks**

>

> **The second-best-connected mobile market on Earth — and what its carrier data means for your trip**



A Finland eSIM drops you into one of the world's best mobile markets, and this guide covers the part that actually trips visitors up: getting onto the network. If a country were ever built for eSIMs, it is Finland. In Opensignal's Global Network Excellence Index for Q1 2025, Finland ranked **second in the world** for overall mobile network experience — ahead of every country except South Korea. In the [Ookla Speedtest Connectivity Report for Finland, 1H 2025](https://www.ookla.com/research/reports/finland-speedtest-connectivity-report-h1-2025), **DNA** took the Best Mobile Network and Best 5G Network titles with a median 5G download of **242.64 Mbps**, while Elisa and Telia were close behind on 5G speed. And 1 GB of local data costs about **USD 0.52**, less than a fifth of the global average. Your Finland eSIM inherits all of this — the real question is not whether you will have fast data, but which of the three carriers your eSIM should attach to in the places you are actually going. Treat this as the pre-flight Finland briefing — the one we would send a friend, jargon removed.



**Quick answer:** Pick Elisa for the widest footprint, DNA for the lowest cost per gigabyte, and install your eSIM before departure either way. Speeds, prices and registration rules later in this Finland guide explain the fine print behind that split.

**The one-paragraph answer:** Chasing the fastest network for remote work or streaming in Helsinki? DNA leads every headline metric — median 135.95 Mbps overall, best gaming and video scores, and the most Opensignal awards (12 of 14 in the May 2025 report). Sticking mostly to cities like Tampere and Turku? Telia's 5G median of 215.69 Mbps is nearly DNA's and it won Opensignal's Video Experience award. Heading north to Lapland or around the lakeland? Elisa's network is the traditional pick in the sparsely populated north and east, where it shares coverage with nobody. Most short-stay visitors skip the choice entirely: a [multi-network Finland eSIM plan](/finland-esim/) attaches to whichever carrier is strongest where you stand — try the [test at no charge](/free-esim/) first if you want proof before paying.

### What Traficom measures — and Finland's 3G shutdown you inherit



Finland's regulator does not just license spectrum; it drive-tests the result. [Traficom has run its own coverage measurements in Eastern Finland](https://traficom.fi/en/news/traficom-has-investigated-coverage-mobile-communication-network-eastern-finland) and again in South Savo, the two regions that produced the most reception complaints, and it maps reports by address so that a complaint about one house becomes one measurement. For a visitor the useful output is not the map but the shape of the transition:



| What happened | Detail | What it means for your handset |

|:---|:---|:---|

| 3G is gone | The three operators completed their 3G shutdowns in 2023 and 2024, done regionally and deliberately rather than in one night | A 3G-only phone falls back to 2G here, not to 4G |

| 2G is staying | The licence decision requires nationwide 2G to keep running to the end of 2029, with a year's notice to Traficom before any closure | An old phone still makes calls — at 2G data speeds |

| Spectrum was re-farmed | Frequencies freed from 3G went straight into 4G and 5G capacity | The shutdown improves modern handsets rather than degrading them |

| Emergency calls are protected | 112 reaches any available network, with or without a SIM card | You never need a working plan to dial 112 |



[Traficom's account of the switch-off](https://traficom.fi/en/news/traficom-tackles-3g-network-shutdown-problems-together-telecommunications-operators) names the one group that suffers: users whose devices work only on 2G or 3G. Finland kept 2G where most of Europe retired it, so the failure mode here is unusual — your phone will not go silent, it will simply crawl.



**VoLTE is the setting to check before you fly.** Finnish 4G and 5G carry voice over VoLTE, and roaming onto a Finnish network with a foreign SIM depends on your home carrier having a VoLTE roaming agreement in place. Telia publishes the technology matrix it works to across [EU and EEA partner networks](https://www.telia.fi/matkalla/eu-eta/kumppaniverkot), and every Nordic neighbour on that list shows VoLTE available. On iPhone, Settings → Cellular → your line → Voice & Data should offer LTE or 5G; on Android, look for "4G calling" or "VoLTE" in the SIM settings. If neither appears, calls fail even when data works normally.



## Finland eSIM coverage: Elisa vs DNA



Finland's carriers cover the population superbly and the territory unevenly — the country is large, two-thirds forest and water, and has one of Europe's lowest population densities. Where you fit in that equation decides your experience more than any carrier logo:



| Region | What to expect |

|:---|:---|

| Helsinki and the south coast | 5G on all three carriers across the capital region and along the E18 to Turku; Helsinki itself measured 185.48 Mbps median, the fastest city in the country. |

| Tampere, Turku, Lakeland towns | Strong 4G everywhere settled, 5G in the cities. Between the lakes the signal follows the roads — islands and lake roads thin out. |

| Lapland (Rovaniemi north) | Solid in Rovaniemi, Levi, Saariselkä and along the E75/E8; aurora-chasing side roads out of the resorts can drop to 4G or nothing within minutes. |

| Eastern border and wild lakeland | The most sparsely populated stretch of the country. Elisa's network has traditionally carried the far north and east; download offline maps regardless of carrier. |

| Åland Islands | Good on the main island from Finnish and even Swedish networks at sea; small skerries are patchy. |



One quirk of the aurora season specifically: winter visitors should not count on public Wi-Fi at glass-igloo resorts and wilderness hotels — mobile data is usually the only reliable link, so size your plan for it. And note that resorts marketed as "in the middle of nowhere" genuinely are: the networks cover the access roads, not the fells.



### Elisa vs DNA: which is better in Finland?



Every number on this page is an annual average. Lapland does not have an annual average — it has a season that multiplies the population of a village, then empties it again.



| Place | Base coverage | What changes between November and March |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Rovaniemi | Dense 5G across the city, with the airport and Santa Claus Village both covered | December turns Santa Claus Village into the busiest cell in Lapland; the coverage holds, but capacity is the limit |

| Levi (Kittilä) | 5G and 4G on the pistes and across the resort | Fringe valleys and the roads out toward the fells thin out; resort Wi-Fi competes with everyone uploading photographs at once |

| Saariselkä | A 4G/5G corridor runs up the E75 | It is a corridor in the literal sense — signal follows the road, and the fell terrain either side of it does not |

| Ivalo and Lake Inari | Long-range 700/800 MHz sites covering open tundra | Long range means low capacity: a lake that is quiet in October is a snowmobile highway in February |



Two mechanics matter more than any coverage map. The first is cold. Lapland between November and March runs from roughly −15°C to −35°C, and lithium-ion cells lose usable capacity fast at those temperatures — a phone showing 60% in a warm minibus can shut down within minutes of a snowmobile stop. Keep it against your body rather than in an outside pocket, and carry the power bank the same way. The second is location. Aurora viewing spots are chosen for darkness and distance from town, which means they are chosen for absence of signal: download the forecast, the map and your guide's number while you still have bars in the village.



The "no signal between fells" rule is literal rather than rhetorical. Elisa has traditionally carried the widest rural footprint in the far north, but nobody covers the interiors of Urho Kekkonen, Lemmenjoki or Pallas-Yllästunturi national parks, and the resort lobby at the edge of them is the last dependable signal you will see. Treat connectivity as a service the resort provides, not a property of the landscape.



### Finland lakeland and archipelago coverage: Saimaa, Åland, Turku



Finland's second geography is water, and water changes the rules three different ways.



| Water | How you move across it | What to expect |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Lake Saimaa and the eastern lakeland | Historic lake cruises out of Savonlinna, Kuopio and Lappeenranta; the Saimaa cycling routes use free cable ferries running 24/7 and paid bike ferries on a timetable | The established cruise routes hold 4G because they hug the shore and the towers sit on both banks; out on open water between islands, signal follows the nearest land |

| The Turku archipelago (Saaristo) | The Archipelago Trail — roughly 250 km, with a 110 km smaller version — stitched together from roads, bridges and ferry crossings; 35 ferry routes serve the islands and most crossings are free | The main islands are solid. Along the trail the only paid crossing is the summer-only Houtskari–Iniö ferry, and the smaller skerries it serves are the patchy part |

| Åland | Ferries from Turku, Naantali and Helsinki in Finland, from Stockholm, Grisslehamn and Kapellskär in Sweden, and from Tallinn in Estonia; Ålandstrafiken's public ferries hop between the islands | Mariehamn and the main island are well covered, but Åland is its own telecoms territory and runs on a local operator rather than the mainland three |



Åland is the genuine special case and the one worth planning around. The archipelago is autonomous and Swedish-speaking, with its own telecoms territory and its own operator, Ålcom, and domestic roaming agreements that let Elisa and DNA customers use that network. The practical consequence is that a plan whose coverage list says only "Finland" may or may not include the islands — confirm it before you rely on it, and if Åland is the destination rather than a stopover, make the plan name it explicitly.



The lake rule is simpler than the island rule: shore means signal, middle means patchy, and the settled islands behave like the mainland. A rented cabin on an outer skerry is the Finnish equivalent of a Norwegian valley — beautiful, and off the grid.



### Elisa vs DNA: coverage compared



Finland's rail network is the easy way to cover real distance, and it is fairly honest about its gaps.



| Corridor | Onboard | Where signal goes |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Helsinki metro and trams | Cell coverage on every station and through every tunnel section; the HSL app issues tickets as live QR codes that need data at the gate | Tunnels between stops drop briefly, then recover. Nothing to configure |

| Helsinki–Tampere | Dense 4G and 5G almost continuously | The reliable stretch of the network — you can work the whole way |

| Tampere–Oulu | 4G through the Ostrobothnian towns | Signal follows the towns and thins through the forest between them |

| Oulu–Rovaniemi and the overnight Santa Claus Express | Free VR Wi-Fi on the long-distance stock, and a power socket at every seat | North of Oulu the gaps between stations widen; the sleeper's Wi-Fi covers the dead hours |

| Any line running past a fell or across a long lake | — | Terrain wins. Cuttings and long water crossings are the usual dropouts |



Because VR's Pendolino Plus and InterCity stock carry free Wi-Fi and a socket at every seat, a dead mobile stretch is not a dead laptop — the routine is the same on a Norwegian ferry or a Lofoten bus: download at the station, work through the gap, upload at the next town. Helsinki is also rebuilding the connectivity underneath the metro, with Telia contracted to deliver a private 5G network for the METKA modernisation programme; that network exists to run the trains rather than your phone, but it tells you how the capital treats connectivity generally.



## Plans and Prices, Compared



Finland is one of the easiest prepaid markets in Europe: there is **no legal SIM-registration requirement**, and DNA states on its own prepaid pages that no registration or ID is needed. In practice, bring your passport anyway — some counters scan it for their own anti-fraud checks. What catches visitors out is distribution, not paperwork:



| Carrier | How visitors buy | eSIM for tourists | Watch out |

|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| Elisa (prepaid brand: Saunalahti) | Operator stores; kiosks sell physical SIMs | Sold in official Elisa stores, not kiosks | Unlimited-data tourist tiers from €29.99 reported by third-party guides |

| DNA | Operator stores, R-kioski, groceries | Sold in official DNA stores; online ordering available | Cheapest starter (~€4.90 with €5 credit) is physical only at kiosks |

Two structural notes. First, Helsinki Airport has no operator stores — only an R-kioski on the arrivals level — so airport purchases are physical SIMs, not eSIMs, with convenience-store-level service. Second, the cheap local MVNOs you may read about (Moi, Oomi and similar) require a Finnish bank account and are closed to visitors. If you land wanting instant data with no store visit, that is precisely the gap a travel eSIM fills: the [current Finland tiers](/finland-esim/) show the plans, and activation is covered step by step in [eSIM setup guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/).



**Direct carrier vs travel eSIM, in one line:** a Finnish prepaid SIM buys you unlimited-ish data and EU roaming rights at local prices — worth the store visit for stays over a few weeks. For a one-to-two-week trip, a travel eSIM delivered by QR code before you fly wins on time, and a multi-network profile can still land you on DNA in Helsinki and Elisa in Lapland without buying twice.

### Finland eSIM pre-flight check, in table form



Finland is the one Nordic market where the handset, not the network, is the likeliest point of failure — 3G is gone here while 2G survives, so an older phone behaves worse in Finland than almost anywhere else in Europe. Run the checks below before you fly.



| # | Check | What a pass looks like | Why Finland in particular |

|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| 1 | The handset does VoLTE | Voice & Data offers LTE or 5G, or Android shows "4G calling" | There is no 3G left to carry a fallback call: a non-VoLTE phone gets data and silence |

| 2 | No carrier lock | Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock reads "No SIM restrictions" | Handsets sold in Finland are unlocked by law; imported contract phones are not |

| 3 | eSIM support confirmed | `*#06#` returns an EID, or the [device check](/compatibility/) confirms the model | Finnish prepaid is overwhelmingly physical, so a travel eSIM is the eSIM route here |

| 4 | QR code and activation code stored twice | A screenshot on the phone **and** a copy in cloud storage | Neither an R-kioski till nor a carrier counter can reissue a travel profile |

| 5 | Roaming ON for the eSIM, OFF for the home SIM | The single most common miss in any roaming market | With Ålcom next door and Estonian networks across the Gulf, a home SIM left roaming will bill you happily |

| 6 | Åland named in the plan, if Åland is on the itinerary | The coverage list mentions Åland or Ålcom | A mainland-only plan goes dark in Mariehamn |

| 7 | Offline maps and the aurora app cached | Downloaded in the last town, not at the trailhead | Between the fells, and between the skerries, the phone is *supposed* to be dark |



## Coverage gaps to know about



Three checks, two minutes:



- **Bands.** Finnish 5G runs on n78 (3.5 GHz) for speed and n28 (700 MHz) for reach; 4G uses bands 1/3/7/20. Nearly every phone sold in the last five years handles these.

- **The 2G oddity.** Finland shut down its 3G networks years ago but kept 2G alive — the opposite of most of Europe. A very old handset that relies on 3G will behave worse here than anywhere else you travel. Anything 4G-capable is fine.

- **Carrier lock.** Phones sold in Finland are unlocked by law, but a handset bought on contract elsewhere may not be. Check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock on iPhone, or run your model through the [phone compatibility list](/compatibility/).

## When a Finnish eSIM stays dark: the four real causes



Run the standard install first — QR scan, label the line, set it as your data line, enable data roaming — from the [QR-install basics](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/). If it fails, the Finland-specific patterns, in the order they actually occur:



1. **Installed but "no service" on arrival.** Open Settings → Cellular → Network Selection, switch off automatic, and check what appears. You should see DNA, Elisa and Telia (often as TeliaSonera). Pick the carrier your plan uses; if you have a multi-network profile, re-enable automatic and let it re-scan.

2. **Signal, but no data.** Confirm the eSIM — not your home SIM — is the selected mobile-data line, and that data roaming is ON for the eSIM line specifically. Then check the APN against the table above if it was a direct-carrier purchase.

3. **Data stops mid-trip in the north.** Usually not a fault: you have left the coverage footprint, not lost your plan. Step back toward a road or village before assuming a technical failure.

4. **Everything else.** Work through the country-agnostic catalogue in [eSIM activation fixes guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/) — lock status, profile reinstall, network reset. For emergencies dial 112; it works on any reachable network, including with no credit.



### Finland eSIM no-service fixes: three checks in order of likelihood



**The profile installed cleanly, and still there is no service after landing**

1. Confirm the eSIM is switched on in Settings → Cellular

2. Point Mobile Data at the eSIM line rather than the home SIM

3. Turn data roaming ON for the eSIM line specifically

4. If nothing appears, set Network Selection to manual, wait for the list, and choose DNA, Elisa or Telia — Telia frequently appears under its legacy name, TeliaSonera

5. Reboot the handset and let it re-register



**Full bars, and nothing loads**

1. Re-verify that the eSIM, not the home SIM, is the selected data line

2. If this was a direct-carrier purchase, check the APN against the table above

3. Confirm a package is actually active — DNA's prepaid bills a daily charge and stops when the credit does, which looks exactly like a broken data connection

4. Toggle airplane mode for ten seconds to force a fresh attach to the cell



**Fast on the first day, crawling by the third**

1. On a travel eSIM you have probably reached the fair-use threshold; top up or change plan

2. On a direct prepaid, a lapsed daily charge is the usual cause — top up in the carrier app, at laturi.dna.fi or at any R-kioski

3. On a multi-network profile, persistent slowness in one place can mean the automatic choice is the weaker carrier: try manual selection and compare



**Not a fault: the signal vanishes between towns.** Finnish coverage follows people, not territory. Between Oulu and Rovaniemi, on a fell road out of Saariselkä, or across the water between skerries, the phone is doing precisely what the network was built to do. Move back toward a road, a village or a shore before you change a single setting.

## Questions and answers: Finland eSIMs



### Who operates the networks in Finland?



For raw speed, DNA — it took Best Mobile Network, Best 5G Network, the highest consistency (96.2%) and 12 of 14 Opensignal awards in the 2025 measurement cycles. But Telia nearly matches its 5G speeds in cities, and Elisa's coverage experience is the reference point in the north and east. For most visitors the correct answer is "whichever is strongest where you are," which a single-carrier plan cannot offer.



### Do Finnish carriers sell prepaid eSIMs to tourists?



Yes, all three sell eSIMs and prepaid to visitors — but the eSIM route is through official Elisa, DNA or Telia stores, not kiosks, and Telia's prepaid is otherwise a convenience-store product. There is no legal registration requirement, though carry your passport as some stores scan it anyway.



### Finland SIM purchase ID requirements



No. Finland has no mandatory prepaid SIM registration, and DNA's own prepaid pages confirm no registration or ID is required. That is one of the friendliest rules in Europe.



### Will my Finland eSIM work in Lapland to see the northern lights?



In and around Rovaniemi, Levi, Saariselkä and along the main E-roads, yes — the networks cover resorts, villages and access routes well. On wilderness roads and fell areas between them, expect gaps on every carrier. Download offline maps while you still have signal and treat connectivity as a resort amenity, not a given.



### Does a Finnish eSIM work in Sweden, Norway and Estonia?



Often partially — Finnish carriers include EU/EEA roaming allowances on their plans, capped by fair-use rules, and Finland, Sweden and Estonia all ride the same EU "roam like at home" framework. For a Nordic multi-country itinerary, compare a dedicated regional plan instead; our [Sweden eSIM guide](/carriers/sweden-esim-carrier-guide/), [Norway eSIM comparison](/norway-esim/) and [Estonia eSIM plans](/estonia-esim/) cover how each neighbor's networks differ.



### Elisa vs DNA 5G: which is better in Finland?



Among the fastest anywhere. Finland ranked 2nd in Opensignal's Global Network Excellence Index (Q1 2025), and DNA's 242.64 Mbps median 5G download is in the top tier of any national market Ookla measures.



### Elisa vs DNA prices compared



Local data itself is remarkably cheap — about USD 0.52 per GB by Cable.co.uk's measure — and Finnish prepaid unlimited plans start well below what tourists pay for roaming. For a short trip, though, a travel eSIM's fixed upfront price usually beats a store visit once you value your first afternoon.



### Can I keep my home number active while using a Finnish eSIM?



Yes. Set the eSIM as your mobile-data line and leave your home SIM for calls and SMS verification. Two cautions: switch off data roaming on the home line so it never bills you for background traffic, and remember data-only eSIMs cannot receive SMS — bank codes still arrive on your physical number.



### Does the Finland eSIM include calls and SMS?



Travel eSIMs are data-only; use WhatsApp, FaceTime or Skype for calls, and dial 112 in an emergency — it is free on any network even without credit. Direct Finnish prepaid plans do include calls and texts, which is one of their genuine advantages.



### Why does my phone show 'Sonera' in Finland?



Telia Finland operates on infrastructure with Sonera heritage, and some handsets display the legacy network name. It is the same network; nothing is wrong.



### What data sells for in Finland



Per gigabyte, no. Finnish data costs about USD 0.52 per GB, and DNA's unlimited Rajaton tiers start at €0.99 a day — no travel eSIM matches that rate. Per trip, frequently yes: you skip the store visit, the "activate in Finland first" rule and the physical SIM, and you are online when the wheels touch down. Match the tool to the length of the stay rather than to the per-gigabyte price.



### Using a Finland eSIM abroad



Yes. Finland sits inside the EU's roam-like-at-home area, so a SIM bought anywhere in the EU uses its domestic allowance here at domestic prices, with a fair-use safeguard if your home plan is unlimited or unusually cheap. The rules were written to run to 2032, and the [European Commission's roaming explainer](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-single-market/en/faq/question-and-answers-roaming) sets out the one exclusion worth remembering: price caps cover terrestrial networks only.



### Will my Finland eSIM work on the Helsinki–Stockholm or Helsinki–Tallinn ferry?



Only while the ship is within range of a land network. Roam-like-at-home applies to terrestrial networks, and a ship's own satellite link is not covered by the price cap — which is exactly the connection your phone grabs in the middle of the Baltic. Switch data roaming off for the open-water leg and back on in port.



## Using the eSIM abroad



Finland's borders are trivially easy to cross in person and slightly less simple for a phone, because each crossing changes both the network and the rules that apply to it.



| Neighbour | The crossing point | What happens to your eSIM |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Sweden | Tornio–Haparanda, where Finland's E8 meets Sweden's E4 and the two towns have merged into one built-up area | No routine passport check, since both are in Schengen. You do cross a currency line, from euro to krona, and a time line — Finland runs an hour ahead of Sweden — and your phone re-registers on a Swedish network within seconds |

| Norway | Kilpisjärvi and the E8 down to Skibotn, the road toward the fjords and Tromsø; Kivilompolo, Karigasniemi and Utsjoki further north | Norway is in Schengen but outside the EU, so this is a customs border as well as a network one. Roaming rights still apply, because Norway is inside the EEA |

| Sweden, far north | Karesuvanto–Karesuando, the crossing from Kiruna toward Kilpisjärvi and Enontekiö | Quiet, and a very long way from the next fuel stop or cell tower |

| Estonia | The Helsinki–Tallinn ferry | Estonia is in the EU, so EU roaming covers it on arrival — but the crossing itself is open water |



If your itinerary leaves Finland more than once, the practical decision is made before departure rather than at a border post: a Finland-only profile stops working the moment you cross, while a Nordic or Europe plan keeps the same data pool across all of it.
