The market has consolidated faster than almost any other in Asia, and the 2026 landscape looks nothing like most older travel guides describe it.

Three structural facts shape everything a visitor experiences. First, **CTM's dominance is overwhelming**: its parent CITIC Telecom's 2024 results show mobile subscribers up 16.3% to 771,000, with 98.4% of its own base already on 5G, in a territory of roughly 680,000 residents — the connection count only makes sense once you add the tourists, commuters and cross-border workers. Second, **5G arrived early here**: Macau issued its 5G licences in late 2022 to exactly two operators, CTM and China Telecom, and both built out urban coverage aggressively. Third, the territory is physically tiny — the whole place fits inside a few square kilometers of dense urban fabric — so a network either works everywhere or fails publicly; there is nowhere to hide.

### CTM: the incumbent that absorbed its rivals

CTM is what a visitor ends up on unless they go out of their way not to be. It runs the territory's fastest measured network (176.04 Mbps median download), the deepest fixed-line backhaul behind the resorts, and the largest retail footprint — nine shops across the Peninsula and Taipa, open seven days a week from mid-morning to half past seven in the evening. Its 5G is marketed as **5.5G**, and 98.4% of its own subscriber base is already on it.

- **What it is best at:** citywide speed and the consistency that comes from running most of the territory's traffic.
- **Visitor route to a CTM line:** prepaid SIM or eSIM from a CTM shop, the airport counter or an authorised reseller, with passport registration at the point of sale.
- **What goes wrong:** the shops keep retail hours — walk up at 9 pm and your only option is whatever the convenience stores still have in stock, which is usually a thinner product.
- **Real scenario:** arriving on the Taipa ferry, taking the free shuttle to a Cotai resort, and finding the resort Wi-Fi fine in the lobby but absent on the walk to Pai Kok LRT station — which is exactly where a CTM-grade profile earns its place.

### Taking your Macau eSIM overseas

China Telecom (Macau) is the number-two operator and the second 5G licensee, and its entire commercial identity is built around the Greater Bay Area. Where CTM sells a Macau product, China Telecom sells a Pearl River Delta product: dual-number plans and bundled roaming that treat Macau, Zhuhai and Hong Kong as a single commercial zone. It measured 103.87 Mbps median download in Ookla's H1 2024 data, behind CTM on the all-technology average — but on 5G speed the two were statistically tied, and China Telecom posted the best 5G consistency in the market at **89.3%**.

- **What it is best at:** itineraries that genuinely loop Macau, Zhuhai and Hong Kong, where one plan can cover all three legs.
- **Visitor route to a China Telecom line:** its own shops and counters, where the Greater Bay Area products live; ask specifically for the cross-border lines rather than the default local card.
- **What goes wrong:** if you only ever stay inside Macau you are paying for coverage you will not use, and its retail network is smaller than CTM's.
- **Real scenario:** a business trip that lands in Macau, crosses the Border Gate to Zhuhai for two days, and returns — the one itinerary where the Bay Area bundles beat anything you can buy per territory.

### CTM vs China Telecom: which is better in Macau?

3 Macau (Hutchison Telephone (Macau)) is the network to avoid planning around, because it is being switched off. CTM completed its purchase of the business in January 2025 for **HK$110 million**, and Hutchison has notified the regulator that it will surrender its 4G licence and end mobile service after **11:59 pm on 30 November 2026**. Its roughly 140,000 customers are being migrated to CTM; most have already moved, and the remainder must complete formalities at a Hutchison or CTM shop before the deadline.

Two consequences for a visitor. First, **no travel eSIM should land you on 3 Macau**, and if your phone shows that network on a manual scan, it is a signal to fix your network selection rather than a bonus. Second, **older guides that describe a four-operator Macau are wrong** — SmarTone left in November 2024 and Hutchison follows at the end of 2026, leaving CTM and China Telecom (Macau) as the only mobile operators in the territory. The closure is confirmed in [Hutchison's own notice](https://three.com.mo/) and in [the Macau Post Daily's report](https://www.macaupostdaily.com/news/29399).

Uniform is the honest answer, and it is backed by the data rather than the brochure. ## Does casino-resort Wi-Fi make an eSIM unnecessary in Macau?

It is the most common assumption among first-time visitors, and it fails on a specific, predictable schedule.

Inside the resorts, free guest Wi-Fi is real and fast — the Venetian, City of Dreams, Wynn and their peers blanket their properties, and Macau's fixed broadband backhaul is genuinely world-class: Ookla's Global Index ranked Macau **6th worldwide for fixed broadband** with a median of **321.88 Mbps**. But guest Wi-Fi ends at the property line. The moment you are on a bus to Taipa, queuing at the border gate, walking from the ferry terminal, or eating at a cha chaan teng in an older neighborhood, you are back on mobile data or nothing. Day-trippers suffer this most: they spend half the day precisely in the places resorts do not cover.

Two more reasons the Wi-Fi fallback is weaker in Macau than visitors expect. Crowds: on festival weekends and holiday evenings, shared resort networks sag under tens of thousands of simultaneous guests, while the cellular networks are provisioned for exactly that load. And navigation: half of Macau's charm is getting lost in the alleys off Senado Square, which requires maps, which requires data.

The one genuine advantage Macau holds over its giant neighbor next door: it sits **outside mainland China's internet filtering**, so Google Maps, WhatsApp, Instagram and every international app behave normally here. An eSIM in Macau is a normal, open-internet connection — no VPN planning required, unlike a trip into Guangdong.

### Macau eSIM coverage: Cotai versus the peninsula

The two halves of the territory fail in opposite directions, and knowing which one you are in tells you what to fix.

- **Cotai is an indoor problem.** The resort strip is where Macau's best coverage lives — dense small cells, huge indoor distributed antenna systems, and enough capacity for a food court full of people streaming. What Cotai lacks is street life between the properties: the walk from the Venetian to the Londoner, or to Pai Kok LRT station, crosses open plazas and footbridges where the resort network no longer reaches and you are back on mobile data.
- **The peninsula is a crowd problem.** Coverage is complete and indoor signal is fine, but the historic centre concentrates tens of thousands of visitors into a few narrow streets. Throughput per user is what suffers — the classic "full bars, slow upload" experience at Senado Square on a public holiday, which no carrier choice will fix.
- **Both mean the same thing.** A data plan is not a substitute for resort Wi-Fi; it is the layer that covers the 40% of a Macau day that happens between buildings, on buses, in queues and at the ferry terminal. And note the local habit: some venues still expect cash, so an internet connection that lets you top up a payment app is itself useful.

## Does your Hong Kong or mainland China eSIM work in Macau?

This is the question most Macau visitors get wrong, because the geography says "same trip" while the telecom map says "different market."

**Hong Kong plans do not automatically include Macau.** The two territories run entirely separate networks and regulators, and a Hong Kong-only eSIM will typically show "no service" — or start metering expensive international roaming — once your ferry clears the harbor or your bus crosses the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge. Some Hong Kong carriers sell plans that explicitly bundle Macau roaming; if yours does not name Macau in the destination list, assume it is not included. Our [Hong Kong eSIM guide](/carriers/hong-kong-esim-carrier-guide/) covers how to read the fine print on the HK side.

**Mainland China plans are their own trap.** Chinese SIMs roam onto Macau networks only with a Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan roaming add-on activated in advance — and once connected, they route traffic back through the mainland, firewall included. Your hotel Wi-Fi in Macau gives you an open internet; your mainland SIM often does not. If your trip continues north across the Gongbei border into Zhuhai, plan that leg separately with our [China eSIM guide](/carriers/china-esim-carrier-guide/).

**The clean solutions.** For the classic Hong Kong-plus-Macau double, buy a plan that names both territories, or carry a dedicated Macau eSIM alongside your Hong Kong profile — dual-SIM phones handle this without ceremony. For mainland-plus-Macau itineraries, look for plans that list all three destinations explicitly, because "Greater China" on a product page does not always mean the same thing on the coverage list.

## Practical apps and payments in Macau

Everything a short visit depends on — transport times, ferries, spending money — runs through a phone here, which is the other reason a data connection matters more than the Wi-Fi fallback suggests.

**Buses and the LRT.** The Transport Bureau's own **巴士報站** ("Bus Traveller") app publishes every bus route and stop with live arrival information, and the companion **Macao Smart Go** app bundles buses, taxis, the LRT and walking routes, plus ferry and flight booking. The Macao Light Rapid Transit covers the Taipa Line, the Seac Pai Van Line and the Hengqin Line, with stations including Barra, Ocean, Pai Kok, Cotai West, Lotus, Cotai East, MUST, Airport and the Taipa Ferry Terminal — useful for hopping between the old town and the casino strip without a taxi. Both apps are listed on [the Macao government's app directory](https://www.gov.mo/zh-hant/entity-page/entity-991/mobile-apps), and the LRT's own route and fare pages are at [mlm.com.mo](https://www.mlm.com.mo/en/).

**Ferries.** Book ahead online for weekend sailings; TurboJET and Cotai Water Jet both sell e-tickets, and the Taipa terminal is the one that lands you beside the Cotai resorts.

**Money.** The pataca (MOP) is the legal tender, and Hong Kong dollars circulate alongside it — but cash is often exchanged at a flat 1:1 in shops, which quietly costs you a few percent. Supermarkets, casinos and buses do not take renminbi, so convert enough patacas for small purchases and rely on mobile payment for the rest.

**Mobile payment.** QR payment via WeChat Pay, Alipay and the local MPay wallet is accepted almost everywhere that has a card terminal, and it has just become easier: **MPay Tap** launched on 1 December 2025, letting you unlock a phone and tap an Alipay Tap terminal at more than 1,000 merchants without opening an app, with terminals supporting over ten international wallets including WeChat Pay, Octopus, Changi Pay and Naver Pay. The [Macao News report on the launch](https://macaonews.org/?p=100563/) sets out the details — and note the practical implication for visitors: your payment apps need data, so a working connection is a spending capability, not just a convenience.

**Taxis and buses.** Neither takes cards. Bus fares are MOP 6 in cash, or MOP 3–4 with a Macau Pass card, which carries a MOP 30 deposit. Taxis start at MOP 19 with additional charges by distance and waiting time.

### Common Macau eSIM questions

Two things change the connectivity experience more than carrier choice, and neither is about network technology.

- **The calendar, not the map, is the bottleneck.** Macau routinely absorbs visitor numbers that can double its resident population, and the surges cluster tightly: Chinese New Year, the October Golden Week, and the territory's own festival weekends. The cellular networks are dimensioned for those peaks; shared resort and café Wi-Fi is not, which is why holiday evenings are when the Wi-Fi fallback fails first.
- **Language friction is real but navigable.** Cantonese and Portuguese are the official languages, with Mandarin and English widely understood at counters, ferry terminals and resorts. What that means for a phone is that the useful apps — the Transport Bureau's live bus arrivals above all — are Chinese-first, so recognising route numbers and stop names is more valuable than reading menus.
- **The practical consequence.** A working data connection closes most of that gap: live translation, live arrival times, and QR payment which removes the need to explain anything at all. It is the cheapest language insurance you will buy for the trip.

### Which carrier blankets most of Macau?

Congestion in Macau is local and predictable, so a little planning recovers most of the lost speed.

- **Queue late or early.** Border Gate and the HZMB Macao Port are calm outside the morning and evening commuter banks; the crossing itself takes minutes when you are not in a surge.
- **Cache before you walk.** Download the day's map and ferry tickets while you still have resort Wi-Fi, then treat mobile data as the reserve for the gaps between buildings.
- **Split the load.** If you are travelling as a pair, run one phone on a Macau profile and keep the other on resort Wi-Fi — Macau's dual-SIM habit is local practice, not a tourist workaround.

### APN values for CTM, China Telecom and 3 Macau eSIMs

There is no APN table in this guide, and that is deliberate. Macau's carriers provision their own profiles automatically, and the APN strings circulating on traveller forums cannot be confirmed against the operators' own published support pages — so rather than print something that may not work, treat automatic configuration as the norm here.

You only need to think about APN settings in three situations:

- You bought a **locally purchased prepaid SIM** and the automatic configuration did not run on insertion.
- You are on an **older handset** that does not accept operator settings pushed over the air.
- Data connects and then immediately stalls, or works in one app and not another — the signature of a wrong or missing APN.

If any of those apply, ask the counter that sold you the card rather than copying a value off a forum. A value typed from memory on a card that was working is the fastest way to break a working connection. If you are on a managed travel eSIM, the profile brings its own configuration and you should change nothing.

## Macau eSIM activation steps and problem fixes

There is no store visit, no passport registration and no local address on a travel eSIM — the profile arrives by email or in-app, and installation is the standard sequence covered in [activation guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/): add eSIM, scan the QR code, label the line, set it as your mobile-data line, enable data roaming. Because Macau's networks are so compact, there are only five failure patterns worth knowing.

### Before the ferry: CTM-era checks for a Macao, China eSIM

Macao, China is compact enough that almost every check here is generic, with two local wrinkles: install the profile before the Hong Kong ferry or the Zhuhai border gate, and make sure the Macao profile is your active data line before you queue.

| # | Check | What "good" looks like |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | Phone is carrier-unlocked | About -> Carrier Lock says "No SIM restrictions" |
| 2 | Device is eSIM-capable | An EID appears when you dial `*#06#`, or the [handset check](/compatibility/) confirms your model |
| 3 | QR code saved | Screenshot stored on the phone and in the cloud before you fly |
| 4 | Profile installed before the crossing | Installed on home or hotel Wi-Fi, not at the ferry terminal |
| 5 | Data line and roaming set | The Macau profile is the active data line, data roaming on for it and off for your home SIM |
| 6 | Ferry or shuttle booked | Sailings sell out on festival weekends; book the crossing, not just the hotel |

### The five Macau profile failures you may hit

**A. Installed but "no service" at the border.** You crossed from Hong Kong or the mainland before the profile ever found a network. Toggle airplane mode once you are genuinely in Macau territory — ferries spend much of the crossing outside coverage — and if it persists, use manual network selection and try CTM first, then China Telecom (Macau).

**B. The network list still shows 3 Macau.** Hutchison's network is winding down toward its 30 November 2026 closure, so landing on it is a dead end. Re-run the manual scan and select CTM or China Telecom (Macau).

**C. Signal, but no data.** Check that the eSIM line is the selected mobile-data line and that data roaming is on for it. This trips up the most travelers who arrive with two profiles installed — one for Hong Kong, one for Macau — and the wrong one set as the data line.

**D. Everything slow on a holiday weekend.** This is congestion, not configuration; Macau absorbs visitor surges that can double its population, and the fix is patience or a different network. The peninsula's historic centre is where it bites hardest.

**E. Data fine in Cotai, patchy in Coloane.** That is coverage design rather than a fault. The southern end of the territory — Hac Sa, Cheoc Van and the hiking trails — is residential-thin rather than urban-dense, so uploads slow before anything drops.

Anything that survives those five checks is a generic error — the [eSIM troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/) covers the rest, and if you want the underlying mechanics, [what eSIM activation actually does](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) explains why a QR code works once and why profiles install but activate later.

## FAQs

### The main Macau carriers

CTM if you have a choice — it is the fastest measured network in the territory with the deepest market position, and from the end of 2026 it will also own the former 3 Macau customer base. China Telecom (Macau) is statistically just as fast on 5G and posts the best consistency score, so a multi-network travel eSIM that can use either is the strongest setup of all.

### CTM vs China Telecom 5G: which is better in Macau?

Effectively yes, since both licensees launched in late 2022 and the territory is 33 km². The Peninsula, Taipa, Cotai and Coloane all have 5G, with no measurable speed difference between CTM and China Telecom on 5G per Ookla. Your phone needs n78 support, which virtually all recent eSIM-capable handsets have.

### What is happening to 3 Macau?

It is closing. CTM bought Hutchison Telephone (Macau) in January 2025 for HK$110 million, and mobile service ends after 11:59 pm on 30 November 2026, with customers migrating to CTM. Do not buy a plan that routes you onto that network, and do not trust guides that still describe four mobile operators in Macau — after SmarTone's exit in 2024, only CTM and China Telecom (Macau) remain.

### Can I use a Hong Kong eSIM in Macau?

Only if your specific plan lists Macau as a covered destination — the territories have separate networks, and Hong Kong-only plans typically stop working (or start metering roaming) at the ferry terminal or the bridge. For a Hong Kong trip with a Macau day visit, either buy a plan that names both, or add a dedicated Macau eSIM.

### Do mainland China SIMs and eSIMs work in Macau?

They roam, but only after you activate the Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan roaming option in advance — and roaming traffic routes through the mainland, filtered internet and all. That is the opposite of what most visitors want in Macau, where the open internet is part of the appeal. Use a dedicated Macau eSIM and keep the mainland plan for the mainland leg.

### Best Macau eSIM by trip type

Inside a resort, yes. Everywhere a day trip actually goes — border queues, ferries, bus stops, old-town streets, restaurants — no. Guest Wi-Fi ends at the property line, and the crowded hours are exactly when shared networks sag. An eSIM covers the 90% of the day that is not spent inside a casino complex.

### Is identification needed for Macau SIMs?

For a locally purchased prepaid SIM from CTM or China Telecom (Macau), yes — bring your passport to the counter. Travel eSIMs handle compliance on the provider's side, so there is nothing to register and no store visit at all.

### CTM vs China Telecom: which Macau carrier is faster?

Faster than almost anywhere. CTM's median download measured 176.04 Mbps with 26 ms latency in Ookla's H1 2024 data, both carriers are statistically tied on 5G speed, and the fixed-broadband backhaul ranks 6th in the world. For context, a gigabyte averages about USD 1.44 per Cable.co.uk — mid-priced globally, trivial for the short stays this territory usually hosts.

### CTM vs China Telecom prices compared

Prepaid tourist cards are priced in patacas and banded by validity: short cards come in under MOP 100, and 7-day cards with a larger allowance run toward MOP 200, with China Telecom's Greater Bay Area cards sitting below that. Carriers reprice these constantly, so treat the bands as a budget guide and confirm at the counter.

### Will my phone take a Macau eSIM?

If it is unlocked and eSIM-capable, almost certainly — Macau uses the common LTE bands 1/3/7/8 and 5G n78. The two exceptions that matter: carrier-locked phones, which must be unlocked first, and mainland-China-market iPhones, whose eSIM hardware is disabled at the firmware level. Check your model against the compatibility list before buying anything.

### Can you buy a Macau eSIM from CTM?

For stays under a week, a travel eSIM — instant, registration-free, and installed before the border. A local CTM prepaid SIM earns its place when you need a Macau phone number for a longer stay, and China Telecom's Bay Area products make sense only for itineraries that genuinely loop Macau, Hong Kong and Guangdong together.

### My eSIM worked in Hong Kong but shows no service in Macau — why?

Because you crossed into a different market, and your Hong Kong profile has no Macau coverage agreement behind it. Switch your data line to a Macau-inclusive profile, or add one on the spot — installation takes minutes over any Wi-Fi, including the resort network you are probably standing in.
