## Will your phone work?

Three things decide whether your phone works on an Australian carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below, and two of them are more important in Australia than almost anywhere else because the country has switched off 3G and has enormous dead zones.

### Which devices can use a Australia eSIM?

Australian carriers build their 5G on specific bands, and the split is what determines whether you see 5G or only 4G. All three national networks use **n78 at 3500 MHz** as the primary 5G band, so almost any modern foreign 5G phone connects there. **Telstra** adds low-band **n5 at 850 MHz** for rural 5G reach, and **Optus** adds **n40 at 2300 MHz** for extra mid-band capacity, while **Vodafone** concentrates on n78 with some n28 at 700 MHz. Phones without n78 still work on 4G, which is fast in the cities and frustrating in the bush.

One Australia-specific trap has nothing to do with 5G. The country shut down its 3G networks completely, so a phone must support **VoLTE** to make calls and to register on the network at all. An old handset that only does 3G will show no service even where coverage exists. Check your exact model, model number not marketing name, against [compatibility page](/compatibility/). For the mechanics before the buying decision, [what happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) covers it.

### Is your handset compatible with Australia networks?
A locked phone is the most common reason an Australia eSIM install simply fails, and Australia has clear but non-uniform rules here. Unlike some markets where every phone is locked, most phones sold outright in Australia are already unlocked. Postpaid handsets from Telstra, Optus and Vodafone are generally sold unlocked, while phones bundled with a prepaid starter pack can be locked to that carrier.

Under the Telecommunications Consumer Protections Code, a carrier must unlock a device at no charge once any contract or minimum term is finished. For a prepaid device that is still locked, the carriers charge a sliding fee based on how long the service has been active: Telstra charges about $80 if used under six months, $25 between six months and two years, and nothing after two years; Optus follows a similar scale; Vodafone charges about $50 under six months and $25 after. The practical advice is to check before you fly.

**Check it:** iPhone → Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock (labelled Service Provider Lock on newer iOS). A "No SIM restrictions" result means the handset is ready. A "SIM locked" reading means no third-party profile will install until an unlock is processed.

**Fix it:** ask the network that supplied the handset to unlock it. Paid-off postpaid devices are normally cleared within a day or two, and there is no fee once the term is met. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone on a prepaid plan, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. Full detail is on the [Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/Details/F2017L01514), which publishes the Determination that the ACMA enforces for prepaid identity and unlocking.

## APN values that work

Most travelers never open the APN screen, and yet it is the first place worth looking when data stalls. What follows covers each carrier value, the cases that really need manual entry, and the menu path on each platform.

### APN values for Telstra, Optus and Vodafone eSIMs

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM directly from an Australian carrier. On a travel eSIM the profile brings its own APN, so leave the settings alone.

| Carrier | APN | Username | Password |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Telstra | `telstra.internet` | blank | blank |
| Optus | `yesinternet` | blank | blank |
| Vodafone | `live.vodafone.com` | blank | blank |

Leave username and password empty. Telstra is strict about its APN string: it must be all lowercase with no spaces. If a carrier requires other values, the activation instructions that came with your profile will state them.

### Australia APN settings, carrier by carrier

- An older handset that does not pick up carrier settings automatically
- A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
- A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
- Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile

### Australia: when the APN won't auto-load
- **iPhone:** Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → enter the APN

After saving, restart the phone. If data still will not flow, check that the data line points at the eSIM rather than your home SIM before changing anything else.

### Four repairs for an Australia eSIM that will not connect

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our [eSIM activation fixes guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The four patterns below are the Australia-specific ones, and the first three come straight from this country's geography.

**A. No signal on the Nullarbor, in the Red Centre or on a remote track**
1. This is expected, not a fault. Only Telstra reaches these places, and even it is intermittent.
2. Switch the eSIM to Telstra manually in Network Selection if your profile allows it.
3. Download offline maps before you leave the last town with signal. For life-threatening emergencies where there is no coverage, a registered personal locator beacon is the correct tool, not a phone.

**B. Installed, but no signal bars in a city**
1. Re-check the lock status and that the eSIM is enabled.
2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → select Telstra, Optus or Vodafone manually instead of automatic.
3. Full network settings reset, then another reboot.

**C. Signal bars, but no internet**
1. Cross-check the APN against the carrier table earlier in this section.
2. Verify data is routed to the eSIM, not your home SIM, not your home SIM.
3. Check you have not exhausted your data allowance; prepaid plans stop or throttle rather than charging overage.

**D. "SOS" or "Emergency calls only"**
1. The eSIM must be toggled on in the SIM manager.
2. Disable or delete any other active SIM or eSIM profile that may conflict.
3. Reboot and reset network settings.
4. As a last resort, remove the profile and reinstall from a fresh QR code.

### Can I run a Telstra eSIM and a travel eSIM together?
Yes, and it is a sensible setup. Leave your home line in the physical slot for calls and bank codes, and put Australian data on the eSIM. In Settings, set the data line to the eSIM and keep the home line for calls. Two things to watch: disable roaming on the home SIM so background traffic is not billed, and note that data-only profiles cannot receive SMS, so bank codes still land on your home number.

### Regional coverage in Australia

Most travel eSIMs in Australia ride on the Optus network, which is excellent on the coast and in cities but disappears for hundreds of kilometres across the Nullarbor and through much of the Outback, where only Telstra reaches. The day you head inland, Optus drops and your eSIM with it. A multi-network eSIM that can attach to Telstra solves this; otherwise plan for no coverage and carry a satellite device for emergencies.

### What an eSIM actually is, in one paragraph

An eSIM is a chip built into the handset that holds carrier profiles in software rather than on a removable card. The carrier transmits a profile, the phone stores it on the chip, and the line then works like any ordinary SIM. The plumbing behind it, from SM-DP+ servers to single-use QR codes, is set out in [what eSIM activation is and how it works](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/).
