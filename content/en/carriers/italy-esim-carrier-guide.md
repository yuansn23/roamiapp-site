## Is your phone compatible with an Italy eSIM carrier?



Three things decide whether your phone works on an Italian carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device specific quirks. All three are covered below.



### Will my phone work with a Italy eSIM?



Italian carriers build 5G on specific bands, notably **n78 at 3500 MHz** for capacity, **n1 at 2100 MHz**, and **n28 at 700 MHz** for reach into stone buildings and valleys. Their 4G uses B1, B3, B7, B8, B20 at 800 MHz and B28 at 700 MHz. Plenty of international handsets, including several iPhone regional variants, ship without the low band n28. Those phones still work: they fall back to LTE once you leave dense urban areas, which is fine downtown and frustrating in a cliff village.



Band tables are not worth memorising before a trip. What actually works is checking the specific model number against the [device compatibility list](/compatibility/). If the mechanics matter more than the carrier list, read [what happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/).



### Is your phone eSIM-ready for Italy?

A locked phone is the most common reason an Italy eSIM install simply fails. Italy has its own rules here. Under Italian SIM locking law, carriers must disclose any subsidy, a subscriber can obtain an unlock code after nine months by paying half the listed subsidy, and the lock must be removed within eighteen months. Across the EU more broadly, SIM unlocking must be performed free of charge once any commitment period ends.



**Check it:** iPhone, go to Settings, General, About, Carrier Lock. If the screen reports "No SIM restrictions", the phone is unlocked for any profile. When the screen reports "SIM locked", outside profiles are blocked until the lock is lifted.



**Fix it:** ask the operator holding the lock to remove it. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and there is no fee under EU and most home carrier rules. Only then try installing your Italy eSIM.



If you bought a used phone, or a phone on a plan, assume it may be locked and check before you fly.

## Pricing compared



Four routes exist, and they differ more in paperwork than in price: TIM for the widest reach, Vodafone for city speed, WINDTRE for 5G availability, and Iliad for price. Each route, step by step.



### Can you buy an Italy eSIM from TIM?



TIM is the old incumbent and the safest choice when you will spend time outside tourist centres, but its tourist product is deliberately simple.



TIM's TIM Tourist plan is **physical SIM only**. It gives 200 GB of 5G data in Italy, 23 GB usable across the EU, unlimited domestic minutes plus 100 international minutes, for 30 days. The published total is **EUR 24.99**, made of a EUR 14.99 plan plus a EUR 10 SIM, with free activation. You buy it at a TIM store, a Rome Fiumicino or Milan Malpensa airport counter, or a tabacchi, with your passport. TIM does support eSIM on its standard plans, but activating one as a foreign visitor normally means a store visit and an identity check rather than a remote QR code.



### Vodafone eSIMs for visitors: gettable?



Vodafone Italy is the fastest network in the big cities and the easiest for English language support, and it does support eSIM on its plans.



Vodafone's visitor product is the **Dolce Vita** range. Dolce Vita Start gives 100 GB of 5G, 25 GB of EU data and 200 minutes for **EUR 14.95** per month. Dolce Vita Ultra gives 300 GB, 41 GB EU and the same minutes for **EUR 24.95** per month. Vodafone advertises zero activation fee, though it does not always publish a separate SIM card charge, so confirm the full total at the counter. The eSIM is issued in store after your passport is scanned, and staff handle the setup.



### Does WINDTRE sell a prepaid eSIM to visitors?



WINDTRE is the only one of the four that sells a tourist plan online with an eSIM option, which makes it the most visitor friendly on paper.



The **Tourist Pass Digital** gives 200 GB of 5G, 23 GB of EU data, unlimited Italian minutes and 100 minutes to 53 countries, for 30 days. The headline price is **EUR 25**, but the same official page's fine print adds a EUR 10 SIM cost and a small iMessage related amount, so budget up to about EUR 35. You buy it online before you fly and collect the eSIM or SIM at a WINDTRE store or airport kiosk on arrival. It is reserved for new foreign customers without an Italian tax code, and it cannot be renewed on the same line.



### Is Iliad eSIM available to tourists?



Iliad is the price disruptor, with transparent low cost high data plans, but it has the fewest staffed stores and the weakest rural predictability.



Iliad's **Giga 150** plan gives 150 GB of 5G, unlimited calls and EU roaming under fair use for about **EUR 9.99** per month. Iliad supports eSIM, and because it runs a network of self service Simbox vending machines in shopping centres, you can issue a card or eSIM against your ID document in minutes. The trade off is less predictable coverage once you leave the main transport corridors, and customer support that is mostly digital.

## Entering the APN



You will probably never open the APN screen, but it explains most sudden data failures. The table below lists every operator value, the situations that require typing them in, and where to find the screen.

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM **directly from an Italian carrier**. If the profile comes from a travel eSIM it already holds the Italian APN.

|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| Vodafone | `mobile.vodafone.it` | blank | blank |

| WINDTRE | `internet.it` | blank | blank |

| Iliad | `iliad` | blank | blank |



Leave username and password empty. If a carrier requires values, the activation instructions that came with your profile will state them.



### Italy carrier APN values



Automatic configuration covers most Italian profiles; these are the cases that still need the values above typed in.



- An older handset that does not pick up carrier settings automatically

- A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan

- A carrier issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run

- A hotspot that connects but will not share data, which on Android needs `dun` added to the APN type

- Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile



### When to type in a Italy APN by hand

- **iPhone:** Settings, Cellular, tap the eSIM line, Cellular Data Network, enter the APN

- **Android:** Settings, Connections, Mobile Networks, Access Point Names, add a new APN

- **iPhone hotspot:** in the same Cellular Data Network screen, also fill Personal Hotspot with the same APN or the hotspot option may disappear



After saving, restart the phone. Should data still fail, verify the selected data line is the Italian profile and not your home card.
