> **Where Mauritius's networks differ: speed, coverage and the counter rules**



A Mauritius eSIM for visitors no longer includes the cheap local prepaid packages, and this guide covers what tourists can actually buy, starting with the flat-rate unlimited 5G tourist SIM. Mauritius runs one of the Indian Ocean's most tourist-friendly connectivity markets — unlimited 5G tourist SIMs for a flat Rs 1,500 — and simultaneously one of its least understood: **visitors can no longer buy the cheap local prepaid packages at all.** Those are restricted to residents with local ID and proof of address, a rule verified across operator counters as recently as February 2026. This guide covers what that means for your wallet, the my.t vs Emtel vs Chili decision, the coverage reality inside Black River Gorges, and the Rodrigues question that almost every Mauritius article gets wrong.



**Quick answer:** Most travellers in Mauritius will be happiest with multi-day; the local networks makes sense for tighter budgets and city-only trips. Run your device through the [compatibility checker](/compatibility/) first — the step Mauritius visitors skip and later regret.



If your phone's eSIM support is unconfirmed, the [device compatibility checker](/compatibility/) settles it first.



**In brief:** my.t (Mauritius Telecom) has the island's best coverage, including the interior and the outer islands; Emtel is the strong challenger with excellent tourist-zone service and a slick app; Chili (MTML) is the budget operator whose network falls short for full-island travel. Both majors sell a **tourist SIM at Rs 1,500 (~€30) including unlimited 5G data and domestic SMS for 30 days** — the same price at the airport as in town, with passport registration mandatory. A data-only travel eSIM bought online is usually cheaper for trips under a week and skips the counter entirely.

### Mauritius tourist SIM rules: why resident prices do not apply



This is the fact to understand before comparing anything: **the cheap local prepaid packages — Rs 100 with 15 GB, and similar — are now reserved for residents** with a Mauritian ID and proof of address. Tourists are directed to the dedicated tourist products across airport stands, official shops and private resellers alike. Verified as recently as February 2026 on the ground, the practical consequences are:



- The tourist SIM from either major costs **Rs 1,500 including VAT** — unlimited 5G data, unlimited domestic SMS, Rs 200 call credit, valid 30 days.

- **Airport counters at SSR International charge the same as town shops** — this is not a marked-up airport. Buying on arrival is genuinely painless: my.t and Emtel counters sit in the arrivals hall and open for international flights.

- **Registration is mandatory** — passport, and for online orders in advance, a photo of your passport plus your stay address.



Which means the price comparison for a short visit is no longer "local SIM vs travel eSIM" on local terms — it is **Rs 1,500 tourist card vs a data-only eSIM from an online provider**, and for trips under a week the eSIM usually wins. The tourist card earns its keep for stays of a week or more, or whenever calling local numbers (drivers, guesthouses, catamaran skippers) matters.



### Why the Mauritius tourist SIM rule is the most useful fact on this page



Almost every Mauritius connectivity guide you will find online quotes the resident prices — Rs 100 for 15 GB, and similar — as if a visitor could walk in and buy them. They cannot. Three consequences follow:



1. **The "Mauritius has the cheapest data in the region" claim is now only true for residents.** Visitors face a flat Rs 1,500 tourist product, which changes the value calculation entirely.

2. **Reseller pricing is inconsistent.** Some private resellers sell the same tourist product with a markup; the airport counter and the official shops do not.

3. **A travel eSIM becomes genuinely competitive on price**, not just on convenience, for anything shorter than about a week.



### my.t: the incumbent with the island's best map



my.t — the consumer brand of state-linked Mauritius Telecom, with France's Orange still holding a major stake — holds the largest coverage footprint: the best signal through the interior, the gorges' rim roads, and the outer islands (Rodrigues and even Agaléga). Its tourist product matches Emtel's pricing exactly. The regulator for the whole market is the [Information and Communication Technologies Authority (ICTA)](https://www.icta.mu/).



### Emtel: the challenger with the tourist-coast advantage



Emtel, the private challenger, is rated excellent across the tourist zones — Grand Baie, Flic en Flac, the airport corridor — and offers the better consumer app for managing your line. Its rural and interior coverage is a step behind my.t but still good. For resort-and-beach itineraries, the choice between the two majors is genuinely close to a coin flip, which is why coverage where *you* are staying — not brand folklore — should decide.



### Chili (MTML): the Mauritius budget option with a thin coverage map



Chili sells cheaper packs (around Rs 700 for a 200 GB tier at last check) but its network coverage lags for full-island travel, and it does not serve Rodrigues. Locals' usual advice for visitors: skip it unless your footprint is strictly urban.

### Which network is best in Mauritius?



Mauritius is 65 km long and ringed by a coastal road, with a central plateau and a handful of deep gorges cutting through it. Three practical patterns emerge:



- **The coast is covered; the interior is not.** Every resort strip has excellent signal. The moment you turn inland toward Chamarel or the gorges, coverage becomes a matter of where the last mast is.

- **The lagoon is not the land.** Signal follows the shore, so a catamaran trip off Grand Baie generally keeps bars while the boat is near the coast, and loses them if it heads far out.

- **Rodrigues is a separate network problem.** The island 560 km east is served by my.t and Emtel with 4G, at speeds below the main island. Chili does not serve it at all.



For market context: [Ookla's Speedtest Global Index](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/mauritius) shows fixed medians around **63 Mbps** in recent editions — modest — but the mobile networks punch above it, with 5G live in Port Louis, Quatre Bornes and Grand Baie. [Cable.co.uk's pricing survey](https://www.cable.co.uk/mobiles/worldwide-data-pricing/) prices 1 GB at roughly **USD 0.67** for locals, and [DataReportal's Digital 2025: Mauritius](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-mauritius) counts 2.14 million mobile connections — 168% of the population. Note the gap between that cheap local per-GB price and the Rs 1,500 tourist card: the resident pricing you will see quoted online is exactly what you can no longer buy.

### Mauritius networks: APN details



The tourist card normally configures itself; these are the situations where it does not:



- You bought a local tourist card rather than a managed travel eSIM

- A profile installed from a manual activation code instead of a QR scan

- An older handset that did not pull carrier settings automatically

- The line did not re-register after a flight to Rodrigues



### When Mauritius APNs don't arrive automatically



On iPhone the field is at Settings → Cellular → [the eSIM line] → Cellular Data Network; on Android it sits at Settings → Network & Internet → Access Point Names. Save, restart, then check the my.t or Emtel line is the one carrying data. Going local? Balance USSD codes are printed on the activation card, so keep it.

### Bring a Mauritius eSIM back: four moves



**A. The eSIM will not install**

1. Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock: confirm "No SIM restrictions"

2. Check the QR is fresh -- a second attempt almost always fails

3. Check whether the profile has been queued on the provider side



**B. Installed, but no signal bars**

1. Turn the eSIM line off and on again

2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose my.t or Emtel manually

3. Turn the phone off and on; if that fails, reset network settings



**C. Signal bars, but no internet**

1. The table above has the right APN -- confirm yours

2. Make sure the eSIM, not the physical SIM, is the data default

3. Roaming must be on for the line itself, not only the phone

4. Make sure you have not emptied the data bucket



**D. Works on the coast, dead in the interior**

1. That is the island's geography, not a fault

2. Check whether you are inside the gorges or past the last mast

3. Move to manual network selection to try the other operator

## Mauritius carrier eSIM questions from the road



### Local SIM or travel eSIM in Mauritius?



No — the cheap resident prepaid packages require a Mauritian ID and proof of address, a rule verified across counters as recently as February 2026. Visitors buy the dedicated tourist SIM (Rs 1,500, unlimited 5G, 30 days) or a travel eSIM bought online.



### Is the airport SIM counter more expensive in Mauritius?



No — my.t and Emtel counters at SSR International charge the same Rs 1,500 as town shops. This is one of the world's non-marked-up airport SIM markets, which is unusual and worth knowing before you assume the airport is a trap.



### Is passport registration required for a SIM in Mauritius?



Yes — passport registration is mandatory, and online advance orders ask for a passport photo and your stay address. Because the provider registers first, online travel eSIMs need no counter visit.



### Which carrier has coverage in Rodrigues?



my.t and Emtel both serve Rodrigues with 4G/LTE; my.t is generally the stronger of the two there. Chili does not serve the island at all — and neither does any single-island mainland-only product, so check before you fly there.



### Will I have signal while hiking in Black River Gorges?



Assume no. The park interior and the waterfall approaches are silent on every network — tell someone your plan, and treat the rim roads as your last coverage.



### Can you tether from a Mauritius eSIM?



Yes — tethering is allowed on the tourist cards from both majors, and the unlimited allowance makes it practical for laptops and tablets. Both carriers treat it as normal handset behaviour, so no add-on is required.



### The right Mauritius carrier for your route



5–10 GB covers maps, messaging, social and video calls comfortably if you use resort Wi-Fi overnight. The tourist card's unlimited allowance makes the question moot if you go local; for a short stay, a smaller travel eSIM is the better buy.



### How good is 5G in Mauritius, and whose network delivers it?



Yes — in Port Louis, Quatre Bornes, Grand Baie and the main tourist zones, from both my.t and Emtel. The interior and Rodrigues are 4G territory, which is entirely adequate for maps and sharing photographs.



### What's the fastest carrier in Mauritius?



The mobile networks are better than the fixed-line numbers suggest. [Ookla's Global Index](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/mauritius) has shown fixed medians around 63 Mbps, but 5G in the towns delivers comfortably more, and the tourist zones are well provisioned for visitor load.



### Budget picks among Mauritius plans



Because they are quoting the resident prepaid rates — Rs 100 for 15 GB and similar tiers — which visitors can no longer buy. The tourist product is a flat Rs 1,500, and that is the number that matters to you. This is the single most common error in Mauritius connectivity articles.



### The cost of Mauritius eSIM plans



Either — the price is identical, which is rare. Buy at the airport if you want connectivity for the transfer; buy in town if you would rather compare operators with more time. There is no financial penalty for the airport.



### Can I use a Mauritius eSIM across the region?



Not on local plans, which are domestic products. ### Should I buy my Mauritius eSIM before or after arrival?



Before. The airport counters are genuinely good and not marked up, but hotels and drivers expect a reachable number on arrival, and a pre-installed profile removes the one variable you cannot control — the queue.



### Do I need a Mauritian number?



Only if you plan to call local numbers — a taxi base, a guesthouse, a boat skipper. Almost all of them also operate on WhatsApp, which a data-only eSIM handles perfectly.



### Is the Rs 1,500 tourist card good value?



For a stay of two weeks or more with unlimited 5G, yes — it is effectively unmetered. For a four-day trip it is overkill, and a sized travel eSIM will cost less while covering the same ground.
