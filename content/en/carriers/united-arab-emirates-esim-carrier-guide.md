> **🇦🇪 United Arab Emirates eSIM 2026**

> **Two networks, a published price list, and a regulator behind every number**



A UAE eSIM is the rare travel purchase where the underlying network is not the question — the menu is. The UAE has been the fastest mobile market on earth for months: Ookla's Speedtest Global Index put the country 1st in the world for median mobile download speed in August 2026, at **675.51 Mbps** with 18 ms latency, and 2nd for fixed broadband at 382.40 Mbps. Against a worldwide mobile median of 109.05 Mbps, that is not a competitive market, it is a different category. The catch sits on the price line: Cable.co.uk prices 1 GB of UAE mobile data at roughly **USD 4.61**, 211th of 237 markets. Most visitors search for a Dubai eSIM rather than a country-wide product, but the airport counters sell the same two networks under either name.



So the real question here is not which network is fastest — both of them are. It is which product, from which regulated seller, at which point in your journey. The UAE sells connectivity the way it sells everything else: as a polished product menu with clear tiers, a regulator (the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority, TDRA) behind every number, and two national brands — du and e& (formerly Etisalat) — competing on bundles rather than on infrastructure quality.



One prerequisite worth ten seconds of your time: confirm your handset is eSIM-capable via the [compatibility checker](/compatibility/), and skim [how an eSIM profile installs](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) if the process is new to you.



**If you read nothing else:** The UAE has two licensed carriers, e& (Etisalat) and du, both TDRA-regulated, plus Virgin Mobile as an MVNO riding du's network. Both carriers sell official tourist products: e&'s Visitor Line starts at **AED 48.99 for 4 GB plus 30 flexi minutes** (28 days, before VAT), and du's tourist bundles start at AED 49, with both offering a free 10 GB / 24-hour tourist eSIM at airport arrivals. Buy at the airport counter or arrival-hall QR code if you want a local number immediately; buy an UAE eSIM online before you fly if you want data live the moment the plane doors open.



## TDRA, du and e&: how the UAE eSIM market is built



Understanding the retail structure takes one paragraph, because the UAE is a two-utility market. The TDRA licenses exactly two mobile network operators: e& UAE, the consumer arm of the group rebranded from Etisalat in 2022 (the old etisalat.ae domain now tells visitors that "Etisalat is now e&"), and du (Emirates Integrated Telecommunications Company). In August 2026 the TDRA board renewed both operators' licences for a further twenty years, and in the same month granted Starlink a licence to offer satellite internet.



Everything else on the shelf is built on those two networks:



| Brand | What it is | eSIM | Who it suits |

|:---|:---|:---|:---|

| e& (Etisalat) | One of two licensed national operators | Visitor Line eSIM online and at counters | Widest spectrum holding; strong in Abu Dhabi and the western routes |

| du | The second licensed national operator | Free Tourist eSIM plus paid bundles online and in app | Dubai-first itineraries; strong city 5G |

| Virgin Mobile UAE | MVNO on du's network | App-based eSIM with passport scan | App-first travelers wanting big short-term data buckets |



### UAE eSIM brands: what each label on the shelf really is



Two structural facts follow. First, any UAE eSIM you buy — from a carrier, an airport counter, or a travel platform — ultimately runs on either e& or du; there is no third network. Second, registration is non-negotiable: TDRA rules require every SIM to be registered against a passport or Emirates ID, tourist products are restricted to visitors aged 18 and over, and each passport is limited to a small number of active lines.

## Passport and ID rules for SIMs in United Arab Emirates



Registration is where the UAE's product polish meets hard regulation. Every mobile number, physical or embedded, must be tied to a verified identity before it will activate, and the document that proves it depends entirely on who you are.



| Your status | Document that registers the line | What follows |

|:---|:---|:---|

| Tourist on arrival | Physical passport, plus the visa or entry stamp where one applies | Visitor products only; line validity tracks your visa |

| Tourist with a visa on arrival | Passport | Same as above; 18-and-over restriction applies |

| GCC citizen | GCC national ID accepted, subject to operator policy | Same visitor products, easier verification |

| UAE resident | Original Emirates ID — copies and scans are not accepted | Resident prepaid or postpaid plans |

| Resident wanting an eSIM swap | Emirates ID, often with UAE Pass verification | Convert a physical SIM to eSIM in the operator app |

| Anyone buying online | Passport details during checkout; 3D-secured card required | Order reference redeemed at a store within six months |



### What documents does a United Arab Emirates SIM purchase require?



The dividing line is the plan, not the network. Tourist packs are built for passports; resident plans, migrations and most account changes are built around the Emirates ID. TDRA's own numbers draw the boundary too: visitors may hold up to two SIMs per provider and residents up to five, and the count is per operator rather than per person.



Two practical consequences follow. If your passport already holds two active UAE lines from a previous trip, a third will be refused until you deactivate one at a store. And if you decide to stay on, you migrate the visitor line to a resident plan by presenting your new Emirates ID at any operator outlet.

## Which phones support a United Arab Emirates eSIM



Spectrum is where the two-utility structure shows up in hardware terms. The TDRA split the country's core 5G band deliberately: of the **n78 (3.5 GHz)** range, e& holds 300 MHz (3300–3600 MHz) and du holds 200 MHz (3600–3800 MHz), and both hold mid-band n41 spectrum, with millimetre-wave allocations (24.25–27.5 GHz) layered on top since 2020. In practice, phones sold in Europe, Asia and the Middle East cover everything that matters; a handset missing n78 simply runs on LTE. Any UAE eSIM from either carrier installs the same way on the same device list.



| Check | What "good" looks like |

|:---|:---|

| 5G bands | n78 and n41 supported (both carriers build on these) |

| 4G bands | B1, B3, B7, B8 and B20 across the two networks |

| Carrier lock | iPhone: Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock reads "No SIM restrictions" |

| eSIM support | `*#06#` shows an EID, or the [eSIM compatibility list](/compatibility/) confirms your model |



### UAE eSIM: three checks before you fly



- **Unlocked.** A carrier-locked handset will install the profile and then refuse to register.

- **eSIM-capable.** Dial `*#06#` and look for an EID before you spend anything.

- **Data line chosen.** Set the UAE eSIM as the mobile-data line, or your home SIM will keep the traffic.



The lock check matters more in the Gulf than most regions because many UAE- and region-sold handsets arrive operator-financed and locked. Run all three before you leave, and an UAE eSIM that you installed at home will register the moment the aircraft doors open.

## Internet calling: the rule every UAE eSIM user meets



One regulatory fact shapes more visitor complaints in the UAE than any other, so it belongs stated plainly: **voice and video calls made through general internet-calling apps are restricted in the UAE**. WhatsApp, FaceTime and Skype calls do not connect on local networks, by TDRA-supervised policy; text messaging and data for browsing, social media, maps and streaming all work normally.



### Is WhatsApp blocked by United Arab Emirates carriers?



What the market did in response is worth knowing, because it turns a limitation into a product feature — and no UAE eSIM changes it, because the restriction is network-level, not device-level. The carriers sell data for licensed internet-calling apps: e&'s Visitor Line premium and unlimited packs include promotional unlimited data for GoChat Messenger, and du's tourist plans support licensed calling apps such as BOTIM. Practically, your family can install the same licensed app, or you rely on ordinary phone calls — the flexi minutes in every tourist pack exist partly for this reason. Plan around it before you fly rather than discovering it at the arrivals gate.

### Four failure patterns specific to an UAE eSIM



**A. The free 24-hour eSIM expired and "nothing works".** It worked exactly as designed: the free tourist eSIM carries 10 GB for one day. A paid pack does not attach itself — buy one through the du or e& app, at a counter, or online, and the same line continues.



**B. Installed, but the line never activates.** The most common UAE-specific cause is incomplete identity verification: TDRA registration requires a passport (and entry stamp) match, sometimes with a selfie, before the line goes live. Re-open the activation flow or the carrier app and finish the KYC step; at a store, staff complete it for you.



**C. "Two SIMs per passport" refusal.** If your passport already holds two active UAE lines — common with repeat visitors — a third will be refused. Deactivate an old line at a carrier store before buying new.



**D. Five bars in Dubai, no service at the border.** A UAE tourist pack is UAE-only; at the Saudi or Omani border it drops to roaming, if it works at all. Either accept pay-per-use roaming from the tourist line or carry a separate regional eSIM for the crossing.

### Can I run an UAE eSIM and my home SIM at the same time?



Yes, and it is the usual setup: your home line stays active for bank codes while the UAE eSIM carries data. Two cautions. Switch off data roaming on the home SIM so background traffic is not billed at home rates, and remember that a data-only eSIM cannot receive SMS, so one-time codes still land on your own number.
