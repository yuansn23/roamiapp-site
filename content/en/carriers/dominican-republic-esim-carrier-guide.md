The Dominican Republic eSIM question is really a resort question. If you are spending a week at an all-inclusive in Punta Cana, the hotel network will cover the lobby, buffet and pool deck — and quietly fall apart in your room, at the quiet pool, or the moment you leave the gates for an excursion. A [Dominican Republic eSIM](/dominican-esim/) keeps you on real carrier networks instead, and since your phone must be eSIM-capable and unlocked to use one, run it through the [compatibility checker](/compatibility/) before you buy anything.

**Quick answer:** Claro if you are moving around Dominican Republic, Altice Dominicana if you base yourself in one city. Speeds, prices and the registration fine print below all support that split — start at the [compatibility page](/compatibility/) to rule out handset problems.

**Quick verdict:** Claro is the fastest network in the Dominican Republic — Ookla's Speedtest Award measured a median download of 169.96 Mbps in Q1–Q2 2026, and named it the fastest mobile network in the entire Caribbean region. Altice Dominicana is a strong second, especially in the Punta Cana and Puerto Plata resort belts, with a median 5G download of 162 Mbps in Ookla's earlier Q1 2024 data. Viva is a small budget network you can safely ignore as a visitor. Buying a local SIM means an in-person passport registration at a carrier store; a prepaid travel eSIM skips the counter entirely and works on landing. Two more numbers worth knowing: Dominican data costs about USD 0.79 per GB — cheap by global standards — and the country ranks 34th worldwide for mobile speed, well above the global median.

## Why all-inclusive resort Wi-Fi fails in the Dominican Republic

Punta Cana alone has tens of thousands of hotel rooms, and almost all of them sit behind the same wall: resort Wi-Fi built for lobbies, not for rooms. The pattern repeats across Bavaro, Playa Dorada in Puerto Plata and the Casa de Campo complex in La Romana. Coverage ends where your room balcony starts, video calls drop at peak evening hours when every guest streams at once, and the moment you book a catamaran day trip to Isla Saona or a buggy tour through the countryside, you are offline — because excursions are exactly where the resort network does not follow you.

There is also a safety and logistics angle. Ride-hailing, WhatsApp — which is the default way Dominicans communicate with businesses and taxis — and offline-free Google Maps all need live data, not a lobby router. A Dominican Republic eSIM solves this differently from a physical SIM: you install it at home on Wi-Fi, it attaches to Claro, Altice or Viva the moment you land at Punta Cana (PUJ) or Las Américas (SDQ), and your home number stays active for banking codes on the side. If you have never installed one, [how eSIM activation works](/faq/what-is-esim-activation-and-how-does-it-work/) explains the mechanics in a few minutes.

Three things about resort Wi-Fi are worth stating plainly, because they decide how you pack:

- **The router is sized for the lobby, not the resort.** Guest reviews across the Bavaro strip repeat the same two complaints — Wi-Fi that is "very poor for such a hotel," and connections that are merely slow in the room but fine on the pool deck. A five-star rating on the room does not translate into a five-star access point 200 metres away.
- **Evening is the contention hour.** Everyone returns from dinner at once and streams. If you need a video call, 7 a.m. is a better bet than 9 p.m.
- **"Free Wi-Fi in all rooms" is an amenity description, not a bandwidth guarantee.** It tells you an access point exists on your floor. It says nothing about whether a call will hold.

None of that is a reason to skip the resort network. It is a reason not to depend on it for anything that matters: a transfer confirmation, a dive operator's WhatsApp, a card payment that needs an SMS code.

### Punta Cana and Bávaro

This is where the resort-versus-network gap is widest and least important, because the mobile network is so strong. Claro and Altice both run 5G along the main Bávaro road, and 4G covers the gaps. If your whole trip is one resort plus a few excursions, a travel eSIM gives you data on the beach, in the taxi, on the catamaran, and in the room — which is precisely where the hotel router gives up.

### Cap Cana coverage

The newest resort zone on the east coast, and the one most likely to have in-room Wi-Fi that actually works, because the infrastructure was built this decade. It also sits squarely inside the Punta Cana coverage footprint. Two independent facts make Cap Cana the easiest connectivity decision on the island.

### Puerto Plata, Playa Dorada, Cabarete and Sosúa coverage

The north coast's coverage is best described as a corridor: strong from Playa Dorada through the city to Sosúa, and weaker on the coastal mountain roads west toward Montecristi and east toward Río San Juan. Cabarete's kitesurf crowd is the reason inDrive is worth having installed here — cash fares and a locally negotiated price beat waiting for an Uber that may not accept the trip.

### Samaná and Las Terrenas: the dead-zone peninsula

The peninsula is the one place in the Dominican Republic where I would change a hotel booking over connectivity, because the rural roads between towns are genuinely dead on every carrier. Las Terrenas has both carrier networks and a strong French-Italian expat business scene that runs on WhatsApp; the villages on the way there do not. Download offline maps for the whole peninsula before you leave Santo Domingo.

### La Romana, Bayahíbe and Casa de Campo coverage

La Romana is the gateway to Saona and Catalina, and Bayahíbe is the port. Coverage in both towns is good — which matters, because it is your last chance to load maps, confirm the boat and message home before the signal drops away with the coastline.

### Santo Domingo city coverage

The capital is a different product entirely. Business hotels in Piantini and Naco, and the boutique conversions in the Zona Colonial, are used by people who work from them, and their Wi-Fi reflects that. The mobile network is the country's densest. The real constraint in Santo Domingo is congestion, not coverage: on cruise days and festival weekends, speeds in the old city sag under the load of every visitor testing the network at once.

### Claro vs Altice: which Dominican Republic carrier is faster?

Claro's headline numbers are the strongest in the market and, unusually for a Caribbean operator, they hold up away from the capital. The Speed Score of **67.59** and the **169.96 Mbps** median download from Ookla's Q1–Q2 2026 measurement are national figures, not city figures, which is why resort staff and long-stay visitors treat Claro as the default.

- **Best at:** reach. Claro is the network that still has signal on the Santo Domingo–Samaná road, in the Cordillera Central towns and on the long transfer legs between resort zones.
- **Visitor purchase route:** a Claro store or an airport counter with your passport, or a packaged prepaid line at a supermarket. Claro also runs its own eSIM migration service through its online store and app for customers who already hold a Claro line.
- **What it costs:** the carrier's prepaid Internet packages are bought and managed from the handset — dial `*112#` for the Internet menu, `*111#` for the single balance menu, or use the Mi Claro app. Claro advertises weekly bonuses of up to 20 GB plus 50 minutes on prepaid, and a recharge bonus from RD$150.
- **What goes wrong:** the paperwork, not the network. The passport registration is in person, the counter staff work in Spanish, and a fresh line can show bars before it shows data.
- **Scenario:** you drive the Santo Domingo–Samaná highway in the rain, the map re-routes you around a landslide, and Claro is the reason the new route loads. That is the whole argument for paying for the biggest footprint.

### Altice: the resort-corridor specialist

Altice Dominicana is the network that most travel profiles will actually attach to on the Punta Cana and Puerto Plata strips, and it has spent the last few years converting a city-first network into a coastal one. It posted a Speed Score of **60.66** in the same Q1–Q2 2026 Ookla measurement — second nationally, and close enough to Claro that the gap is invisible on a resort corridor.

- **Best at:** density in the places tourists spend money — the Bávaro strip, Santo Domingo, Santiago and the north-coast resort belt. On a beach holiday that never leaves the resort zone, Altice is genuinely equivalent.
- **Visitor purchase route:** Altice stores, and prepaid SIMs sold in supermarkets and phone shops. The carrier's own eSIM path exists for existing lines.
- **What it costs:** Altice sells short-validity prepaid data packs it calls *paqueticos*, which is the cheapest way to buy a few days of data as a visitor.
- **What goes wrong:** the footprint thins faster than Claro's once you leave the corridor. Buying Altice for a Cheap-Bávaro price and then driving to Jarabacoa or Samaná is the classic mismatch.
- **Scenario:** a week in Bávaro where the only long drive is the airport transfer. Altice or Claro, either is fine; you will never notice which one you are on.

### Trimming the bill: cheapest Dominican Republic plans

Viva (Grupo Telemicro) is the third operator and the cheapest, and it is the one to skip. Ookla measured a Speed Score of **29.27** for Viva in Q1–Q2 2026 — less than half of Altice and under half of Claro — and it remains a 4G-only network in practice. Viva's prepaid packs are the cheapest on the island, which is exactly why its SIMs are sold to residents who never leave the city, not to visitors who will spend a week between a resort, a boat and a mountain road. If a kiosk offers you a Viva SIM because it is the cheapest, ask what else they have.

## Documentation Rules for SIMs

Buying local is genuinely cheap. The friction is all administrative, and knowing the steps in advance is most of the work.

### Is there paperwork for SIMs in Dominican Republic?

1. **Go to a carrier-owned store or an airport counter.** Claro, Altice and Viva all sell prepaid lines this way. City stores are usually cheaper than the airport kiosks, and quieter.
2. **Bring your physical passport.** Dominican law requires prepaid lines to be registered to an identity, and for a visitor that identity is the passport. Staff copy it, register the line to your name, and in many outlets photograph you holding the SIM.
3. **Expect 20–45 minutes**, in Spanish-heavy customer service, before you have a working line.
4. **Test data before you leave the counter.** A freshly registered prepaid line can show signal bars before the data package is active. If nothing loads, ask staff to confirm the package rather than editing your APN.
5. **Top up with vouchers or the carrier app.** Prepaid credit and data packs are recharged with scratch cards or e-vouchers sold in supermarkets and corner shops, or through Mi Claro for Claro customers.

The same identity logic applies if you buy a local eSIM directly from Claro or Altice: the profile is digital, but the paperwork is not optional.

### Does Dominican Republic support your device model?

Indotel runs the **SSN — Sistema de Series Negadas** — a national database of IMEIs and serials reported stolen, lost or blocked by Dominican operators. It is a public lookup on the regulator's portal, and it exists for a specific reason: a device whose IMEI sits on the list cannot register on Dominican networks, no matter which SIM is inside it.

- **Find your IMEI** by dialling `*#06#`, or from Settings → About on either platform.
- **Check it before you travel** if the phone is second-hand, refurbished or bought abroad. This is the single most useful pre-trip check for a device with a murky history.
- **If your IMEI shows as blocked**, the fix is administrative, not technical: contact the operator that registered the block, or file a claim with Indotel with proof of purchase. Sorting that at a counter in Punta Cana is a day lost.
- **One caveat:** the database reflects the Dominican registry. A handset blacklisted in another country may not appear here, and vice versa.

## Dominican Republic cruise-day eSIM connectivity: the main ports

Yes — with one honest caveat about timing. The main cruise ports (Amber Cove and Taino Bay in Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, La Romana, Samaná) all sit in towns with dense 4G/5G coverage, so a Dominican Republic eSIM gives you live data from the moment the ship docks: Uber and taxi coordination, Google Maps, WhatsApp calls home, and a speedtest to prove it. Ships themselves run on satellite or maritime networks, not local towers, so expect no connection at sea and full connection at the pier.

The practical warning is about activation day. If your ship's first stop is the Dominican Republic and your eSIM has never connected anywhere, install and set it up before boarding rather than troubleshooting on a pier with patchy port Wi-Fi. Install the profile at home on Wi-Fi, leave it disabled, and toggle it on when you see Dominican towers. If your itinerary continues to the mainland, the same logic applies across borders — a [United States travel eSIM](/united-states-esim/) covers the pre- or post-cruise nights in Miami or Fort Lauderdale, where a DR-only plan goes dark.

### Amber Cove (Puerto Plata) cruise coverage

[Amber Cove](https://www.ambercoveport.com/faqs) is a purpose-built private terminal on the Maimón peninsula, opened by Carnival Corporation in 2015 and served by Carnival's brands plus a rotating list of other lines. That design has direct connectivity consequences.

- **There is no pedestrian exit.** The complex is surrounded by undeveloped terrain, so you cannot walk out to the public road. Authorised transport inside the terminal is the only way out.
- **Ride-hailing is not permitted in.** Unauthorised taxis and app drivers are turned away, and departures use a regulated taxi dispatch plaza inside the welcome area. Your Uber app is useless here; the taxi rank is the tool.
- **The Wi-Fi is a paid product, not an amenity.** Guest reports put the paid tier at roughly USD 8 per day for the faster connection, and onboard cruise internet packages generally do not extend to shore facilities.
- **There is no swimmable beach at the pier.** The shoreline is concrete and wharf-based; a beach day means a taxi to Playa Dorada or another north-coast beach.
- **One ATM dispenses pesos only.** Useful if you want to top up a local line; useless if you arrived with dollars and no local cash.

Which is the whole case for a travel eSIM on an Amber Cove day: you step off the ship with data already working, at the pier, without buying the port's Wi-Fi pass.

### Taino Bay (Puerto Plata) cruise coverage

Taino Bay is roughly a 20-minute drive west of Amber Cove and works the other way round: it sits against the city, so the historic centre and San Felipe fortress are a 15–20 minute walk from the berth. Mobile coverage is the town's own, which means it is dense and free. Vendors at the port sell local SIMs, but a travel eSIM installed at home saves you the queue.

### Santo Domingo cruise ports: Don Diego and Sans Soucí

Ships berth at Don Diego Quay or in the Sans Soucí zone, roughly 2 km from the city centre — a 5–10 minute drive, and walkable to the Zona Colonial for anyone comfortable on foot. This is the best-connected cruise call in the country: the capital's 5G footprint reaches the waterfront, Uber operates here, and the metro runs on RD$20 cash tickets if you want to go further than the colonial grid. If your itinerary offers only one Dominican port, Santo Domingo is the one where live data changes the day most.

### La Romana and Samaná cruise coverage

La Romana's ships dock at the Casa de Campo resort port, with Altos de Chavón about five minutes away and a low walkability score that makes a taxi or shuttle the realistic plan. La Romana is also the launch point for Saona Island catamaran trips, which matters for the next section. Samaná calls are usually tendered into Santa Bárbara de Samaná, where coverage is good in town — and disappears the moment a whale-watching or Los Haitises boat turns into the bay.

## Excursion coverage: where the resort network ends and the boat starts

Excursion coverage is the clearest argument for carrying your own data. Every one of the headline day trips takes you outside the resort's footprint by design.

### Saona Island excursion coverage

The most popular excursion in the country and the one where coverage behaves most predictably badly. You leave Bayahíbe with full signal and lose it as the boat moves offshore. Signal is intermittent near Mano Juan, the island's only village, and on the beaches facing the mainland; at the natural pool mid-crossing there is effectively nothing. There is no Wi-Fi on the island. Practical consequence: screenshot your pickup time, the operator's WhatsApp and your return boat details before you board, and agree a meeting point with your group.

### Catalina Island excursion coverage

Catalina sits about 1.5 miles off the mainland and receives signal on and off depending on how far the boat is from shore. Enough for an occasional WhatsApp message, not enough for an upload. The same rule applies: everything you might need to look up, look up before the anchor drops.

### Hoyo Azul and Scape Park (Cap Cana) coverage

An inland cenote inside a park, which means the coverage profile is inverted — you have signal at reception and in the car park, and you lose it as you descend into the sinkhole. Book and pay before you arrive; there is nothing to settle at the water.

### Damajagua waterfalls (27 Charcos) coverage

The falls sit inland from Puerto Plata on the Imbert corridor, above the town of the same name. Signal holds through Puerto Plata and Imbert and fades in the gorge. Bring a waterproof pouch rather than planning to shoot video on the ladders, and load the road map for the return leg in town.

### The Samaná whale season: coverage and planning

This one has a fixed calendar and a real connectivity gap, so plan both. The season runs from **15 January to 30 March 2026**, set and enforced by the Ministry of Environment, with peak activity in mid-February through mid-March. Boats leave from Santa Bárbara de Samaná and the run to the observation areas takes you well into the bay, where a phone that was streaming in the marina will not hold a call. Regulations are strict and worth knowing before you book: a minimum approach distance of **80 metres**, a maximum of three boats per whale group, and no swimming with whales in the bay. Operators must carry a permit — Moto Marina, Victoria Marine and Whale Samaná are the names you will see.

### Jarabacoa, Constanza and Pico Duarte: the Cordillera dead zone

The Cordillera Central is the country's adventure region and its most honest dead zone. Jarabacoa and Constanza have solid town coverage on Claro; the roads between them, the waterfall trails, and everything above the treeline do not. A Pico Duarte ascent — the Caribbean's highest peak, up in the 3,000-metre range — is a two- or three-day mule-supported trek with no coverage on route. That is a satellite messenger trip, not an eSIM trip. Everything else in the Cordillera is a download-the-map-first trip.

### Boat-day eSIM prep: what to do before any boat leaves

- Download the offline map area for the excursion, not just the resort.
- Screenshot the booking confirmation and the operator's WhatsApp number.
- Tell someone ashore the boat name and expected return time.
- Set your phone to a low-power mode; offshore searching for a signal drains a battery faster than almost anything else.

## 5G in Dominican Republic: Claro, Altice and Viva 5G coverage compared

Both major carriers have deployed 5G on the **n78 (3.5 GHz)** band, which is why the measured 5G speeds are high and the coverage islands are small. The honest picture:

- **Where it exists:** Santo Domingo, Santiago, Punta Cana and Bávaro, Puerto Plata, and the business and tourist hubs around them.
- **How often you are on it:** in Ookla's Q1 2024 measurement, Claro's 5G availability was **38.5%** and Altice's **32.1%**. Even on a 5G phone in a 5G city, most sessions run on 4G.
- **What 4G delivers:** the Dominican 4G layer is fast and widely deployed, and it is what you will use for maps, ride-hailing and calls.
- **What this means for buying:** do not pay a premium for the letters 5G. Pay for coverage where you are going, and let the phone take 5G when it finds it.

### Claro vs Altice: which is better in Dominican Republic?

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs and never registers — are covered in our [eSIM problem-solving guide](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/). The patterns specific to this market:

**A. The eSIM will not install**
1. Check Settings → General → About and confirm the phone is not carrier-locked.
2. Re-check the QR image — a code that has been scanned once will not work again.
3. Have the provider check the profile's status and issue a replacement if it is stuck.

**B. Installed, but no signal bars**
1. Re-check the lock status.
2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → pick Claro, Altice or Viva manually instead of automatic.
3. Full network settings reset, then another reboot.

**C. Signal bars, but no data**
1. Set the DR profile as the line that carries mobile data.
2. Enable roaming for the DR line only, and leave your home line's roaming off.
3. Check the allowance. A used-up data pack leaves bars with nothing behind them, and a fresh local prepaid line can show bars before registration completes.

**D. "SOS" or "Emergency calls only"**
1. Make sure the DR profile is enabled in your phone's SIM manager.
2. Disable your home line's data or remove other profiles while the DR profile attaches.
3. Reboot and reset network settings.
4. Final step: remove the profile and re-add it with a fresh QR from your provider.

If you are mid-excursion — on a catamaran to Saona, in the Damajagua gorge, above Jarabacoa — the display is telling you the truth. No profile fixes physics; the connection returns with the coastline.

## Dominican Republic carrier eSIMs: ask and answer

### Is identification needed for Dominican Republic SIMs?

Only if you buy directly from a Dominican carrier — Indotel requires prepaid lines to be registered to an identity, so stores and airport counters photocopy your passport. A travel eSIM is a roaming profile bought online before you fly, so no in-person registration exists to skip.

### Which carrier blankets most of Dominican Republic?

Altice and Claro are both excellent on the resort strip, with 5G along the main corridor. Claro holds up better the moment you leave it — toward Miches, Higüey or the Samaná road — which is why it is the safer single-network pick.

### Activating on Claro, Altice and Viva

Local prepaid data averages about USD 0.79 per GB (Cable.co.uk, 80th of 237 markets), and travel eSIMs cost more for the convenience of instant delivery and multi-network access. Compare both on the [Dominican Republic eSIM plans page](/dominican-esim/) before deciding.

### Is a hotel's Wi-Fi good enough instead of an eSIM?

For email in the lobby, yes. For anything time-critical, no — and the gap is widest in exactly the places you will need it. Guest reviews of Bávaro resorts consistently flag in-room Wi-Fi as the weakest facility on the property; the connection is also worst in the evening when every guest returns from dinner. Treat the resort router as a bonus and your eSIM as the plan.

### Does WhatsApp calling work on Dominican Republic networks?

Yes. Keep your home SIM active for calls and SMS verification and run data on the eSIM — WhatsApp stays tied to your home number. Just switch off data roaming on the home line so it does not bill you for background traffic.

### Will my eSIM work in Jarabacoa, Constanza or the mountains?

Claro covers the Cordillera Central towns well; the drives between them are patchy on every network. If the mountains are the point of your trip, a multi-network profile is worth more than any single-carrier plan.

### Claro vs Altice 5G: which is better in Dominican Republic?

Yes. Claro and Altice have deployed 5G in Santiago, Punta Cana and other tourist and business hubs, though most of the country still runs on fast 4G. Ookla's 2024 measurement put Claro's median 5G download at 185 Mbps where 5G is available.

### Do cruise passengers need a Dominican SIM at all?

If the DR is one stop among several, a regional or Caribbean-capable travel eSIM is simpler than a country plan. If it is your only land day, a short-duration Dominican eSIM still beats the ship's expensive satellite Wi-Fi for the hours you are ashore.

### Does a travel eSIM work at Amber Cove, given the port's rules?

Yes, and it is the reason to carry one. Amber Cove has no pedestrian exit and does not admit app-based ride pickups, so your phone is how you arrange and confirm the regulated taxi, check the excursion meeting time and message home — and the port's own Wi-Fi is a paid product rather than a free amenity.

### Is 20 GB overkill for Dominican Republic?

Travel eSIMs simply stop when the allowance ends, and you top up online in minutes. Local unlimited plans usually throttle to a reduced speed after a fair usage threshold rather than cutting off.

### Can I use two eSIMs at once?

Most phones store several profiles but run one data line at a time. A common setup: home SIM for calls and codes, Dominican eSIM for data, and a stored profile for the next country on your itinerary.

### What does a gigabyte cost in Dominican Republic?

Per gigabyte, yes — local data averages about USD 0.79 per GB and the prepaid packs are among the cheapest in the Caribbean. Add the passport registration, the counter queue in Spanish, the top-up rhythm and the single-network limit, and the gap narrows sharply for a one- to two-week trip. Over a month, buy local.
