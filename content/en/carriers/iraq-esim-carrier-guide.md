## Your eSIM basics: two licensing regimes, one country

Every misunderstanding about an Iraq eSIM starts here. The federal regulator — the **Communications and Media Commission (CMC)**, established in 2004 and headquartered in Baghdad — licenses the three national mobile operators: Zain Iraq, Asiacell and Korek Telecom. The Kurdistan Regional Government runs its own oversight through the **Ministry of Transport and Communications** in Erbil, and its own ecosystem has grown alongside the federal trio: **Fastlink** and **Newroz Telecom** operate inside the Kurdistan Region, while Korek, though federally licensed, is Erbil-based and functions as the region's home operator.

Iraq counts 26 international crossings — 20 under federal control and six under the Kurdistan Region, including four land crossings and two airports. That administrative split shows up in telecoms too: the big operators' signals span both regimes, but regulatory enforcement does not apply evenly, and that is where visitors get caught.

| | Federal Iraq | Kurdistan Region |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Regulator | Communications and Media Commission (CMC), Baghdad | KRG Ministry of Transport and Communications, Erbil |
| Federally licensed operators | Zain Iraq, Asiacell, Korek | The same three, enforced unevenly |
| Region-specific operators | None of consequence | Fastlink, Newroz Telecom, Tishknet |
| 5G status | Not commercial; federal and KRG authorities announced coordinated 5G planning in August 2026 | Earlier exploratory steps; coordination with Baghdad ongoing |
| Visitor's easiest route | A registered in-store SIM, or a travel eSIM riding Asiacell or Zain radio | A dual-network travel eSIM, or a Fastlink line bought locally |
| Where the rules bite | Identity registration and device checks apply strictly | Registration is lighter, but a region-only visa will not cover Baghdad |

The Korek dispute is the clearest demonstration of why the split matters. In June 2026 the CMC declared Korek's operating licence expired, and in August it moved to shut the operator's switches, exchanges and sales offices in Baghdad; reporting through September 2026 put the number of governorates where Korek offices had closed at eight, with no outage recorded. A Baghdad court then issued a stay suspending that decision, and the KRG's transport minister said publicly that Korek would keep operating inside the Kurdistan Region regardless. So: **do not build an Iraq eSIM plan around Korek service in federal Iraq**, and treat Korek inside Kurdistan as strong but legally unsettled.

## Is your phone ready for your eSIM? Bands, locks and setup

An Iraq eSIM connects to Iraqi 4G, which runs on LTE bands common across Europe, the Middle East and Asia — 1800 MHz (Band 3) among them — so virtually any unlocked international handset attaches without drama. There is **no commercial 5G network in operation yet**: federal authorities coordinated 5G planning with the Kurdistan Region in August 2026, but an Iraq eSIM today means 4G LTE, full stop.

### Iraq eSIM compatibility: the three checks that decide it

- **Carrier lock.** A phone locked to your home carrier will not accept any third-party profile. On iPhone, check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock; "No SIM restrictions" is what you want.
- **eSIM support.** Dial `*#06#` — an EID on screen means the phone can hold a profile. If you are unsure about your exact model, the [compatibility page](/compatibility/) settles it by model number, not marketing name.
- **Regional quirks.** iPhones sold in mainland China have the eSIM hardware disabled, and some carrier-purchased Samsungs grey out the eSIM menu until the original carrier unlocks the feature.

### Iraq eSIM dual-SIM behaviour worth knowing

Keep your home SIM live for bank codes and calls, point mobile data at the Iraq eSIM, and switch data roaming off on the home line so background traffic does not bill against it. Data-only travel profiles cannot receive SMS, so nothing time-critical should depend on your Iraqi line.

## Registering an Iraq eSIM: CMC rules for foreign visitors

**Registration comes first, everywhere.** Iraqi law requires every mobile line, SIM or eSIM, to be registered against an identity document, and the rule applies to foreigners exactly as it does to residents. At an official operator store, staff scan your passport, record your details in the registration portal, and at Zain Iraq the process includes a photograph and fingerprint. Expect 15 to 30 minutes.

### Identity checks at Iraq SIM counters

1. Choose an official operator store or a staffed airport counter — Baghdad International or Erbil International both have them.
2. Hand over your original passport with the physical visa sticker or entry stamp already inside it.
3. Give a local address: hotel, guesthouse or host address. Nothing is posted to it.
4. Let staff record your details in the operator's registration portal; at Zain Iraq expect a photograph and fingerprint too.
5. Receive the eSIM QR code, printed or on screen, and scan it while the counter staff are still with you.
6. Buy the first bundle with cash, then confirm data works with Wi-Fi switched off before you leave.

### Iraq internet curbs and rhythms worth planning around

During national school exams, Iraq has scheduled nationwide internet curbs — dozens of outages were documented across the 2025 exam season — so a dead connection between roughly 6 and 8 in the morning during exam season is the state doing exactly what it announced.

## Working APN entries

Here is the honest version: there is no reliably published, operator-confirmed APN table for Iraq that can be verified the way it can for Canada or Germany, and guessing APN strings causes more failures than it fixes. What is actually true:

- **Travel eSIM profiles configure themselves.** The profile carries its own APN invisibly. If data fails on a travel Iraq eSIM, the APN is almost never the cause — check the roaming toggle and the selected data line first.
- **Carrier-issued lines auto-provision.** When Asiacell, Zain Iraq or Fastlink staff install your eSIM or SIM in store, the line and its settings activate on their network side. If anything is missing, the store that issued the line is the correct source for the exact values.
- **Manual entry paths**, if a technician ever gives you values: on iPhone, Settings → Cellular → your eSIM line → Cellular Data Network; on Android, Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names. Restart after saving.

| Situation | Where to look | Expected answer |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Travel eSIM, data fails on arrival | Provider's support chat | Profile-side settings, not your phone |
| Carrier eSIM issued in store | The issuing store's counter | Exact APN on request |
| Signal present, no data | Settings → Cellular → data line | Confirm the eSIM, not your home SIM, is selected |
| Data stopped mid-trip | Operator app or provider dashboard | Allowance exhausted, or a scheduled national outage |
| Roaming in Kurdistan on a federal line | Network selection screen | Reselect manually; the profile may be clinging to a fading operator |
| Data works, calls do not | Provider's plan description | Most travel profiles are data-only by design |
| New device, profile moved across | Provider's installation guide | Reinstall the profile; QR codes are single-use |

If a store-issued line shows signal but no data after you leave, return to the issuing store rather than experimenting — registration errors are common in Iraq and trivially fixed at the counter.

## Questions and answers

### Does Asiacell sell eSIMs to tourists?

Yes, with a condition. Asiacell issues eSIM QR codes at its main shops for new lines, and Iraqi identity registration — passport for visitors — applies exactly as it does to physical SIMs. Tourists who want to skip the shop buy a travel eSIM riding the Asiacell network, installed before the flight.

### Can you buy an Iraq eSIM from Asiacell?

No. Zain Iraq's eSIM is purchased in a store after ID registration; the operator's own new-line page walks customers through exactly that flow. Visitors abroad who want Zain Iraq coverage need a store visit after landing or a travel eSIM that specifically names it as a partner.

### Is Korek eSIM a safe choice in 2026?

Inside the Kurdistan Region, Korek remains the dominant network and the KRG has committed to keeping it operating. But its federal licence is disputed, the CMC moved to shut its Baghdad facilities during 2026, and a court stay is the only thing holding enforcement off. For a visitor, Korek should be a fallback, never the primary.

### Signing up: Iraq SIM registration

If the eSIM comes from an Iraqi operator, yes — passport registration, a photograph and often a fingerprint, done in person at an official store. Travel eSIMs bought abroad need no registration, because they operate under the provider's roaming agreements rather than an Iraqi subscription.

### Getting hold of a Iraq eSIM

Baghdad International and Erbil International both have carrier counters in arrivals, and Erbil's include Korek and Zain Iraq. Those counters sell physical SIMs and complete the registration on the spot. They do not sell travel eSIMs, and airport queues after a long flight are a poor place to install one.

### Will one eSIM work in both federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region?

Physically, yes — every major operator's signal reaches across the internal border. Practically, strength flips: Asiacell and Zain Iraq lead in the federal cities, Korek leads in Erbil and Duhok. A dual-network travel eSIM handles the transitions automatically.

### Why did my internet stop during the morning?

If it is national school exam season, Iraq deliberately curtails connectivity during exam hours to prevent cheating — the practice is officially announced and applies to every operator. Outages in the 2025 season numbered in the dozens between May and September. No settings change on your phone will bypass it.
