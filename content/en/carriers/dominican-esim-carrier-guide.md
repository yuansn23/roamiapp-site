## Claro, Altice and Viva: what the numbers show

### Claro: the nationwide default

Claro (América Móvil) is the country's largest operator and its coverage lead is the kind that decides itineraries: best in every provincial capital, strongest along the resort corridors of the east and north coasts, and the most consistent signal on the highways. Ookla's carrier-level measurements have repeatedly shown Claro ahead in both urban and rural medians, and its 5G network — running on the 3.5 GHz band in the main cities — is the country's most developed. For anyone mixing beach time with road travel, Claro is the network a Dominican Republic eSIM should reach first.

### Altice vs Claro: which Dominican carrier is faster?

Altice (the merged Orange–Tricom network) concentrates capacity where the offices are. Its 5G delivers the fastest median downloads in Santo Domingo and holds strong coverage through Santiago's commercial districts, which makes it the natural pick for business travellers and digital nomads whose week happens indoors and in the two big cities. Outside the urban strip the map thins noticeably faster than Claro's — fine for a capital-and-resorts trip, limiting for a ring-road itinerary.

### Altice vs Claro: which is better in the Dominican Republic?

Viva runs the smallest network, focused on prepaid and value segments, with 4G coverage across the major tourist zones and provincial towns. Speeds are adequate for WhatsApp, maps and social uploads rather than streaming, and 5G is not part of its story. For a week at an all-inclusive where the phone's job is messaging and the odd video call, it is entirely sufficient — and the cheapest local way to get it.

### Altice vs Claro 5G: which is better in the Dominican Republic?

Both Claro and Altice switched on 5G on the 3.5 GHz band (n78) starting in December 2021, and coverage has expanded steadily since, concentrated in Santo Domingo, Santiago and the resort hubs. Viva remains 4G. In practice, a 5G-capable phone on a Claro- or Altice-riding eSIM sees 5G across the main cities and drops to a strong 4G layer everywhere else — which, at 125.69 Mbps national median, is still fast by Caribbean standards.

## SIM and eSIM registration in the Dominican Republic

This is where the Dominican Republic differs from most of the Caribbean. **Every mobile line in the Dominican Republic must be registered to an identified person**, a rule in force since 2014 and tightened since: under Indotel's activation procedure, a line is activated in person at a carrier store or authorised dealer, with photo ID — passport for visitors — the buyer's photograph taken, and the **device's IMEI registered against the line**. The registration enforcement is real: unregistered lines can be cut off without notice, and in 2025 Indotel moved again to tighten prepaid identity validation (Resolution 064-2025), with carriers urging users to validate or lose their numbers.

What does that mean in practice?

- **Buying a local SIM as a tourist works**, but it means a store visit with your passport, your photo and your phone's IMEI — budget an hour in Santo Domingo or a resort-town dealer.
- **IMEI concerns do not apply to roaming profiles.** A travel eSIM issued abroad attaches to the local network under a roaming agreement; it is not a locally registered line, and there is no IMEI block to worry about. The block risk belongs to grey-market physical SIM cards sold on the street, which can and do get terminated — a clean, registered profile with a top-up option is the calmer purchase.
- **Buy nothing from street vendors.** The registered-channel requirement exists precisely because unregistered lines cause problems — the same problems you would inherit.

If your phone is locked to a US carrier, check its status before travel: Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock should read "No SIM restrictions". A locked phone will refuse any third-party profile, Dominican or otherwise.

## What 1 GB costs on each plan

The Dominican Republic's two dominant trip shapes stress a phone in opposite ways, and it is worth sizing the plan to the right one.

**The all-inclusive week.** Resort Wi-Fi is generally good in the lobby and weak at the pool's far end, which is exactly where you will want it. A modest data plan (3–5 GB) covers the gap: ride-hailing into Punta Cana town, restaurant bookings, and the excursion logistics that all run on WhatsApp. Excursion operators confirm pickups, send boat times and share photos through WhatsApp groups — a data-only eSIM with your home number handles every part of that.

**The road trip.** Samaná, the Samaná–Las Terrenas road, the DR-7 toll road and the interior runs are where Claro's coverage lead becomes tangible. Two habits: download offline maps before leaving the resort zone (the gaps on the Samaná road are real), and screenshot reservations — a dead stretch should cost you a view, not a check-in.

**One more option worth naming:** travellers on US carriers often arrive with roaming included in their existing plans. That works, but it is metered, throttled after a cap, and keeps your number exposed to roaming charges on background traffic. A local-data profile with the home SIM's roaming switched off remains the cleaner and cheaper default for stays beyond a few days.

## Installing your your eSIM: the pre-flight steps

All of this happens before you board, and none of it involves a shop front: pay, take the profile by email, add it in Settings, then enable data roaming for the eSIM line. Punta Cana resorts have Wi-Fi, but debugging a profile on hotel bandwidth is a worse evening than doing it at home. Each platform's menus are in the [setup walkthrough](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/); when something breaks, the [common failure list](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/) is the place to start.

Island-specific advice is short. Install at home — airport arrivals Wi-Fi is the wrong place to debug a profile, and your ride from Las Américas airport is booked over data. On landing, confirm the eSIM is the selected data line and that roaming is enabled for it specifically. If the profile installs but finds no network, restart once, then manually select Claro from the carrier list — automatic selection occasionally parks on the smallest network first.

One regional note: the Dominican Republic's networks use bands US visitors will recognise (850/1900 MHz legacy plus AWS), so compatibility is rarely an issue for North American handsets. Everyone else: one lookup on the [compatibility list](/compatibility/) settles it.

## Dominican Republic eSIM faults, and the three-minute fixes

**Nothing installs.** A contract handset bought abroad is the usual reason: check the carrier lock before anything else, then rule out a QR code that has already been consumed, since they work once.

**It installs and never finds a network.** Flight mode for a fifteen-second count first, then a manual selection — Claro or Altice rather than Viva, whose footprint thins quickly outside the resort corridors. A restart between the two attempts clears most cases.

**Full bars, no data.** The eSIM must be the selected data line, roaming must be on for that line, and on a locally bought SIM the APN must match the table above. On a travel eSIM, the third cause does not apply.

**"Emergency calls only".** Disabling the other active profile fixes this more often than any of the harder resets. If it does not, reboot, reset network settings, and reinstall from a fresh code as a last resort.

Everything outside these patterns lives in [the general eSIM troubleshooting catalogue](/faq/esim-activation-errors-troubleshooting-guide/).

### Cruise-port days and a Dominican Republic eSIM

The Dominican Republic is a major cruise call, and the connectivity question is narrower than it looks: the profile works normally whenever the ship is alongside and your handset can see land networks, and it does nothing out at sea, where only the ship's own Wi-Fi or satellite service reaches you.

Two habits prevent surprises. Switch mobile data back on after docking rather than leaving roaming enabled throughout, and check whether your plan bills by calendar day or by volume — a port call consumes very little of either, while a sea day with roaming left active can see the phone latch onto a maritime network that is billed at premium rates or not included at all. It is possible and it is inconvenient. Indotel's prepaid identity-validation rules mean a shop visit and a document check, layered on top of the IMEI formalities that apply to grey-market handsets.
