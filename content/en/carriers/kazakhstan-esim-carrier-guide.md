## Local networks open to visitor eSIMs



Three operators split essentially the entire mobile market, each at roughly a third of subscribers: **Kcell** (retailed as Kcell and Activ), **Beeline** (run by KaR-Tel, majority-owned by VEON), and **Tele2/Altel** (operated by Mobile Telecom-Service). There are no meaningful MVNOs and no fourth national network — so the real question is not "which brand" but "which network," because every travel eSIM in Kazakhstan ultimately roams onto one of these three.

### Kcell vs Activ: which Kazakhstan carrier is faster?



Kcell is the network to be on if your itinerary lives in the cities and the mountain day trips around them. Ookla's H1 2025 data put its all-technology median download at **87.61 Mbps** and its median 5G download at **368.95 Mbps** — the highest 5G figure recorded in the country. Kcell's own full-year 2025 reporting, summarised in [Opensignal's Central Asia analysis](https://insights.opensignal.com/2026/05/central-asia-mobile-future-from-5g-hype-to-measurable-network-excellence/dt), shows what that has cost to build: KZT 102.8 billion of capex in 2025 alone, 5G population coverage of **53.54%**, 4G coverage of **86.01%**, 1,600-plus 5G base stations live across 23 cities, and 965 new base stations that brought 4G to 592 additional villages.



- **What it is best at:** raw speed in Almaty and Astana, and 5G density in the business districts where you are most likely to take a video call.

- **Visitor route to a Kcell line:** an airport kiosk or city store, with the original passport for registration; the "Tourist" pack runs about 3,000 ₸ for roughly 20 GB over 14 days.

- **What goes wrong:** Kcell's rural reach trails Beeline's, so it is the wrong single-carrier choice for a long intercity road trip. It is also the pricier of the three on prepaid.

- **Real scenario:** you land at Almaty (ALA), buy a Kcell tourist pack at the arrivals kiosk, and spend three days on 5G through the city, Medeu and the Big Almaty Lake road — where coverage holds fine. The moment you turn east toward Charyn, the advantage evaporates.



### Kcell vs Activ 5G: which is better in Kazakhstan?



Beeline is the network that behaves best where the map is empty. It declined the 2022 spectrum auction outright, so there is no commercial Beeline 5G to connect to, in any city — but its low-band 4G reaches further into the rural regions than either rival. nPerf's 2026 national barometer, built from tests run between April 2025 and March 2026, measures the trade-off in numbers: Beeline's average download was **26.9 Mbps** against Kcell's 73.3 Mbps, with the slowest latency of the three at 56.5 ms — and it still leads the market on reach.



- **What it is best at:** keeping a usable signal on long drives where the other two have already dropped.

- **Visitor route to a Beeline line:** the same airport-counter-or-store path as Kcell; the "Welcome"-style tourist pack is around 3,500 ₸ for roughly 25 GB over 30 days.

- **What goes wrong:** in the 5G cities Beeline is simply the slowest ceiling on offer, and its stores are the busiest, so a counter visit can eat an hour.

- **Real scenario:** the drive from Almaty into the eastern national parks via Basshi — around 250 km — is where Beeline earns its keep; it is also the network most travel eSIMs steer onto for exactly this reason.

- **One thing to watch:** Beeline is extending beyond terrestrial towers, having partnered with **Starlink Direct to Cell** to reach areas ground infrastructure cannot, per the same Opensignal report. Treat that as a direction of travel rather than a product you can buy today.



### Tele2 and Altel: the challenger that won the regions



Tele2 took Ookla's Best Mobile Network award in Kazakhstan with a Connectivity Score of **72.37**, and was the fastest provider in **nine of the country's regions** — the western oil belt, the north-east and the Atyrau corridor included. Its 5G was deployed jointly with Kcell under the same consortium licence, so it is a genuine second 5G network rather than a reseller. nPerf's 2026 tests put its average download at **45.6 Mbps** with the best streaming performance rate of the three operators, **65.8%**.



- **What it is best at:** regional consistency and the cheapest data packages of the three.

- **Visitor route to a Tele2 line:** fewer physical sales points than Kcell or Beeline, which is the practical catch — plan the purchase rather than assuming a counter will be there.

- **What goes wrong:** city performance is excellent but rural coverage is thinner than Beeline's, and store staff are less used to foreign passports.

- **Real scenario:** the Shymkent to Turkistan axis — the classic Silk Road leg to the Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi — sits inside one of the country's strongest regions, where Tele2's regional leadership is directly useful.

## How far does your eSIM reach beyond the two cities?



This is the part most generic Kazakhstan eSIM pages skip, and it is the single biggest gap between expectation and reality on the ground. Kazakhstan is the size of Western Europe with fewer people than the Netherlands, and its network map follows the population: dense in the cities and along the main corridors, thin to nonexistent everywhere else.



What the measured data shows:



- **The regions are not one market.** Almaty Region records the fastest median mobile download (82.63 Mbps); Ulytau, in the middle of the country, records just 29.88 Mbps — an eightfold gap within the same country on the same month of data.

- **Tele2 is the rural-regions leader.** It was the fastest provider across nine regions in Ookla's H1 2025 data, including Atyrau, Pavlodar and the western oil-belt regions, while Kcell dominated the Almaty-side corridor.

- **The Silk Road route holds up better than the long trans-country legs.** The Shymkent–Turkistan axis sits inside the country's third-fastest region, so the classic southern heritage route is well served. Charyn Canyon, Kolsai Lakes and the Altai mountains east of Almaty are a different story: coverage fades within an hour of the city and disappears entirely in the canyons and valleys. Download offline maps before leaving Almaty, and treat any signal you find there as a bonus.

- **Beeline's 4G remains the widest safety net.** Independent operator comparisons consistently credit Beeline with the broadest population coverage outside the 5G cities — the reason most travel eSIM providers that target long-distance highways steer onto KaR-Tel's network.



Two extra realities for remote travel. First, winter cold in the north and long drives anywhere will drain your battery faster than any network setting — carry a power bank. Second, a handful of international services are unreliable in Kazakhstan; if your trip depends on specific apps, sort out any VPN arrangement before departure rather than after landing.

## 5G nationwide: Kcell, Activ and Beeline Kazakhstan 5G coverage compared



Kazakhstan launched 5G commercially in December 2022, when Kcell-Activ and the Tele2/Altel consortium won 100 MHz each in the 3.6–3.8 GHz band. Deployment has been fast by regional standards: more than 3,000 5G base stations were standing by April 2025, with 30–50% of mobile traffic in Almaty and Astana already carried over 5G. The license obligations run to 2027, when every regional center is supposed to have 5G.



The scale of the build is worth a number: the two 100 MHz lots were offered at a starting price of 1.76 billion tenge each and closed at 62.7 billion and 93.4 billion tenge respectively — roughly USD 132.5 million and USD 201.6 million — which is why the operators' 5G plans carry a target of more than 7,000 base stations between them.



For a visitor, three things follow. Your phone needs **n78 (3.5 GHz)** support to see 5G at all; most modern eSIM-capable flagships have it. Outside the major cities you will spend your time on 4G — bands B1, B3, B7, B8 and B20 — which is fine, because Kazakhstan's 4G is fast: the national median mobile download sits at **89.11 Mbps**, 52nd in Ookla's Speedtest Global Index. And if you are on Beeline, 5G is not on the menu at all, by choice. The full rollout story is documented by [The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/04/kazakhstan-installs-over-3000-5g-base-stations/) and tracked independently by [nPerf's Kazakhstan 2025 report](https://blog.nperf.com/kazakhstan-2025-5g-delivers-on-its-promises-and-continues-to-improve-mobile-network-performance).



The gap between 5G coverage and 5G use is the honest caveat. Opensignal's Q1 2026 Central Asia analysis found Kazakhstan's 5G availability at **29.0%** and the time 5G users actually spent connected to 5G at **23%** — meaning most of the time, even in a 5G footprint, your phone is working on 4G. Do not build an itinerary around 5G; build it around 4G and treat 5G as the bonus.

Kazakhstan's rail network is the civilised way to cross the country, and it is also an unplanned connectivity test. The headline service is the **Tulpar-Talgo** between Almaty and Astana: roughly 1,300 km of open country in around 16 hours, tracking the western shore of Lake Balkhash overnight between the old and the new capital. Talgo sets also run on the Almaty–Kostanay route (from December 2025), the Astana–Shymkent run, and, since 14 December 2025, a daily Almaty–Tashkent service that carries more than 400 passengers a day. The [European Rail Timetable's December 2025 newsline](https://europeanrailtimetable.eu/blogs/railway-news/newslines-december-4-2025) lists the full slate of new and extended Talgo services.



What that means for your phone:



- **Leave the city and the map empties.** Between Almaty and Astana, coverage exists in towns and vanishes in the gaps. The longest dead stretches run across the Betpak-Dala interior and along the western shore of Lake Balkhash — hours with no bars at all, on any carrier.

- **Treat the train as an offline day.** Download films, books and offline maps before boarding; the bistro car and the berths do not provide a hotspot.

- **Power matters more than signal.** Modernised Talgo carriages carry power sockets in many compartments, but not all classes and not all trains, so a charged power bank is the safer plan.

- **Buy in advance.** Popular berths on the overnight services sell out around weekends and holidays, and the Almaty–Tashkent Talgo is a single daily departure.

- **A Kazakh number is rarely worth it on the train.** You will not use it in the dead zones, and the arrival-side booking apps work over Wi-Fi at both stations. A pre-installed profile that reattaches to whichever network appears at the next town is the more useful setup.



## Prices across the carriers



Local purchase is straightforward but bureaucratic, and the rules matter more than the prices.



**Passport registration is mandatory.** Every SIM card in Kazakhstan must be registered to a passport, in person, at an official store or kiosk. Bring the physical passport you crossed the border with — copies are not accepted — and expect five to fifteen minutes at the counter while your details go into the national database. Kcell and Beeline both staff arrivals-hall kiosks at Almaty (ALA) and Astana (NQZ) airports; airport pricing runs higher than city stores.



**Device (IMEI) registration is the newer wrinkle.** Kazakhstan has been phasing in rules that block unregistered handsets from local networks — enforcement of device registration began in late 2025. Short-term visitors running a travel eSIM are generally unaffected, but if you plan to put a local SIM in your own phone for a long stay, complete device registration with the carrier (Kcell, for example, runs its own [device registration portal](https://www.kcell.kz/en)). Watch for one exception: eSIM hardware is disabled on iPhones sold in mainland China, which no local network can work around.

### Do you show ID for a Kazakhstan SIM?



If you do want a local line, this is the sequence, and there are no shortcuts in it.



1. **Bring the passport you entered on.** The registration is tied to the document you crossed the border with; a photocopy or a photo on your phone will be refused.

2. **Choose an official sales point.** Carrier stores and airport kiosks can register you; informal street stalls sell cards that may already be registered to someone else, which is a problem when the network verifies ownership.

3. **Expect the wait.** The in-store process takes five to fifteen minutes when there is no queue, and considerably longer at Beeline's busiest counters and at airport arrivals banks.

4. **Ask the operator to test the connection before you leave.** Staff will usually run a data check at the counter; taking the walk-away test yourself, with the SIM as the active data line, saves a return trip.

5. **If you are on your own handset for an extended stay, register the device too.** Phones first brought into Kazakhstan after 24 March 2025 are subject to IMEI checks, and unregistered devices can lose access to local mobile services.

6. **Keep the SIM in the device you registered it in.** Switching a registered card into a different handset is exactly the change the device rules are designed to catch.

## Getting around Kazakhstan with data: Yandex Go and offline maps



In Kazakhstan, connectivity is not a luxury for tourists — it is what makes the transport network work.



**Ride-hailing is the safe way to take a taxi.** Official travel guidance for Kazakhstan is blunt about unlicensed cabs and the overcharging that comes with them, and recommends booking through an app so the destination is fixed and the fare is known before you get in. **Yandex Go** is the dominant app, with inDrive and Uber also present. Two practical warnings: many foreign credit cards cannot be registered in the app, so carry small cash as a fallback; and Yandex Go's own pricing varies by city — the economy tier started at 420 ₸ plus 65 ₸ per kilometre in Almaty and Astana in 2025, making them the most expensive taxi cities in the country, against 220 ₸ plus 60 ₸ per kilometre in Taraz and 240 ₸ plus 55 ₸ per kilometre in Turkistan.



**Download maps before you leave the city.** 2GIS is the local favourite for offlinable city and regional maps with business listings; Maps.me is better for rural and remote tracks; Google Maps is the fallback for highway navigation. All three work offline if you cache the regions in advance, and outside the cities, offline is the only mode you will get.



**Hotel Wi-Fi is common and uneven.** City hotels usually deliver something workable for messaging and browsing, but quality ranges widely and speed falls off in the evening. One specific trap: some hotel and airport networks authenticate you by sending a code to a phone number — which a data-only eSIM cannot receive. Keep your home line active for that, or ask reception for a manual login.



**Public Wi-Fi is everywhere but time-limited** — airports, malls, cafés and some buses — and generally fine for short bursts rather than a working day.

Kazakhstan shares long land borders with four neighbours, and every one of them is a different telecom market. Your Kazakh data does not travel with you, roaming charges from your home carrier are expensive, and a border zone can latch your phone onto a foreign network without asking.



- **Uzbekistan (south).** The main southern crossing points sit on the Shymkent–Tashkent axis, and the Silk Road overland route continues from Turkistan across the border. Buy a separate plan for the Uzbek leg; if you are continuing overland, sort it before you leave Shymkent.

- **Kyrgyzstan (south-east).** The Almaty–Bishkek corridor is a short, busy crossing, popular as a weekend hop. It is a separate network on the other side, so a Kazakh SIM stops at the boundary.

- **Russia (north).** Rail links are the usual route — the Karaganda–Samara service was extended through to Moscow twice weekly in 2025. Read our [Russia eSIM carrier guide](/carriers/russia-esim-carrier-guide/) before you cross, since the network and roaming picture changes completely.

- **China (east).** The Khorgos International Centre of Boundary Cooperation, on the Almaty–Yining corridor, is a genuine oddity: a 5.6 km² cross-border free zone that Chinese and Kazakh citizens and third-country nationals can enter visa-free on a passport, with duty-free shopping on both sides. Traffic through it hit a record in 2025, and clearance has been automated to the point of a "15-second" contactless process. Cross into Guangdong or Xinjiang proper and you are on mainland networks — that is a different trip, covered in our [China eSIM guide](/carriers/china-esim-carrier-guide/).

- **Mongolia (north-east)** is the other great overland crossing, though distant from the main tourist circuits; our [Mongolia eSIM guide](/carriers/mongolia-esim-carrier-guide/) covers the networks on that side.



Planning to continue along the old Silk Road into Russia or China? Check each leg as its own market, and install the next country's profile while you still have Wi-Fi on the Kazakh side of the border.

### Five Kazakh eSIM failures, in the order they turn up



**A. The profile will not install.**

1. Re-check the lock status — a locked phone is the most common cause.

2. Confirm the QR code has not been scanned already; most are single-use.

3. Ask the provider whether the profile is still pending at their end before generating a new one.



**B. No network registration on landing.** Airports at Almaty and Astana sit far from the city centers and cell coverage can be congested at peak arrival banks.

1. Give the phone two minutes, then toggle airplane mode.

2. Switch network selection from automatic to manual and try Kcell, then Tele2, then Beeline in turn — a travel eSIM profile will list the carriers it may use.

3. Reboot if the manual scan returns nothing.



**C. Registered, but data will not flow.**

1. Confirm the eSIM is the active mobile-data line; in dual-SIM setups the phone silently keeps using your home SIM.

2. Switch data roaming **on** for the eSIM and **off** for your home SIM, so you are not billed for background traffic.

3. Recheck the APN table above — but only for locally bought SIMs.

4. Reset network settings and retry.



**D. Bars but no internet in the cities, only in the towns.** This is not a fault. Beyond the Almaty–Astana corridor, 4G coverage is islanded between towns, and the canyon parks east of Almaty have no signal at all.

1. Verify you are outside a known gap by checking the route table above rather than resetting settings.

2. Move to high ground or a settlement before troubleshooting further.

3. Continue with offline maps; the connection returns at the next tower.



**E. "SOS" or "Emergency calls only."**

1. Verify the Kazakh profile is toggled on where your phone lists its SIMs.

2. Disable or delete any other active profile, including a leftover local prepaid eSIM.

3. Reboot, then reset network settings.

4. As a last resort, remove the profile and reinstall from a fresh QR code.

### Kazakhstan SIMs: the paperwork involved



Yes, without exception: every SIM must be registered to a passport in person. Longer stays on your own device can also require IMEI/device registration, which Kazakhstan began enforcing in late 2025. ### Registration steps for Kazakhstan SIMs

In the towns along the way, usually; in the parks and open country between cities, no. Coverage east of Almaty thins within an hour of the city, and the scenic parks beyond it are effectively dead zones. Download offline maps and fuel up on battery before leaving the highway network.
