## Regional eSIM lock status by carrier (deep dive)

Not all Pixel phones are equal when it comes to digital SIM availability – carrier policies and regional firmware play a huge role. Here's a breakdown by region and carrier.

### Japan (SoftBank, au/KDDI, NTT Docomo)

**Status:** Most Japanese carrier variants have eSIM **disabled at the firmware level**.

- **SoftBank and au (KDDI):** Historically, both carriers disable the feature on all Android devices they sell, including Pixels. This is not a SIM lock – it's a permanent firmware restriction. Even after unlocking the SIM for international use, the digital SIM functionality remains inaccessible.
- **NTT Docomo:** Docomo has been more eSIM-friendly on iPhones, but Android devices (including Pixels) typically have the feature disabled as well.
- **How to identify a Japanese carrier Pixel:** Check the boot screen for carrier logos, or look for carrier-specific apps pre-installed. The model number may also end with a region-specific suffix.

**Workaround:** None. You cannot re-enable the feature on these devices. If you need it, you must buy an international version (directly from Google Store) or a secondhand device originally sold outside Japan.

### I Have a Japanese Pixel – What Are My Options?

**Problem:** You already own a Japanese Pixel (SoftBank/au) and just discovered the digital SIM feature is disabled.

**Option 1: Use a physical SIM instead**
- If your Pixel has a physical SIM tray (all Japanese Pixels do), just buy a physical travel SIM.
- For multi-country travel, you'll need to swap physical SIMs (less convenient but works).

**Option 2: Sell it and buy an international Pixel**
- Japanese Pixels with the feature disabled still work perfectly with physical SIMs – they're not broken.
- Sell on eBay/Mercari (be honest about the limitation) and buy a used Pixel 6a/7 international version.

**Option 3: Keep it as a backup device**
- Use it as a Wi‑Fi-only media device or backup phone.
- Buy a separate international Pixel for travel.

**What NOT to do:**
- ❌ Don't try to flash international firmware – this does NOT re-enable the feature (the restriction is in the baseband, not the OS).
- ❌ Don't pay anyone claiming they can "unlock" it on Japanese Pixels – it's a hardware-level restriction.
- ❌ Don't buy a "SIM unlock" service expecting the digital SIM feature to work – SIM unlock and eSIM enable are completely different things.

### Carrier Lock vs. eSIM Function Lock – Two Different Things

This is a common point of confusion. Let's clarify:

| Type of Lock | What It Means | Can It Be Fixed? | Impact on Digital SIM |
|--------------|---------------|------------------|----------------------|
| **SIM Carrier Lock** | Phone can only use SIMs from one carrier (e.g., AT&T) | ✅ Yes – request unlock once paid off | After unlock, works with any carrier |
| **eSIM Function Lock** | eSIM hardware is disabled at firmware level (Japanese Pixels) | ❌ No – permanent | Never works, even after SIM unlock |
| **Regional eSIM Block** | Carrier-specific restriction (e.g., some AT&T models) | ⚠️ Sometimes – may require carrier intervention | Certain profiles may be blocked |

**Key takeaway:** A SIM-unlocked Japanese Pixel is STILL eSIM-locked. The two locks are independent. Always check EID (`*#06#`) – if there's no EID, the feature is permanently disabled.

### USA (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile, and unlocked)

**Status:** Carrier-locked Pixels vary; unlocked Pixels have full access.

- **Verizon (locked):** Verizon allows the feature on its locked Pixels (Pixel 4 and newer). You can use a travel eSIM alongside the Verizon physical SIM without contacting Verizon. However, activating a second profile on Verizon's own network may require their app or customer service.
- **AT&T (locked):** AT&T's policy is more restrictive. Some AT&T-locked Pixels (especially older models) may block non-AT&T profiles. For travel, an unlocked Pixel is strongly recommended.
- **T‑Mobile (locked):** T‑Mobile generally allows the feature on their locked Pixels, though the device must be paid off and SIM-unlocked for some features.
- **Factory unlocked (direct from Google or retailers like Best Buy):** Full support, no restrictions whatsoever. This is the best option for international travelers.

### Europe (EU)

**Status:** Virtually all Pixels sold in the EU are fully compatible.

- EU regulations require carriers to allow device unlocking and do not mandate disabling the feature. Most European Pixels are sold SIM-unlocked by law.
- The only potential exceptions are carrier-contracted devices from smaller regional providers (e.g., some German or French operators). Even then, the feature is rarely disabled.

### China

**Status:** Pixels are not officially sold in mainland China, so nearly all Pixels in China are imported international versions. These retain full support.

- However, Chinese domestic carriers (China Mobile, China Unicom, China Telecom) **do not offer eSIM to tourists or non-residents**. You cannot buy a local Chinese digital SIM plan without a Chinese ID and long-term contract.
- For travel in China, you must use an international travel provider (which work perfectly on Pixels, using roaming agreements with carriers outside China).

### India

**Status:** Pixels sold in India (via Flipkart, Google Store) are fully compatible and SIM-unlocked.

- Indian carriers like Jio and Airtel offer eSIM conversion for postpaid customers. The process requires visiting a store for in-person verification (no remote activation for foreigners).

### Australia & Canada

**Status:** Unlocked Pixels from Google or major retailers (JB Hi-Fi, Amazon AU, Best Buy CA) support the feature fully. Carrier-locked devices from Telstra, Optus, Rogers, Bell may have restrictions – check with your carrier.


## Dual eSIM support on Pixel 7 and newer

Starting with **Android 13** and the **Pixel 7 series**, Google introduced the ability to have **two eSIMs active simultaneously** – no physical SIM card needed.

### How to Enable Dual eSIM on Pixel 7/8/9/10

1. Ensure you have at least two eSIM profiles downloaded (stored on the device).
2. Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**.
3. Tap on the first eSIM and set it as active.
4. Tap on the second eSIM and also set it as active.
5. If the option is grayed out, you may need to disable the physical SIM slot first (if a physical SIM is inserted).

**Note:** Dual eSIM only works when no physical SIM is inserted, or the physical SIM slot is turned off (Settings > Network & Internet > Physical SIM > Turn off).

### Limitations of Dual eSIM on Pixel

- **Not all carriers support dual eSIM:** Some operators' profiles are not designed to work alongside another active profile. You may see an "eSIM profile not supported" error.
- **Battery impact:** Keeping two digital SIMs active uses slightly more power than a single SIM, but the difference is usually negligible (1-3% per day).
- **DSDS (Dual SIM Dual Standby) only:** Both profiles can receive calls and texts, but when one is actively using data, the other may not receive simultaneous data. This is standard behavior for all dual-SIM phones.

### Use Cases for Dual eSIM on Pixel

- **Traveling across multiple countries:** Keep a European eSIM active while also having a Japan eSIM ready (just toggle data between them).
- **Work and personal on one device without a physical SIM:** Use two different carriers or numbers, both via eSIM.
- **Testing new providers without removing your main eSIM:** Download a trial profile from a new carrier and activate it alongside your primary profile – no swapping required.

## How to transfer eSIM from old Pixel to new Pixel – step by step

Upgrading to a new Pixel? You don't want to lose your active eSIM profiles. Here's how to transfer them safely.

### Method 1: Using Carrier App (Easiest)

Many modern providers (especially travel eSIMs) allow you to re-download the profile on a new device using their app.

- **Google Fi:** Open the Fi app on your new Pixel, sign in, and follow the "Activate on this device" prompt. The profile will automatically transfer.
- **Visible:** Install the Visible app on the new phone, log in, and go to Account > Device > Activate eSIM. The old profile will be deactivated remotely.
- **US Mobile:** Use the US Mobile app to "Swap eSIM" to a new device.
- **Airalo, Holafly, Nomad:** Most travel plans are one-time install only. You cannot transfer them – you'll need to purchase a new plan if you already used the data. However, if the profile is still unused, you can re-scan the QR code on the new Pixel.

### Method 2: Using Android's Built-in "Copy eSIM" (Android 13+)

**Caution:** This feature is carrier-dependent and not universally supported.

On your old Pixel (if running Android 13+):

1. Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**.
2. Tap on the eSIM you want to transfer.
3. Look for an option like **"Copy eSIM to another device"** or **"Transfer"**.
4. Follow the on-screen instructions – you'll typically need to scan a QR code from your new Pixel.

On your new Pixel:

1. During initial setup or later via **Settings > Network & Internet > Add Carrier**.
2. Choose **"Copy from another device"** (if available).
3. Scan the QR code shown on the old Pixel.

### Method 3: Manual Re-installation (Universal)

If the above methods fail:

1. **Locate the original activation QR code or activation code** – check your email or carrier app.
2. On the new Pixel, go to **Settings > Network & Internet > Add Carrier**.
3. Scan the QR code or enter the activation code manually.
4. The profile will download as a new one on the new device. **The old profile on the previous phone will remain installed but inactive** (you should delete it later to avoid confusion).

### What to Do Before Selling or Trading In Your Old Pixel

- **Do not simply factory reset** – this may delete eSIM profiles, but they remain tied to the device's EID and could cause activation issues if not properly removed.
- **Proper removal:** Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**, tap the eSIM, and select **"Erase SIM"** or **"Remove"**. This tells the carrier's server that the profile is deactivated on that device.
- **After erasing,** you can factory reset safely.

### Common Transfer Errors and Solutions

| Error Message | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------------|--------------|----------|
| "eSIM not supported on this device" | Carrier doesn't allow profile transfer | Buy a new plan instead. |
| "QR code expired" | Travel plans often expire after a set period (e.g., 30 days) | Contact carrier support for a new QR. |
| "Profile already used" | One-time install profile cannot be reinstalled | Purchase a new plan. |
| "Carrier doesn't support eSIM transfer" | Some postpaid carrier profiles are tied to IMEI | Contact carrier to re-issue on new IMEI. |

## Can't Scan the QR Code? Manual Activation on Pixel

**Problem:** You're in a bright environment, the QR code is on a computer screen with glare, or you have a printed QR code that's damaged.

**Solution:** Enter the activation details manually. Every eSIM QR code contains the same information that can be typed in manually.

### How to Activate eSIM Manually on Pixel

1. Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > Add Carrier**.
2. Tap **"Need help?"** or **"Enter code manually"** (exact wording may vary by Android version).
3. You'll see one or more fields – typically:
   - **Activation Code** (often starts with `LPA:1$` or is a long string of letters/numbers)
   - **Confirmation Code** (optional, 4-8 digits)
   - **SM-DP+ Address** (sometimes pre-filled)
4. Enter the details exactly as shown in your provider's email.

### Where to Find the Manual Activation Details

- **Airalo:** In the app, tap the eSIM plan → "Details" → "Manual installation" – shows SM-DP+ and Activation Code.
- **Roami:** In the confirmation email – look for "Manual activation details" or "If you cannot scan the QR code."
- **Holafly:** Email includes a **"Manual installation"** section with SM-DP+ and activation code.
- **Nomad:** In the app → eSIM details → "Manual install" option.
- **Google Fi:** You cannot manually enter – you must use the Fi app.

### Pro Tip: Screenshot the Manual Details Before Departure

**If you're traveling and may not have Wi-Fi to pull up your email again, screenshot the manual activation details and save them to your phone's photo library.** This way, even if you have no data connection, you can still access the code.

### Error: "Invalid Activation Code"

**Possible causes:**
- You typed the code incorrectly (these are case-sensitive).
- The code has already been used on another device.
- The code expired (some travel codes expire 30 days after purchase).
- You're entering the **Confirmation Code** in the **Activation Code** field (or vice versa).

**Fix:** Re-check your email/app for the exact values. If you can't resolve it, contact your provider's support.


## eSIM Activated but No Data? Configure APN on Pixel

**Symptom:** Your eSIM shows signal bars (full network connection) but you have no internet – websites won't load, apps say "no connection."

**Root cause:** The eSIM profile was installed, but the Access Point Name (APN) settings are missing or incorrect. This is common with travel eSIMs – some auto-configure APN, others require manual entry.

### How to Check/Edit APN on Pixel

1. Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**.
2. Tap on the eSIM that has no data.
3. Tap **Access Point Names** (if you don't see this, scroll down – it's there).
4. You'll see a list of APNs. If the list is empty, tap the **+** (Add) button to create one.
5. Fill in the **APN** field (and possibly **Username/Password** – check your provider's documentation).
6. Tap **Save** (three dots > Save).
7. Select the newly created APN as active.
8. Toggle Airplane Mode on/off to force the network to re-register.

If the APN field is grayed out and you can't edit it:

## Troubleshooting eSIM activation errors – error codes and fixes

Even on compatible Pixels, activation can sometimes fail. Here are the most common error codes and how to fix them.

### Error 1: "EID not found" or "No EID"

**When it appears:** Dialing `*#06#` shows only IMEI numbers, no EID.

**Cause:** Your Pixel is either:
- A Japanese carrier variant (SoftBank, au) – the feature is permanently disabled.
- A very old Pixel (1 or 2) – no hardware.
- Rare firmware corruption.

**Solution:** If it's a Japanese variant, there is no solution – use physical SIM only. If you believe your device should have the feature, try a factory reset (backup first). Still no EID? Contact Google support.

### Error 2: "Activation failed: Invalid QR code"

**When it appears:** After scanning a QR code, the phone rejects it.

**Possible causes and fixes:**
- **QR code is damaged or low quality:** Try zooming in or requesting a new QR from the carrier.
- **QR code expired:** Many travel plans have an expiration date (e.g., 30 days from purchase). Check the email – you may need a new plan.
- **QR code is for a different device type:** Some QR codes are device-specific. Ensure it's for "Android eSIM" or "Generic eSIM".
- **Manual entry error:** If entering a code manually, double-check the activation code and confirmation code (often two separate fields).

### Error 3: "Profile not supported by this device"

**When it appears:** The profile downloads but then fails to install.

**Cause:** The carrier's profile requires a feature your Pixel doesn't have (e.g., certain 5G bands, or a newer version of the LPA).

**Solution:** 
- Update your Pixel to the latest Android version.
- Contact the provider and ask if they support your specific Pixel model (e.g., "Does this work with Pixel 6?").
- Try a different provider.

### Error 4: "Carrier locked – cannot activate eSIM"

**When it appears:** You try to add a non-carrier profile (e.g., a travel eSIM) and the phone says "Device is SIM locked" or "Carrier lock prevents eSIM activation."

**Cause:** Your Pixel is carrier-locked (e.g., to AT&T, T‑Mobile, or a Japanese carrier) and the original carrier restricts usage for non-approved profiles.

**Solution:**
- **For US carrier locks:** Once the device is paid off, request a permanent SIM unlock from your carrier. After unlock, any provider should work.
- **For Japanese carrier locks:** As noted earlier, the feature is permanently disabled at firmware level; SIM unlock doesn't help.
- **For other regions:** Contact your carrier to ask if they allow the feature on locked devices.

### Error 5: "Unable to download profile – network error"

**When it appears:** The download starts but hangs or fails.

**Cause:** Wi-Fi connection issues or carrier server problems.

**Solutions:**
- Switch to a different Wi-Fi network or use mobile hotspot.
- Try again in 5-10 minutes – carrier servers may be temporarily overloaded.
- Disable any VPN or ad-blocker that could interfere.
- Manually set DNS to 8.8.8.8 (Google DNS) temporarily.

### Error 6: "eSIM profile already used"

**When it appears:** You scan a QR code that was previously installed on another device.

**Cause:** Most profiles are single-use. Once installed on a device, they cannot be reinstalled elsewhere (for security reasons).

**Solution:** 
- If you still have the original device, you can delete the profile from there (Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs > Erase SIM) and sometimes that frees it for re-installation – but not always.
- Otherwise, purchase a new plan.

### Error 7: "Device does not meet eSIM requirements"

**When it appears:** A specific carrier's app (e.g., Visible, Google Fi) refuses to activate.

**Cause:** The carrier's app checks for certain device certifications or Android security patch levels.

**Solutions:**
- Update your Pixel to the latest security patch (Settings > System > System Update).
- Clear the carrier app's cache and data (Settings > Apps > [Carrier app] > Storage & Cache > Clear Cache/Clear Data).
- Try activating via QR code instead of the app (if the carrier offers that option).

### General Troubleshooting Steps (try these before contacting support)

1. **Restart your Pixel** – simple but often works.
2. **Check for Android updates** – Settings > System > System Update.
3. **Reset network settings** – Settings > System > Reset Options > Reset Wi-Fi, mobile & Bluetooth (this does not delete eSIM profiles).
4. **Delete other eSIM profiles** – If you have many stored, delete unused ones to free up space (some Pixels have a limit of 5–10 stored profiles).
5. **Factory reset** – Last resort. Back up your data first. After reset, try activating again before restoring from backup.

For more advanced eSIM troubleshooting, see our **[eSIM Deep Troubleshooting Guide 2026](/faq/esim-deep-troubleshooting-guide-2026/)** .

- You choose which SIM to use for mobile data, default calls, and default SMS.

**Common use cases:**
- **International travel:** Keep your home physical SIM for calls/SMS, add a local data eSIM for affordable internet.
- **Work/personal split:** Physical SIM for personal number, eSIM for work number.

3. **Scan the QR code** sent to your email (or enter activation code manually).
4. Follow prompts to download the profile.
5. Choose your default SIM for calls, SMS, and data.
6. **Turn on Data Roaming** for the eSIM line when traveling.

## How to verify eSIM on any Pixel (universal method)

No matter which Pixel you have (3/4/5/6/7/8/9/10), use this simple method:

1. Open the **Phone dialer**.
2. Dial `*#06#` and press call.
3. Look for a **32-digit EID (Embedded Identity Document)**. If present, your phone supports eSIM.
4. If you only see IMEI numbers (IMEI1, IMEI2) and no EID, the feature is disabled – typically on Japanese carrier versions.

Alternatively, check via Settings: **Settings > Network & Internet > Mobile Network** – look for **"Download a SIM instead?"** or **"Add eSIM"**.

**Can I use two eSIMs at the same time on a Pixel?**
- **Pixel 6 and older:** No. You can store multiple profiles but only activate one at a time (alongside one physical SIM).
- **Pixel 7 and newer:** Yes, you can enable **dual eSIM** (two profiles active simultaneously) by disabling the physical SIM slot. See the Dual eSIM section for details.

**Can I use a physical SIM and an eSIM at the same time?**
Yes. All Pixel 3 and newer models support DSDS (Dual SIM Dual Standby), allowing one physical SIM and one eSIM to be active simultaneously.

**Does eSIM on Pixel support 5G?**
Yes – Pixel 6 and newer support 5G on eSIM. Pixel 5 and older are 4G/LTE only.

**Why can't I transfer my eSIM from an old Pixel to a new one?**
Some carriers require you to release the profile from the old device via their app before activating on a new one. Others allow re-download using the original QR code. Always check your provider's transfer policy (see the Transfer eSIM section above).

**Which Pixel is best for eSIM travel on a budget?**
**Pixel 6a** offers the best value: cheap, full support, 5G, and good battery life. Second choice: **Pixel 5** (non-Japanese) if found at a lower price.

**Can I use an eSIM from a different country than where I bought my Pixel?**
Yes. The feature is region-agnostic. A US-purchased Pixel 6 can install and use a Japanese eSIM, a European eSIM, or any other global plan, as long as the provider supports the device.

**Does Pixel work with eSIM in China?**
Yes – international providers offer China plans that work on Pixel 6 and newer. Local Chinese carriers do not offer the feature to tourists.

**What should I do if I see an "activation failed" error?**
Refer to the Troubleshooting eSIM Activation Errors section above, which covers common error codes and fixes.

**Can I flash international firmware on a Japanese Pixel to enable eSIM?**
**No.** This is a common misconception. The restriction on Japanese Pixels is in the **baseband firmware** (modem level), not the Android OS. Flashing international ROMs will not enable the feature. The only real solution is to buy a non-Japanese Pixel.

**What is the cheapest Pixel with full eSIM support (no regional restrictions)?**
**Pixel 6a** – typically $120-150 used. It has full support on every device, regardless of region. Older Pixel 5 can be cheaper (~$80-100) but only works if it's a non-Japanese version – and you need to verify before buying.

**Why does my Pixel show "No Service" after installing an eSIM?**
This is usually one of three issues:
1. **Data Roaming is OFF** – go to Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs > [eSIM] and toggle **Data Roaming** ON.
2. **APN settings are incorrect** – see the APN section above to configure.
3. **Manual network selection needed** – go to Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs > [eSIM] > Network Operators → turn off Automatic → pick a network manually.

**How many eSIM profiles can my Pixel store?**
- **Pixel 6 and newer:** Up to 10 profiles (though only one or two can be active at a time).
- **Pixel 5 and older:** Up to 5 profiles.
- **Pixel 2:** Not applicable – no eSIM hardware.

**How long does it take for my eSIM to connect after activation?**
- **Pixel 6 and newer:** 1-3 minutes.
- **Pixel 5 and older:** 2-5 minutes.
- If it takes longer than 15 minutes, something is wrong – check Data Roaming, APN settings, and try manual network selection.

**What is the SM-DP+ address?**
The SM-DP+ (Subscription Manager Data Preparation) address is the server that delivers your carrier profile. Most users don't need to know it – scanning the QR code automatically fills it. If you're entering details manually, you'll need the SM-DP+ address and activation code from your provider.
