This is where most travelers get tripped up. You must verify your phone's hardware supports digital connectivity before paying. To view the complete list of compatible devices, check Roami's updated **[2026 compatibility database](/compatibility/)**.

### The Universal EID Check – `*#06#`

The fastest universal check: Open your phone's dialer and type `*#06#`. If a long barcode labeled **EID** appears on your screen, your phone supports digital plans.

### iPhone Compatibility

| Model | Works? | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **iPhone XR / XS / XS Max** | ✅ Yes | First models with digital SIM support |
| **iPhone 11 – 16** | ✅ Yes | All models (except China/HK/Macau) |
| **iPhone 14/15/16 (US)** | ✅ Yes (digital only) | No physical SIM tray |
| **iPhone (China/HK/Macau)** | ❌ No (except iPhone 16) | Dual physical SIM instead |

**How to verify your iPhone:**
1. Go to **Settings**.
2. Tap **General > About**.
3. Scroll down and look for **Available SIM** or an **EID** number.
4. If you cannot find an EID, abort your purchase immediately.

**Critical warning:** If you bought your iPhone in **Mainland China, Hong Kong, or Macau**, it almost certainly does NOT support digital plans (Apple builds dual physical SIM trays for these specific regions).

### Android Compatibility

Android compatibility is fragmented. **Google Pixel 3** and newer, alongside **Samsung Galaxy S20** and newer (excluding some FE models), generally support digital connectivity.

**How to verify your Android:**
1. Go to **Settings**.
2. Tap **Network & Internet** (or **Connections**).
3. Tap **SIMs** (or **SIM Manager**).

## Why a US Travel Data Plan Is Essential in 2026

A **digital travel plan** (commonly called an eSIM) is a microchip soldered directly to your phone's motherboard. You don't need a SIM ejector tool. You simply scan a QR code to download and activate a local carrier's network profile in seconds. The technology is governed by the [GSMA standard SGP.22](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), ensuring global interoperability.

In 2026, using a digital plan for US travel is effectively mandatory. Starting with the iPhone 14 series, Apple **completely removed the physical SIM tray** for all US models. This hardware revolution forced US carriers to shift entirely to digital, making prepaid physical cards increasingly difficult to find at airports or convenience stores. The [official Apple support page for eSIM](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108072) confirms this transition.

Relying on your home carrier's international roaming guarantees high daily fees and significant network latency. By purchasing a **travel data plan** in advance, your phone automatically connects to a 5G network the second your plane lands, allowing you to hail an Uber immediately.

## How to Choose the Best Data Plan for USA Travel Based on Your Itinerary

Do not blindly buy the most expensive option. Match your plan to your exact stay duration and data habits to maximize your money.

### Short-Term Travel (1-15 Days): Fixed Data Plans

For a one or two-week vacation, hotels and restaurants provide free Wi-Fi. Your outdoor data needs are strictly for Google Maps, Uber, WhatsApp, and quick web searches. A **5GB to 10GB fixed plan** is more than enough.

**Estimated data usage per day (typical travel):**
- Google Maps (30 min): ~50MB
- Instagram/TikTok scrolling (1 hr): ~200–400MB
- WhatsApp messages (all day): ~20MB
- Uber/Lyft rides (3–4 rides): ~10MB
- One 10‑min video call: ~150MB
- **Total typical day: ~500MB–1GB**

### Long-Term Business/Family Visits (15-30+ Days): Larger or Unlimited Plans

If you are staying over half a month or consume heavy video content, an unlimited data plan removes the stress of tracking megabytes. However, you must understand the **Fair Usage Policy (FUP)**. Almost all "unlimited" plans have a hidden high-speed cap. Once you exceed a specific daily threshold (e.g., 2GB or 3GB), carriers throttle your speed down to 2G/3G levels (128kbps - 384kbps). Always check the FUP limits in your plan details before checking out.

### Cross-Border Itineraries: North America (US, Canada, Mexico) Regional Plans

If you are visiting Vancouver or Cancun during your US trip, buy a **North America Regional plan**. These plans force your phone to auto-switch to partner networks when you cross borders (e.g., Rogers in Canada, Telcel in Mexico).

## Hidden Fees 2026 – What No One Tells You About US Travel Data Plans

The advertised price is rarely the final price. Here's the fine print most travelers miss:

### 1. Activation / Service Fees
Some providers charge a one‑time **activation fee** ($1–$5) that's not included in the headline price. Always check the checkout total before entering your credit card.

### 2. Top‑Up Price Inflation
The cheapest plan is often a "loss leader." When you need to add more data, the price per GB can be **2–3x higher** than the initial plan. Example: A 1GB/7‑day plan for $4.99 might have a 1GB top‑up for $8.99. **Buy enough data upfront.**

### 3. Daily Cap "Unlimited" Traps
As noted in the Fair Usage Policy section, "unlimited" usually means **1GB–3GB of high‑speed data per day**, then throttled to 128–256kbps. If you stream video daily, you'll hit this cap every day.

### 4. Auto‑Renewal Billing
Most monthly plans auto‑renew by default. If you don't cancel before the renewal date, your card will be charged again – even if you've already left the US.

**How to stop auto‑renewal:**
- **iPhone:** Settings > Apple ID > Subscriptions → find the plan → Cancel Subscription.
- **Android:** Google Play > Subscriptions → find the plan → Cancel.
- **Provider app:** Most travel plan apps have a "Manage Plan" section where you can toggle auto‑renewal off.

### 5. No Refund Policies
Almost all travel data plans are **non‑refundable** once the QR code is issued. If your phone turns out to be incompatible, your money is gone. Always verify EID **before** purchasing.

### Hidden Fees Quick Checklist

| Fee Type | What to Check | Red Flag |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Activation fee | Checkout total vs. advertised price | Any fee > $2 |
| Top‑up cost per GB | Compare to initial plan's per‑GB price | Top‑up > 1.5x initial per‑GB |
| Auto‑renewal | Check if it's on by default | "Monthly" plans with auto‑renew |
| Refund policy | Read the TOS before checkout | "No refunds after QR issue" |
| Fair Usage Policy | Daily high‑speed cap | Cap < 1GB/day |

### How to Check Your Remaining Data Balance

Most travel plan providers have an app or dashboard where you can check remaining data. Alternatively:
- **iPhone:** Settings > Cellular > [your plan] → look for "Current Period" data usage (reset this manually when you start your trip).
- **Android:** Settings > Connections > Data Usage > Mobile Data Usage → select your plan.

### What Happens When Your Plan Expires

When your plan reaches its expiration date:
- **Data stops working** – you won't be able to browse, use maps, or send messages
- **Your phone number (if any) stays active** – but only for incoming calls (you can't make outgoing calls or use data)
- **Top‑up extends validity** – adding data usually extends the expiration date
- **If you don't top up within 30-60 days**, the profile is deactivated and you'll need a new QR code

**Best practice:** Set a reminder 2‑3 days before your plan expires so you can top up before you lose connectivity.

## Step-by-Step Guide: How to Buy and Activate a US Travel Plan

After verifying compatibility, purchase your plan. You will receive a **QR code** via email.

### Core Rule: When to Scan the QR Code

> ⚠️ **Critical:** You must scan and install this profile **while connected to stable home Wi-Fi** OR **immediately after arriving in the US with airport Wi-Fi**. Do NOT wait until you're outside the airport without internet access.

**Important distinction:**
- **Scan QR code** → can be done **anytime** (before or after arrival). It just installs the profile.
- **Activate the line** → toggle **ON** only **after you land in the US**. If you toggle it on before departure, your phone may attempt to connect to a US tower from abroad, leading to roaming charges or activation errors.

### Step 1: iOS Installation and Setup Guide

For iPhone users, installation takes 60 seconds:

1. **Scan to install:** Connect to Wi-Fi. Go to **Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM**. Select "Use QR Code" and scan the QR code from your email.
2. **Rename the label:** The system asks you to label the new line. Rename it to **"US Travel"** so you don't confuse it with your primary number.
3. **Keep off before flight:** While still at home, go to **Settings > Cellular**, tap your travel line, and toggle the line **OFF**.
4. **Activate upon landing:** Once the plane lands and you're in the US, toggle the travel line **ON**. Switch your **Cellular Data** to the travel line and you MUST toggle **Data Roaming** to the ON position.

### Step 2: Android Installation and Setup Guide

Android menus vary slightly by manufacturer, but the logic is identical:

1. **Scan to install:** Connect to Wi-Fi. Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > SIMs**. Tap the "+" icon or "Download a SIM instead". Scan your QR code.
2. **Keep off before flight:** In your SIM manager, ensure this newly downloaded profile is toggled **OFF**.
3. **Activate upon landing:** When you arrive in the US, toggle the plan **ON**.
4. **Critical settings:** Set this plan as your default for Mobile Data. Tap into the plan's settings and toggle **Roaming** to the ON position. It will not connect otherwise.

### Step 3: Dual‑SIM Best Practices (Keep Your Home SIM Active)

If you want to keep your home number active for SMS/calls while using the US plan for data:

| Setting | Recommended Value | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Default Voice Line** | Home SIM (your regular number) | Keep receiving calls on your home number |
| **Cellular Data** | US Travel Plan | Use US data plan, not expensive roaming |
| **Data Roaming (Home SIM)** | OFF | Prevent accidental roaming charges |
| **Data Roaming (US Plan)** | ON | Required for US data to work |
| **Allow Cellular Data Switching** | ON | Allows calls/SMS on home SIM to use US plan data for Wi‑Fi Calling |

**To set this up on iPhone:** Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data → select US plan. Then Settings > Cellular > Default Voice Line → select your home SIM. Turn Data Roaming OFF for home SIM, ON for US plan.

## US Travel Data Troubleshooting – Common Issues and Fixes

Even with perfect execution, network handshakes occasionally fail. Here is the technical checklist to force your phone online. For advanced issues, see our **[eSIM deep troubleshooting guide](/faq/esim-deep-troubleshooting-guide-2026/)** and **[dual-eSIM fixes for iPhone](/faq/dual-esim-not-working-12-fixes-for-iphone/)**.

### No Signal/No Internet After Landing?

If your phone shows "No Service" or has bars but no data, run through this sequence:

1. **Check Data Roaming:** This causes 90% of failures. Go into your plan's settings and verify **Data Roaming** is toggled ON.
2. **Check Default Data Line:** Ensure your phone is routing data through the travel plan, not your home country's physical SIM.
3. **Turn Off Primary SIM:** Dual‑SIM conflicts happen. Temporarily toggle your home physical SIM **OFF** so the phone only reads the travel plan.
4. **Reset Network Connection:** Turn on Airplane Mode for 10 seconds, then turn it off. If that fails, restart the phone entirely to force a new tower ping.

### Signal Bars but No Data? Check APN Settings

If after activation no data network is available, APN settings may need adjustment:

1. **iPhone:** Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data Network.
2. **Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Access Point Names.
3. Common APN values: `globaldata` for Airalo, `internet` for Roami. Most travel plans auto‑configure the APN, so check your provider's website for the exact value.

### How to Manually Select a Network (Field Test Mode)

If you have signal but can't connect, force your phone to try a different network:

**On iPhone:** Settings > Cellular > [your plan] > Network Selection → turn OFF "Automatic" → wait for the list of networks → select a different one (e.g., if T‑Mobile isn't working, try AT&T or Verizon if your plan supports it).

**On Android:** Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > Network Operators → turn OFF "Select automatically" → choose a different network.

> ⚠️ **Warning:** Only do this if your plan supports multiple networks. Some travel plans are locked to a single carrier – trying to manually select another network will just show "No Service."

### Why Did My "Unlimited Data" Suddenly Slow Down?

If your speeds were blazing fast for three days and suddenly you cannot load a photo, you hit the **FUP (Fair Usage Policy)** cap. As explained earlier, carriers throttle your speed to 128kbps once you burn through your daily high‑speed allowance.

**The fix:** This throttle resets automatically at **midnight local time**. If you need high‑speed data immediately for work, your only option is to buy and install a fresh data pack or top‑up.

### Network Priority – Why Some Plans Are Slower Than Others

Not all data is created equal. Travel plans are often deprioritized compared to local carrier customers:

| User Type | Network Priority | Speed During Congestion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Local carrier postpaid customer | Highest | Full speed |
| Local carrier prepaid customer | Medium | May slow during peak times |
| Travel plan user (via reseller) | Lowest | First to be slowed when network is busy |

**What this means for you:** In crowded areas (Times Square, sports stadiums, concerts), your travel plan may slow down even if you have data remaining. Multi‑network plans help because they can switch to a less congested carrier.


## Frequently Asked Questions About US Travel Data Plans

**Is Airalo or Roami cheaper for the USA?**
Roami is generally cheaper for data‑heavy plans (10GB: $26.99 vs $34.00 for Airalo). Roami also offers multi‑carrier switching (T‑Mobile, AT&T, Verizon) while Airalo typically uses a single network. For unlimited data plans, Roami is often the better value. Read our **[full Airalo vs Holafly comparison](/blog/best-usa-esim-comparison-airalo-holafly/)**.

**Does a US travel plan include a phone number?**
Most travel data plans are data‑only. If you need a US number for calls or SMS verification codes, look for a plan that includes a phone number (like Airalo Discover+). Note that these plans are usually more expensive per GB.

**What is the best data plan for USA travel?**
The best choice depends on your itinerary:
- Cities → T‑Mobile network (fastest 5G)
- National Parks → AT&T or Verizon (better rural coverage)
- Multi‑network switching → Roami covers all three

**Can I use a US plan in Canada or Mexico?**
Only if you buy a North America Regional plan that explicitly includes those countries. Standard US plans stop working at the border.

**Why does my plan show "No Service" after landing?**
Most commonly, Data Roaming is not turned ON, or the phone is still using your home SIM for data. Double‑check both settings. See the troubleshooting section above.

**What is a free trial?**
Some providers offer a free trial (e.g., Roami offers 100MB of data) so you can test compatibility and coverage before buying a paid plan. You can **[claim your free trial here](/free-esim/)**.

**What is the cheapest data plan for the USA?**
Prices vary by data allowance. As of August 2026:
- Roami's 1GB/7‑day plan starts at $1.99.
- Always compare **cost per GB** rather than the headline price, especially for larger plans.

**How do I stop auto‑renewal on my plan?**
- **iPhone:** Settings > Apple ID > Subscriptions → find the plan → Cancel Subscription.
- **Android:** Google Play > Subscriptions → find the plan → Cancel.
- **Provider app:** Most travel plan apps have a "Manage Plan" section.

**Can I use my US plan as a hotspot for my laptop?**
Most US plans allow hotspot/tethering. Check the provider's terms – some restrict hotspot usage on "unlimited" plans.

**What happens if I run out of data?**
You'll need to buy a top‑up pack. Top‑up prices are usually higher per GB than the initial plan – buy a larger initial plan if you're unsure about usage.

**Can I keep my home SIM active for iMessage while using US plan for data?**
Yes. On iPhone, set Default Voice Line to your home SIM, Cellular Data to the US plan, and turn on Allow Cellular Data Switching. Your home number will still work for iMessage and FaceTime over the US plan's data connection.

**How early should I buy before my trip?**
Buy and scan the QR code **1–3 days before departure** while on stable Wi‑Fi. This gives you time to troubleshoot activation issues before you leave. Do NOT wait until you're at the airport with spotty Wi‑Fi.

**What is a confirmation code and where do I find it?**
Some providers require a confirmation/activation code (usually 4–8 digits) during installation. Check your provider's email – it's often listed separately from the QR code. If you can't find it, check the provider's app or support page.

**Which provider has the best coverage for US road trips?**
Roami (which uses AT&T + Verizon + T‑Mobile) offers the best overall road trip coverage because it automatically switches between all three networks. Single‑network plans like Airalo (T‑Mobile only) will have dead zones in rural areas.

**What is the best US plan for families or groups?**
If you're traveling with family:
- **Option 1:** Buy one plan with a generous data allowance and use Personal Hotspot to share with others.
- **Option 2:** Buy separate plans for each person – this is more expensive but gives everyone independence.
- **Recommendation:** For families, Option 1 is usually more cost‑effective. A 10‑15GB plan can easily cover 2‑3 people for a week if they're not heavy streamers.
