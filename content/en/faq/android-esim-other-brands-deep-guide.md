## Android eSIM Quick Answer – Does Your Phone Support It?

**The short answer:** Unlike iPhones and Google Pixels (which have consistent eSIM support), the broader Android market is a fragmented mix of brands, models, and regional variations. Some flagships fully support eSIM; most budget and mid‑range phones do not.

Here's the fastest way to check your specific phone in 10 seconds:

1. Open your phone's dialer.
2. Dial `*#06#` and press call.
3. Look for a **32‑digit EID number** on your screen.
4. See it? **Your phone supports eSIM.** Only see IMEI numbers? **Your phone does NOT support eSIM.**

For the full list of **eSIM compatible Android phones**, see our **[master compatibility guide](/compatibility/)**.

**Why does this matter for travelers?** eSIM lets you buy a local data plan before you leave home and activate it instantly upon arrival. No hunting for a SIM card stall at the airport, no fumbling with SIM ejector tools – just land and connect.

## How to Identify Your Exact Phone Model and Variant (Crucial for eSIM)

One of the biggest reasons users get confused is they don't know which version of their phone they have. The same model name can have eSIM in one region but not in another.

### Step 1: Find your model number

| Brand | Where to Find Model Number |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Samsung** | Settings > About Phone > Model Number (e.g., SM-S918B = Global, SM-S918U = US) |
| **OnePlus** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., CPH2581 = Global, CPH2583 = India) |
| **Xiaomi** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., 2211133G = Global, 2211133C = China) |
| **Huawei** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., LIO-L29 = Global, LIO-AL00 = China) |
| **Sony** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., XQ-CT72 = Global, XQ-CT44 = Japan) |
| **Motorola** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., XT2321-1 = US, XT2321-2 = Global) |
| **Google Pixel** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., GP4BC = Global, GX7AS = Japan) |
| **Asus** | Settings > About Phone > Model (e.g., AI2301 = Global, AI2302 = US) |

### Step 2: Decode your model number suffix

| Suffix Meaning | Samsung | OnePlus | Xiaomi | Huawei |
|----------------|---------|---------|--------|--------|
| **Global / International** | SM-S9xxB, SM-AxxE | CPH (any) | ...G, ...I | Lxx-L29, Lxx-LX9 |
| **US (Snapdragon)** | SM-S9xxU, SM-S9xxU1 | - | ... | - |
| **China** | SM-S9xx0 | - | ...C | Lxx-AL00, Lxx-TL00 |
| **Japan** | SC-xx (Docomo) | - | ... | Lxx-Dxx |

### Step 3: Check your software channel (Samsung)

On Samsung phones, the **CSC code** determines which region's firmware you're running:
- **Settings > About Phone > Software Information > Service Provider SW Ver**
- If the CSC ends in **"U"** or **"U1"** – US model, eSIM may be disabled (especially on S20)
- If the CSC ends in **"B"** – international model, eSIM is likely available
- If the CSC ends in **"CHC"** – China model, no eSIM

**The bottom line:** If your model number ends in **"U"** (Samsung US) or **"CN"** (Xiaomi China) or **"AL00"** (Huawei China), eSIM is very likely disabled. Global versions ending in **"B"** (Samsung), **"G"** (Xiaomi), or **"L29"** (Huawei) are your best bet for eSIM support.

## How to Check If Your Android Phone Supports eSIM (Universal Method)

Before diving into brand‑specific details, use this universal method to check **does my android phone have eSIM**:

1. Open the **Phone dialer** app.
2. Dial `*#06#` (just like a phone number) and press call.
3. Look at the screen. If you see a number labeled **EID** (Embedded Identity Document) – typically 32 digits – your phone has the required embedded SIM hardware.
4. If you only see IMEI numbers (IMEI1, IMEI2) and no EID, your phone does **not** support eSIM.

This code works on all Android phones – Motorola, OnePlus, Xiaomi, Huawei, LG, Sony, and others. It's the fastest way to get a definitive answer to **does my android phone have eSIM**. If you're shopping for a new phone, the tables below tell you which models are known to work.

> **Pro Tip:** Even if your phone shows an EID, some carrier‑locked versions may have eSIM disabled in firmware. If you bought your phone from a US carrier (Verizon, AT&T, T‑Mobile), confirm with the brand‑specific notes below.

## eSIM vs Physical SIM on Android – Which Should You Use?

If your Android phone supports both eSIM and physical SIM, here's how they compare to help you decide.

| Factor | eSIM | Physical SIM |
|--------|------|--------------|
| **Activation** | Scan QR code, done in 2 minutes | Insert card into SIM tray |
| **Buy before travel?** | ✅ Yes – buy online from anywhere | ❌ No – must buy at destination or order in advance |
| **Lost/stolen phone** | ✅ More secure – can't be removed by thief | ❌ Card can be removed and used |
| **Switching phones** | ⚠️ One‑time install (most travel eSIMs) – check provider policy | ✅ Easy – just move the card |
| **Multiple profiles** | ✅ Store multiple eSIMs, switch as needed | ❌ Only one carrier per card |
| **Availability** | Flagships only; limited on mid‑range and budget | All Android phones |
| **Cost** | Travel eSIMs often cheaper than international roaming | Local physical SIMs cheaper for long‑term stays |
| **Regional support** | Works globally with travel eSIM providers | Depends on local carrier availability |

**Which should you choose?**
- **For travel (1-30 days):** eSIM wins – buy before you leave, activate on arrival, no hunting for SIM cards at the airport.
- **For long‑term stays (3+ months):** Local physical SIM usually offers better per‑GB rates and a local phone number.
- **For maximum flexibility:** Use both – keep your home physical SIM for calls/SMS and a travel eSIM for data (dual SIM support varies by brand).

## Regional Differences: Android eSIM and Carrier Locks

One of the biggest traps for Android users is regional variation. Even if a model supports eSIM globally, your specific version may not.

### USA Carrier‑Locked Phones

| Carrier | eSIM on Locked Phones | What to Do |
|---------|----------------------|------------|
| **T‑Mobile** | Disabled on most non‑Samsung Androids | Request unlock after device is paid off |
| **Verizon** | Disabled on many Android models | Request unlock; some models may never get eSIM |
| **AT&T** | Disabled on most Android models | Request unlock; AT&T‑branded phones often lack eSIM menu entirely |
| **Unlocked (factory)** | ✅ Full eSIM support | Buy unlocked directly from manufacturer |

**What to do:** If your phone is paid off, request a SIM unlock from your carrier. After unlocking, eSIM *might* appear, but it's not guaranteed. Some carriers remove the eSIM menu entirely in firmware. The safe choice: buy a factory‑unlocked phone directly from the manufacturer (Motorola.com, OnePlus.com, etc.) or a retailer like Best Buy.

### China / Hong Kong

- **China domestic models** almost never have eSIM – regardless of brand. This applies to Xiaomi, Huawei, OnePlus, Oppo, Vivo, and Honor.
- **Hong Kong models** often work (they're considered "global" versions).
- **What to do:** Check `*#06#`. If no EID, use a physical SIM. There is no reliable software fix.

### Japan

- **Japanese carrier variants (Docomo, au, SoftBank)** frequently disable eSIM – even on Sony Xperia and other flagships.
- **What to do:** Buy a global version (e.g., from Amazon US or Europe) for guaranteed eSIM.

### Europe / Middle East / Southeast Asia

- These regions typically get **full eSIM support** on global versions of flagship phones.
- If you bought your phone in these regions, you're in the best position for eSIM compatibility.

## Troubleshooting: eSIM Not Working on Android

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| **No "Add eSIM" option in Settings** | Carrier‑locked device, China ROM, or region‑restricted firmware | Check SIM lock status. If locked, contact carrier. If it's a China ROM or US carrier model, eSIM may be permanently disabled – no fix. |
| **"EID not found" after dialing `*#06#`** | Hardware eSIM is absent or disabled | Common on mid‑range/budget phones. No solution – use physical SIM. |
| **eSIM activation fails with "Invalid QR code"** | QR may be expired or damaged | Request a new QR code from your provider. Try entering the activation code manually instead. |
| **"Carrier lock" error when adding eSIM** | Your phone is SIM‑locked to a carrier | Contact your carrier to request an unlock. Some carriers allow eSIM even on locked phones; others do not. |
| **eSIM profile downloads but no signal** | Data Roaming not enabled, or APN settings missing | Turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line. Check APN settings (some travel eSIMs require manual entry). Restart phone. |
| **eSIM shows "Activating" for hours** | Unstable Wi‑Fi or carrier activation server busy | Wait 10‑15 minutes. Toggle Airplane Mode on and off. If still stuck, restart and re‑scan QR. |
| **No EID but you're sure your model supports eSIM** | Software update needed or CSC code mismatch | Check for system updates. On Samsung, check your CSC code – some regions have eSIM disabled. |
| **APN settings grayed out** | Carrier has locked APN settings | This is common on postpaid carrier eSIMs. Contact your carrier for assistance – you cannot manually change it. |
| **eSIM works but drains battery fast** | Phone searching for weak signal | Try setting network to 4G/LTE instead of 5G. Disable the eSIM when not in use (Settings > SIMs > toggle off). |

## Android eSIM vs iPhone eSIM – How Do They Compare?

If you're switching between Android and iPhone (or deciding which to travel with), here's how the eSIM experience stacks up:

| Factor | Android (flagship) | iPhone (XR/SE2+) |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Activation path** | Settings > Network & Internet > Mobile Network > Add Carrier (varies by brand) | Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM |
| **QR code scan** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |
| **Dual SIM** | Physical + eSIM (most flagships) | Physical + eSIM (most) or dual eSIM (US 14+) |
| **Carrier lock** | Many carrier‑locked models disable eSIM | Carrier‑locked models usually still allow eSIM |
| **Transfer to new phone** | Most travel eSIMs are one‑time install | Same – most travel eSIMs are one‑time install |
| **Regional consistency** | Highly fragmented – varies by model, region, and carrier | Generally consistent across models and regions |
| **Budget options with eSIM** | Very few (Pixel a‑series, some Motorola) | iPhone SE (2020/2022) is affordable |
| **First connection time** | 2‑5 minutes (varies by brand) | 1‑3 minutes (consistent) |

**Bottom line:** The underlying eSIM technology (GSMA SGP.22) is identical on both platforms – but Android's eSIM experience is far more fragmented. If you want a guaranteed, hassle‑free eSIM experience, iPhone is the safer bet. If you're on Android, stick to the models listed above.
