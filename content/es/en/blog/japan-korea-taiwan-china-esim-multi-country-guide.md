East Asia is the most connected region in the world — and also one of the most frustrating for travelers who need reliable internet. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, China, Hong Kong, and Macau all have world-class mobile networks. The problem isn't infrastructure; it's that every time you cross a border, your connectivity breaks. Install it once before you leave home, and it works automatically as you travel from Tokyo to Seoul, Taipei to Shanghai, or Sydney to Osaka. No hunting for local SIM cards at each airport, no juggling multiple QR codes, no surprise roaming bills.

## 4. China and Japan: The Trickiest Region to Get Right

China is the most challenging destination in East Asia for eSIM travelers. The Great Firewall blocks Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, and most Western apps — and many eSIM providers either don't cover China at all or only provide access to Chinese apps through domestic networks. According to a 2025 Reddit thread on r/eSIM, China-related connectivity issues account for over 40% of all troubleshooting posts in the subreddit.

### The China Connectivity Problem

The mobile networks across China are genuinely world-class. China Mobile, China Unicom, and China Telecom have 5G coverage in every major city and most rural areas. The problem is getting a SIM card that actually works seamlessly with Western apps, especially when China's heavily restricted internet and separate network requirements come into play.

**Here's what makes China different:**

- **The Great Firewall:** Most Western apps are blocked without a VPN. Google Maps, WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, and even Gmail don't work on domestic Chinese networks.
- **Network restrictions:** Many international eSIMs don't work in mainland China at all because they don't have agreements with Chinese carriers.
- **Hong Kong and Macau are different:** They operate on separate networks with access to the open internet. An eSIM that works in Hong Kong won't necessarily work in mainland China.

### Daily vs Total Data Plans

Regional eSIMs come in two flavors:

**Daily Plans:** You get a certain amount of high-speed data per day (e.g., 500MB, 1GB, 2GB). After that, speeds drop to 128kbps and reset at midnight.

**Total Plans:** You get a fixed amount of data for the entire validity period (e.g., 3GB, 5GB, 10GB). After that, speeds drop to 128kbps.

**Which to choose?** Daily plans are better if you use consistent amounts of data each day. Total plans are better if you have heavy usage days and light usage days.
