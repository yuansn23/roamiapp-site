Two providers can sell near-identical plans, yet one delivers 5G in rural Hokkaido while the other drops to no service the moment you leave Tokyo.

## 2. The Networks That Matter: Docomo vs SoftBank vs KDDI

Japan has three major mobile networks, and every eSIM provider resells access to one or more of them. Before comparing brands, understand what they mean for your trip.

| Network | Market Position | 5G Availability | Rural Coverage | Typical Speed |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **NTT Docomo** | Largest network | 38.4% | **Best** — the only reliable option in rural areas, Hokkaido, and the Alps | Strong, consistent |
| **SoftBank** | Strong in cities | 32% | Weak outside cities | **Fastest median** (62.1 Mbps) |
| **KDDI (au)** | Solid mid-tier | 26.5% | Moderate | Good in cities |

**The practical takeaway:** if your itinerary is Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka, any network works. If you're headed to Hokkaido ski resorts, the Japanese Alps, Okinawa's outer islands, or rural Kyushu, you need a provider that rides **NTT Docomo**.

A provider that runs on **both** Docomo and KDDI (auto-switching) is the safest choice — you get rural reach plus urban speed without manual network selection.

## 5. Roaming-Type vs Local-Type eSIMs

This is the single most under-discussed difference in the Japan eSIM market, and it explains most "why is my eSIM slow?" complaints.

| | Roaming-Type (Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi, Saily) | Local-Type (CDJapan Rental, Sakura, Mobal) |
|---|---|---|
| **Data routing** | Through overseas servers (HK/Singapore) | Direct to Japan's network |
| **Latency** | 150–200ms | 20–50ms |
| **Japanese IP** | Usually not | Yes |
| **Price** | Cheaper, more flexible | Slightly higher |
| **Japan-only apps** | May be geo-blocked | Full access |

For most tourists, roaming-type latency is fine — maps, messaging, and social media don't care about 150ms. It only matters if you need a Japanese IP for apps, gaming, or business video calls. In that case, choose a local-type provider.

### Key Takeaways

1. **The network matters more than the brand.** Ubigi on Docomo is structurally different from Airalo on SoftBank. Choose based on your itinerary, not just price.

2. **Roaming-type vs. local-type is the fundamental divide.** If you need low latency, a Japanese IP, or Japan-only services, choose a local-type provider like CDJapan Rental.

3. **"Unlimited" requires reading the fine print.** Every provider has FUP limits. Ubigi throttles to 2Mbps after ~60GB. Airalo caps at 3GB/day. Holafly has a 2–3GB/day full-speed cap.

4. **Install before you leave.** This is the single most important piece of advice across every review platform.

5. **Check phone compatibility.** Ubigi is incompatible with Pixel 9 and 10. Chinese-market phones often lack eSIM hardware.

### Your 5-Step Action Plan

1. **Map your itinerary:** Are you staying in cities or heading to rural areas? This determines whether you need a **docomo esim japan** (rural) or if a SoftBank/KDDI provider (city) is sufficient.

2. **Choose your provider type:** Roaming-type (cheaper, more flexible) or local-type (lower latency, Japanese IP, best for business).

3. **Pick your plan:** Estimate your data needs — light (1–2GB/week), moderate (3–5GB/week), or heavy (7–10GB+/week).

4. **Check phone compatibility:** Ensure your device supports eSIM and isn't carrier-locked. Pixel 9/10 users should avoid Ubigi.

5. **Purchase and install before departure:** Install the eSIM at home on WiFi — not at the airport.
