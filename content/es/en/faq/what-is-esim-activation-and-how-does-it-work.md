## eSIM Activation – 10-Second Quick Answer

**What is it?** A digital SIM download that gets your phone on a mobile network in under 2 minutes – no plastic card needed.

**How does the process work?**
- You scan a QR code (or use a carrier app)
- Your phone downloads a digital profile from the carrier's secure server
- Your phone connects to the local network instantly

**Does your phone support it?** Dial `*#06#` – if you see a 32-digit EID, yes. If only IMEI numbers appear, no.

**What do you need?** A stable Wi‑Fi connection + an eSIM plan (QR code or app)

**How long does the whole thing take?** QR scan: ~1-2 minutes. First network connection: up to 10-15 minutes.

> 💡 **Key requirement:** You MUST have an active internet connection (Wi‑Fi) to complete the setup. It cannot be done offline. This is a hard requirement – the profile must be downloaded from the carrier's server.

## What Is an eSIM? – A Simple Explanation

An eSIM (Embedded Subscriber Identity Module) is a tiny, programmable chip permanently soldered into your phone's motherboard during manufacturing. It does exactly the same job as a traditional plastic SIM card – proving your identity to a mobile network – but it's 100% digital.

Because it's rewritable, you can change carriers or add data plans without ever needing to eject a SIM tray or wait for a physical card to arrive in the mail. The technology is governed by the [GSMA standard SGP.22](https://www.gsma.com/esim/), ensuring global interoperability across devices and carriers. The [official Apple support page for eSIM](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108072) provides detailed information on compatible iPhone models, and [Android's official eSIM support page](https://support.google.com/android/answer/11241215) covers Android devices.

**Think of it this way:** A physical SIM is like a plastic key you carry around. An eSIM is like a digital key that lives in your phone – you can get a new key delivered electronically without ever visiting a locksmith.


## How Does the Process Actually Work – Behind the Scenes

When you buy a physical SIM, the network data is already hardcoded onto the chip. With an eSIM, the chip in your phone is initially blank. The activation process is simply filling that blank chip with your carrier's data.

Here's what happens behind the scenes:

- **The SM-DP+ Server:** Your carrier stores your unique digital profile on a secure server called the SM-DP+ (Subscription Manager Data Preparation). Think of it as a digital warehouse where your profile sits until you're ready to download it.

- **The Handshake:** When you scan the QR code, your phone reads it, connects to the internet, and reaches out to that specific server.

- **The Download:** Your phone securely downloads the profile and installs it onto the embedded chip. Once installed, it can connect to local cell towers just like a physical SIM.

### What Is an eSIM Confirmation Code?

Some carriers require a **confirmation code** (also called an activation code or verification code) during the setup process. This is a 4‑ to 8‑digit code that you enter after scanning the QR code or during manual entry.

**Where to find it:** The confirmation code is usually in the same email as your QR code. Look for a section labeled "Confirmation Code," "Activation Code," "Verification Code," or "SM-DP+ Code."

**Why some carriers require it:** It adds an extra layer of security – even if someone steals your QR code, they can't activate the profile without the confirmation code.

**What if you can't find it:** Check your spam folder. If it's not there, contact your provider's support with your order number.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** Because the profile must be downloaded from a server, you absolutely must have an active internet connection (Wi‑Fi) to complete the setup. It cannot be done offline. If you're traveling and don't have Wi‑Fi, use airport free Wi‑Fi or a mobile hotspot from a friend's phone.

### Can You Activate Without Wi‑Fi?

**No. This is impossible.**

The profile must be downloaded from the carrier's SM‑DP+ server over the internet. Without an internet connection, your phone has no way to reach the server and download the profile.

**What to do if you don't have Wi‑Fi:**
- Use airport free Wi‑Fi (most international airports offer this)
- Use a friend's mobile hotspot
- Use a café or hotel Wi‑Fi
- Buy a physical SIM first (as a fallback) and use its data connection to set up the digital one

**Important:** Install your plan **before you leave home** while on your home Wi‑Fi. This is the single most important tip for travellers.

### eSIM Activation vs. eSIM Download – What's the Difference?

People often use these terms interchangeably, but they're slightly different:

| Term | What It Actually Means |
|------|----------------------|
| **Download** | The technical act of transferring the profile from the carrier's server to your phone (takes 10‑60 seconds) |
| **Activation** | The entire process – checking compatibility, buying a plan, scanning the QR code, downloading the profile, and connecting to the network |

**In practice:** When someone says "I'm activating my eSIM," they usually mean the whole process. When a carrier says "the profile downloaded successfully," they mean the technical transfer is complete. For most users, the distinction doesn't matter – just follow the steps and you'll be connected.

### What Happens to Your Physical SIM When You Add a Digital Plan?

**Absolutely nothing.** Your physical SIM remains fully active and independent.

When you set up a digital plan:
- Your physical SIM continues to work normally for calls, texts, and data (if you set it as the data line).
- The digital plan adds a second line to your phone.
- You can choose which line to use for data, calls, and texts.
- Both lines can be active at the same time (dual SIM dual standby).

**Think of it this way:** Your physical SIM is your primary phone number. The digital plan is an additional line (like a second SIM card) that you can switch on and off as needed.

### Can One Profile Be Used on Multiple Devices?

**No. This is impossible.**

Each profile is cryptographically bound to the **EID** (eUICC Identifier) of the device it was activated on. Your phone's EID is unique – it's like a serial number for your eSIM hardware.

If you try to scan the same QR code on two phones, the second phone will get an error: "QR code already used" or "Activation failed."

**What you can do instead:**
- Buy a separate plan for each device
- Use Personal Hotspot from your phone to share data with your tablet or laptop
- If you have a carrier‑based plan (not travel), some carriers allow transfer between devices (see our [eSIM transfer guide](/faq/how-to-transfer-esim-between-iphone-and-android/))

**Exception:** Some carriers offer "multi‑device" plans where you can have multiple profiles on the same account – but each device still gets its own unique profile. The plan is shared across devices, but the profiles are device‑specific.


## How to Activate – 3 Standard Methods

Depending on your carrier and device, there are three primary ways to trigger the setup. For a complete walkthrough across all phone models, see our **[general eSIM activation guide](/faq/how-to-activate-an-esim/)**.

### Method 1: Scanning a QR Code (Most Common)
This is the standard approach for most **travel plans**.

1. Go to **Settings > Cellular** (or Mobile Data).
2. Tap **Add eSIM** or **Add Data Plan**.
3. Select **Use QR Code**.
4. Scan the QR code provided by your carrier and follow the on‑screen prompts.
5. If prompted, enter your **confirmation code** (4‑8 digits from your email).
6. Wait for the profile to download (takes 10‑60 seconds).
7. Label your plan (e.g., "Japan Trip" or "US Data").

### Method 2: Manual Entry (Camera Broken or QR Code on the Phone)
If your camera is broken or you cannot scan the QR code (e.g., the code is on the phone you're trying to set up), you can enter the details manually.

1. On the QR code scanning screen, tap **Enter Details Manually** at the bottom.
2. Input the **SM-DP+ Address** and the **Activation Code** provided in your confirmation email.
3. If your carrier requires a **confirmation code**, enter it when prompted.
4. Wait for the profile to download and activate.

### Method 3: Carrier App Push (In-App Activation)
Many modern carriers allow you to set up directly through their app with a single button press. The app automatically communicates with your phone's settings to install the profile without needing a QR code.

**How it works:**
1. Download your carrier's app (e.g., T‑Mobile, Verizon, Orange, EE).
2. Log in to your account.
3. Look for "Activate eSIM" or "Get eSIM" in the app.
4. Tap the button – the app automatically sends the profile to your phone.
5. Follow the on‑screen prompts to complete the setup.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** App‑based setup is usually the fastest method (under 1 minute) and doesn't require scanning a QR code. It's also less prone to errors like "QR code invalid."


## How to Check If Your Phone Supports Digital SIMs – Compatibility Checker Guide

Before you even try to set up a digital plan, you need to confirm that your phone actually has the required hardware. The last thing you want is to buy a travel plan only to discover your phone doesn't support it.

### Method 1: The Universal EID Check (Works on Almost Every Phone)

This is the fastest and most reliable way to answer **"does my phone have eSIM"** or **"does my phone support eSIM"**.

1. Open your phone's **dialer** (the app you use to make phone calls).
2. Dial `*#06#` (just like a phone number) and press call.
3. A screen will appear with your device's identifiers. Look for a number labeled **EID** (Embedded Identity Document) – it should be a 32‑digit code.
   - **If you see an EID:** Congratulations – your phone has the hardware. You can proceed with setup.
   - **If you only see IMEI numbers (IMEI1, IMEI2) and no EID:** Your phone does **not** support digital SIMs. You must use a physical SIM.

This method works on iPhones (all models with eSIM) and almost every Android phone, including Samsung, Google Pixel, OnePlus, Motorola, and Xiaomi.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** If you have an iPhone and want to know **how to check eSIM on iPhone**, this is the exact same code. On iPhones, you can also find the EID in **Settings > General > About** – scroll down to the **Digital SIM** or **Available SIM** section.

### Method 2: Checking via Settings (Detailed Path for Each OS)

If you prefer not to dial codes, or if `*#06#` doesn't work on your device (rare, but happens on some carrier‑locked phones), you can find the EID or eSIM menu through your phone's settings.

**On iPhone (iOS):**
- Go to **Settings > General > About**.
- Scroll down until you see **Digital SIM** or **Available SIM**.
- You will find your **EID** listed there.
- Additionally, you can go to **Settings > Cellular** and look for the **Add eSIM** or **Add Cellular Plan** option. If it's grayed out or missing, your iPhone may be a mainland China model (which has no eSIM) or carrier‑locked.

**On Samsung (One UI):**
- Go to **Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager**.
- If you see an option like **Add eSIM** or **Download eSIM**, your phone supports it.
- To find the EID, go to **Settings > About Phone > Status Information** – look for **EID**.

**On Google Pixel and Stock Android:**
- Go to **Settings > Network & Internet > Mobile Network**.
- Look for **Add Carrier** or **Download a SIM instead?**.
- To find the EID: **Settings > About Phone > SIM Status** – the EID will appear if it's present.

**On OnePlus, Xiaomi, Motorola (Android):**
- The path varies slightly, but generally: **Settings > Network & Internet > Mobile Network > Advanced > Add eSIM**.
- To check on Android, dial `*#06#` – it's universal.

### Method 3: Using IMEI to Check Compatibility (Online Tools)

If you don't have access to your phone (e.g., you're buying a used device online), you can use the phone's IMEI number to check support:

1. Find the IMEI number – dial `*#06#` on the device, or look under **Settings > About Phone**.
2. Go to an online **eSIM compatibility checker** (many providers offer a free IMEI lookup tool).
3. Enter the IMEI. The tool will tell you whether that specific device model supports digital SIMs.

> Important: The IMEI alone doesn't contain eSIM info, but it identifies your exact phone model. Tools cross‑reference IMEI against manufacturer databases.

### Regional Model Differences – Hidden Traps

Even if your phone model is "supposed" to support digital SIMs, regional variants can have them disabled:

| Region/Model | Support | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **iPhone (mainland China)** | ❌ No | Dual physical SIM hardware – digital chip removed |
| **Samsung S20 FE (US carrier)** | ❌ No | Carrier‑disabled to reduce costs |
| **Google Pixel 4a (Japan)** | ❌ No | Carrier‑disabled (Docomo/SoftBank) |
| **Samsung Galaxy A series (US)** | ⚠️ Varies | Some models have it, some don't |
| **Xiaomi (China ROM)** | ❌ No | China domestic models lack the hardware |
| **Huawei (China domestic)** | ❌ No | No Google services, no digital SIM |

**What to do:** Always check for EID using `*#06#` – this is the definitive test. Don't assume your phone supports it just because you saw a forum post saying it does.

### What If Your Phone Is Not Compatible?

If you've gone through all these steps and found no EID or eSIM menu, your phone cannot use digital SIMs. You have three options:

- **Use a physical travel SIM** – many providers also ship physical SIM cards.
- **Buy a local SIM at your destination** – still works on any phone.
- **Upgrade to a newer phone** – consider a Google Pixel 6a, Samsung Galaxy S21 or newer, or an iPhone XR or newer (excluding mainland China models).

## Common Activation Errors & How to Fix Them

| Error / Symptom | Most Likely Cause | Fix |
|----------------|-------------------|-----|
| **"Cellular Plan Cannot Be Added"** | Phone is carrier‑locked, or QR code already used (one‑time use). | Verify phone is unlocked: Settings > General > About > Carrier Lock must say "No SIM restrictions." If unlocked, request a new QR code. |
| **Stuck on "Activating..."** | Wi‑Fi connection dropped, or carrier server busy. | Do NOT delete the profile. Turn on Airplane Mode for 30 seconds, then turn it off. Restart phone if still stuck. |
| **"Invalid QR Code"** | QR code is expired or damaged. | Request a new QR code from your provider. Try manual entry instead. |
| **No signal after completion** | Data Roaming not enabled, or APN settings missing. | Toggle Data Roaming ON. Configure APN manually (see table below). |
| **Profile appears but no data** | Default data line set to physical SIM. | Go to Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data – select your new line as the data line. |
| **"eSIM Not Supported" on iPhone** | iPhone is from mainland China (dual physical SIM) or older than XR/SE2. | Check model in Settings > General > About. China models have no eSIM. |
| **QR code scanning not working** | Camera permissions denied, or screen damage. | Grant camera permissions, or use manual entry method. |
| **"Activation failed – server unreachable"** | Wi‑Fi blocking the connection (corporate network, VPN). | Disable VPN. Switch to a different Wi‑Fi network or use mobile hotspot. |
| **"Confirmation code required"** | Your carrier requires a 4‑8 digit verification code. | Check your email for the code. If not found, contact support. |
| **"QR code already used"** | The QR code was scanned on another device. | Travel plans are one‑time use – you need a new QR code from your provider. |
| **Takes more than 15 minutes to connect** | Phone is having trouble finding the local network. | Toggle Airplane Mode on/off. Manually select a network (Settings > Cellular > [line] > Network Selection). |
| **"Profile download failed"** | Intermittent Wi‑Fi connection. | Move closer to the router. Try a different Wi‑Fi network. Restart the process. |
| **"Activation failed" after scanning** | QR code is for a different region or carrier. | Confirm the QR code matches the plan you bought. Contact provider for correct code. |

## 3 Most Common Activation Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

Based on our support data, these are the top three mistakes that trip people up:

### Mistake 1: Trying to Activate Without Wi‑Fi

This is the #1 reason activation fails. You cannot download a profile without an internet connection.

**How to avoid:** Install your plan before you leave home on your home Wi‑Fi. If you're already travelling, find free airport or hotel Wi‑Fi before attempting.

### Mistake 2: Not Turning On Data Roaming

Your profile is downloaded correctly, but you have no signal. The problem? Data Roaming is still turned off.

**How to avoid:** After installation, go to Settings > Cellular > [your new line] and toggle Data Roaming ON. This is required for travel plans to work.

### Mistake 3: Deleting the Profile Mid-Activation

You see "Activating..." for a few minutes, panic, and delete the profile. Now you can't re-scan the QR code.

**How to avoid:** Be patient. Wait up to 15 minutes. If it's still stuck, restart your phone – don't delete the profile. If you delete it, you'll need a new QR code from your provider.


## FAQs (People Also Ask)

**Can I use my physical SIM and a digital plan at the same time?**
Yes! Most modern smartphones support Dual SIM functionality. You can keep your physical SIM active for calls and texts from home, while using your digital plan purely for local data. Both lines are active simultaneously (dual standby).

**Does setting up a digital plan drain my battery faster?**
Using a digital plan itself does not drain the battery. However, running two active lines (Dual SIM) simultaneously can cause slight battery drain as your phone is constantly searching for two different network signals. The difference is usually 2‑5% per day.

**How long does the whole process take?**
The download and setup normally takes less than 2 minutes. However, connecting to the local network for the first time can sometimes take up to 10-15 minutes depending on signal strength. If it takes longer than 15 minutes, see the troubleshooting table above.

**How do I find my EID if I already have a plan installed?**
On iPhone, go to **Settings > General > About** and scroll to the **Digital SIM** section. On Android, dial `*#06#` – the EID will appear alongside IMEI numbers.

**Can I check compatibility using IMEI before buying a used phone?**
Yes. Many online **eSIM compatibility checker** tools accept an IMEI number. You can also check the manufacturer's official specifications for that model. This is especially useful when buying a phone from eBay or a secondhand marketplace.

**Can I transfer my plan from my old phone to a new one?**
Yes – but the method depends on your carrier and phone models. For step‑by‑step instructions covering both platforms, see **[How to Transfer eSIM Between iPhone and Android](/faq/how-to-transfer-esim-between-iphone-and-android/)**.

**What's the difference between a travel plan and a local carrier plan?**
A travel plan is data‑only, bought online before your trip, and activates instantly via QR code. It has no phone number. A local carrier plan includes a phone number, requires ID verification, and is better for long‑term stays. See our **[UK eSIM guide](/united-kingdom-esim/)** for a detailed comparison.

**What if I run out of data on my travel plan?**
You can top up online through your provider's app or website – the new data is added automatically without scanning a new QR code. Some providers offer one‑click top‑up.

**Why is my plan showing "No Service" after setup?**
Most commonly, Data Roaming is not turned ON, or your phone is still using your home SIM for data. Double‑check both settings. Also ensure your phone is set to automatically select the network. See the troubleshooting table above for more fixes.

**Can I use a UK plan in other European countries?**
Only if your plan includes EU roaming. Travel plans often include EU roaming, while local UK carrier plans (EE, O2, Vodafone) typically charge £2/day for EU usage. Check your plan details before traveling.

**What is a confirmation code and why do I need it?**
A confirmation code is a 4‑8 digit code required by some carriers during setup. It adds an extra layer of security – even if someone steals your QR code, they can't activate the profile without the confirmation code. Find it in your provider's email.

**What if I don't have Wi‑Fi to activate my plan?**
You must have Wi‑Fi – it's impossible to activate without an internet connection. Use airport free Wi‑Fi, a friend's mobile hotspot, or a café/hotel Wi‑Fi. Best practice: install before you leave home on your own Wi‑Fi.

**Can I use the same profile on my phone and my tablet?**
No – each profile is bound to a single device's EID. You need a separate plan for each device. Your phone's profile cannot be used on your tablet or watch.

Most modern phones can store 5‑8 profiles, but only one or two can be active at a time (depending on the model). iPhone 13 and newer supports two active. Older iPhones (XR/11/12/SE) support one active plus one physical SIM.

**Does my phone need to be unlocked to use a travel plan?**
Yes – if your phone is carrier‑locked, it will reject profiles from other networks. Check Settings > General > About > Carrier Lock – it must say "No SIM restrictions." Contact your carrier to request an unlock.

**What does "eSIM activation failed" mean?**
It means the profile couldn't be downloaded or installed correctly. Common causes: no Wi‑Fi connection, expired QR code, carrier server issues, or phone not compatible. Check the troubleshooting table above for specific fixes.

**Can I activate a plan for someone else's phone?**
Yes – you can buy a plan and forward the QR code to them. However, they need to have a compatible phone and a stable Wi‑Fi connection. The QR code can only be scanned once, so make sure they're ready to install it.
