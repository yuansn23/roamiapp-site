What Is a Travel eSIM?

Before we dive into comparisons and plans, let’s start with the basics.

A travel SIM is a digital SIM card designed specifically for international travel. Unlike a physical SIM card that you insert into your phone, it is a software-based profile that you download to your phone’s embedded eUICC chip. Once installed, it connects to local mobile networks in your destination country, giving you data access without the need for a physical card or expensive international roaming.

Key characteristics of a travel SIM:

  • Digital-only: No physical card to insert, lose, or damage.
  • Pre-purchase: Buy online before you leave home, install at home on Wi-Fi.
  • Destination-specific: Plans are designed for specific countries or regions (e.g., Japan eSIM, Europe eSIM, Global eSIM).
  • Data-only: Most travel SIMs provide only data – no phone number for calls or SMS. You use apps like WhatsApp, FaceTime, and Skype for communication.
  • Instant activation: Activate on arrival by turning on Data Roaming – no store visit, no ID check, no language barrier.

Why travelers are switching to travel SIMs: Traditional options – carrier roaming ($10-15/day) and local physical SIMs (need to find a store, wait in line, show passport) – are either expensive or inconvenient. A travel SIM offers the best of both: low cost and instant setup.

Install Multiple Travel eSIMs Before You Leave

Here’s a pro tip most travelers don’t know:

Your iPhone can store 8 or more SIM profiles at once (on iPhone 13 and newer, the limit is actually quite high – you can store dozens). However, you can only have 2 active lines at any time.

Why this matters for travel:

If your trip includes multiple countries (e.g., Japan → USA → Europe), you can install all your travel SIMs before you leave home while you have reliable Wi‑Fi. Then, as you move between countries, you simply:

  1. Turn off the previous country’s SIM.
  2. Turn on the next country’s SIM.
  3. Set it as the Cellular Data line.

Step‑by‑step:

Step Action Why
1 Buy eSIM for Country A, Country B, Country C before departure Need Wi‑Fi to install
2 Install all profiles (scan QR codes or tap install links) All saved on phone
3 Keep all travel SIMs turned OFF until you land in each country Avoid accidental usage
4 Upon arrival in Country A: Turn ON that eSIM, turn on Data Roaming Instant connectivity
5 Before leaving Country A: Turn OFF that eSIM Avoid roaming charges in Country B
6 Upon arrival in Country B: Turn ON that eSIM Repeat for each country

💡 Important: iPhone stores SIM profiles, but you can’t have more than 2 active. Always keep your home SIM active (with Data Roaming OFF) for iMessage and calls, and activate one travel SIM as your data line. When you arrive in a new country, deactivate the old travel SIM and activate the new one.

Example: 3‑week trip itinerary

  • Week 1: Japan → install Japan SIM (Docomo network) before leaving.
  • Week 2: USA → install USA SIM (multi-carrier) before leaving.
  • Week 3: Europe → install Europe regional SIM before leaving.

All three installed at home, activated one at a time upon arrival. No hunting for SIM cards, no airport kiosks, no language barriers.

Managing Your Travel eSIM While Abroad

A travel SIM works alongside your home SIM as a second line. A few simple habits keep costs under control:

  • Set your data line before you fly: Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data → select your travel SIM.
  • Turn off data roaming on your home line: Settings > Cellular > [home line] → Data Roaming OFF, so you don’t get charged while your travel SIM handles data.
  • Turn on data roaming for the travel SIM line so it can connect to local networks.
  • When you return home, switch your data line back to your home carrier. You can delete the travel SIM once the plan expires.

How to Get and Activate a Travel eSIM Before Your Trip

Do this before you leave home (while you still have Wi‑Fi). This covers how to get a SIM and SIM activation in one go:

  1. Purchase your travel SIM from your chosen provider (app or website).
  2. Install the SIM profile – usually by scanning a SIM QR code or tapping an “Install” link.
    • To scan QR: Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM > Use QR Code.
  3. Label the SIM – give it a name like “Japan Travel” so you don’t confuse it with your home line.
  4. Turn off “Cellular Data” for this SIM until you arrive – to avoid accidentally using it before departure.
  5. Leave it installed – do not delete it.

Upon arrival at your destination:

  • Turn on Data Roaming for the travel SIM (Settings > Cellular > tap the travel SIM > toggle on Data Roaming).
  • Go to Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data → select the travel SIM.
  • Enjoy instant connectivity.

⚠️ If you have an embedded SIM‑only iPhone (US model): Your home SIM might still try to roam. To avoid charges, turn off Data Roaming for the home line and/or disable “Allow Cellular Data Switching.”

Travel eSIM No Signal Fixes

You landed. You turned on Data Roaming. Still no signal. Don’t panic.

Follow these steps in order – 90% of activation failures are resolved by step 3.

Step Action Where Expected Result
1 Toggle Airplane Mode ON, wait 5 seconds, then OFF Control Center Forces iPhone to re‑scan for networks
2 Restart iPhone Hold power + volume buttons Clears network cache
3 Manually select a network Settings > Cellular > [travel eSIM] > Network Selection → turn OFF Automatic → pick a local carrier manually (e.g., Docomo in Japan, Orange in France) Some carriers don’t broadcast automatically – manual selection fixes this
4 Check APN settings Settings > Cellular > [travel eSIM] > Cellular Data Network – make sure APN field is filled Most providers auto‑fill, but some require manual entry (see APN section in FAQ)
5 Still no signal? Contact provider support (Roami has 24/7 human chat) They can reprovision your profile remotely

Pro tip: Before you even board the plane, screenshot the APN settings from your provider’s app or website. If you land and have no Wi‑Fi, you’ll have the APN value saved offline.

⚠️ Common mistake: Some travelers forget to turn on Data Roaming for the travel SIM specifically. The SIM is installed but Data Roaming is OFF by default – you must toggle it ON. Settings > Cellular > [travel eSIM] → toggle Data Roaming.

How to Use Dual eSIM for Travel

If your iPhone supports dual eSIM (iPhone 13 and newer), this is the ideal setup:

  • Home SIM (primary): Keep active for calls, iMessage, and SMS from your home country. Turn off Data Roaming for this line to avoid huge bills.
  • Travel SIM (secondary): Use for all mobile data. Set as the default Cellular Data line.

This way, your home number can still receive calls (over Wi‑Fi Calling if your carrier supports it, using the travel SIM’s data connection) and your travel SIM handles maps, social media, and browsing.

For best results:

  • Enable Allow Cellular Data Switching – the phone can use the travel SIM’s data for your home line’s Wi‑Fi Calling.
  • Disable “Send as SMS” for iMessage if your home plan has expensive SMS.

Frequently Asked Questions About Travel eSIM

Q1: What is a travel SIM? A: A travel SIM is a digital SIM card that provides mobile data in multiple countries. You buy it online before departure, install it on your phone by scanning a QR code, and activate it when you arrive at your destination. Unlike physical SIM cards, there’s nothing to insert or lose.

Q2: Do travel SIMs come with a phone number? Most global providers (Roami, Airalo, Nomad) offer data‑only SIMs – no SMS or voice. You can still use WhatsApp, FaceTime, Skype, and other VoIP apps. If you need a local number (for reservations, banking), buy from a local carrier in‑country.

Q3: Can I make phone calls with a data‑only eSIM? Yes, using VoIP apps (WhatsApp call, FaceTime audio, Skype, Google Meet). Regular cellular calls require a voice plan.

Q4: Will my iMessage and FaceTime still work with my home number while using a travel SIM? Yes – iMessage and FaceTime are tied to your Apple ID and the phone number registered with iMessage. As long as your home SIM is active (even with data roaming off), iMessage will work.

Q5: How much data do I need for a 7‑day trip?

  • Light use (maps, WhatsApp, occasional browsing): 1–2GB
  • Moderate (social media, music streaming, some video): 3–5GB
  • Heavy (video calls, streaming Netflix, hotspot for laptop): 10GB+

Choose your SIM plans for international travel accordingly.

Q6: Can I use a travel SIM on a cruise ship? Most cruise ship cellular is not covered by standard travel SIMs. You’ll need the ship’s Wi‑Fi or a maritime roaming plan.

Q7: What if I run out of data mid‑trip? Most providers let you top up through their app instantly – no new QR code needed. Just buy an additional data pack.

Q8: Is eSIM safe? Can someone hack it? Embedded SIMs are more secure than physical SIMs. They comply with the GSMA’s SGP.22 standard, which prevents cloning or extraction. See the eSIM security overview in our iPhone 16 guide.

Q9: What is the best travel SIM for Europe? For most travelers, Roami or Airalo Europe regional plans offer the best value. Europe SIM or Europe SIM plans typically cover 30+ countries.

Q10: What is the best travel SIM for Japan? Ubigi and Roami are top choices. Japan SIM or Japan SIM plans are available from all major providers. For rural coverage, choose a plan with Docomo network access.

Q11: What is the best travel SIM for USA? Roami (multi-carrier T‑Mobile/AT&T/Verizon) offers the most reliable coverage. For a US number, Airalo Discover+ is the best option. See our USA eSIM guide for details.

Q12: Can I get a free eSIM trial? Yes! Roami offers a free trial eSIM so you can test the service before buying a full plan.

Q13: What is an international eSIM card? An international eSIM card is a digital SIM that works across multiple countries. Providers like Roami and Airalo offer global SIM card options that cover 190+ countries with a single purchase.

Q13b: Is an “international eSIM” the same as a “travel SIM”? Yes. They are two names for the same product, and you’ll also see it written as an international travel SIM or a global travel SIM (or “global travel SIM card”). All of them mean a prepaid, data-focused SIM you buy before your trip so you don’t have to hunt for a physical SIM abroad. The only real distinction is scope: an “international” or “global” plan spans multiple countries, while a “travel” plan is usually tied to one country or region. Choose based on your itinerary, not the label.

Q14: How do I get an eSIM for travel? Simply download your provider’s app, purchase a plan, and install the SIM profile via QR code. See our how to get an eSIM guide for detailed steps.

Q15: Can I install multiple travel SIMs on my iPhone before leaving? Yes! iPhone can store 8+ SIM profiles. Install all your country/regional SIMs before departure while you have Wi‑Fi, then activate them one at a time as you travel. See Section 5 for the full guide.

Q16: What if I need to call a local restaurant or hotel while traveling? A data‑only SIM doesn’t give you a local phone number. To make local calls, use WhatsApp/Skype/Google Voice VoIP calls, or keep your home SIM active (with Data Roaming ON only if you’re willing to pay per‑minute charges). Alternatively, buy a local carrier eSIM with voice if you’re staying long‑term.

Q17: What APN settings do I need to enter for my travel SIM? Most providers auto‑configure APN. If you have signal but no data, go to Settings > Cellular > [travel eSIM] > Cellular Data Network and fill in the APN field. Common values:

  • Roami: internet
  • Airalo: globaldata
  • Holafly: hola
  • Nomad: nbdata Always screenshot your provider’s APN settings before departure in case you need to enter them offline.

Q18: When should I NOT use a travel SIM? A travel SIM is not the best choice if:

  • You’re staying long-term (>6 months) – a local carrier plan is cheaper per month.
  • You need a local phone number for calls and SMS – travel SIMs are data-only.
  • Your phone is carrier-locked – you cannot install any eSIM from another provider.
  • You’re traveling to a country with very limited SIM coverage – check provider coverage maps first.

Q19: What should I do with my travel SIM after I return home? You have two options:

  1. Keep it installed but turned off – it stays stored on your phone and you can reuse it if you visit the same country again.
  2. Delete it – go to Settings > Cellular, tap the SIM, and select “Delete eSIM”. Once deleted, you cannot recover it – you’d need to buy a new plan.

Travel eSIM Final Takeaway

If you travel internationally at least once a year, switching to travel SIMs will save you money and hassle. The optimal strategy in 2026:

  • Before departure: Buy and install all your travel SIMs for every country you’ll visit. Install them at home on Wi‑Fi.
  • At departure gate: Disable Data Roaming on your home SIM.
  • On arrival: Enable Data Roaming on the relevant travel SIM to get connected.
  • Between countries: Turn off the old travel SIM, turn on the new one.
  • During trip: Use travel SIM for all data; keep home eSIM for iMessage and occasional calls (over Wi‑Fi Calling).
  • After return: Disable travel SIMs (or keep them for next trip – they stay stored on your phone).
