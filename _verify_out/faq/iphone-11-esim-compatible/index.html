Which iPhones Support eSIM?

eSIM arrived on iPhones with the 2018 models: iPhone XR, XS, and XS Max. All later iPhones (11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, and SE 2nd/3rd gen) also support eSIM – with one major regional exception: mainland China models (including Hong Kong in some cases) either lack the embedded SIM hardware or use dual physical SIM slots.

  • iPhone XR, XS, XS Max (2018): ✅ First iPhones with eSIM
  • iPhone 11, 11 Pro, 11 Pro Max: ✅ Full eSIM support (mainland China: dual physical SIM instead)
  • iPhone SE (2nd gen 2020, 3rd gen 2022): ✅ eSIM support
  • iPhone 12, 12 mini, 12 Pro, 12 Pro Max: ✅ eSIM + first 5G support
  • iPhone 13, 13 mini, 13 Pro, 13 Pro Max: ✅ eSIM + improved dual-line capabilities
  • iPhone 14, 14 Plus, 14 Pro, 14 Pro Max: ✅ US models are eSIM only (no physical SIM tray); other regions have both
  • iPhone 15, 15 Plus, 15 Pro, 15 Pro Max: ✅ Same as iPhone 14 – US eSIM only, global keeps the tray
  • iPhone 16, 16 Plus, 16 Pro, 16 Pro Max: ✅ Support continues; US remains eSIM only
  • iPhone 16e: ✅ New budget model – supports eSIM (region‑dependent for physical SIM)
  • iPhone 17, 17 Pro, 17 Pro Max, 17 Air: ✅ Expected full support; US models likely eSIM only

iPhone 8, 8 Plus, and iPhone X (2017) do NOT support eSIM – they use physical SIM only. If you own one, you cannot use any digital SIM service.

The official Apple eSIM support page provides additional details on compatible models. The technology is governed by the GSMA standard SGP.22, ensuring global interoperability across devices and carriers.

When Did iPhones Start Using eSIM?

  • 2017 and earlier (iPhone 8, X, etc.) – No eSIM. Physical SIM only.
  • 2018 (iPhone XR, XS, XS Max) – First eSIM iPhones. Dual SIM: one physical nano‑SIM + one eSIM.
  • 2019 (iPhone 11 series) – Same eSIM functionality as 2018 models; mainland China gets dual physical SIM instead.
  • 2020 (iPhone SE 2nd gen, iPhone 12 series) – eSIM continues; iPhone 12 adds 5G support.
  • 2021 (iPhone 13 series) – First support for two active lines (dual eSIM) on some carriers.
  • 2022 (iPhone SE 3rd gen, iPhone 14 series) – US models become eSIM only (no physical SIM tray).
  • 2023 (iPhone 15 series) – US continues eSIM only; global retains the physical tray.
  • 2024 (iPhone 16 series) – Same as iPhone 15; iPhone 16e introduced as budget eSIM model.
  • 2025 (iPhone 17 series) – Expected to continue the same regional split.

So the first iPhone with eSIM was the iPhone XR/XS in September 2018.

How to Verify If Your iPhone Has eSIM

Dial *#06# to Check for eSIM

  1. Open the Phone app.
  2. Dial *#06# and press call.
  3. Look for a 32‑digit EID (Embedded Identity Document – think of it as your phone’s digital SIM serial number). If present, your iPhone has eSIM hardware.
  4. If you only see IMEI numbers (IMEI1, IMEI2) and no EID, your device lacks the hardware (typical for mainland China models or iPhones older than XR).

Check in Settings

Go to Settings > General > About. Scroll down. If you see “Available SIM” or “Digital SIM” with an EID number, eSIM is present.

Try Adding a Cellular Plan

Go to Settings > Cellular > Add Cellular Plan. If the option is visible and tappable, your phone supports eSIM. If grayed out or missing, no luck.

Detailed Breakdown by iPhone Generation

iPhone 8, 8 Plus, and iPhone X

These models predate embedded-SIM hardware entirely. If you own an iPhone 8 or iPhone X, you cannot use any digital SIM – physical SIM only. The same applies to the iPhone 7 and earlier.

iPhone XR, XS, and XS Max

The iPhone XR, XS, and XS Max are the first iPhones with eSIM support. All three models work with one physical SIM and one digital SIM simultaneously (dual SIM dual standby). They do not support 5G. For the XR’s exact model numbers (A2105–A2109) and a used‑buying checklist, see our dedicated iPhone XR eSIM guide.

iPhone 11, 11 Pro, and 11 Pro Max

iPhone 11 series (including the Pro and Pro Max) supports eSIM globally. However, mainland China versions have dual physical SIM slots and no digital SIM. If you have a global iPhone 11, you can use eSIM for 4G LTE data.

iPhone SE

Both generations of the iPhone SE support eSIM. They are excellent budget options, offering the same digital SIM functionality as the flagship models at a lower price. For the SE’s generation‑by‑generation breakdown, model numbers, and carrier‑unlock policies, see our dedicated iPhone SE eSIM guide.

iPhone 12 Series

The iPhone 12, 12 mini, 12 Pro, and 12 Pro Max all support eSIM. They were the first iPhones to offer 5G over a digital SIM. Dual SIM works as one physical SIM + one eSIM.

iPhone 13 Series

iPhone 13 series (including mini, Pro, Pro Max) introduces dual eSIM capability – you can have two active lines simultaneously (no physical SIM needed). This is also the first generation where iPhone 13 dual eSIM became a standard feature.

iPhone 14 Series

All iPhone 14 models support it, but with a major change: US models have no physical SIM tray at all. Global models still offer a physical SIM slot plus eSIM. iPhone 14 eSIM only is the reality for US customers.

iPhone 15 Series

iPhone 15 series follows the same regional split: US models are eSIM only; global models have both. So whether you need iPhone 15 eSIM or physical SIM depends entirely on where you buy it.

iPhone 16 Series

iPhone 16 series continues the same pattern. US models are eSIM only; international versions retain the physical SIM slot. iPhone 16 dual eSIM is standard across the lineup.

iPhone 16e

The iPhone 16e is Apple’s new budget model, positioned below the main iPhone 16 lineup. It supports eSIM on all models – US versions ship without a SIM tray, while global versions retain a physical slot alongside it.

iPhone 17 Series

iPhone 17 series (including 17, 17 Pro, 17 Pro Max, and 17 Air) continues the same regional split established with iPhone 14. US models are expected to drop the physical tray; global models retain both options.

How Many eSIM Profiles Can an iPhone Store?

iPhones can store up to eight SIM profiles at a time, but the exact number depends on the iPhone model and iOS version.

iPhone 13 series and newer ~8 2 (dual eSIM)

How to Manage Multiple eSIM Profiles

  • Delete unused profiles: Settings > Cellular > tap the plan > Remove Cellular Plan. This frees up storage for new profiles.
  • Switch between stored eSIMs: Settings > Cellular > tap the desired plan > Turn On This Line.
  • Rename profiles: Settings > Cellular > tap the plan > Cellular Plan Label – choose a custom name (e.g., “Japan Trip”).

Using Physical SIM and eSIM Together

All iPhones from XR through 13 (global) and global 14–17 support one physical SIM + one eSIM active at the same time. US eSIM‑only models (14–17) use dual lines instead.

How to Set Up Dual SIM

  1. Insert your physical SIM (or have your first eSIM active).
  2. Add a second line via Settings > Cellular > Add Cellular Plan.
  3. In Settings > Cellular, assign:
    • Default Voice Line – which line makes calls
    • Default Data Line – which line uses mobile internet
    • Cellular Data Switching – allow automatic switching if the primary data line loses signal (use with caution abroad to avoid roaming charges)

Recommended Dual SIM Settings for Travel

Setting Recommended Value Why
Default Voice Line Home SIM (your regular number) Keep receiving calls on your home number
Cellular Data Travel eSIM Use local data plan, not expensive roaming
Data Roaming (Home SIM) OFF Prevent accidental roaming charges
Data Roaming (Travel eSIM) ON Required for the travel line to work
Allow Cellular Data Switching ON Allows calls/SMS on home SIM to use travel eSIM data for Wi‑Fi Calling

Traveling from the US to Japan

  • Keep your US physical SIM (or home eSIM) for calls/SMS.
  • Buy a Japan travel eSIM for data.
  • Set the Japan line as Default Data Line and turn on Data Roaming for that line.

eSIM vs Physical SIM

The short version: an embedded SIM swaps carriers instantly and can’t be physically stolen; a physical SIM works on every phone and doubles as a backup where digital SIM coverage is limited. For a full feature‑by‑feature comparison, see our What is eSIM? guide.

  • Hong Kong & Macau models: Typically physical SIM + eSIM (same as global).

  • Global models (Europe, Asia non‑China, etc.): Physical SIM + eSIM.

eSIM-Only iPhones

What “eSIM Only” Means

  • No physical SIM slot.
  • You must activate at least one digital SIM to use cellular.
  • Dual SIM works via two digital lines (dual eSIM).

First‑Time Setup for eSIM‑Only iPhone

  • Transfer a SIM profile from your old iPhone during setup.
  • Or activate via carrier app.
  • Or skip and add later via Settings > Cellular > Add Cellular Plan.

Traveling with an eSIM‑Only iPhone

  • Install your travel profile before departure (on Wi‑Fi).
  • At destination, set it as Default Data Line and turn on Data Roaming.
  • Keep your home line active for calls (but beware roaming charges if you answer).

What If Your eSIM‑Only iPhone Breaks Abroad?

You cannot insert a physical SIM as a backup. Your options:

  • Buy a new digital SIM from a local carrier that supports online purchase.
  • Use Wi‑Fi only.
  • Keep a backup QR code in your email.
  • Carry a secondary device (old phone) with a physical SIM slot as backup.

Transferring eSIM to a New iPhone

Moving an eSIM to a new iPhone usually happens automatically via Quick Transfer during setup (iOS 16+), or through your carrier’s app. Travel profiles are often one‑time installs, so keep your original QR code. Full methods — including physical‑SIM conversion and the delete‑and‑reinstall fallback — are covered in our transfer guide.

What Happens to eSIM When You Sell or Reset Your iPhone?

Secure Erasure Steps Before Selling Your iPhone

Step Action
1 Settings > Cellular > tap each eSIM > Remove Cellular Plan (this releases the profile from the device)
2 Remove any physical SIM card
3 Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Erase All Content and Settings
4 This removes all SIM profiles permanently – the next owner cannot access your lines

What Happens If You Don’t Remove Profiles Before Reset?

  • The SIM profile becomes orphaned – it’s still tied to your carrier but inactive on the device.
  • The next owner won’t be able to use it (it’s still tied to your account).
  • You may still be billed for the plan if it’s active.

Can You Reactivate a Line After a Factory Reset?

  • Travel plans: Usually not – they’re one‑time install. Contact your provider for a new QR code.
  • Carrier lines (Verizon, T‑Mobile, etc.): Yes – use the carrier app to re‑download the profile.
  • If you kept the original QR code: You may be able to re‑scan it (if not expired).

⚠️ Warning: Always remove SIM profiles before erasing your iPhone. Orphaned profiles can cause activation issues for the next owner and billing issues for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the iPhone 11 series support eSIM? Yes – the iPhone 11, 11 Pro, and 11 Pro Max all support eSIM, provided you have the global version (not mainland China). You can verify by dialing *#06# – if an EID appears, you’re ready to go.

Does the iPhone 12 support eSIM while also using a physical SIM? Yes. All iPhone 12 models (global versions) allow one physical SIM and one digital SIM active at the same time. Set the physical SIM for calls/SMS and the digital line for data.

Does the iPhone 13 have dual eSIM capability? Yes. The iPhone 13 series (including Pro and Pro Max) supports two active lines simultaneously. This means you can run two travel plans, or a home line plus a travel profile, without any physical card.

Does the iPhone 14 (US model) have a physical SIM slot? No. US models of the iPhone 14, 15, 16, and 17 are eSIM only. They have no physical SIM tray. Global models still include a physical SIM slot alongside digital SIM.

Does the iPhone 16e support eSIM? Yes. The iPhone 16e supports eSIM on all models. US versions ship without a SIM tray; global versions add a physical slot.

Can I travel to China with a US eSIM‑only iPhone? Yes, but you cannot use a Chinese local carrier’s eSIM (they don’t offer it to tourists). You must buy an international travel plan before you go. Install it on Wi‑Fi, then turn on Data Roaming when you arrive – it will work on roaming partners like China Unicom.

What is the first iPhone with eSIM? The iPhone XR, XS, and XS Max (released September 2018) are the first iPhones to support it. If you have an iPhone 8 or iPhone X, you cannot use eSIM.

Why does my iPhone say “Cellular Plan Cannot Be Added”? You may have too many stored profiles (iPhones can store around 8). Go to Settings > Cellular, delete any unused plans, restart your phone, and try again. Also ensure the QR code hasn’t expired.

What does “eSIM only” mean on US iPhones? It means the iPhone has no physical SIM tray – you must use downloaded profiles for cellular service. Dual SIM works via two digital lines (dual eSIM).

How many eSIM profiles can I store on my iPhone? iPhone can store up to 8 profiles, but only one or two can be active at once (depending on the model). See the storage limit table above.

What happens to my eSIM if I reset my iPhone? If you erase all content and settings without removing your line first, the profile becomes orphaned. Always remove SIM profiles before resetting (Settings > Cellular > Remove Cellular Plan).

Does the iPhone 8 support eSIM? No – the iPhone 8, 8 Plus, and iPhone X do not have eSIM hardware. They use physical SIM only.

What’s the cheapest iPhone with eSIM? The iPhone XR (used) is the cheapest eSIM‑capable iPhone at around $100–150. The iPhone SE (3rd gen) is the cheapest new option. See our iPhone XR guide and iPhone SE guide for the full breakdown.

What’s the difference between iPhone 13 dual eSIM and iPhone 12 dual SIM? iPhone 12 supports one physical SIM + one eSIM. iPhone 13 supports two active digital lines (no physical SIM needed) OR one physical SIM + one eSIM. This makes iPhone 13 the first model with true dual-line capability.

Can I use an eSIM on iPhone 17 if I bought it in the US? Yes – but US iPhone 17 models are eSIM‑only (no physical SIM slot). You’ll need a digital line for all cellular service.

How do I fix eSIM not showing up in Settings after an iOS update? This is a known iOS bug. Go to Settings > General > About – if your EID number appears but the line doesn’t show in Cellular, restart your iPhone. If that doesn’t work, go to Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM – sometimes the phone detects the existing profile and re-adds it automatically.
