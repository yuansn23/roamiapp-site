Why Must “Unlimited” Come with “Limited”?

Before Buying Holafly, Check Your Usage Tier

Four Scenarios Where We Don’t Recommend Buying

1. Light users with daily usage under 2GB You only check maps, send WeChat messages, and scroll Instagram occasionally. A per‑GB plan is more than enough for you. Holafly’s daily billing makes you pay 2‑4 times more for “unlimited” that you’ll never fully use.

2. Users who frequently need to hotspot to laptops or companions Holafly’s standard hotspot cap is 500MB to 1GB per day. If you need to work on your laptop or share with friends, this limit will frustrate you – hotspot is suspended once you exceed the cap. If that’s you, consider Airalo or Roami – they have much looser hotspot policies.

3. Budget‑conscious users chasing the lowest cost per GB Holafly’s unit price is far higher than per‑GB competitors. If your main decision metric is “cost per GB,” Holafly loses every time.

4. Users who want to “stock up” but aren’t sure of travel dates Holafly’s plan counts down from the moment you activate it – not by usage, but by days. If you’re unsure when you’ll depart, or want to “buy and save for later,” that money will likely go to waste.

Three Scenarios Where We Recommend Buying

1. Heavy streamers or frequent video conference users You watch 3‑5 hours of YouTube/Netflix/TikTok daily, or join video calls daily. Per‑GB plans feel like “counting drops while showering” – Holafly removes that anxiety altogether.

2. Anxious users who hate tracking remaining data “How much left?” “Will I exceed?” “Should I save?” – if you don’t want these thoughts occupying mental bandwidth during your trip, Holafly’s “unlimited” promise is the peace of mind you pay for.

3. Travelers with fixed itineraries across multiple countries For example, a 10‑day trip across 4 European countries. Holafly’s regional plans (like Europe 33‑country) are far more convenient than buying separate per‑GB plans for each country – you don’t have to replan data after every border crossing.

Key takeaways: Daily usage >5GB + no need for frequent hotspot + fixed itinerary = Holafly’s ideal user profile. If any of these three doesn’t fit, you need to reconsider.

Holafly eSIM Installation Guide and the Three Most Common Pitfalls

Standard Installation Process (about 5 minutes)

  1. After purchase, check your email for the QR code or installation link.
  2. iOS 17.4 and above: you can long‑press the QR code in the email to start installation.
  3. Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → Scan QR code.
  4. Follow prompts to complete installation – it will activate automatically upon arrival.

Or install via the Holafly App:

  • Download the app → log in with your purchase email → go to “Purchases” → tap “Install” on the corresponding eSIM.

The 3 Most Common Pitfalls

Pitfall 1: Installing on the wrong device The eSIM profile can only be installed once. If you scan the QR code on phone A, you cannot transfer it to phone B. Confirm your device before scanning – and if you’re not sure whether your phone supports eSIM at all, check our eSIM compatible devices list first.

Pitfall 2: Activating too early, wasting days Holafly’s plan starts counting from the moment of activation, not from your arrival. If you activate a few days early, you’ll lose valid days.

Pitfall 3: Not installing before departure Installation requires a stable internet connection. If you wait until after landing and the airport has no WiFi, you won’t be able to complete it. Install it at home on WiFi before you leave.

Key takeaways: Installation itself is simple, but “wrong device,” “too early activation,” and “installing after arrival” are the three most common tripwires.

Holafly eSIM FAQs: 10 Questions Answered Directly

Q1: Is Holafly’s “unlimited data” truly unlimited?

A: Not technically unlimited, but “conditionally unlimited.” It is subject to the Fair Use Policy – Europe: 5GB/day then throttled to 2 Mbps; US/Japan: 3GB/day then throttled to 1 Mbps. “Unlimited” means “you won’t be cut off,” not “full speed throughout.” If your daily usage stays below those thresholds, you won’t feel the throttle at all; if you exceed, the second half of your day will feel much slower.

Q2: Does Holafly support 5G?

A: Yes. In destinations with 5G coverage (e.g., major European cities, US, Japan, South Korea), Holafly can use 5G. Actual speeds depend on the local partner’s network quality – third‑party speed tests show peaks of up to 267 Mbps in some cities like France, Germany, and Japan. Check Holafly’s website for 5G availability at your destination.

Q3: Can I hotspot with Holafly?

A: Yes, but with a daily cap. Standard plans have a hotspot limit of 500MB to 1GB per day. Exceeding that suspends hotspot until the next day. If you rely on hotspot for work or sharing, Holafly may not suit you – Airalo or Roami are far less restrictive. Roami’s plans generally allow normal hotspot sharing without a hard daily 500MB cap.

Q4: Does Holafly provide a phone number?

A: Most plans do not. The only exception is the Holafly Plans Unlimited monthly subscription, which includes a local phone number and free incoming SMS. The standard daily unlimited plans are data‑only. If you need to receive SMS verification codes from banks or Google, only the Unlimited monthly plan will work.

Q5: Can I get a refund if I bought the wrong plan?

A: Yes, for unused eSIMs within 6 months of purchase – 100% full refund (cash), the most generous policy in the industry. If you request a refund due to technical connection issues, a $3.50 admin fee may be deducted. Subscription plans have a 14‑day refund window. Refunds typically take 5‑10 business days depending on your bank.

Q6: Can I top up after using up the plan?

A: No. Holafly’s eSIM is a “one‑time profile.” Once used or expired, you must buy a new eSIM – you cannot add data directly in the app like with Airalo. This is Holafly’s biggest flexibility shortcoming – if your trip unexpectedly extends by 3 days, you can’t “pay to extend,” you have to buy a new plan covering a longer period.

Q7: Can Holafly be used in mainland China?

A: Yes. But it is strongly recommended to install before departure, because setup goes more smoothly on your home network. After arrival, the eSIM will automatically connect to the local partner network. Also note that in some destinations the availability of certain international services (e.g., Google‑related apps) is determined by local regulations rather than by the provider – this applies to any travel eSIM, not just Holafly.

Q8: Is Holafly cheaper than traditional international roaming?

A: About 70‑90% cheaper. For 7 days in Japan, traditional roaming costs about $50‑80, while Holafly is around $24‑27. However, per‑GB eSIMs (like Airalo, Saily) are usually cheaper than Holafly – Holafly’s premium buys the psychological safety of “unlimited.” If you use under 2GB/day, a per‑GB plan will be more economical.
