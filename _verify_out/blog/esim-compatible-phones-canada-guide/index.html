Quick Answer: Does Your Phone Support eSIM?

Fastest check: Open your dialer, type *#06#, and look for EID (a 32-digit code).

  • Only IMEI, no EID → your phone does not support eSIM.

  • Samsung: Settings → Connections → SIM Manager → look for “Add eSIM.”

Canada eSIM EID Check in 30 Seconds

Before buying, confirm the handset has embedded-SIM hardware. The fastest method works on both iPhone and Android.

Dial *#06# to Check EID

  1. Open the dialer.
  2. Type *#06#.
  3. Look for EID (eSIM Identifier), a 32-digit code.

If you see EID: your phone has eSIM hardware.
If you only see IMEI: your phone does not support eSIM.

What you’ll see: On iPhone, the EID appears below the IMEI. On Samsung, it may appear as “EID” or “eSIM ID.” If you see only two IMEI numbers (IMEI1 and IMEI2), there is no embedded-SIM hardware inside.

Check eSIM Settings by Phone Brand

  • iPhone: Settings → General → About → look for “Available SIM” or “Carrier Lock.”
  • Samsung: Settings → Connections → SIM Manager → look for “Add eSIM.”
  • Google Pixel: Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → look for “Download a SIM instead?”
  • Other Android brands: search “eSIM” in Settings.

Seeing the option means the phone is compatible. If not, check the regional notes in the tables below—some models have the hardware but it is disabled by the carrier or missing in certain regional versions.

Check Carrier Lock Status

Even a fully compatible handset must be carrier-unlocked.

  • iPhone: Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock should say “No SIM restrictions.”
  • Android: Settings → About Phone → Status → check network/SIM status.

In Canada, the CRTC requires Bell, Rogers, Telus, and Freedom Mobile to unlock devices free of charge. See the CRTC Wireless Code.

Important: iPhones sold in mainland China generally do not support eSIM. Per Apple’s official documentation, the only mainland China exceptions are iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, iPhone 17e (model A3635), and iPhone Air (model A3518). In Hong Kong and Macao, certain iPhone models ship with two nano-SIM slots instead of eSIM support. Always check your exact model number.

Why eSIM Compatibility Matters in Canada

eSIM Is Hardware, Not Software

eSIM requires an eUICC chip (embedded Universal Integrated Circuit Card) built into the phone. This chip is separate from the physical SIM slot. The GSMA develops global eSIM specifications to ensure interoperability between devices and carriers.

This means:

  • A software update cannot add eSIM if the hardware is missing.
  • The same model may support the technology in one region but not another.
  • Carriers can disable eSIM through software locks, even when the hardware exists.

2026 eSIM Changes You Should Know

eSIM-only phones are now mainstream.

  • iPhone 17 Air: eSIM-only globally.
  • iPhone 17 / 17 Pro / 17 Pro Max / 17e: eSIM-only in 12 countries, including the US, Canada, Japan, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Guam, and US Virgin Islands.
  • iPhone 14–16 (US versions): eSIM-only. Canadian versions of iPhone 14, 15, and 16 retain a physical SIM tray.
  • Google Pixel 10 (US version): eSIM-only.
  • Samsung Galaxy S26 (some markets): moving toward eSIM-only.

Important correction: Canadian versions of iPhone 14, iPhone 15, and iPhone 16 have a physical SIM card tray. Only the Canadian iPhone 17 series is eSIM-only. This is a common source of confusion in many online guides.

Mid-range phones now support eSIM. Samsung A series (A54, A55, A56, A57) and Google Pixel a series (8a, 9a, 10a) now support eSIM.

Regional differences remain huge. The same phone may support eSIM in Canada but not in mainland China, Taiwan, or Korea. Always confirm the exact regional version.

A note on our sources: The device compatibility information below is based on publicly available manufacturer documentation — including Apple’s official eSIM support page — GSMA specifications, and user reports from communities like Reddit. We do not present our own testing claims.

eSIM vs Physical SIM: What the Terms Mean

Before you buy, it helps to know the identifiers you will see on your phone:

  • IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity): a 15-digit number that identifies the physical device. A phone has one IMEI per SIM slot.
  • EID (eSIM Identifier): a 32-digit number that identifies the embedded eUICC chip. If your phone has an EID, it has the necessary hardware.
  • ICCID (Integrated Circuit Card Identifier): the serial number of a specific SIM profile — physical or embedded.
  • Carrier lock: a software restriction that ties a phone to one network until it is unlocked.
  • Dual SIM: running two lines at once, usually one physical nano-SIM plus one embedded profile, or two embedded profiles.

Knowing these terms makes the compatibility checks below faster to follow, and helps you read the regional-version notes without confusion.

Samsung eSIM Carrier Lock Removal in Canada

Samsung devices sold by Canadian carriers often have eSIM disabled. Here’s how to fix it:

  1. Call your carrier: Bell, Rogers, Telus, or Freedom Mobile.
  2. Request eSIM enablement and network unlock. CRTC requires free unlock.
  3. Provide your IMEI: Dial *#06# to get it.
  4. Wait for confirmation: Usually 1–2 business days.
  5. Restart phone and check SIM Manager: “Add eSIM” should appear.

If the carrier refuses: File a complaint with the CCTS (Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services).

Canada Carrier Lock and eSIM Unlock Steps

Even if your phone supports eSIM, it must be carrier-unlocked to use a third-party provider.

In Canada, the CRTC requires Bell, Rogers, Telus, and Freedom Mobile to unlock devices free of charge. See the CRTC Wireless Code.

How to Check If Your Phone Is Locked

  • iPhone: Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. It should say “No SIM restrictions.”
  • Android: Settings → About Phone → Status → check network/SIM status.

How to Remove a Canadian Carrier Lock

  1. Check lock status (see above).
  2. Contact your carrier: Bell, Rogers, Telus, or Freedom Mobile.
  3. Request a free unlock. CRTC requires it.
  4. Provide your IMEI: Dial *#06# to get it.
  5. Wait for confirmation: Usually 1–2 business days.
  6. Insert a non-carrier SIM or try installing a third-party eSIM. If it works, the phone is unlocked.
  7. If eSIM remains disabled (Samsung): Ask the carrier to enable it specifically. Some carriers disable it by default.

Pro tip: If you bought a used Samsung from a Canadian carrier, the eSIM may be permanently disabled. Check with the carrier before buying.

If Your Phone Does Not Support eSIM

If you checked and found no EID or no eSIM option, you have four options:

Use a Physical SIM Card

Canadian carriers (Bell, Rogers, Telus, Freedom Mobile) and some travel SIM providers offer physical SIM cards. Buy at a store after arrival, or order online before departure.

Pros: Works with all phones.
Cons: Need to swap cards; may need to queue; cannot keep original number unless phone has dual physical slots.

Rent a Portable Wi-Fi Device

A pocket Wi-Fi router connects to a Canadian network and shares internet via Wi-Fi.

Pros: Multi-device sharing; no phone hardware dependency.
Cons: Extra device to carry and charge; rental cost usually higher than eSIM.

Use a Backup Phone or Buy an eSIM-Compatible Phone

If you travel often, a phone that supports eSIM is a long-term solution. In 2026, it is available in mid-range models too.

Buying advice:

  • Confirm eSIM support (check EID).
  • Confirm the phone is unlocked.
  • Check regional version — Canadian iPhone 17 is eSIM-only; Canadian iPhone 14, 15, and 16 have physical SIM trays.

Rely on Wi-Fi and VoIP Apps

For short trips with low data needs, use hotel/cafe/airport Wi-Fi with WhatsApp, WeChat, or FaceTime.

Pros: Zero cost.
Cons: Depends on Wi-Fi; no navigation or ride-hailing while moving; unreliable in emergencies.

Does my phone support eSIM?

Can I use an eSIM on a phone bought in another country?

Depends on model and regional version. If the hardware supports it and the phone is unlocked, it usually works. Some regional versions (mainland China iPhone, Taiwan Samsung) do not support eSIM. Check EID with *#06#.

Is iPhone 17 eSIM-only in Canada?

Can a carrier-locked phone use eSIM?

No. The phone must be carrier-unlocked. In Canada, CRTC requires Bell, Rogers, Telus, and Freedom Mobile to unlock free of charge.

  • iPhone Air: eSIM-only globally.

How do I check eSIM compatibility for a specific model?

Dial *#06# and look for EID. If EID appears, the hardware supports eSIM. Then check if your carrier has disabled it or if the regional version blocks it.
