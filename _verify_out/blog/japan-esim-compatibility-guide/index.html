Part 1: eSIM Compatibility — The Big Picture

The three factors that decide everything

Before we dive into specific brands, it’s important to understand the three factors that determine whether your phone will work with a Japan eSIM.

Factor 1: Hardware Support

Your phone must have an eSIM chip built in. Most flagship and many mid-range phones from 2018 onward have this hardware. However, some budget models and older devices don’t.

Factor 2: Country of Purchase

This is the most common trap for travelers. Even within the same model line, certain regional variants — particularly Chinese-market and some Hong Kong/Macau models — do not support eSIM at all.

For example:

  • China mainland iPhones have eSIM physically disabled across all models.
  • Samsung phones sold in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and some Asian markets lack the eSIM chip entirely.
  • Chinese-brand phones (Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo) sold domestically typically lack eSIM due to local regulatory requirements.

The rule of thumb: If you bought your phone in mainland China, assume it does NOT support eSIM unless you’ve confirmed otherwise.

Factor 3: Carrier Lock Status

Even if your phone has eSIM hardware, it won’t work with a Japan eSIM if it’s carrier-locked — tied to a specific mobile carrier. You can use a Japan eSIM on a carrier-unlocked phone only.

To check if your phone is unlocked:

  • iPhone: Settings → General → About → look for “Carrier Lock”
  • Samsung: Settings → Connections → SIM Card Manager
  • Google Pixel: Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs

If it says “SIM locked” or “Carrier locked,” contact your carrier to request an unlock before you travel.

The Universal Check: The *#06# Method

Regardless of brand or model, there’s one foolproof way to check eSIM compatibility:

  1. Open your phone’s dialer
  2. Type *#06# and press call
  3. Look for an EID number (32 digits) alongside your IMEI numbers

EID stands for Embedded Identity Document — a unique identifier for your phone’s eSIM hardware.

If you see an EID → your phone has eSIM hardware ✅ If you only see IMEI numbers → no eSIM hardware ❌

This method works on all phones — iPhone, Samsung, Google Pixel, Xiaomi, OnePlus, and every other brand.

Part 2: Band Support: The Compatibility Check Most Travellers Skip

Every guide tells you to look for an EID. Almost none tells you to check which radio bands your phone can actually hear, and that is the difference between a plan that works and a plan that works everywhere.

Japan’s low bands are not the ones your phone has

Japan carries its rural and in-building coverage on 800 MHz blocks that are specific to the country: Docomo’s Band 19 and KDDI’s Band 18, which appears in specifications as Band 26. SoftBank uses Band 8 at 900 MHz, a globally harmonised block and therefore the one most likely to be present in a handset bought anywhere else. A phone built for North America or Europe may support none of Docomo’s low band at all, leaving it on the higher 2.1 GHz and 1.8 GHz layers — which reach less far and enter buildings less well. Our network and band breakdown explains what each carrier runs; this section is about your side of it.

How to look up your own model in two minutes

Read the model number from Settings → General → About on iPhone, or Settings → About phone on Android. Search that exact model number together with the word “bands”, and read the manufacturer’s specification page rather than a retailer listing, which often omits the detail. Note what you find for LTE bands 1, 3, 8, 19 and 28, and for 5G bands n77 and n78.

What your specification shows What it means for your Japan eSIM
Band 8 and Band 28 present SoftBank-routed plans should perform well, including indoors
Band 19 present as well Docomo becomes a safe choice, including rural and mountain travel
Neither Band 19 nor Band 18 Prefer a SoftBank or KDDI plan over Docomo despite the coverage maps
n77 present You will see 5G on SoftBank, KDDI and Rakuten deployments
n79 missing Docomo’s 5G may be limited to its n78 coverage

What the 3G switch-off changed for voice

Japan has no 3G network left. au closed its 3G service in 2022, SoftBank in 2024 and Docomo in 2026, so everything now runs over 4G or 5G and voice calls use VoLTE. A 3G-only handset gets nothing at all — no data, no calls, no SMS. A handset with the right data bands but no Japanese VoLTE support can end up with working data and no ability to place a call, which matters most in an emergency. VoLTE on Japanese networks is standard from iPhone 8 onward and on recent international Android flagships, but it is worth confirming on older devices, grey-market imports and handsets bought for a single trip.

Part 3: Two eSIMs at Once: Running a Japan eSIM Beside Your Home Line

Whether your phone can use a Japan eSIM while keeping your home number alive is a hardware question, and the answer changed with the iPhone 13.

What each generation actually supports

Device Simultaneous lines What it means in practice
iPhone 13 and later Two active eSIMs, or one eSIM plus one physical SIM The physical slot is optional; both lines can be eSIM profiles
iPhone XS, XR, 11, 12 and SE (2nd gen) One active eSIM plus one physical SIM Works, but the second line needs the physical slot
eSIM-only models (US iPhone 14 onward; Japan’s iPhone 17 series) Two active eSIMs No tray exists, so both lines must be profiles
Recent Samsung Galaxy and Pixel flagships Usually one physical SIM plus one eSIM, region-dependent Verify your specific variant, since regional models differ
Older mid-range Android Often no eSIM at all Physical SIM only

Why the two-line setup is the one most travellers want

The configuration that solves verification codes is the Japan eSIM carrying data while the home line stays active purely to receive SMS. That needs two lines running at once. On an iPhone 13 or newer — including the tray-less models — both can be eSIM profiles, because these handsets store eight or more profiles and keep two active simultaneously. On an older phone you need the physical slot free for your home SIM, which is an argument for buying the Japan plan as an eSIM rather than as a card.

The setting that catches everybody out

Leave data roaming off on your home line. That single setting is what prevents the phone from treating Japan as a roaming market on your home tariff and billing you for background data. The Japan line is the opposite case: its roaming setting needs to be on, because a travel eSIM connects as a roaming profile even in the country it was bought for.

Part 9: Moving an eSIM to a New Phone

Upgrade your handset before or during a trip and the Japan eSIM does not come along automatically.

The transfer route, and its limits

Apple supports moving an eSIM between iPhones through the built-in eSIM Quick Transfer, which migrates an existing line to a new handset without involving the provider — but the mechanism requires carrier support, and travel eSIM providers differ in whether they offer it. Where it is not supported, the provider has to issue the profile again, usually through a support request rather than self-service.

The trap that costs you a plan

A travel eSIM’s QR code is normally single-use, and deleting a profile is not like uninstalling an app. If you remove the eSIM while troubleshooting and the code has already been consumed, you may need the provider to reissue it — which is why the standard advice during a connection problem is to restart the phone and check the settings before touching the profile.

Doing it before you fly, not at the gate

If you are changing phones anyway, make the move at least a week before departure. Install the Japan eSIM on the new handset, confirm the line appears in your cellular settings, and leave it switched off until you land.

Part 10: Japan-Bought Phones: What Changes

eSIM-only handsets are now the norm in Japan

Apple’s iPhone 17, 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max sold in Japan have no physical SIM tray at all, and the iPhone Air has none in any market. Buy a new iPhone in Japan and an eSIM is not a preference, it is the only route. That is convenient for travel, since the profile installs before you fly, and awkward if you were counting on picking up a physical tourist card at the airport.

Carrier-branded Android handsets are the opposite problem

Japanese carrier variants of Android phones, particularly Pixel and Galaxy models sold on a Docomo, au or SoftBank contract, commonly have eSIM disabled at the firmware level. This is not a SIM lock, and unlocking the handset does not restore eSIM. If you are buying a used phone in Japan specifically for travel eSIMs, buy a factory-unlocked international model instead of a domestic carrier SKU.

Three checks when buying used anywhere

Dial *#06# and look for an EID number, confirm the handset is carrier-unlocked, and make sure the model number matches an international variant. A phone that fails the first check cannot use a travel plan at all, however inexpensive it was, and the physical SIM route is what remains.

Japan eSIM Compatibility FAQ: Locked and Unlocked Phones

Can I use a Japan eSIM with an iPhone bought in China?

No. Most iPhone models sold in mainland China do NOT support eSIM at all. Apple physically removes the eSIM hardware from China-market devices.

Can I use a Japan eSIM with a Samsung phone from Hong Kong?

Probably not. Hong Kong variants of Samsung phones typically lack eSIM hardware. Check with *#06# — if you don’t see an EID, it won’t work.

Can I use a Japan eSIM with a Google Pixel bought in Japan?

Only if it’s a factory-unlocked international version. Japanese carrier variants (SoftBank, au/KDDI, NTT Docomo) have eSIM disabled at the firmware level. This is a permanent restriction — even after SIM unlock, eSIM remains inaccessible.

What’s the safest phone for Japan eSIM?

iPhone (international version, not China-market) or Google Pixel 6 or newer (non-Japanese carrier version) have the most reliable eSIM support.

Do I need a carrier-unlocked phone for Japan eSIM?

Yes. Your phone must be carrier-unlocked to use a Japan eSIM. Check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. “No SIM restrictions” means unlocked.

Can I use a Japan eSIM with a Japanese carrier-locked phone?

No. Japanese carrier-locked phones (SoftBank, au, Docomo) typically have eSIM disabled at the firmware level — even if the phone model supports eSIM hardware.

Does my phone need 5G for a Japan eSIM?

No. Japan eSIM works on 4G/LTE networks as well. 5G is an added benefit — Japan’s 5G networks rank among the world’s fastest in Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index, so 5G-capable devices see the biggest gains in cities.

Can I use a Japan eSIM with an iPad?

Yes! The following iPad models support eSIM: iPad Pro 11" & 13" (M4, M5), iPad Air 11" & 13" (M3, M4), iPad Mini (7th gen), and iPad (11th gen).

What if I’m not sure about my phone?

The *#06# test is the most reliable method. If an EID number appears, your phone supports eSIM. If not, it doesn’t. Still unsure? Install a try an eSIM for free — if it connects, your phone is ready for a paid Japan plan.

Can I use a Japan eSIM with a phone that has a physical SIM card slot?

Yes. Most eSIM-compatible phones support Dual SIM — one physical SIM and one eSIM active simultaneously. You can keep your home SIM for calls/SMS while using the eSIM for data.

Do I need to check my phone’s bands for Japan?

It is worth two minutes. Japan’s rural low bands, Docomo’s Band 19 and KDDI’s Band 18, are specific to the country, while SoftBank’s Band 8 is globally common. A phone without the Japanese low bands still works, but it drops signal first in tunnels, basements and rural areas.

How many eSIMs can my phone run at the same time?

From the iPhone 13 onward, two active eSIMs — or one eSIM plus a physical SIM. Earlier eSIM iPhones run one active eSIM alongside a physical SIM. Recent Galaxy and Pixel flagships generally allow one physical SIM and one eSIM together, though regional variants differ, so check your model.

Why does my phone show data but cannot make calls in Japan?

Because Japan closed its 3G networks — au in 2022, SoftBank in 2024 and Docomo in 2026 — so calls now run as VoLTE over 4G or 5G. A handset without Japanese VoLTE support can register for data and still be unable to place an ordinary call. Emergency numbers 110 and 119 should always be checked before you rely on them.

Can I transfer my Japan eSIM to a new phone?

Sometimes. iPhone-to-iPhone transfers use eSIM Quick Transfer where the provider supports it; otherwise the provider must reissue the profile. A travel eSIM’s QR code is normally single-use, so do not delete a working profile while troubleshooting — ask support instead.

Two More Japan eSIM Checks Worth Doing Before You Buy

Compatibility is one of three pre-purchase checks, and the other two are quick:

  • Network fit. For travel outside the big cities, which carrier your eSIM uses matters more than the brand - the carrier-by-carrier coverage explains the differences.
  • Whether you need a number. If a school, landlord or service insists on a Japanese number, a data-only eSIM will not provide one; the who can get a Japanese number guide covers who can get one and how.

If your phone passes the eSIM check and you only need data, you can go straight on to choosing a plan.

Final Thoughts: Your Japan eSIM Compatibility Checklist

Before buying a Japan eSIM:

  • Dial *#06# — look for an EID number
  • Check your phone is carrier-unlocked
  • Check your country of purchase (China models usually don’t work)
  • If you have a Samsung, confirm it’s not a Taiwan/Hong Kong/Korean variant
  • If you have a Google Pixel, confirm it’s not a Japanese carrier variant
  • If you have a Xiaomi/OPPO/Vivo, confirm it’s a global version

The bottom line: Most phones from 2018 onward support eSIM — but regional variations matter more than the model itself. A 5-minute check with *#06# saves you from disappointment at the airport.
