Quick Answer: Do You Need a Canadian Phone Number?

You need a Canadian number if you:

  • Register for Uber, DoorDash, or Lyft in Canada
  • Receive bank verification codes (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC)
  • Register for government services, school systems, or job applications
  • Let Airbnb hosts or hotels contact you by SMS
  • Use two-factor authentication for Canadian services

You do NOT need a Canadian number if you:

  • Only use WhatsApp, WeChat, or FaceTime over data
  • Stay for a short city trip (3–7 days)
  • Use navigation and messaging only

Canadian Number Format for eSIM

Canadian mobile numbers use North American Numbering Plan (NANP): +1 (XXX) XXX-XXXX

  • +1: country code (shared with US)
  • Area code (3 digits): 416 (Toronto), 604 (Vancouver), 514 (Montreal), 403 (Calgary), 613 (Ottawa)
  • Local number (7 digits)

When you get a Canadian eSIM number, you receive a 10-digit number (excluding +1), exactly like local Canadian numbers.

Why Numbered eSIMs Cost More

The price difference is not arbitrary. Here is what you are paying for:

  • Number allocation: A real Canadian number must be assigned from a carrier’s pool.
  • Voice and SMS network access: Standard calls and SMS require interconnection with Canadian carriers.
  • Regulatory compliance: CRTC rules, emergency services (911) support, and number portability requirements.
  • Signaling: Network signaling for call setup, SMS delivery, and roaming.
  • Customer support: Numbered eSIMs often require more support for activation and troubleshooting.

What you are NOT paying for: The data itself. A $18.90 numbered plan and a $2 data-only plan may use the same amount of data. The difference is the number. If you are undecided between the two, Roami’s Canada eSIM plans start at $1.99 so you can test data-only service before paying for a number.

Bank SMS with Canadian eSIM

Bank SMS verification is one of the main reasons travelers need a Canadian number. Here is what to know before you buy.

Which eSIM Providers Work with Bank SMS

Based on public user reports and provider documentation, Canadian numbers from CanadianSIM, ByteSIM, Airalo add-on, and Holafly are generally reported to work with major Canadian banks such as RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, and CIBC.

US numbers (such as Saily’s optional US +1) are generally not accepted by Canadian banks, because they require a Canadian area code.

VoIP-based numbers (some Holafly plans) may work for some services but are less reliable for bank SMS. Always confirm with your provider before relying on a VoIP number for bank verification.

What to Test After eSIM Activation

Once your numbered Canada eSIM is active, run these tests. Roami’s Canada eSIM includes 24/7 human support if any of them fail.

  1. Receive SMS: Ask a friend or family member to send you an SMS. Confirm it arrives.
  2. Local call: Call a local number (hotel front desk, friend). Confirm two-way audio.
  3. International call (if included): If your plan includes international minutes (CanadianSIM 1,000 min, ByteSIM 30+ countries), test a call to confirm quality and billing.

If a test fails:

  • Confirm the eSIM line is enabled.
  • Confirm the default voice line is set to the eSIM.
  • Manually select a network (Bell/Rogers/Telus).
  • Restart your phone.
  • Contact provider support.

CRTC Rules on eSIM Activation Fees

On June 12, 2026, CRTC rules took effect prohibiting carriers from charging fees for activating, modifying, or canceling mobile plans. SIM/eSIM fees “may be considered activation fees” and are banned.

What this means: If you get a numbered eSIM from a Canadian carrier (Bell, Rogers, Telus), you should not be charged extra eSIM activation or SIM fees. Telus attempted a $15 “SIM purchase fee” on June 11, 2026; CRTC sent a formal letter saying it may violate the rules. You can refuse any eSIM-related “activation fee.” See the CRTC Wireless Code.

Note: Travel eSIM provider prices (Airalo, Ubigi, etc.) are product prices, not carrier activation fees. This rule protects you when getting a number from a Canadian carrier.

What to do if you are charged a fee:

  1. Ask the carrier to explain the fee.
  2. Reference the CRTC ruling.
  3. If they refuse to remove it, file a complaint with the CCTS.

eSIM Activation, Testing & Common Mistakes

eSIM Installation vs Activation

Installation: Downloads eSIM profile. Requires Wi-Fi. Can be done before departure. eSIM appears in cellular list but does not connect, use data, or start validity clock.

Activation: Triggers when your Canada eSIM first connects to the Canadian network. Validity starts, data counts, number becomes active.

For numbered eSIMs: Number assignment usually happens at activation. You may not see the number until you land and activate. If you need the number in advance (e.g., for entry forms), contact provider support.

For full installation steps, see our eSIM installation guide.

Test Your Canadian eSIM Number

After activating a numbered Canada eSIM, run three tests:

Test 1: Local call

  • Call a local number (hotel front desk, friend)
  • Confirm two-way audio

Test 2: Receive SMS

  • Ask friend/family to send SMS
  • Confirm receipt
  • Most critical—bank codes, app registrations depend on SMS

Test 3: International call

  • If plan includes international (CanadianSIM 1,000 min, ByteSIM 30+ countries), test a call
  • Confirm quality and billing

If tests fail:

  1. Confirm eSIM line enabled and data roaming on
  2. Confirm default voice line set to eSIM
  3. Manually select network (Bell/Rogers/Telus)
  4. Restart phone
  5. Contact provider support

Can I activate before departure?

Yes, most travel numbered eSIMs (CanadianSIM, ByteSIM, Holafly, Airalo add-on) can be installed before departure and activate on landing. Local carrier prepaid (Koodo, Lucky, Chatr) usually requires arrival.

Worth knowing: Canada does not require government ID registration for prepaid SIMs — unlike Germany, Japan, or Indonesia. You can walk into a convenience store, buy a Lucky Mobile or Chatr prepaid voucher with cash, and activate without showing a passport. This makes a local prepaid SIM a viable same-day fallback if your eSIM fails on arrival, though you sacrifice the pre-departure setup that travel eSIMs offer.

Can I receive bank verification codes?

Yes, if plan includes standard SMS (CanadianSIM, ByteSIM include unlimited SMS), bank supports Canadian numbers, and eSIM is active. Confirm SMS support before buying—some “numbers” are VoIP only.

Can Saily’s US number be used in Canada?

US +1 number. Works for WhatsApp and US SMS/calls. Canadian banks/government often require Canadian area code. Uber Canada may not accept US number. For Canadian local services, choose CanadianSIM or ByteSIM.

Do I need a Canadian number for a short tourist trip?

Usually not. WhatsApp, WeChat, and FaceTime work over data. However, if you need to register for Uber Canada, receive bank SMS, or contact an Airbnb host by phone, you need a Canadian number. For short trips, an Airalo add-on or ByteSIM may be sufficient.

Can I use a numbered eSIM for two-factor authentication?

Yes, if the number supports standard SMS. CanadianSIM, ByteSIM, and Airalo add-on support standard SMS. Some VoIP-based numbers may not work with all services. Test before relying on it.

Can I keep my current number and still use a Canada eSIM?

Yes — that is the normal setup. Your home SIM keeps its number for calls, texting and one-time codes while the Canada eSIM handles data, so there is nothing to “keep” on the eSIM side. Just leave your primary line active for SMS roaming; receiving texts abroad is usually free even when calls are not.

Do Canada eSIMs support Wi-Fi calling, talk and text?

Wi-Fi calling depends on the carrier that owns the number, not on the eSIM carrying your data. A Canadian number from a local prepaid brand or a numbered travel plan can support talk and text over the cellular network; data-only eSIMs support neither and route everything through WhatsApp or FaceTime instead. If voice quality matters to you, confirm VoLTE and Wi-Fi calling support with the provider before buying — it varies more between MVNOs than between the big three networks.

Can I get a new Canadian number instead of keeping mine?

Yes. Numbered plans such as CanadianSIM, ByteSIM and PhoneBox assign a fresh Canadian +1 number at activation, which is exactly what newcomers and long-stay visitors need for local registrations. A new number will not receive SMS codes sent to your old number, so forward critical services first.
