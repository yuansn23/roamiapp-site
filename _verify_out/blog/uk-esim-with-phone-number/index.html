Why might you need a UK phone number with your eSIM?

A UK phone number is essential if you need SMS verification for banking apps, ride-hailing services, food delivery, NHS healthcare registration, or job applications during your stay in Great Britain. Data-only eSIMs can’t receive SMS, which means they can’t pass the 2FA checks that virtually every UK service runs.

Most tourists, in practice, do not need one.

If you’re in London for four days and your main activities are the Tower of London, Borough Market, and a West End show, your existing Uber account and WhatsApp will work fine over data. You don’t need a UK number for that. Where it bites you is when you’re opening a Monzo account, registering with a GP, or applying for a flat — the kind of stuff that only matters on trips longer than two weeks.

giffgaff’s goodybags run from 6 GBP to 35 GBP per month for a UK phone number with data. Data-only eSIMs from providers like Roami cost less and work well for users who don’t need to receive SMS verification codes from Uber, banks, or food delivery apps.

The catch? giffgaff’s activation can take up to several hours. Lebara typically completes within 30 minutes. If you’re arriving tomorrow, buy tonight.

Can I get a UK phone number with an eSIM?

Yes, several UK providers now offer eSIM plans that include a real UK mobile number with full calling, SMS, and data capabilities. You can get a UK number on an eSIM whether you are planning a London city break or a multi-city tour, by choosing a full-service provider like giffgaff, Lebara or Lyca Mobile. These are proper mobile numbers, not VoIP or virtual SIMs.

The UK eSIM data only vs with calling plan decision comes down to one question: do you need SMS verification for banking, Uber, and other services? Most providers fall into two camps.

Data-only plans — these give you a data connection only. No phone number, no SMS, no calls. Roami falls into this category. Many travellers find data-only is all they actually need, as long as they’re prepared for the limitations.

And let’s be real about those limitations: you can’t sign up for Deliveroo from scratch. You can’t receive that SMS code from your new Monzo account. You can’t get NHS appointment reminders. For most tourists, that’s not a problem. But it catches people out.

Full-service plans — these include a UK phone number with voice minutes, SMS, and data. Providers like giffgaff, Lebara, and Lyca Mobile all offer these, and they’re the closest thing to having a proper UK SIM without a physical card.

Full-service plans often cost more than data-only options, especially for short trips. You’ll typically need to go through some form of identity verification. The UK has no blanket statutory ID registration rule for mobile lines, but full-service providers run their own compliance checks before assigning a number.

Do I need ID to buy a UK eSIM? Usually, yes, for full-service plans. There’s no blanket UK law requiring ID registration for mobile lines, but full-service providers run their own identity checks before assigning a number — in practice you’ll generally be asked for a photo of your passport or national ID card.

For data-only plans like Roami, the process is simpler — usually just an email address and payment.

Do I need a UK address for an eSIM? No, not for tourist-friendly providers. giffgaff and Lebara let you register with your home address and a passport. Some providers may ask for a UK address, in which case your hotel or Airbnb address should work for verification.

One common issue: Lebara’s form can be finicky about address formatting, particularly with flat numbers in UK addresses. Plan for 10 extra minutes of form-fiddling if you run into validation issues.

How does a UK eSIM for Uber and essential apps work?

A UK phone number is required to create accounts on Uber, Bolt, Deliveroo, and Just Eat — and for SMS-based two-factor authentication on UK banking apps like Monzo, Starling, and HSBC. Data-only eSIMs can’t provide this, which is why a UK eSIM for Uber and public transport apps matters so much for visitors. An eSIM for the UK with a full phone number is the only reliable way to pass SMS verification for local services.

Banking and financial services

This is the most critical use case. If you’re planning to open a UK bank account, you’ll need a UK phone number for SMS verification. Almost every UK bank uses two-factor authentication via text message — Monzo, Starling, Revolut, HSBC, and Lloyds all do it. Without a UK number, you hit a wall. Even if you’re just using your existing credit card abroad, some payment systems trigger SMS verification for unusual transactions.

The Revolut exception is worth knowing: if you already have a Revolut account from home, it’ll work over data without SMS re-verification. It’s only new UK account creation that triggers the SMS requirement.

Ride-hailing and food delivery apps

Uber, Bolt, Deliveroo, Just Eat, and Uber Eats all require a phone number to create and verify your account. Your home number may not work for SMS while you’re abroad, or may charge ridiculous roaming rates. When an Uber driver needs to call you about your pickup location and your home number is unreachable, the ride gets cancelled. This isn’t a hypothetical — it’s a common traveller frustration. A UK number solves this cleanly.

NHS and healthcare access

Registering with a GP, booking an appointment, or accessing NHS services requires a UK phone number for SMS appointment reminders, test results, and prescription notifications. Even registering for NHS login requires a UK mobile number for verification. For trips longer than a few weeks, this matters.

GP registration in the UK can take over a week even with all documents ready, based on NHS processing timeframes. Plan accordingly.

Job applications and professional contact

Putting a foreign number on your CV when looking for work in the UK is a red flag for employers. A UK number makes you look local and accessible.

Public Wi-Fi and services

Many public Wi-Fi networks in UK train stations, coffee shops, libraries, and shopping centres require SMS verification to connect. Without a UK number, you’re limited to mobile data. Station WiFi networks at major London terminals, for instance, often require SMS or email verification — so having a UK number active can make the difference between connecting and going without.

What a UK eSIM Number Adds to Your Trip Cost

A UK number is a separate purchase from your data plan, and the two price lines behave in opposite ways. Data scales with the length of your trip; a UK voice plan does not, because prepaid bundles are sold in fixed 30-day blocks whether you stay a week or a month. Here is what the voice half actually costs at the published September 2026 rates:

Read the last two columns against each other. The same 5 GBP bundle costs you 71p a day on a short trip and 17p a day on a long one — a four-fold difference for an identical product. Your data line behaves the other way round: a 3-day plan stretched across a week costs more per day than a 30-day tier, because you have to buy it again.

Two consequences follow. On a short trip, treat the number as a fixed fee, not a daily rate — you are buying a month of voice service to cover seven days, which is why a 5 GBP bundle can look like a surprisingly large share of a short-trip bill. On a long stay, it is almost free, because the 30-day bundle is finally being used in full.

One waste to know about: the entry bundles all ship with data you will not use, so you are really buying the number and the data is the wrapper it comes in — no cheaper UK product sells a bare mobile number with SMS and nothing else. If the number exists purely to receive one or two verification codes, use the entry bundle as a receiver only, leave data roaming off on that line, and route all internet traffic to the data eSIM.

Can you use a data-only UK eSIM with a VoIP workaround?

Yes, VoIP services like Google Voice and Sipgate can give you a UK phone number over a data-only eSIM — but they won’t reliably receive SMS verification codes from UK banks, Uber, or the NHS, because many services flag VoIP numbers as non-mobile.

Independent testing shows consistent results: real UK mobile numbers (giffgaff) receive SMS verification from all major services including Monzo, Uber, and Deliveroo. VoIP numbers typically receive none. Zadarma and similar VoIP services receive some but not all. Your mileage may vary, but the pattern is clear: VoIP won’t cut it if SMS verification matters.

VoIP services comparison

Other VoIP providers — UK virtual numbers from various VoIP resellers, typically 2-4 GBP/month. You get a working number that can receive calls, with voicemail included. SMS reception is unreliable at best, and many services block these numbers outright.

Google Voice — Generally only available to US users. If you already have a Google Voice number from home, it’ll work over data in the UK, but it’s not a UK number.

Zadarma — UK virtual numbers from roughly 2 GBP/month. SMS support is limited.

When VoIP works: Making and receiving voice calls, voicemail, WhatsApp and iMessage registration, non-SMS two-factor apps (authenticator apps work fine).

When VoIP falls short: Bank SMS verification (almost always blocked), Uber and Deliveroo account creation, NHS services, government service logins, any SMS-based OTP system.

The bottom line: VoIP is a workaround, not a replacement. If you actually need SMS verification, get a real UK mobile number. You’ll save yourself the headache of finding out the hard way.

Option 2: Keep your home SIM + UK eSIM

Keep your home SIM active for calls and SMS while using a UK eSIM for internet. Most modern phones support dual SIM mode where your home SIM handles calls and SMS while the UK eSIM handles all data. The catch: if your home provider charges for incoming SMS while roaming, costs can add up.

Some US carriers bill SMS while roaming at per-message rates, and the costs quietly accumulate when your banking apps and ride services text you all day. Switch SMS to your UK number immediately after landing if your home carrier charges for incoming texts.

Option 3: Full-service plan only

Get a full-service plan from giffgaff or Lebara — data, calls, and a UK number all in one package. This is the simplest option and works fine for most people. But data speeds won’t match a dedicated multi-network data plan with auto-switching. Speed tests at busy London locations like King’s Cross station typically favour multi-network eSIMs (Roami), which hop to whichever carrier is least congested, over single-network eSIMs (O2 via giffgaff) tied to one carrier’s capacity. If you’re just doing email and maps, you won’t care. If you’re on Zoom calls all day, you will.

Can I keep my existing number with a UK eSIM?

You can’t port your home (non-UK) number to a UK eSIM, but you can keep your home number active through dual SIM mode — your home SIM stays in the phone for calls and SMS while your UK eSIM handles data. That’s the short answer. The longer one: number porting doesn’t cross borders, period.

Porting your home number: Number porting only works within the same country. You can’t port a US, Canadian, or Australian number to a UK mobile service. You’d have to give up your home number entirely and get a UK number instead. Don’t do that.

Dual SIM method (recommended): Keep your home SIM active (physical or eSIM) and add a UK eSIM as a second line. Your home number stays reachable for calls and SMS, while you use the UK eSIM for data and optionally a UK number. This is covered in the UK eSIM install guide.

But here’s something rarely discussed: some UK mobile networks route international calls differently, which can affect call stability on cross-border voice. It’s not a dealbreaker, but if calls are mission-critical for your trip, make a few test calls before you rely on any single line.

Virtual UK numbers for business: If you want a UK number that looks professional, some providers offer virtual UK numbers with area codes like 020 (London), 0161 (Manchester), or 0121 (Birmingham). These are usually VoIP-based and work best paired with a data plan. Same SMS limitation applies, though — most can’t receive shortcode verification texts.

What if my UK eSIM cannot receive SMS verification codes?

If your UK eSIM can’t receive SMS verification codes, first confirm your plan actually includes SMS capability — many data-only plans can’t receive SMS at all, regardless of settings. That’s the first thing to check and the most common cause.

Common causes and fixes

Is the plan actually full-service? Double-check that your plan includes SMS. Some travel plans advertise as “with phone number” but only support data and maybe voice — not SMS. This kind of misleading marketing isn’t unheard of among budget providers. Read the fine print.

Is SMS properly configured? On iPhone: Settings > Messages > Send & Receive, make sure your UK number is selected. On Android: check that your messaging app is set to use the correct SIM for SMS. In a dual SIM setup, your phone might be sending SMS from your home SIM.

Is your provider blocking shortcodes? Some budget providers route SMS differently. Certain shortcodes used by banks and apps may not reach your eSIM. If you consistently can’t receive SMS from a specific service, try switching to manual network selection, checking with your provider about shortcode support, using an authenticator app instead of SMS for 2FA, or contacting support to request voice call verification instead.

For example, verification texts from some banks send via shortcodes that don’t reach every MVNO the same way — if one specific sender never arrives, check with your provider about shortcode support before assuming the problem is your phone.

Is iMessage interfering? On iPhone, if iMessage activation is pending or stuck, SMS from other iPhone users gets delivered through iMessage rather than traditional SMS. Go to Settings > Messages and toggle iMessage off and on. Check that your UK number is listed and verified under Send & Receive.

Two-Factor Authentication: When a UK eSIM Can Skip SMS

Most of the demand for a UK number comes from two-factor authentication, and that demand is softer than it looks. SMS is the weakest of the common second factors, and a growing share of services will let you replace it with something that works over any data connection — including a data-only eSIM with no number at all. Authenticator apps are the direct substitute: a time-based code generator works entirely offline, is tied to your account rather than your SIM, and keeps working when you change number or lose a phone. Banking apps increasingly authenticate inside the app too, asking you to approve a login or payment on a device that is already signed in — a flow that needs data, not SMS.

Creating an Uber, Bolt, Deliveroo or Just Eat account Usually no One-off SMS at signup, though an existing account travels fine
Re-verifying an account you have not used for months Often no Some services fall back to SMS on re-verification

The pattern is worth internalising: SMS is needed at the moment an account is created, and much less often afterwards. If you already hold accounts with the services you rely on, a data-only eSIM will carry you through the trip. If you are moving to the UK and will be opening banking, healthcare and employment-linked accounts, a number is not optional.

That gives you one piece of homework worth doing before you fly: go through your accounts while your home number is still active and move anything that offers an authenticator app onto it. Ten minutes at home removes most of the situations where you would otherwise be stranded abroad waiting for a code that cannot arrive. Receiving a UK SMS Verification Code on an eSIM While Abroad

The single most common question about UK numbers is whether they still work once you have left. The answer is mechanical rather than mysterious: an SMS is delivered to your UK number over whichever network that line is registered on. If your UK line is switched on and roaming is enabled, it registers on a local network in whatever country you are in and the text arrives normally.

Receiving texts abroad is normally free. giffgaff’s published roaming rate tables list receiving texts at no charge both inside and outside the EU zone. Sending is a different matter — that is charged — but inbound verification codes are the case that matters here, and they cost nothing.

Your situation Will the code arrive? What to do
UK line in the phone, roaming on Yes Nothing — make sure roaming is enabled on that line in settings
UK line in the phone, roaming off No Enable roaming on the UK line, and turn data roaming off so it cannot spend
UK line left at home No, not until it is back online Ask someone to read you the code, or forward it
UK line replaced by a VoIP number Unreliable Expect shortcode verification to fail — see the VoIP section above

Two practical points follow.

Data roaming off, voice roaming on is the configuration you want. A number that has to receive a bank code needs roaming enabled, but it does not need data — turning data roaming off keeps the line reachable without letting it quietly drain its own allowance. And codes do not queue indefinitely: if a text arrives while your line is unregistered, most prepaid services will not hold it and deliver it later.

Order the code while you can still receive it. Many services let you resend a code but limit repeated attempts, so trigger verification at a moment you know your UK line is live rather than at an airport gate with the phone in your bag. If you added the number later but never used it, it may still be inside the provider’s activation window and not yet live — our UK carrier eSIM guide covers what each network’s activation actually requires.

Which plan shape do you actually need? Data-only, voice, SMS or talk and text

Most confusion about UK eSIM phone numbers comes from mixing up four different products that all get called “a plan with a number”. Sort out which shape you need and the rest of the decision collapses.

Plan shape What it includes Who needs it Realistic source
Data-only Internet, no number, no SMS, no calls Short trips, app-based communication Travel eSIM providers
Voice only Calls out, no SMS Almost nobody travelling Rare as a standalone product
SMS only Inbound texts, no data Almost nobody as a standalone buy Not sold separately in the UK
Number only A live UK number that receives SMS Anyone who needs 2FA at the lowest possible cost The voice half of a cheap MVNO plan
Talk and text Calls plus SMS, little or no data Visitors pairing voice with a data eSIM giffgaff, Lebara, Lyca
Calls and data Voice plus a data allowance, SMS included Anyone wanting one line for everything MVNO bundles, network prepaid plans

Two honest notes about that table. First, SMS-only and voice-only eSIM plans barely exist as products in the UK — they show up in search autocomplete because people are reasoning from what they want rather than what is sold. If you only need to receive verification texts, the cheapest real product is a low-cost talk-and-text plan from an MVNO, used purely for SMS while a data eSIM handles internet. Second, “number only” is a use case, not a product line: you achieve it by buying a voice plan and simply not using the voice part. Travellers asking on Reddit about a UK eSIM data-only plan that can still receive the one bank code they need are usually better served by that hybrid than by hunting for a product that does not exist.

The practical split most visitors land on: a data-only UK eSIM for connectivity plus a talk-and-text MVNO plan for the number. That configuration covers calls, SMS verification and data without paying twice for data you will not use.

UK virtual numbers, landline numbers and second numbers

There is a third option between “no number” and “full MVNO plan”, and it is the most misunderstood one.

Virtual UK numbers are VoIP numbers hosted by a service rather than a SIM. They work for outbound and inbound calls over a data connection, and they come with real UK area codes — 020 for London, 0161 for Manchester, 0121 for Birmingham. Several providers sell them from roughly 2-4 GBP per month, and for a professional-looking contact number on a CV or a business listing they do the job.

Where they fall down is SMS verification. Most virtual numbers cannot receive shortcode verification texts, which is exactly what UK banks, Uber, Deliveroo and the NHS app send. A virtual number that receives a normal text message from a friend may still reject a bank’s shortcode. If your requirement is account verification, treat a virtual number as unproven and test it before you rely on it.

UK landline numbers over eSIM are the same category. A landline-format number (020, 0117 and so on) cannot be issued on a mobile eSIM by a UK network — landline numbering is a different range with different regulation — so what you get is a VoIP landline number riding on your data connection, with the same shortcode limitations.

Second numbers and new numbers. If you want a second UK mobile number alongside your primary line, that is a talk-and-text plan, not a virtual number. If you want a brand-new UK number rather than porting anything, that is simply a fresh prepaid activation — there is no transfer step, and you buy it the same way you buy any other UK eSIM plan. Buying a UK eSIM number online means precisely this: the plan arrives with a number assigned, and the number is yours for as long as the line stays active.

One warning on number porting. You cannot port a US, Canadian, Australian or EU number onto a UK eSIM, and you cannot port a UK number onto a travel eSIM that has no number range at all. Porting only moves numbers within the same country. Keep your home line alive on dual SIM if you need your original number reachable — the UK eSIM dual SIM setup guide covers the configuration.

How to Keep a UK eSIM Number Alive After You Leave

A UK prepaid number does not sit dormant forever. Leave it long enough without activity and the provider reclaims it, and a recycled number is not recoverable — you get a new one if you come back.

The window is six months of no activity. giffgaff deactivates a SIM that has not been used for six months. Four things reset the clock: a single chargeable call, text or MMS to another number, one connection to mobile data, or one top-up or plan purchase. Receiving a call or sitting on Wi-Fi does not. giffgaff sends warning emails about a month and about five days before deactivation, so the process is hard to miss if the email on the account is still live.

Stage Window What you can still do
Active Any 6-month period Reset the clock with one call, text, data session or top-up
Warning emails sent 1 month, then 5 days, before deactivation Take any of the four actions above
Deactivated Up to 30 days after Request a PAC code and port the number to another UK provider
Reclaimed After 30 days Nothing — the number returns to the pool
Credit on the account Up to 30 days after deactivation Refundable by contacting the provider

The 30-day PAC window is the useful part and the one people miss. Even after the SIM is switched off, a UK number can still be rescued by moving it to another network — but only inside that window. Past it, the number is gone and any credit expires with it.

In practice, keeping a UK number costs about 30p a year. Set a repeating calendar reminder for roughly 170 days, and when it fires, send one text from the UK line. That single chargeable action resets the six-month clock. You do not need to keep buying bundles, and you do not need to keep the line in a phone you carry — a spare handset left on charge once in a while does the job.

One caveat worth stating plainly: these are giffgaff’s published terms, and Lebara, Lyca and the network-owned prepaid products each set their own inactivity windows and recovery rules. Read the terms of the provider you actually used rather than assuming six months.
