2026 Turkey eSIM Recommendations and Price Comparison

If you’re planning a trip to Turkey—the Hagia Sophia in Istanbul, hot air balloons in Cappadocia, the white travertines of Pamukkale, or the Mediterranean coast of Antalya—you may have noticed one key difference from other European countries: Turkey eSIM cannot be purchased upon arrival.

This is not a technical issue; it’s a policy issue.

On July 10, 2025, Turkey’s telecommunications regulator BTK (Bilgi Teknolojileri ve İletişim Kurumu) blocked the websites and apps of eight international travel eSIM providers within Turkey. Affected brands include Airalo, Holafly, Saily, Nomad, Instabridge, Mobimatter, Alosim, and BNESIM.

Key distinction: What’s blocked is the purchasing channel, not the eSIM itself.

If you’ve already installed the eSIM on your phone before departure, you can use it normally upon arrival in Turkey by turning on data roaming—just as you would in France or Spain. However, if you arrive at Istanbul Airport and remember to buy an eSIM only then, opening the Airalo app or Holafly website will fail.

This is the most fundamental difference between the Turkish eSIM market and those of France or Spain. Turkey is not covered by the EU’s “Roam Like At Home” rules, so roaming packages from your home operator typically do not include Turkey either. When choosing a Turkey eSIM package, the BTK block must be your primary consideration.

According to the GSMA Europe Mobile Report, Turkey is establishing an eSIM regulatory system independent of the EU. This article revolves around these core differences: What is Turkey’s operator network really like? Which brand offers the best coverage? How do you ensure you stay connected given the BTK block? And most importantly—the pre-departure installation checklist you must complete.

If you are weighing Airalo vs Holafly in Turkey, note that both are on BTK’s confirmed block list, so the comparison matters less than getting the profile installed before you fly. The Holafly eSIM for Turkey rides Turkcell plus Türk Telekom and is unlimited-only; Airalo rides Türk Telekom alone, which is the weaker of the two if your route runs through Cappadocia or the east.

Two searches shape most Turkey decisions. The first is the Turkey eSIM 120 day IMEI rule — foreign handsets get 120 days of network access before registration is required, and every short-trip traveller should understand which side of that line they fall on. The second is Turkey eSIM long stay: once a trip stretches past the tourist window, both the IMEI rules and the plan economics change completely, which is why a 180-day tier exists at all.

Detailed Explanation of the Turkey eSIM BTK Block Policy

What is the BTK Block and Why Does It Exist?

BTK (Bilgi Teknolojileri ve İletişim Kurumu) is Turkey’s Information and Communication Technologies Authority. As of July 10, 2025, BTK blocked access to the websites and apps of numerous international eSIM service providers within Turkey, based on Turkey’s data localization legislation.

Official reason for the block: These providers did not comply with Turkey’s data localization requirements—which mandate that user data be stored on servers physically located within Turkey.

This is not because Airalo or Holafly did something wrong, but rather because Turkey is building a regulatory system independent of the EU’s GDPR. In principle, the same wave of regulation could affect any foreign provider.

What Exactly is Blocked and What Still Works?

Separating these two aspects is very important, because “Brand X doesn’t work in Turkey” could refer to two completely different issues:

Scenario Feasible Explanation
Data on an already installed eSIM ✅ Still works The block targets the websites/apps used for purchase and management, not the eSIM profile already on your phone
Opening the app or website to purchase/top up ❌ Blocked The app will keep spinning, time out, or display “No network connection” errors

Simply put: If you arrive in Turkey with an already-installed eSIM that is providing data normally, everything is fine—you can ignore the app for the rest of your trip. The problem only arises if you need to purchase a new plan or add data while inside Turkey.

What If You Forget to Buy Before Departure?

If you arrive at the airport/on the plane and realize you haven’t bought an eSIM:

  • Option 1: Use the airport’s free WiFi to purchase and install (Istanbul Airport offers 1 hour of free WiFi)
  • Option 2: Ask family/friends back home to log in and buy for you, and forward the QR code screenshot (Note: You only need to scan the QR code for installation; no need to access the provider’s site in Turkey)
  • Option 3: Buy a local Turkcell SIM card at the airport upon arrival (requires passport + IMEI registration, queue 1-3 hours)
  • Option 4: Use international roaming as a backup (expensive, but usable in emergencies)

If you are already in Turkey and run out of data, here are practical solutions in order of priority:

  1. Connect to WiFi first: Hotel, café, or airport WiFi is not affected by the BTK block and is often sufficient to access provider websites and complete a purchase.
  2. Find a provider that is still accessible: As of September 2026, some providers’ websites and checkout pages are still accessible on Turkish networks (Note: no guarantee of permanent availability).
  3. Sort your next top-up before you fly: Screenshot the QR code and buy one tier higher than you estimate—a mid-trip top-up from inside Turkey is the one thing you cannot count on.

Best strategy: Don’t rely on these contingency plans—install before you go. Airport WiFi can be unreliable, and QR code screenshots may fail to load due to network issues.

Core conclusion: Buy enough, install it, then go. It’s better to buy a bit more than to gamble on “I’ll top up later.” The Turkey eSIM FAQ has more details on the BTK block.

Turkey 5G Commercial Rollout Progress (2026 Update)

Turkey completed its $2.95 billion 5G spectrum auction at the end of October 2025, with all three operators acquiring frequencies. Commercial 5G services are expected to launch in April 2026, starting with Istanbul, Ankara, and Izmir.

Practical impact for travelers: 5G coverage is still limited. Your travel eSIM should be judged primarily on reliable 4G/LTE and network coverage, not “5G” marketing.

Key advice: For Cappadocia, eastern provinces, or rural road trips, you must choose an eSIM that connects to Turkcell’s network. For Istanbul + western coast only, Vodafone or Türk Telekom are sufficient. can help you quickly lock in the most suitable choice.

Turkish Holiday Network Performance

During Ramadan Feast (Şeker Bayramı), Sacrifice Feast (Kurban Bayramı), and Republic Day (October 29), local user traffic surges in Turkey, and networks at popular tourist sites (Blue Mosque, Grand Bazaar, Cappadocia) may become congested. An eSIM with automatic multi-network switching (Roami) has a clear advantage during congestion—single-operator eSIMs may become completely unconnectable during peak times.

Turkey eSIM Questions & Answers

Q1: Do I need a passport or ID for a Turkey eSIM?

No. Travel eSIMs purchased abroad operate under international roaming agreements; no identity verification is required for purchasing and activating a data-only plan. This differs from local Turkish SIM cards, which require passport registration and IMEI registration. If you need to receive SMS verification codes or make calls, keep your home physical SIM card and enable international roaming. More answers in the Turkey eSIM FAQ.

Q2: Can I really not buy an eSIM in Turkey?

Yes. As of July 10, 2025, BTK blocked access to the websites and apps of eight international eSIM providers within Turkey, including major brands like Airalo, Holafly, Saily, and Nomad. You must complete the purchase and installation of your eSIM before departure. This is not a suggestion—it’s a hard requirement.

Q3: Will the eSIM still work normally after I arrive in Turkey?

Yes. The BTK block targets purchasing channels, not the eSIM technology itself. As long as you’ve installed the eSIM profile on your phone before departure, it will connect to the local network automatically within 2 minutes of turning on data roaming upon arrival in Turkey—no different from using it in France or Spain.

Q4: Can I open the Airalo/Holafly/Saily app while in Turkey?

Most likely not. The app will keep spinning, time out, or display “No network connection” errors. This is because BTK has blocked these brands’ domains at the DNS level. If you need to check remaining data or purchase a new plan, complete all operations before departure.

Q5: What should I do if I run out of data in Turkey? What are the practical solutions?

Here are the solutions in order of success rate:

  1. Connect to WiFi to purchase: Hotel, café, or airport WiFi is not affected by the BTK block—connect and you can normally access provider websites to purchase a new plan
  2. Find a provider that is still accessible: As of September 2026, some providers’ websites and checkout pages are still accessible on Turkish networks (Note: no guarantee of permanent availability)
  3. Pre-load a larger tier before departure: Both the store and the top-up pages sit behind the same blocked domains, so carrying extra data is more dependable than any workaround.
  4. Ask family/friends back home to buy for you: Have them log into your account and purchase, then send you the new QR code (requires scanning to install)

Best solution: Buy enough data before departure to avoid topping up mid-trip in Turkey.

Q6: Which operator has the best coverage in Turkey?

Turkcell. Its 4G population coverage reaches 98%+, median download speed 67.4 Mbps, and it’s often the only operator with signal in eastern Turkey, Cappadocia’s mountainous areas, and rural regions. If your itinerary extends beyond Istanbul, choosing an eSIM that connects to Turkcell’s network is the safest bet. Vodafone performs well along the western coast (Antalya, Bodrum), and Türk Telekom is usable in major cities but weaker in rural areas.

Q7: Can Airalo, Holafly, Saily, and Ubigi be used in Turkey?

The eSIMs themselves can be used, but the websites and apps of Airalo, Holafly, Saily, and Nomad have been blocked by BTK (Ubigi is not on the confirmed eight-brand list, though restrictions can change). Key distinction: If you’ve already installed them before departure, data services work normally upon arrival; if you wait until you’re in Turkey to buy, you won’t be able to access the blocked brands’ services. Roami is currently not confirmed to be on the block list, but we recommend installing it before departure as well—don’t take the risk.

Key distinction: Travel eSIMs purchased abroad use roaming agreements, so short-term travelers are not affected by IMEI registration.
