Turkey eSIM Ban & Availability: BTK Rules, IMEI Risks & What Works

eSIM is legal in Turkey, but the government’s BTK block can still stop you buying or managing a plan after you land. This guide explains what is actually banned, what the IMEI rule means, and what still works.

Turkey’s eSIM Rules at a Glance

  • eSIM is legal in Turkey, and the BTK block only targets foreign provider websites and apps, not installed profiles.
  • A pre-installed eSIM connects normally once you land.
  • The IMEI 120-day rule affects long-stay phones, not tourists on a short trip.
  • Buy and install your eSIM before you arrive, because you cannot rely on the provider app inside Turkey.

What This Turkey eSIM Ban Guide Solves

This guide solves the regulatory layer: what actually determines whether your Turkey eSIM will work, and what you can legally do about it. It does not repeat installation steps, device checks, provider comparisons or pricing. Those are covered in the linked deep-dive articles.

Turkey has two separate regulatory tracks. They operate independently. A traveller can comply with one and still be caught by the other:

  1. BTK platform block — blocks foreign eSIM provider websites and apps from Turkish mobile networks.
  2. IMEI registration rule — blocks foreign phones from Turkish networks after 120 days.

The practical takeaway: a pre-installed Turkey eSIM works normally after landing. The block targets buying and managing eSIMs, not using them.

What Exactly Is the BTK eSIM Platform Block?

The BTK block is a platform block, not a technology ban. It targets foreign eSIM provider websites and apps from Turkish mobile networks. It does not disable eSIM hardware or stop installed profiles from connecting.

What the BTK Actually Regulates

The BTK is Turkey’s Information and Communication Technologies Authority. It regulates electronic communications, service registration, licensing and platform compliance. Its blocking decisions target foreign eSIM provider websites and apps, not eSIM technology itself. The BTK’s authority comes from Law No. 5809 on Electronic Communications. Foreign eSIM providers without Turkish operating licenses fall under this enforcement.

The Three BTK Decision Numbers

The blocking decisions were issued in three waves. Three waves over two months indicate a deliberate regulatory process, not a one-time technical action.

Decision number Date Scope
E-98966759-450.08-36681 July 10, 2025 First wave — initial provider list
E-98966759-450.08-37512 July 17, 2025 Second wave — expanded list
E-98966759-450.08-48093 September 15, 2025 Third wave — additional providers

Why the Block Happened

The BTK’s stated rationale: foreign eSIM providers were operating without Turkish licensing and without complying with local rules on identity verification, data retention and lawful interception. The block is framed as enforcement against unlicensed operators, not as a restriction on eSIM technology or Turkish citizens’ ability to use eSIMs.

How DNS Enforcement Works

The BTK enforces the block at the DNS level. When a device on a Turkish mobile network tries to resolve a blocked provider’s domain, the DNS query returns no valid result. The website does not load. The app cannot reach its backend.

An installed eSIM profile does not depend on DNS resolution. The profile is stored on the device’s eUICC chip. It connects to Turkcell, Vodafone or Türk Telekom through the carrier’s own network infrastructure. The provider’s domain is not involved in the data session. The GSMA SGP.22 specification defines how this provisioning works independently of the provider’s website.

What the Block Does Not Touch

  • Does not disable eSIM hardware
  • Does not stop installed eSIM profiles from connecting
  • Does not stop Turkish operators from selling eSIMs locally
  • Does not affect Wi-Fi using non-BTK DNS
  • Does not prevent emergency calls (112)
  • Does not affect roaming profiles from foreign carriers

What the Block Does Target

  • Blocks provider websites on Turkish mobile networks
  • Blocks provider apps on Turkish mobile networks
  • Blocks new purchases from blocked providers inside Turkey
  • Blocks top-ups and account management through blocked platforms
  • Blocks in-app support chats that rely on the provider’s backend

How Does the Turkish IMEI Rule Affect Tourists?

The IMEI rule is separate from the BTK block. It applies to foreign phones used on Turkish networks for more than 120 days. A two-week tourist never reaches this threshold. Long-stay visitors and digital nomads must plan for it.

How the 120-Day IMEI Clock Starts

When a foreign phone first connects to a Turkish mobile network, the BTK logs its IMEI and starts a 120-day countdown. The BTK sends a warning SMS. That SMS date is the official start.

After 120 days, an unregistered device is blocked from all Turkish mobile networks. It still works on Wi-Fi. It cannot make calls, send SMS or use mobile data on any Turkish carrier.

The 240-Day eSIM Extension Is Closed (May 2026)

Before May 2026, a dual-SIM or eSIM phone could stretch the IMEI clock to 240 days by using each SIM slot for a separate 120-day window. The BTK closed this loophole effective 1 May 2026. A foreign phone now gets a single 120-day clock, regardless of how many SIM slots it has.

IMEI Registration Fee and Process in Turkey

The 2026 IMEI registration fee is 54,258 Turkish Lira, approximately US$1,200. Payment is made through the e-Devlet IMEI registration portal. After payment, a registration application must be submitted and approved. There is also a frequency restriction. If you have previously registered a phone, you usually need to wait at least three calendar years before registering another.

What Happens After the IMEI Is Blocked

  • The device cannot make calls, send SMS or use mobile data on any Turkish carrier.
  • It still works on Wi-Fi.
  • It still functions as a camera, map device and messaging tool over Wi-Fi.
  • Emergency calls (112) may still work on some networks.
  • The block applies to the IMEI, not the SIM or eSIM. Removing and reinserting the SIM does not help.

Does a Travel eSIM Trigger the IMEI Clock

A travel eSIM from an international provider operates through roaming. The BTK clock is typically triggered by inserting a Turkish SIM into a foreign device. An international eSIM profile usually avoids this trigger.

If you also use a local Turkish SIM for a local number, that will trigger the IMEI clock. The eSIM and physical SIM share the same IMEI. If the IMEI is later blocked, both stop working on Turkish networks.

The e-Devlet IMEI Registration Process

  1. Create an e-Devlet account (requires Turkish ID or residence permit).
  2. Navigate to the IMEI registration section.
  3. Pay the registration fee (54,258 TL in 2026).
  4. Submit the registration application.
  5. Wait for approval.
  6. The IMEI becomes permanently registered.

For short-stay tourists, this process is irrelevant. The 120-day clock will not run out during a two-week holiday. For anyone staying longer than four months, IMEI registration or a locally purchased phone becomes a planning necessity.

FCDO Advisory on eSIM Access

The UK FCDO has updated its Turkey travel advice to include eSIM restrictions. This confirms the block is a sustained regulatory action, not a temporary glitch. The advisory recommends downloading, configuring and activating your eSIM before departure.

What the FCDO Actually Says

The advisory states that access to some independent, international eSIM services is restricted within Turkey. It warns that British holidaymakers may not be able to activate certain new eSIMs after landing — so far the only official travel warning to British travelers that names eSIM activation specifically. The recommendation is specific: ensure your eSIM is fully downloaded, configured and activated while still connected to UK networks before departure. It is also the clearest external acknowledgment of how Turkey’s eSIM regulation now reads from outside the country. See the UK FCDO Turkey travel advice.

What the FCDO Advisory Does Not Say

  • It does not say eSIMs are banned in Turkey.
  • It does not say Turkey is unsafe for connected travel.
  • It does not say travellers should avoid Turkey.
  • It does not say the restriction applies to all eSIM services.
  • It does not say the block affects installed eSIM profiles.

Why the FCDO Update Matters

The FCDO update confirms two things:

  1. The BTK block is a sustained regulatory action, not a temporary glitch.
  2. The UK government treats this as traveller-protection information.

The US State Department advisory does not currently include eSIM-specific guidance. The GSMA publishes the SGP.22 eSIM specification but does not issue travel advisories.

Quick Recap: Staying Within the Rules

You have now covered the BTK platform block, the IMEI clock, and the FCDO travel advisory. The conclusion is that the block stops you buying and managing an eSIM, not using one. Next, we look at what VPNs and DNS can and cannot fix, and the legal ways to stay connected.

VPN and DNS Workarounds in Turkey

A VPN can restore access to blocked provider websites. A DNS change can work on Wi-Fi you control. Neither is a substitute for pre-departure installation. The reliable fix is to buy and install before departure.

The DNS Bypass: When It Works

The BTK block is enforced at the DNS level. It can be bypassed by using a DNS server not controlled by the BTK. Google DNS (8.8.8.8) and Cloudflare DNS (1.1.1.1) are common alternatives.

How to change DNS on iPhone:

  1. Open Settings → Wi-Fi.
  2. Tap the (i) icon next to your Wi-Fi network.
  3. Tap Configure DNS → Manual.
  4. Add 8.8.8.8 or 1.1.1.1.
  5. Save and reconnect.

How to change DNS on Android:

  1. Open Settings → Network & Internet.
  2. Tap Private DNS.
  3. Enter dns.google or 1dot1dot1dot1.cloudflare-dns.com.
  4. Save.

Limitations: DNS bypass only works on Wi-Fi. It does not work on Turkish mobile networks, where the carrier enforces the BTK DNS. It also requires you to control the Wi-Fi network’s DNS settings, which you cannot do at most hotels and airports.

The VPN Bypass: What It Restores

A VPN routes your traffic through a server outside Turkey, which restores access to blocked provider websites and apps.

How to set up a VPN:

  1. Install a VPN app before departure.
  2. Subscribe to a reputable VPN service.
  3. Test the VPN on Wi-Fi before you fly.
  4. After landing, connect to the VPN over Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Limitations:

  • VPNs consume battery and may slow your connection.
  • Some VPN protocols are blocked or throttled in Turkey.
  • A VPN does not restore account management if the provider’s app requires a direct connection.
  • A VPN does not restore in-app support chats.
  • A VPN does not help if your eSIM profile was never installed.

What a VPN Cannot Fix

  • A carrier-locked phone
  • A missing eUICC chip
  • A QR code that was already used
  • A provider backend that is down
  • A refund dispute with a blocked provider

What a VPN Can Fix

  • Accessing the provider’s website to check your account
  • Accessing the provider’s website to top up
  • Accessing email support
  • Accessing web-based chat support
  • Reading help documentation

Myth: Using an eSIM in Turkey Is Illegal

FAQ: Turkey eSIM Ban and IMEI

Is eSIM banned in Turkey?

No. eSIM technology is legal and operational. The BTK blocked access to websites and apps of more than 50 international eSIM providers from inside Turkey.

What is a platform block?

A platform block prevents access to specific websites and apps. A technology ban would prevent eSIM profiles from working on Turkish networks. Turkey has implemented a platform block.

What are the official BTK decision numbers?

The three blocking decisions are E-98966759-450.08-36681 (July 10, 2025), E-98966759-450.08-37512 (July 17, 2025) and E-98966759-450.08-48093 (September 15, 2025).

Which providers are blocked?

Airalo, Holafly, Saily, Nomad, Ubigi, GigSky, Flexiroam, Yesim, Jetpac, Maya Mobile, Roamless, BNESIM, Alosim, Instabridge, Mobimatter and many others. More than 50 providers are confirmed blocked.

Which providers are not blocked?

Klook is one of the few providers not on the confirmed BTK block list. The non-blocked list changes frequently, so do not rely on it as a long-term strategy.

Can I use my eSIM after landing?

Yes, if it was installed before departure. Pre-installed eSIMs connect normally. The block targets purchasing and managing eSIMs, not using them.

Can I buy a Turkey eSIM after arriving?

For most blocked providers, no. Airport Wi-Fi is inconsistent. Airport SIM counters are expensive and require passport registration. Klook may be accessible as a backup.

Can I top up from inside Turkey?

No, not if your provider is blocked. Buy enough data upfront to cover your entire trip.

Does the BTK block affect Wi-Fi?

The block is enforced at DNS level on Turkish mobile networks. Hotel and airport Wi-Fi using Google DNS or Cloudflare DNS may bypass the filter. This is not reliable.

Can a VPN bypass the block?

A VPN can route traffic outside Turkey and restore website access. It does not restore account management, top-ups or support. It is a workaround, not a primary solution.

What is the 120-day IMEI rule?

Foreign phones used on Turkish networks for more than 120 days must be registered with the BTK or be blocked from Turkish mobile networks. The 2026 fee is 54,258 TL, about US$1,200.

Does a travel eSIM trigger the IMEI clock?

A travel eSIM from an international provider operates through roaming and typically does not trigger the IMEI clock. A local Turkish SIM does trigger it.

What was the 240-day eSIM extension?

It was a loophole that let dual-SIM and eSIM phones stretch the IMEI clock to 240 days by using each SIM slot for a separate 120-day window. The BTK closed it effective 1 May 2026, so a foreign phone now gets a single 120-day clock.

What is the IMEI registration fee?

The fee is 54,258 Turkish Lira, approximately US$1,200. Payment is made through e-Devlet, followed by an application and approval.

Does the IMEI rule apply to eSIM-only phones?

Yes. Any foreign phone that connects to a Turkish mobile network is subject to the IMEI rule. A travel eSIM from an international provider does not trigger the clock.

What does the FCDO say about Turkey eSIMs?

The FCDO warns that access to some international eSIM services is restricted within Turkey. It advises travellers to download, configure and activate their eSIM before departure.

Are local Turkish eSIMs affected by the block?

No. Local Turkish eSIMs from Turkcell, Vodafone Turkey and Türk Telekom are sold domestically and are not affected by the block on foreign platforms.

Is Turkey eSIM illegal?

No. Using an eSIM in Turkey is not illegal. The restrictions apply to unlicensed foreign eSIM platforms operating within Turkish networks.

Is the Turkey eSIM block permanent?

The block has been in effect since July 2025 and has expanded through multiple BTK decisions. As of mid-2026, there is no indication it will be lifted.

What happens if I arrive without an eSIM?

You can use airport Wi-Fi, try a non-blocked provider, or buy a local SIM at an airport counter. All options are less convenient and more expensive than buying before departure.

What happens if my IMEI is blocked?

The device cannot make calls, send SMS or use mobile data on any Turkish carrier. It still works on Wi-Fi. Emergency calls (112) may still work on some networks. The block applies to the IMEI, not the SIM or eSIM.

Can I register my IMEI if I’m only staying for two weeks?

You do not need to. The 120-day clock will not run out during a two-week trip. IMEI registration is only relevant for stays longer than 120 days.

Does the eSIM block apply to cruise ship passengers?

Cruise ships typically use satellite internet. When docked in Turkish ports, a pre-installed travel eSIM connects normally. For Greek island stops, a regional eSIM covering both Turkey and Greece is recommended. See the regional eSIM country list guide.

Can I use a Turkey eSIM on a locked phone?

No. A carrier-locked phone cannot install a third-party eSIM profile. You must unlock the phone first. See the EID and carrier lock check guide.

Can I use a Europe eSIM in Turkey?

Only if the plan explicitly includes Turkey in its country list. Many “Europe” eSIMs exclude Turkey. Nomad’s 36-country Europe plan includes Turkey; its 35-country plan does not.

Does Turkey eSIM support 5G?

Yes. Turkey launched commercial 5G on April 1, 2026. Early 5G speeds averaged 201 Mbps. Turkcell leads on 5G speed. Coverage is still limited outside major cities. For real-world 5G data, see OpenSignal’s speed measurements.

What is the difference between the BTK block and the IMEI rule?

The BTK block restricts access to foreign eSIM provider platforms. The IMEI rule restricts device network access after 120 days. They are separate regulatory tracks with different enforcement mechanisms.

Is the BTK block applied to all eSIM providers?

No. More than 20 providers remain accessible from Turkish networks as of mid-2026. The block is not comprehensive. However, the list changes frequently, so it is not a reliable strategy for advance planning.

What is the best way to stay connected for a long stay?

For long stays, a local Turkish SIM for voice/SMS plus a travel eSIM for data is the best combination. Monitor the 120-day IMEI clock and plan for registration if you extend your stay.
