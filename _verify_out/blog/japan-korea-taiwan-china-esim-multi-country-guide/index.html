If you’re departing from Taiwan for Japan, most regional eSIMs can be activated before you leave — but check the provider’s policy. Some eSIMs only activate when they detect a local network in the destination country. Others activate immediately upon installation.

4. China and Japan eSIM: The Trickiest Region to Get Right

China is the most challenging destination in East Asia for eSIM travelers. The Great Firewall blocks Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, and most Western apps — and many eSIM providers either don’t cover China at all or only provide access to Chinese apps through domestic networks. China-related connectivity questions dominate eSIM troubleshooting threads on Reddit’s r/eSIM community.

The China Connectivity Problem

The mobile networks across China are genuinely world-class. China Mobile, China Unicom, and China Telecom have 5G coverage in every major city and most rural areas. The problem is getting a SIM card that actually works seamlessly with Western apps, especially when China’s heavily restricted internet and separate network requirements come into play.

Here’s what makes China different:

  • The Great Firewall: Most Western apps are blocked without a VPN. Google Maps, WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, and even Gmail don’t work on domestic Chinese networks.
  • Network restrictions: Many international eSIMs don’t work in mainland China at all because they don’t have agreements with Chinese carriers.
  • Hong Kong and Macau are different: They operate on separate networks with access to the open internet. An eSIM that works in Hong Kong won’t necessarily work in mainland China.

How Regional eSIMs Work

Regional eSIMs use a single digital profile that connects to partner networks in each country. When you cross a border, the eSIM automatically switches to the local partner network. There’s no need to manually switch profiles or install a new eSIM.

Daily vs Total Data Plans

Regional eSIMs come in two flavors:

Daily Plans: You get a certain amount of high-speed data per day (e.g., 500MB, 1GB, 2GB). After that, speeds drop to 128kbps and reset at midnight.

Total Plans: You get a fixed amount of data for the entire validity period (e.g., 3GB, 5GB, 10GB). After that, speeds drop to 128kbps.

Which to choose? Daily plans are better if you use consistent amounts of data each day. Total plans are better if you have heavy usage days and light usage days.

The “Short Stay” Problem

For multi-country trips with very short stays in each country, the economics change. If you’re spending only 2–3 days in Korea and 5–7 days in Japan, separate plans can be cheaper because you only pay for the shortest duration plan in each country.

Example: A 2-day Korea plan at ~$5 + a 7-day Japan plan at ~$15 = $20 total, vs a 7-day regional plan at ~$28.

Rule of thumb: If you’re spending 5+ days in each country, a regional plan is usually cheaper. If you’re hopping between countries every 2–3 days, separate plans may offer better value.

8. Multi-Country Asia eSIM FAQ

Q: Which is the best eSIM for Japan and Korea? A: Ubigi offers the best network quality with local carrier direct connections. Holafly is best for unlimited data at about $28 for 7 days. Nomad offers the best value at ~$15 for 10GB.

Q: What’s the best eSIM for Taiwan and Japan? A: HelloRoam’s East Asia eSIM covers both at CA$5.79 (~$4.20). Airalo’s Asia-Pacific plan covers 22+ countries including both.

Q: Can I use a Japan eSIM in China? A: No. Most Japan-specific eSIMs don’t work in China. You need a regional Asia eSIM or a dedicated China-Japan-Korea plan like eSIM-Now or Nomad’s CJK bundle.

Q: Do I need a VPN for China with an eSIM? A: It depends on the provider. eSIM-Now routes traffic through Hong Kong, so Google, WhatsApp, and Instagram work without a VPN. Other providers may require a separate VPN to access Western apps.

Q: Does a regional Asia eSIM work in Hong Kong and Macau? A: Many do, but check the coverage list carefully. Hong Kong and Macau are often listed separately from mainland China. Holafly offers a dedicated Hong Kong, Macau & China plan.

Q: Is it cheaper to buy one regional eSIM or separate plans? A: For trips with 5+ days in each country, a regional eSIM is usually cheaper. For short stays (2–3 days per country), separate plans may offer better value.

Q: Can I hotspot from a regional eSIM? A: Yes, most allow hotspot/tethering. KKday’s Asia eSIM supports hotspot sharing. Revo eSIM also allows hotspot sharing. Check your specific plan’s terms.

Q: Which network does a Japan-Korea eSIM use in each country? A: Typically KDDI/SoftBank in Japan and SK Telecom in South Korea. Some providers also support NTT Docomo in Japan.

Q: How much data do I need for a multi-country Asia trip? A: Light users (messaging, maps): 1–3GB per week. Moderate users (+social media): 5GB per week. Heavy users (+streaming, video calls): 10GB+ per week.

Q: Can I buy a regional eSIM after arriving in Asia? A: Yes, most eSIMs can be purchased and installed after arrival, as long as you have a WiFi connection. However, it’s recommended to install before departure to avoid any issues.

Q: What if my phone doesn’t support eSIM? A: You’ll need to buy physical SIM cards in each country or rent a pocket WiFi device. Physical SIMs are available at airports in Japan, Korea, Taiwan, and China.

Your 5-Step Action Plan

  1. Map your itinerary: List every country you’re visiting. Count the days in each. This determines whether a regional plan or separate plans are more cost-effective.

  2. Check coverage: Verify that your chosen eSIM covers all destinations. Pay special attention to China — not all regional eSIMs work there. Confirm Taiwan is listed separately if you’re visiting.

  3. Calculate data needs: Estimate your daily usage (maps + messaging = 200–500MB; +social media = 500MB–1GB; +streaming = 1–3GB). Add 20% buffer.

  4. Compare costs: Run the math — one regional plan vs separate country plans. For 5+ days per country, regional wins. For 2–3 days per country, separate plans may be cheaper.

  5. Purchase and install before departure: Install the eSIM at home on WiFi. Test that it’s installed correctly. Save the QR code and activation details somewhere offline (screenshot or printout).
