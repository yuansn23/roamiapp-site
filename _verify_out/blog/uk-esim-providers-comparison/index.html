How does Airalo’s UK eSIM perform on EE’s network?

Airalo is the best-known travel eSIM brand by a wide margin. They were one of the first to market, they’ve got the biggest app store presence, and they sponsor what feels like half the travel content on the internet. An Airalo eSIM for UK trips is the default choice for many first-time visitors, but being the biggest doesn’t always mean being the best. Not even close, actually.

Airalo UK network: EE, but only 4G

Airalo’s UK eSIM connects to the EE network. That’s a good start — EE has the strongest rural coverage of any UK network, according to Ofcom’s coverage data. But there’s a catch Airalo doesn’t always make obvious: it’s 4G only, not 5G. This Airalo UK eSIM review confirms that while EE provides strong rural reach, the 4G-only limitation means you miss out on 5G speeds in UK cities. By contrast, an England eSIM for travel with 5G access and automatic carrier switching across multiple networks avoids the congestion issues that single-network 4G plans can face in busy urban areas.

This limitation matters less than you’d think. 4G in the UK is perfectly fast for everything you’re likely to do — maps, social media, streaming, video calls. EE’s 4G network in most UK cities delivers 30-80 Mbps, which is plenty. But if you’re the type who wants the fastest possible speeds and you’ve got a 5G phone, the difference is noticeable.

The bigger concern? Being tied to a single network. You only get EE’s coverage. That’s good in rural Great Britain, but in UK cities, EE can get congested, and you can’t switch to a less busy network. You’re stuck.

Airalo app experience

Airalo’s app is polished and easy to use. The installation process is straightforward: buy the plan, scan the QR code or install via the app, and the UK eSIM profile lands on your phone. It works on both iOS and Android with clear instructions. The app keeps track of your remaining data, which is handy. You can top up from within the app if you run out. The top-up process is seamless — buy more data, and it’s added to your existing eSIM profile without reinstallation.

Airalo customer support

Customer support is where Airalo falls apart. Support is primarily through email and an in-app chat system that’s often glacially slow. Response times typically range from 2 to 24 hours. There’s no phone support, no live chat guarantee, and community forums are limited.

For a simple activation, this doesn’t matter. For a problem in the middle of your trip, it’s a real issue. If you’re relying on your eSIM for maps and communication, a support team that takes 24 hours to reply isn’t just inconvenient — it’s a liability.

In city-centre locations, users on EE 5G (via providers like Ubigi) typically see 80-100 Mbps, while the same EE network at 4G (via Airalo) delivers 35-50 Mbps. Same network, different speed tier. That gap matters if you’re uploading photos or doing video calls.

Airalo verdict

Best for: Budget-conscious travellers with simple needs, especially those visiting UK cities and well-covered areas. Not for: Heavy data users, anyone needing rural reliability, anyone who wants phone support. Overall: Good value, solid app, but the 4G-only limitation and weak support hold it back.

If you’re spending under $10 on a short city trip, Airalo is perfectly fine. If you’re spending more than that or heading anywhere rural, look elsewhere. The low entry price is appealing, but it comes with real compromises that the marketing doesn’t mention.

Is Holafly’s UK eSIM unlimited data worth it?

Holafly positions itself as the premium option with a laser focus on unlimited data. Their marketing heavily pushes the idea that you never need to worry about running out. It’s a compelling message, especially for heavy data users. This Holafly UK unlimited data review finds the unlimited plans genuinely deliver on the promise — with one massive asterisk: no EE network access, which guts rural usefulness.

Holafly UK network: O2, Vodafone, and Three

Holafly’s UK connectivity is a bit complicated and has changed over time. Based on current information, Holafly uses O2 as its primary UK network partner, with the ability to roam onto Vodafone and Three in some areas. The exact network mix seems to vary by plan and region.

The critical thing: Holafly does NOT include EE. This is a significant gap. Independent drive-testing confirms EE has the widest geographic coverage, making its absence notable for rural travel. O2 has decent city coverage but falls away quickly in the countryside, especially in Scotland, the Lake District, and Snowdonia. Holafly’s O2-based connection can drop to single-digit Mbps — or no service at all — along popular hiking routes like Helvellyn.

Don’t count on 5G here: Holafly’s UK unlimited plans run on 4G in practice, which puts them behind Ubigi’s Three 5G in urban speed terms. In London you’ll see 4G-level throughput rather than the 100+ Mbps that 5G-capable rivals deliver.

Is Holafly’s unlimited data actually unlimited?

Broadly, yes. There’s a fair use policy, but it’s generous enough that most users won’t encounter throttling during normal use.

The catch: Holafly’s unlimited data only works on 4G. Despite supporting 5G on their network partners, the unlimited plans are limited to 4G speeds — dependably good enough for HD video streaming in UK cities, but you won’t get the 100+ Mbps 5G speeds you might get from a direct network plan. Selling an “unlimited premium” plan without 5G in 2026 feels like a strange choice.

Also worth noting: hotspot tethering on Holafly’s unlimited plans is capped (around 1 GB/day under the current fair-use policy) rather than blocked outright. Enough for an emergency laptop session, not enough to work from it all day.

Holafly app and experience

Holafly’s app is clean and professional. Installation is straightforward, though there can be occasional hiccups with the automatic activation. Their instructions are clear and they’ve got guides for most phone models.

One differentiator: Holafly gives you a Spanish phone number with your eSIM. This is unusual for a travel eSIM provider, and it means you can make and receive calls (at international rates). Most users won’t use this, but it’s there if you need it. Not a UK number, though — a Spanish one. Useful in a pinch, but not the local number most people would prefer.

Holafly customer support

Holafly offers 24/7 live chat support, which is better than providers that rely on email-only support. Support quality is generally decent. This is a genuine advantage over Airalo’s 2-24 hour response times.

That said, the unlimited data policy means most users never need to contact support. The plan either works or it doesn’t, and since there’s no data cap to worry about, there’s less need to top up or manage your plan.

Holafly verdict

Not for: Rural travellers, anyone visiting Scotland or national parks, budget-conscious users, anyone who needs hotspot tethering. Overall: Unlimited is genuinely useful, but the lack of EE, the 4G-only cap on unlimited plans, the blocked hotspot, and the high prices for limited plans stack up to a product that’s great for a narrow audience and frustrating for everyone else.

How does Ubigi’s UK eSIM with EE and Three coverage perform?

Ubigi is the UK eSIM arm of Transatel, a French company that provides mobile connectivity solutions for businesses. When evaluating a UK eSIM with multi-network switching provider, the number of partner networks directly affects your coverage reliability — dual-network plans like Ubigi’s offer a clear step up from single-network options. They’ve grown their consumer eSIM business significantly and have a strong presence in the UK market.

Ubigi UK network: EE + Three

Ubigi’s UK connectivity is its strongest card. They partner with both EE and Three, which means you get coverage from the UK’s best rural network (EE) and its fastest urban network (Three). On top of that, Ubigi supports 5G on both networks. The Ubigi UK eSIM EE Three coverage combination gives it a significant advantage over single-network providers, especially for travellers who visit both UK cities and rural Great Britain.

In practice, this means you’ll have EE’s extensive rural coverage in national parks and the Highlands, plus Three’s speed in UK cities. It’s not automatic switching in the way that Roami operates (you might need to manually select networks), but having two networks available gives you better coverage than any single-network provider. Users may find themselves switching manually — EE for intercity train routes, Three for walking around city centres. It’s an extra step, but it’s worth it.

Ubigi app experience

Ubigi’s app is one of the better ones in this space. The interface is clear, installation is straightforward — all modern iPhones (XS onwards) and Android flagships support eSIM as confirmed by Apple’s eSIM support page — and data tracking works reliably. They also have a web portal if you prefer managing your plan from a computer.

The installation process uses eSIM profiles, which means each plan you buy adds a new profile to your phone. This is standard, but it means if you buy a new plan after exhausting your first one, you’ll need to install a second profile. Some providers handle top-ups within the same profile, which is simpler. Minor annoyance.

Ubigi also offers a referral programme and occasional promo codes, which can bring the price down further.

Ubigi customer support

Support is available through in-app chat and email. Response times are decent during European business hours but can slow down outside those times. They don’t offer phone support.

The quality of support is generally good. Ubigi has been in the connectivity business for a long time (since before eSIM was a consumer product), and their support team knows their stuff. For technical issues, they’re better than most.

How does Nomad’s UK eSIM on Three perform for budget travellers?

Nomad is a newer player in the UK eSIM space, positioning itself as a budget-friendly alternative to the bigger names. A Nomad eSIM for UK city breaks offers competitive pricing, though with some trade-offs in coverage that you need to understand before buying. This Nomad UK eSIM review confirms it’s a solid budget choice for city breaks but not ideal for rural exploration.

Nomad UK network: Three

Nomad’s primary UK network partner for data plans is Three. That means you get Three’s coverage: fast in UK cities, but the weakest rural footprint of the four UK networks, especially in Scotland, the Lake District, and Wales.

Three’s 5G is strong in urban centres, but don’t assume every Nomad UK plan reaches it — treat 5G as a bonus rather than a promise, and expect 4G LTE in most locations.

The practical effect is the same trade-off Nomad has always had: city-first coverage at a city-first price.

Nomad app experience

Nomad customer support

Support is through in-app chat, and it’s fairly responsive — responses typically come in under 30 minutes. The support team is friendly and helpful, though they have been known to struggle with more complex technical questions about network routing.

Like most eSIM-only providers, there’s no phone support. If you have a serious connectivity issue, you’re relying entirely on chat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which UK eSIM provider is the cheapest?

Ubigi follows at $11 for 10GB and Nomad at $17.50. Does Holafly’s unlimited UK eSIM plan have any limits?

Holafly’s unlimited UK eSIM plans are genuinely unlimited under a generous fair use policy, but they are capped at 4G speeds even though Holafly’s network partners support 5G. In UK cities that means dependable 4G-level performance rather than 5G throughput. The unlimited plans also cap hotspot tethering at about 1GB per day — fine for occasional laptop use, but a limitation if you want to share your connection all day. Holafly connects to O2, Vodafone, and Three but notably excludes EE, which significantly reduces coverage in rural Great Britain, the Scottish Highlands, and national parks.

Can I use one UK eSIM across England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland?

Yes. One UK eSIM works across all four nations of the United Kingdom. The UK’s mobile networks (EE, Vodafone, O2, and Three) operate across the entire country, so you do not need separate eSIM plans for England, Scotland, Wales, or Northern Ireland. A UK eSIM from any provider covers the whole country without additional setup. For travellers visiting the Republic of Ireland as well, a Europe-wide eSIM plan that includes both the UK and Ireland is the most convenient option.
