What Is an eSIM and How Does It Work in Thailand?

In short: An eSIM is a digital SIM built into your phone. You install a network profile by scanning a QR code — no physical card needed. Thailand fully supports eSIM through AIS, TrueMove H, and DTAC. If your phone shows an EID number when you dial *#06#, it supports eSIM.

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a digital SIM soldered onto your phone’s motherboard. Instead of inserting a physical card into a tray, you download a network profile directly to your phone by scanning a QR code — the process usually takes under two minutes. The eSIM standard is maintained by the GSMA, and Apple publishes a plain-language overview in its eSIM support article.

Thailand fully supports eSIM. All three major operators — AIS, TrueMove H, and DTAC — offer eSIM for tourists and locals alike. Since the 2023 merger, DTAC and TrueMove H now form True Corp, so the market is effectively a duopoly of AIS and True Corp. Whichever travel eSIM you pick, it rides on one of those two networks under the hood.

If your phone shows an EID number when you dial *#06#, it supports eSIM.

You manage the eSIM in Settings → Cellular / Mobile Data. It lives inside your phone’s secure chip, not in the SIM tray.

Key takeaway: If your phone has an EID and is unlocked, you can use a Thailand eSIM. No physical SIM needed.

Why Choose eSIM Over a Physical SIM in Thailand?

Key takeaway: An eSIM saves time at the airport, keeps your home number active, and eliminates the risk of losing a tiny card. For most travellers with a compatible phone, it’s the simplest option.

For most travellers, an eSIM is the better choice for three reasons.

Save time. At Bangkok’s Suvarnabhumi (BKK) and Don Mueang (DMK) airports, SIM counters can have queues of 20–40 minutes at peak times. With an eSIM you buy and install before you leave home, then activate with one tap after landing.

Keep your home number. Your physical SIM stays in the phone, so you still receive calls from home and bank SMS verification codes, while the Thai eSIM handles data. This is dual SIM dual standby.

No card to lose. There’s no tiny card to drop or lose. After your trip, delete the profile from settings.

If your phone supports eSIM, it’s usually the simpler option. Pocket WiFi still makes sense for groups with many devices, but it adds another device to carry and charge.

Thailand eSIM QR Code: SM-DP+ and Activation Code

After purchase, the platform emails you a QR code or manual-entry details:

  • SM-DP+ address — the server address for the eSIM profile
  • Activation code — a unique code for your eSIM
  • QR code — the easiest way to install

Screenshot the QR code to your camera roll. If you can’t access email after landing, you can still install from that saved image.

Some plans, especially free trial eSIMs, require you to wait until you arrive to install — otherwise the timer starts early. Read the provider’s instructions carefully.

Thailand eSIM QR Code Won’t Scan? Quick Fixes

If your phone can’t scan the QR code, most platforms also provide manual entry details. Go to Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → Enter Details Manually, and enter the SM-DP+ address and activation code exactly as shown.

Common scan failures and fixes:

  • Blurry QR image → ask the platform to resend, or use manual entry
  • Low screen brightness → increase brightness and retry
  • Dirty camera lens → clean it and retry

If manual entry also fails, contact your provider’s support.

Bottom line: Always screenshot the QR code and save the manual details. You may not have email access when you land.

How to Activate a Thailand eSIM After Landing

In short: Thailand eSIMs must be activated in Thailand. After landing, connect to airport Wi-Fi, go to Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM, scan your saved QR code, turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line, and wait 2–5 minutes.

When to Activate a Thailand eSIM (Avoid Early Activation)

Thai operator eSIMs need to connect to a Thai network to complete registration. Scanning too early, outside Thailand, causes activation to fail. This is the most common reason for a Thailand eSIM not working on day one.

Step-by-step (iPhone and Android)

Step Action
1 Buy the eSIM before departure — pay online, receive the QR code by email
2 Screenshot the QR code — in case you can’t access email after landing
3 After landing, connect to airport free Wi-Fi (available at BKK and DMK)
4 Settings → Mobile/Cellular → Add eSIM → Scan the QR code
5 Turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line
6 Wait 2–5 minutes, then test with Google Maps or the browser

Important notes:

  • QR codes are typically valid for 30 days.
  • Plan validity starts from activation, not purchase — and for most travel eSIM platforms, including Roami, the countdown starts from your first data connection, not the moment you install the profile.
  • On Thai operator eSIMs, the day you activate on a Thai network counts as day 1 (Thailand time, GMT+7).

Key takeaway: Do not activate outside Thailand. If activation fails, do not delete the eSIM — retry after landing.

How to Activate a Thailand eSIM on iPhone

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi.
  2. Settings → Cellular (or Mobile Data).
  3. Tap Add eSIM.
  4. Scan the QR code you saved.
  5. Label the line (e.g. “Thailand eSIM”).
  6. Turn on Data Roaming for that line.
  7. Set Cellular Data to the Thailand eSIM.
  8. Keep your home SIM as Default Voice Line.

How to Activate a Thailand eSIM on Android

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi.
  2. Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs.
  3. Tap Add eSIM or Download a SIM instead.
  4. Scan the QR code.
  5. Enable Data Roaming for the eSIM.
  6. Set mobile data to the eSIM.
  7. Restart if needed.

Bottom line: Activation takes 2–5 minutes on a Thai network. Data Roaming is the step most people miss.

AIS vs True vs DTAC: Activation and Registration Differences

In short: AIS requires passport verification within 24 hours before departure. TrueMove H and DTAC require passport registration at purchase. All three are on the True Corp network after the 2023 merger.

Thai operators have different activation requirements. This section only covers activation — for coverage and speed, see the AIS vs True comparison.

Key takeaway: If you buy AIS, complete passport verification in the final 24 hours before your flight. Don’t do it a week early.

Device-Specific Notes: iPhone, Samsung, Pixel, Huawei, Xiaomi

In short: Menu names differ by brand, but the process is the same: find Add eSIM, scan the QR code, turn on Data Roaming, and set the eSIM as your data line. Huawei and Xiaomi support varies by model.

Most modern phones follow a similar path, but menu names differ. Here are the key differences.

iPhone

  • Go to Settings → Cellular (or Mobile Data).
  • Tap Add eSIM.
  • Scan QR code or enter details manually.
  • Label the line, turn on Data Roaming, set Cellular Data to the eSIM.
  • Keep your home SIM as Default Voice Line.
  • iOS 16 and later support eSIM transfer between iPhones during setup.

Samsung Galaxy

  • Go to Settings → Connections → SIM Card Manager.
  • Tap Add mobile plan or Add eSIM.
  • Scan the QR code.
  • Enable Data Roaming for the eSIM.
  • Set the eSIM as the preferred SIM for mobile data.

Google Pixel

  • Go to Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs.
  • Tap Add eSIM or Download a SIM instead.
  • Scan the QR code.
  • Turn on Data Roaming.
  • Set mobile data to the eSIM.

Huawei

  • Not all Huawei models support eSIM. Check your specific model first.
  • If supported, go to Settings → Mobile Network → SIM Management.
  • Look for Add eSIM or eSIM options.
  • If you do not see eSIM settings, your model likely does not support it.

Xiaomi

  • Global models such as Xiaomi 13 and 14 often support eSIM.
  • Chinese versions usually do not.
  • Go to Settings → SIM cards & mobile networks.
  • Look for Add eSIM.
  • If the option is missing, your device may not support eSIM.

Not sure? Dial *#06#. If you see an EID, your phone supports eSIM regardless of brand.

Key takeaway: If you see an EID, your phone supports eSIM. If you don’t, use a physical SIM.

Common eSIM Myths and Misunderstandings

In short: You don’t need Wi-Fi after installation. Installing does not activate. Not all eSIMs include a phone number. Unlimited is not always full speed. Never delete a failed eSIM.

Myth 1: “I need Wi-Fi to use my eSIM.” You need Wi-Fi only for installation and QR scanning. Once installed and activated, the eSIM uses the local mobile network.

Myth 2: “Installing the eSIM activates it immediately.” Installation and activation are separate. You can install before you fly; activation happens when the eSIM connects to a Thai network.

Myth 3: “All eSIMs include a phone number.” Most international eSIMs are data-only. Thai local operator eSIMs include a Thai number, but they require passport registration.

Myth 4: “Unlimited means full speed all the time.” Many unlimited plans have a fair-usage policy. Speeds drop after a daily high-speed allowance.

Myth 5: “If activation fails, I should delete the eSIM.” Do not delete it. Most providers will not reissue the QR code. Keep the profile and retry after landing.

Bottom line: Understand these myths before you travel — they prevent most first-time eSIM mistakes.

Key takeaway: Treat your eSIM like a physical SIM — don’t delete it unless you’re sure you won’t need it again.

Thailand eSIM FAQ

Q1: Can I install the eSIM before I arrive in Thailand? You can install the profile, but don’t activate it. You can scan the QR code before departure to download the profile, but leave that line turned off. The safer approach: screenshot the QR code, then scan and install after arriving using airport Wi-Fi.

Q2: Can I activate a Thailand eSIM outside Thailand? No. Thai operator eSIMs must connect to a Thai network to complete registration. Activating early usually fails. If your phone shows “Activating” or “Activation failed” outside Thailand, don’t delete the eSIM — keep it and retry after landing.

Q3: Can I use an eSIM without a physical SIM? Yes — if your phone supports eSIM, you can use it as your primary connection with no physical card inserted, as long as the device is eSIM-compatible and unlocked.

Q4: Does Huawei support eSIM? Some Huawei models, such as Mate 40 and P40 series, support eSIM, but not all. Check your specific model before buying. If it doesn’t support eSIM, you’ll need a physical SIM.

Q5: Can I use a Thailand eSIM and my home SIM at the same time? Yes — this is dual SIM dual standby. Set the Thailand eSIM as Cellular Data and keep your home SIM for calls and SMS. See the Dual SIM section above.

Q6: What happens if I delete my Thailand eSIM by mistake? You usually can’t reinstall it — most providers don’t reissue QR codes. Contact support immediately. Some platforms, such as Roami, offer 24/7 live chat and may help with reissuance.

Q7: My iPhone eSIM isn’t working — what do I do? Check in order: (1) Data Roaming is ON for the eSIM line; (2) the APN is correct; (3) Network Selection is Automatic or manually set to AIS/True; (4) restart your iPhone; (5) if it still fails, don’t delete the eSIM — contact your provider.

Q8: Can I install a Thailand eSIM on a carrier-locked phone? No. Carrier-locked phones, such as US AT&T or T-Mobile contract phones, cannot use another carrier’s eSIM. Contact your carrier to unlock the phone before travelling.

Q9: How do I activate a Thailand eSIM on iPhone? Install the profile before departure via Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → scan the QR code. After landing, connect to airport Wi-Fi, turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line, and set Cellular Data to the Thailand eSIM. Activation takes 2–5 minutes. Full steps are in the iPhone section above.
