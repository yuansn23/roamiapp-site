Airalo USA eSIM detail: speed and coverage

Airalo USA eSIM review: speed and coverage — Airalo connects to T-Mobile and Verizon networks in the US. Speed depends on which network you’re connected to at any given moment.

Airalo’s speed and coverage by network

  • T-Mobile 5G (cities): fastest download and upload speeds
  • Verizon 5G (urban): fast, with solid upload performance
  • Verizon LTE (rural): moderate speeds, broad coverage
  • T-Mobile LTE (suburban): good speeds

Coverage summary: Airalo’s dual-network access (T-Mobile + Verizon) gives it an advantage over single-network providers. In cities, T-Mobile provides the fastest speeds. In rural areas, Verizon’s LTE network offers more consistent coverage. The speed you get depends on which network your phone connects to.

Setup and activation: Airalo’s app is the smoothest in the industry. Setup is a three-step flow with no account creation, which is the main reason first-timers seldom get stuck on it. For a step-by-step walkthrough of the installation, the USA eSIM setup guide covers device-specific instructions.

Pricing: Unlimited-only lineup — $11.50 (3 days), $18.50 (5d), $25.00 (7d), $34.00 (10d), $46.00 (15d), $68.00 (30d), displayed in your local currency at checkout. For a full breakdown of pricing across all providers, see the USA eSIM price comparison.

Hotspot: Unlimited, included.

Customer support: App-based chat with chatbot triage first. Response times vary.

Best for: Short city trips. First-time eSIM users. Multi-country travelers with 200+ country coverage.

Holafly USA eSIM detail: unlimited data

Holafly USA eSIM unlimited data review — Holafly sells only unlimited data plans. No usage tracking, no top-ups, and nothing to keep an eye on. It runs on AT&T, which has solid coverage in most places including rural areas.

Pricing: 5d $20.50, 7d $27.50, 10d $36.50, 15d $50.50, 30d $73.90. For a full comparison of unlimited plans, see the USA eSIM unlimited data guide.

Holafly’s speed and coverage on AT&T

  • AT&T 5G (cities): fast download and upload speeds
  • AT&T 5G (suburban): good speeds
  • AT&T LTE (rural): moderate speeds, broad coverage

Customer support: This is where Holafly stands apart. Its 24/7 live chat support in multiple languages is the best in the industry. If you’re worried about installation or troubleshooting, Holafly’s support team is available within minutes. Airalo and Nomad rely heavily on chatbot triage, and getting a human can take hours. Ubigi’s support is email-priority. Saily offers in-app chat but with slower response times.

The hotspot limitation: Hotspot capped at 1GB/day. This is important if you plan to connect a laptop. For a single phone user, it’s fine.

Best for: Heavy data users. Unlimited streaming without counting GBs. Travelers who want 24/7 support.

Skip if: You’re a light user who would pay less with a fixed-data plan. You need reliable laptop hotspot.

Holafly fair use policy, in detail

Holafly’s “unlimited” plans have a fair use policy that travelers should understand:

Plan duration Fair use threshold After threshold

7 days ~2GB/day Possible throttling during congestion
15 days ~1.5GB/day Possible throttling during congestion

What this means in practice: Holafly’s unlimited plans are truly unlimited in the sense that there is no hard data cap. However, if you consistently use significantly more data than the average user, your speeds may be reduced during peak network congestion. For typical usage (maps, social media, messaging, some streaming), most users do not hit the fair use threshold. For heavy streamers who use 5-10GB per day, throttling is possible during evening peak hours.

Other USA eSIM companies worth knowing

The brands above are the ones most travelers compare first, but they are not the whole market. If none of them fits, these providers reach the same US networks with a different trade-off:

Provider US networks What makes it different
Maya Mobile Verizon + AT&T Unusually long validity — plans run for months rather than weeks — plus an advertised 180-day money-back guarantee. Hotspot is included on every plan, and there is no app to install: the whole account is managed from the website.
Jetpac Verizon + AT&T Aimed at frequent flyers. Unlimited plans run from 3 to 30 days, and WhatsApp, Uber, Grab and Google Maps keep working after the data runs out. Searched often as “Jetpack”.
Breeze Verizon The budget end of the market: small short-trip plans with no hidden fees, aimed at weekend trips that only need maps and messaging.
GigSky Own APN, multi-network Built around cruise ships and in-flight connectivity rather than city travel, which makes it the pick when the trip includes a cruise or onboard Wi-Fi.
Simify AT&T Short-validity visitor plans with low entry prices. Independent speed tests place it below the larger brands, so treat it as a backup rather than a primary plan.
Yesim Verizon Very low pay-as-you-go entry prices per gigabyte. Several independent tests report slower speeds than the leaders, so it suits light use.
Revolut 1GLOBAL (roaming) Not a dedicated eSIM company — the plans sit inside the Revolut banking app. Convenient if you already bank with Revolut, and Ultra subscribers get monthly global data included. Data-only, no US number.

Two things are worth knowing about this part of the market. First, most of these companies are resellers, not operators: they buy wholesale access from 1GLOBAL, Gigs or the US carriers, so two brands can deliver an identical network at different prices. Second, because a reseller’s support is app- or chat-based, an operator-direct plan remains the safer choice when a dropped connection carries a cost.

Some names that appear in “USA eSIM” searches are not providers at all. Mobimatter and Travelkon are marketplaces that resell other companies’ plans, uSwitch and MoneySavingExpert are UK price-comparison sites, and Klook bundles data into travel bookings made elsewhere. Buying through a marketplace adds a support layer between you and the network, which matters if a profile fails mid-trip. Lycamobile is a genuine US prepaid operator but sells local plans rather than travel products, and Lebara runs MVNO services in several European markets with no US product — in both cases the fix is to check the provider’s own country list before paying.

One further route sits outside the eSIM market: a UK operator bundle that includes US roaming. Revolut Mobile runs on Vodafone’s infrastructure and includes a 20GB roaming allowance covering the US as well as the EU, which can undercut a standalone data plan if you were switching UK network anyway.

No. They connect to the same US networks and provide identical coverage and speeds. The difference is pricing, support, and features like hotspot and dual-network access. A USA eSIM from Airalo on T-Mobile’s network works exactly like a T-Mobile prepaid eSIM — the network experience is identical.
