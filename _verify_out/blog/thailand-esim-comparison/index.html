Travelling in Thailand without a working Thailand eSIM is almost impossible. But the decision is more complex than “which brand is cheapest.” Local carriers AIS and True Corp (which now includes DTAC) compete with international platforms like Airalo, Nomad, Holafly, Saily, and Ubigi. They use different underlying networks and follow different registration rules. Pick wrong, and you could end up with weak signal on an island or throttled data when you need a map most.

This comparison focuses on the decision that matters most: which network and which platform fit your itinerary. It covers AIS vs True vs DTAC, coverage by destination, platform reputations, data-only vs Thai number, and best use cases. It does not repeat full price tables — for that, see the cheapest Thailand eSIM plans by data tier. It also does not repeat buying steps — for that, see the Thailand eSIM buying guide. If you have already bought and need to install, go to the setup and activation guide.

Quick decision rule: choose the network first, then the brand. Pick AIS for islands, mountains, and remote areas. Pick True Corp for Bangkok and city-focused trips. Choose an international platform to skip passport registration (data-only).

Core Takeaways: AIS vs True vs International Platforms

  • Best for islands & mountains: AIS — widest reach, most reliable on Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao, Pai, and Mae Hong Son.
  • Best for Bangkok & cities: True Corp (TrueMove H + DTAC) — dense 5G, often cheaper.
  • Best for no passport registration: International platforms (Airalo, Saily, Nomad, Ubigi, etc.) — data-only, buy before you fly.
  • Best for a Thai phone number: Local AIS/True eSIM via Klook, KKday, Agoda, or Trip.com.
  • DTAC is now part of True Corp: Same network, same coverage, often cheaper branding.
  • Best dual-network reliability: Ubigi, Travelsim Asia, eSIM-Now, Roami — auto-switch between AIS and True.
  • Best cheap entry: Travelsim Asia (1GB/$1.99) or eSIM-Now (5GB/30 days/$6.96).

Thailand eSIM Networks: AIS vs True Corp Duopoly

In short: Thailand’s mobile market is a duopoly of AIS and True Corp (TrueMove H + DTAC). Every travel eSIM you buy ultimately runs on one of these two networks. So choosing a Thailand eSIM means choosing the underlying network first.

In 2023, DTAC and TrueMove H merged to form True Corp. Thailand went from three major players to a duopoly of AIS and True Corp. Official announcements from Thailand’s National Broadcasting and Telecommunications Commission (NBTC) confirm this structural change.

Both networks have excellent 4G coverage nationwide and 5G in major cities. The key takeaway: regardless of platform, your Thailand eSIM connects to either AIS or True Corp (including DTAC).

Network Also Known As Best Known For Registration
AIS AIS Islands, mountains, rural reach Passport required
True Corp TrueMove H, DTAC City 5G, value pricing Passport required
International platforms Airalo, Nomad, Saily, etc. No passport, data-only Not required

Bottom line: You are always choosing between AIS and True Corp when you buy a Thailand eSIM. Pick the network that matches where you will travel, not just the brand name on the app.

AIS eSIM Review: Best Coverage for Islands & Mountains

In short: AIS is the best choice for island-hopping and mountain trekking. It has the widest network reach and is often the only carrier with stable signal on remote islands and northern highlands.

AIS is Thailand’s largest mobile operator and the safest choice for island-hopping and mountain trekking. It has the widest network reach and is often the only carrier with stable signal on remote islands and northern highlands.

If you are visiting Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, or Koh Tao, or heading to Chiang Mai, Pai, or Mae Hong Son, AIS is the most reliable option for stable internet. Travellers consistently report that AIS holds a signal where other networks drop out. You can browse current tourist packages on the AIS website.

In cities, AIS is also fast and reliable. Its 5G is strong along Bangkok’s BTS/MRT lines, in Sukhumvit and Silom.

AIS eSIM activation note: since early 2025, all foreign travellers must complete passport verification before their Thailand eSIM from AIS will activate. You need your passport and phone number, complete verification within 24 hours before departure, and keep the eSIM active to receive the confirmation SMS. Max 3 eSIMs per passport. If activation fails outside Thailand, don’t delete the eSIM — retry after landing.

Who AIS is best for:

  • Island-hopping (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao)
  • Northern mountain trekking (Chiang Mai, Pai, Mae Hong Son)
  • National-park adventures
  • Any trip that leaves the major cities

Key takeaway: If your itinerary includes islands or northern mountains, AIS is the safe bet. It costs a little more but keeps you connected where others drop out.

True Corp eSIM Review: Top 5G Choice for Thai Cities

In short: True Corp (TrueMove H + DTAC) is the best value for Bangkok and major cities. It has dense 5G and packages are usually a little cheaper than AIS. Coverage on remote islands and mountains is weaker than AIS.

True Corp (TrueMove H + DTAC) is the best value for Bangkok and major cities. It has dense 5G and packages are usually a little cheaper than AIS. Coverage on remote islands and mountains is weaker than AIS.

True Corp is the merged entity of DTAC and TrueMove H. The merger combined the two operators’ spectrum, which boosted urban network performance. In Bangkok, Chiang Mai, and Phuket, True Corp has dense 5G — but in rural and remote areas, AIS remains ahead. Tourist plans for both brands are listed on the True Corp website.

True Corp excels in 5G availability in city centres, with strong signal along BTS/MRT lines, Sukhumvit, and Silom. Packages are usually a little cheaper than AIS, often around $1.50–$3 less for comparable plans.

Who True Corp is best for:

  • Major city trips (Bangkok, Pattaya)
  • Popular islands like Phuket and Krabi (not remote islands)
  • Budget-conscious travellers
  • Digital nomads mainly in urban areas

Bottom line: If you are staying in Bangkok, Pattaya, or other cities, True Corp gives you excellent 5G at a lower price. Just don’t rely on it for remote islands or deep mountain areas.

DTAC eSIM: Now Part of True Corp

In short: DTAC merged into True Corp in 2023. DTAC-branded Thailand eSIMs still exist but use the same network as TrueMove H. Coverage and speeds are identical.

DTAC was once famous for its “Happy Tourist” series — a backpacker favourite. After the 2023 merger with TrueMove H, the DTAC brand still exists, but the network is fully integrated with True Corp. You can still buy DTAC-branded eSIM packages, but the underlying network is the same as TrueMove H — coverage and speeds match. If you see a DTAC package cheaper than TrueMove H, grab it; the network is identical.

Key takeaway: DTAC is no longer a separate network. Treat any DTAC plan as a True Corp plan.

Common Thailand eSIM Comparison Mistakes

In short: Most mistakes come from ignoring the underlying Thailand eSIM network, assuming unlimited means full speed, picking a data-only profile when a Thai number is required, jumping on AIS activation too soon, or forgetting DTAC is now True Corp.

1. Choosing a brand without checking the underlying network. Airalo runs on TrueMove H, Saily usually runs on AIS, and Nomad and Holafly usually run on True Corp. If you need island coverage, the underlying network matters more than the brand name.

2. Assuming “unlimited” means full speed. Most unlimited plans have a fair-usage policy. After a daily high-speed allowance, speeds drop sharply. Check the FUP before buying.

3. Buying a data-only plan and then needing a Thai number. Data-only Thailand eSIMs cannot be converted into local numbers. If you need Grab or LINE registration, buy a local operator plan from the start.

4. Activating AIS too early. AIS only opens its registration window in the final day before your flight — activate earlier and the profile can lapse before you even land. The full timing rules are in the setup guide.

5. Forgetting that DTAC is now True Corp. DTAC-branded plans use the same network as TrueMove H. If DTAC is cheaper, buy it — coverage is identical.

6. Assuming all international platforms are the same. They are not. Airalo runs on TrueMove H while Saily runs on AIS. Choose by network, not by brand familiarity.

Key takeaway: Check the underlying network, read the fair-usage policy, and decide on a Thai number before you buy. These three checks prevent most comparison mistakes.

Thailand eSIM Comparison FAQ

Q1: Which network is better for islands — AIS or True? AIS is the clear winner. On Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, and Koh Tao, AIS provides stable coverage while True Corp is weak or absent. If your itinerary includes any Gulf islands, choose AIS.

Q2: Does Airalo work well in Thailand? Yes — Airalo primarily uses the TrueMove H network in Thailand, which gives it strong city and urban coverage, and it doesn’t require passport registration. If you are heading to remote islands or the northern mountains, a Saily plan (AIS-based) is the better network fit.

Q3: Which network covers northern Thailand best? AIS is the reliable network in northern mountain areas like Pai, Mae Hong Son, and Doi Inthanon. True Corp often loses signal outside major cities.

Q4: Is True Corp good for Bangkok? Yes — True Corp has dense 5G in Bangkok, especially along BTS/MRT lines, Sukhumvit, and Silom. For city-only trips it’s a great value.

Q5: Do I need a Thai phone number? Only if you need to register for local apps like Grab, LINE, or Foodpanda. Local carrier Thailand eSIMs (AIS/True) include a Thai number; international platforms are data-only.

Q6: Which is the best Thailand eSIM for tourists overall? Islands/mountains → AIS (local or via Saily). City trips → True Corp or Nomad. No ID verification → Airalo / Saily / Travelsim Asia.

Q7: Is DTAC the same as True Corp now? Yes — DTAC merged into True Corp in 2023. DTAC-branded eSIMs still exist but use the same network as TrueMove H; coverage and speeds are identical.

Q8: Which platform has the best coverage without passport registration? Saily uses the AIS network and doesn’t require registration, while Airalo runs on TrueMove H. Both skip the passport hassle — choose by the network your itinerary needs.

Q9: Can I use a Thailand eSIM in other countries? Most Thailand eSIM plans are Thailand-only. For multi-country travel, look for a regional Asia eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, Ubigi, and Saily offer regional packages). And if your next trip is to the US rather than Thailand, our best USA eSIM comparison breaks down the three-carrier question there.

Q10: Is AIS worth the extra cost over True Corp? If your itinerary includes islands or northern mountains, yes — AIS is the reliable option there. If you’re staying in Bangkok, Pattaya, or Phuket, True Corp is fine and often cheaper. The extra cost only makes sense if you’re leaving the major cities.

Q11: What is the fastest network in Thailand for 5G? Both AIS and True Corp have excellent 5G in cities. True Corp often has denser 5G in Bangkok, while AIS has broader rural 5G. For most travellers, the difference is negligible in cities.

Q12: Can I switch between AIS and True on the same eSIM? Some international platforms, such as Ubigi, Travelsim Asia, eSIM-Now, and Roami, use dual-network eSIMs that can switch between AIS and True automatically. Local operator eSIMs are locked to one network.

Q13: Which platform has the best customer support? Roami offers 24/7 live human support. Airalo and Saily also have responsive support, but Roami’s live chat is a standout for travellers who want real-time help.

Q14: Does True Corp work on Koh Samui? True Corp has some coverage on Koh Samui, but it is weaker and less stable than AIS, especially around the island’s quieter beaches and hills. If Koh Samui is on your itinerary, choose AIS.
