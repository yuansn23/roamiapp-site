The UK has four physical mobile networks that own infrastructure: EE (around 88% 4G landmass coverage) plus Vodafone, O2 and Three, which cluster in the low-to-mid 80s on the latest per-network figures, according to independent testing. If you’re a tourist picking a UK eSIM — whether a UK eSIM for a multi-city tour or a simple data plan for London — here’s what actually matters: EE dominates rural areas and Scotland, Three is fastest in cities but weakest in the countryside, Vodafone offers strong city coverage and now sells prepaid eSIM through Pay as you go Plus, and O2 includes valuable free EU roaming.

  • EE: Best rural and Scotland coverage, prepaid eSIM available from 10 GBP for 10 GB
  • Vodafone: Strong city coverage, PAYG eSIM now available directly — though Roami or an MVNO is still the smoother route
  • O2: Good city coverage, free EU roaming included, prepaid eSIM from 15 GBP for 10 GB
  • Three: Fastest urban speeds, cheapest plans from 10 GBP for 12 GB, poor rural coverage
  • Roami multi-network: Auto-switches between EE, Vodafone, and O2 — from $1.99 (1GB/3 days), 10GB at $7.99 (3 days), longer durations priced at checkout

Every other provider — giffgaff, SMARTY, VOXI, Lebara — is an MVNO (mobile virtual network operator) renting capacity from one of these four MNOs. Here’s how they compare for your trip, based on Ofcom coverage data (2026), RootMetrics and Opensignal independent testing, and provider websites.

Every UK mobile network operator issues eSIMs, and most MVNOs do too — so the question is rarely “does it support eSIM” but “does it support eSIM on the plan I want”. Here is the short version; the ordering routes, ID requirements and QR-code mechanics for each brand are set out in our UK carrier eSIM guide.

Two clarifications for anyone comparing tables like this one. First, carrier support for eSIM is not the same as carrier support for eSIM roaming — a UK network eSIM works in the UK, and its behaviour in the EU is a separate policy that has changed repeatedly since Brexit. Can Tourists Buy a Vodafone eSIM Travel Plan?

Yes — but with friction. Vodafone UK now sells prepaid pay-as-you-go eSIM plans: its Pay as you go Plus bundles come with eSIM support, no contract and no credit check, ordered online from Vodafone’s PAYG page (verified September 2026). The ordering route, QR-code retrieval and top-up path are walked through step by step in our UK carrier eSIM guide. What you still can’t do is walk into a Vodafone shop and pick up a tourist eSIM over the counter — eSIM orders go through the website, and the plan is managed in the My Vodafone app after you land.

Here’s what Vodafone UK offers for eSIM:

  • Postpaid contracts: Available to UK residents with credit checks and proof of address. Not available to tourists.
  • Vodafone Travel eSIM: A separate product designed for UK residents travelling abroad. It’s not a UK plan for visitors.
  • Pay As You Go eSIM: Available — Pay as you go Plus plans support eSIM at online checkout with no credit check, and existing customers can convert a physical SIM to eSIM.

Even so, most visitors get Vodafone coverage one of these two ways:

  1. Buy a travel eSIM that uses the Vodafone network. Roami’s UK travel eSIM includes Vodafone as one of its partner networks and connects automatically when it’s the strongest signal. A UK data eSIM with multi-network access effectively solves the Vodafone problem by routing you through whichever of the three networks is strongest at your location.
  2. Buy a physical SIM from a Vodafone UK shop. You’ll need to show ID and physically swap SIMs. It’s a hassle, but it works.

A thorough UK eSIM EE vs Vodafone coverage comparison shows EE has the edge in rural Great Britain while Vodafone is strong in UK cities and along major transport routes. If you need Vodafone coverage, use a multi-network travel eSIM or a Vodafone-based MVNO instead. It’s that simple.

How EE Pay-As-You-Go eSIM Works for Tourists

EE prepaid eSIMs are available to tourists without a UK address or credit check. Their network covers approximately 88% of the UK landmass with 4G — the most extensive rural coverage in Great Britain, according to EE’s official coverage page. An EE eSIM UK plan is the safest pick for visitors heading to the Highlands, Lake District, or anywhere off the beaten path.

EE coverage

EE’s coverage gap over other networks is most pronounced in Scotland. If you’re visiting the Highlands, the Hebrides, or the Northern Isles, EE is the network you want. In England and Wales, EE has better coverage in the Lake District, the Yorkshire Dales, the Peak District, and Snowdonia than any other network. An EE-based eSIM works reliably for countryside trips.

EE is not magic either. It loses signal for several minutes through the steepest section of the A82 through Glen Coe — better than Three, which can be dead for 20+ minutes on the same route, but not perfect.

EE speeds

In rural areas, expect 10-40 Mbps on 4G, dropping to single digits in very remote spots. What O2’s Prepaid eSIM Visitor Plan Offers

O2 sells prepaid eSIMs to tourists with free EU roaming included. Its 4G landmass footprint sits in the low-to-mid 80s — strongest in cities, weakest in rural Scotland and Wales, according to O2’s coverage checker.

O2 coverage

Where O2 works well: London and all major English cities, most towns and suburbs, major transport routes, and southern England generally. Where O2 struggles: the Scottish Highlands and islands, rural Wales outside main towns, parts of the Lake District and Yorkshire Dales, and rural stretches in Devon and Cornwall.

On the North Coast 500, O2 has virtually no coverage outside Inverness, Wick, and Thurso. Travellers report 45-minute stretches with zero bars on multiple sections. If you’re doing the NC500, don’t pick O2. It’s that straightforward.

O2’s big selling point for tourists is free EU roaming. If you’re visiting the UK and then travelling to Ireland, France, or Spain, O2 lets you use your UK data in those countries at no extra cost. EE, Three, and Vodafone have reintroduced roaming fees for new customers after Brexit. O2’s inclusion is genuinely valuable for multi-country trips.

But here’s the downside: O2’s standard travel plan uses only O2’s network. If you venture into rural Great Britain where O2 doesn’t reach, you’ll have no signal. No fallback. No backup. Just a “No Service” banner and regret.

  • Condition: You want a UK phone number for calls and texts -> Recommendation: O2 or giffgaff

Is Three’s eSIM Good for Prepaid Tourists?

Three offers the cheapest prepaid eSIM plans in the UK — 12 GB for 10 GBP and 100 GB for 25 GBP — but its 4G network is concentrated almost entirely in urban areas and has the weakest rural reach of the four, according to Three’s coverage checker and the independent drive-testing both RootMetrics and Opensignal publish. An esim Three UK prepaid plan is the clear winner on price, but you trade away rural coverage for those savings.

Three coverage

Three has strong coverage in London, Birmingham, Manchester, Glasgow, and other major UK cities, often with faster speeds than competitors. But in the Scottish Highlands, Three is nearly useless outside main towns. In Snowdonia, coverage is patchy at best. In the Lake District, it works in Keswick and Windermere but drops out on the fells. Three also struggles with indoor coverage — its signal penetrates buildings less effectively than EE or Vodafone. Three can show no signal in ground-floor pubs in Keswick while EE holds 3 bars at the same table.

Roaming in Europe costs an extra 2 GBP per day for the first 12 GB of your allowance. So that cheap UK plan gets expensive fast the moment you cross the Channel.

  • Condition: You’re on a tight budget and staying mainly in UK cities -> Recommendation: Three prepaid eSIM
  • Condition: You need a lot of data for the price -> Recommendation: Three 100 GB or Unlimited plan

A quick UK eSIM O2 vs Three network comparison reveals O2 offers better rural coverage and free EU roaming, while Three provides faster urban speeds and cheaper data. Pick your poison: rural reach or urban speed. You can’t have both with a single-network plan.

Vodafone eSIM Coverage for Tourists

Vodafone covers roughly 82-85% of the UK landmass with 4G, making it a close second to EE for rural coverage in Great Britain — and it now sells prepaid eSIMs through its Pay as you go Plus plans, according to Vodafone’s own consumer pages (checked September 2026). Ordering directly works, but the friction is real: a UK-facing checkout and plan management in the My Vodafone app, all of which a travel eSIM removes.

Do I need ID to buy an eSIM in the UK?

It depends on where you buy. UK networks and MVNOs require ID. Travel eSIM providers don’t.

Buying from a UK network directly (EE, O2, Three, Vodafone physical SIM): Yes, you need ID. UK networks ask for a passport or national ID card when selling prepaid SIMs, and online purchases usually go through digital ID checks.

Buying from an MVNO (giffgaff, Lebara, SMARTY, VOXI): Usually yes, but the process is simpler. Most accept a passport photo or digital upload. Some use automated verification. giffgaff’s verification takes about 3 minutes via their app — surprisingly smooth.

Buying from a travel eSIM provider (Roami, Airalo, etc.): No, you don’t need ID. Travel eSIMs operate outside the UK’s mobile identification requirements — you buy and download. That’s one of the main reasons tourists choose them.

Why the ID rule exists: Networks apply ID checks as anti-fraud practice and to comply with the rules that govern UK mobile services. Travel eSIMs that only offer data are generally treated differently because they don’t provide a regulated mobile service with a UK number.

Which Phones Support UK eSIM?

Most modern phones from 2019 onwards support eSIM. Older models and some market-specific variants don’t. Check your device before buying — our device compatibility hub lists supported models, and the GSMA eSIM specification explains the underlying standard.

iPhone: iPhone XS/XR and all later models support eSIM. Dual eSIM (two active eSIMs, no physical SIM) works from iPhone 13 onwards. iPhone SE (2nd gen and later) supports eSIM. All iPhone 14 and later US models are eSIM only (no physical SIM). If you’ve got a US iPhone 14 or 15, you literally don’t have a choice — it’s eSIM or nothing.

Samsung: Galaxy S20 series and later. Galaxy Z Flip and Z Fold series. Galaxy A series: limited (A54 and later in some markets). Samsung’s eSIM support is more fragmented than Apple’s — always check your specific model.

Google Pixel: Pixel 3 and later (Pixel 6 and later recommended). Pixel Fold supports eSIM.

Huawei: Limited support. P40 and Mate 40 series have eSIM in some markets. Newer models restricted by US sanctions. Honestly, if you’re using a recent Huawei, eSIM compatibility is the least of your problems.

OnePlus: OnePlus 11 and later. OnePlus 10 series has eSIM in some markets.

How to check your phone

  1. iPhone: Settings > General > About > Scroll to “Available SIMs” or “eSIM”
  2. Samsung: Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager > Add eSIM
  3. Google Pixel: Settings > Network & Internet > Add SIM > Download a SIM instead
  4. Check Apple’s support page for iPhone compatibility

Carrier-locked phones: If your phone is locked to a US carrier (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile), it can only use eSIMs from that carrier. US iPhones from iPhone 14 onwards have no physical SIM slot, so a locked US iPhone 14 or later cannot use a UK eSIM at all. Request an unlock before travel: T-Mobile (after 40 days), Verizon (auto after 60 days), AT&T (after 60 days or when device payments complete). Don’t discover your phone is locked at 35,000 feet.

Dual SIM With a UK eSIM: The Two-Minute Version

Dual SIM lets you keep your home SIM active for calls and texts while using a UK eSIM for data. Most modern iPhones and Android flagships support this. It’s the setup most travellers use on every trip — here is the short form, with the per-model detail in our dual SIM setup guide.

Dual SIM line assignment on iPhone

  1. Install your UK eSIM before you leave (scan the QR code)
  2. On arrival, go to Settings > Cellular
  3. Set your home SIM for “voice and SMS”
  4. Set your UK eSIM for “data”
  5. Enable “Allow Cellular Data Switching”

Dual SIM line assignment on Android

  1. Install the UK eSIM before you leave
  2. Go to Settings > Connections > SIM Card Manager
  3. Set your home SIM for calls
  4. Set your UK eSIM for mobile data
  5. Enable “SIM card switching” if available

Battery impact: Dual SIM uses roughly 10-15% more battery because the phone maintains connections to two networks. Carry a power bank for all-day use — on a heavy-use day, a full charge that would normally see you to bedtime might need an evening top-up with dual SIM active.

For conflicts — one line showing “No Service”, SMS leaving from the wrong number, or 2FA codes not arriving — work through the dual SIM setup guide rather than resetting network settings first.

How WiFi Calling Works on a UK eSIM

WiFi calling lets you make and receive calls through your home carrier over any WiFi connection — including your hotel WiFi in the UK — without incurring international roaming charges. Enable WiFi calling on your home SIM before travel, keep your home SIM active in dual SIM mode, and connect to hotel or accommodation WiFi. Your phone routes calls over WiFi automatically.

Activation Routes Compared: Direct Network, MVNO or Travel eSIM

Activation varies by provider type, but the general process is straightforward.

Direct network eSIM (EE, O2, Three)

  1. Buy the plan online or in-store
  2. Provide ID (passport scan or in-store verification)
  3. Receive a QR code by email
  4. Scan the QR code with your phone’s camera
  5. Follow on-screen prompts to install
  6. eSIM activates when you connect to the UK network

MVNO eSIM (giffgaff, SMARTY, Lebara, VOXI)

  1. Sign up on the provider’s website or app
  2. Provide ID (digital verification)
  3. Download the eSIM profile (usually immediately)
  4. Scan or download to your phone
  5. Activate by connecting to the UK network

Travel eSIM (Roami, Airalo, Holafly)

  1. Download the provider’s app
  2. Buy a UK plan within the app
  3. Install the eSIM through the app (guided setup)
  4. eSIM activates automatically on arrival (or manually, depending on the plan)

Common activation issues and fixes

“No Service” after installing: Restart your phone to force network registration. This fixes it in most cases. eSIM shows “Activation Pending”: Ensure you have internet access (WiFi or another SIM) during installation. eSIMs need an internet connection to complete activation. Data not working after activation: Enable “Data Roaming” for the eSIM in your phone settings. This is the number one issue travellers face. eSIM not showing: Go to Settings > Cellular > Add Cellular Plan and scan the QR code again. APN settings not configured: Some eSIMs need manual APN configuration. For Roami, APN is set automatically. For others, check the provider’s setup guide.
