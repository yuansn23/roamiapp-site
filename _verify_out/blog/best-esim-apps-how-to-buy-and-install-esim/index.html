What to look for in an eSIM app

An eSIM app is not just a storefront — it is the tool you will use to monitor data, top up, and reach support mid-trip. Five things separate the good ones from the rest:

What to check Why it matters Good vs bad
Country coverage in-app See your destination before paying Clear country list vs buried search
Transparent pricing Per-GB price up front “From $X” with no surprises vs hidden fees
Live data meter Know when you are running low Real-time counter vs no tracking
Top-up & multi-plan Trips extend and routes change Easy add-ons vs force-new-plan
In-app support Problems happen abroad Live chat vs email-only

The two features that matter most in practice are a real-time data meter and in-app support. You cannot budget what you cannot see — and a meter that tracks your data usage in near real time is the difference between an easy trip and a surprise outage. When an eSIM app will not activate a profile, you also want a human inside the same app, not a separate support ticket and a 24-hour wait. A polished guided flow matters for first-timers too: a good app tells you what to tap next, so you are never guessing whether you did it right. Finally, confirm the plan runs on 4G/5G where you are going — a prepaid data plan that only connects at 3G or throttled LTE in your destination is not a bargain.

How buying an eSIM actually works

Buying eSIM data is fast, and nearly all eSIM apps follow the same order — the mistakes people make are almost always a skipped early step. The complete sequence:

1. Check compatibility first — before you pay. Confirm your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM. Most phones from 2018 onward do, but a carrier-locked device usually does not, and a refund is not guaranteed if you find out after purchase. Look for an “Add eSIM” option in settings, or check the eSIM compatible devices list and the iPhone eSIM compatibility guide.

2. Pick your plan in the eSIM app. Choose destination (or region), data amount, and validity. Match validity to your trip length, not to the cheapest price — a 7-day plan on a 14-day trip means buying twice.

3. Pay. Most apps accept card and PayPal, with Apple Pay or Google Pay for one-tap checkout. The eSIM profile is generated immediately after payment — there is nothing physical to wait for.

4. Receive your eSIM. You get a QR code plus a manual activation code (the “SM-DP+ address” and matching activation code) in the app and by email. Keep the manual code — it is your fallback if the QR scan fails. Despite the name people use, an eSIM card is not a physical card; it is a digital profile, so it arrives instantly with nothing to ship.

5. Install and activate. Scan the QR code or tap in-app install, then set the eSIM as your data line. Do this on Wi-Fi, ideally before you fly.

The single most common error in any eSIM app flow is buying before checking compatibility — the one step you cannot easily undo. The second is waiting to install until you have landed, when your only connection is the airport Wi-Fi everyone else is hammering.

App install vs QR code vs manual entry

Once you have paid inside your eSIM app, there are three ways to get the profile onto your phone. They end at the same place but have different failure points:

Install method How it works Best for Watch out for
In-app install Tap “install” and the app pushes the profile Most people, first-timers Requires the app to stay installed
QR code scan Scan a code with your camera No-app or quick setup Needs a second screen or printed code
Manual entry Type the SM-DP+ address and activation code Backup, no camera Most error-prone — typos break it

In-app install is the smoothest: the app handles the technical steps, so there is nothing to mis-scan or mistype. It is the best default for anyone new to eSIMs.

QR code scanning is the classic method and still works everywhere. The one gotcha: if the QR code is displayed on the same phone you are installing to, you cannot point that phone’s camera at its own screen. Keep a copy on a second device, or use the manual code as backup.

Manual entry exists as a fallback. It works, but the activation code is a long, error-prone string — typically formatted like LPA:1$smdp.example.com$ACTIVATION-CODE. A single mistyped character means the profile will not download, so treat it as a last resort. Worth remembering: the app is a storefront, not a requirement — every one of these providers also installs from a plain QR code, so a web purchase is just as valid.

Where each method lives on iOS vs Android

The exact taps differ by platform, which is the source of most “where do I even find this” confusion:

  • iPhone (iOS): Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM. From there you either scan the QR code or choose “Use QR Code” / “Enter Details Manually”. After install, label the line (e.g., “Travel”) and set it as the default for Cellular Data, while keeping your home line for calls.
  • Android (Samsung/Google Pixel): Settings → Connections → SIM manager → Add eSIM (or Network & Internet → SIMs → Add). Scan the QR or enter the SM-DP+ address and activation code. Then set the eSIM as the preferred data SIM.

APN and the two settings that fix most “no data” problems

The most common post-install complaint is “I have signal but no internet.” Two settings fix almost all of these cases:

1. APN (Access Point Name). The APN tells your phone how to reach the provider’s data gateway. Most eSIMs configure it automatically, but some Android phones need it entered manually — usually under the eSIM’s “Access Point Names” screen. Your provider’s app or support page lists the exact APN (name and value). If you have bars but no data, this is the first thing to check.

2. Data routing (dual SIM). With two active lines, your phone must know which one carries data. If your home SIM is still set as the data line while you are abroad, you will be billed roaming charges — or see no data if roaming is off. Set the eSIM as the default for data and leave the home SIM for calls and texts only.

A third, less common culprit is roaming toggles: some eSIMs require “Data Roaming” to be switched on for that line even though it is not true roaming — because the profile connects through a foreign gateway. If you have an APN set and still no data, toggling Data Roaming on for the eSIM line (never your home line) often resolves it.

What is a “global eSIM store”?

You will see global eSIM store in searches and marketing, and it is less exotic than it sounds. A global eSIM store is a provider that buys wholesale mobile data from local carriers in many countries, packages it into plans, and resells it through one app or website — no physical SIM card to ship or insert. Instead of signing up with a French carrier, a Japanese carrier, and a US carrier separately, the store handles the roaming agreements and hands you one plan that works across borders.

This is how one app can sell a plan that works in 200+ countries: the provider has negotiated with local networks everywhere, not built its own towers. For you as a traveler, the practical benefits are:

  • One app and one account for every destination you ever visit.
  • Regional and global plans that cover multiple countries without swapping SIMs.
  • Up-front pricing in your own currency, with no surprise carrier bills afterward.

The trade-off is that you are always a resale customer of the underlying network, so two different “global eSIM stores” can both cover France yet perform differently — the quality of their local network partnerships is what you are really buying. Frequently asked questions

What is the best eSIM app for travelers?

It depends on what you value. For automatic carrier switching plus a live data meter and in-app support, Roami is the strongest option. For the largest plan catalog, Airalo has the most destinations. For 24/7 human support, Holafly leads. Most travelers are best served by an app with a real-time data meter and in-app top-ups.

Can I buy an eSIM without an app?

Yes. Many providers sell eSIMs through a website and deliver a QR code by email, so an app is not required. But an app makes the flow smoother — in-app install, a live data meter, and support in one place — which is why most people end up using one.

Do I need a new eSIM for every country I visit?

No. Regional and global eSIMs cover multiple countries in one plan. You only need a separate eSIM when visiting a country outside your plan’s coverage, or when your data runs out and top-ups are unavailable.

How long does it take to buy and install an eSIM?

About five minutes end to end — pick a plan, pay, scan or tap to install, and activate. The eSIM is delivered instantly after payment, so there is no shipping or waiting. The only slow part is installing over a weak connection, so do it on stable Wi-Fi.

I have signal but no data — what do I check?

Check three things in order: (1) the APN is set correctly for the eSIM line, (2) the eSIM is selected as your default data line (not your home SIM), and (3) Data Roaming is enabled for the eSIM line only. For a full step-by-step, see the eSIM troubleshooting guide.
