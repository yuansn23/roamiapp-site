USA eSIM unlimited data: what “unlimited” actually means

Quick answer: Most USA eSIM unlimited plans have a daily high-speed cap between 2GB and 5GB, then throttle to roughly 1Mbps. Holafly is the exception — check the cap, not the word “unlimited”. The GSMA explains how eSIM profiles and fair-use policies work at the network level.

Key takeaways: Holafly is the only provider that offers a USA eSIM no data cap, with truly unlimited data without a daily high-speed cap on its standard plans. Holafly caps tethering at 1GB per day. Visible by Verizon offers truly unlimited data at $25/month but requires a US address and identity verification. Ubigi’s unlimited plan includes unlimited tethering at no extra charge but runs on T-Mobile’s network, which is excellent in cities but weak in rural areas.

All unlimited plans have limits. The question is what kind. GSMA’s eSIM technical specifications confirm that data management policies like FUP are set by providers, not by the technology itself.

America eSIM unlimited data plans also differ in ways the headline number hides. Read the coverage map for the exact route rather than trusting the plan name, because an unlimited allowance on a network that thins out at the edge of a metro area is worth less than a capped plan that keeps signal. Confirm whether the eSIM profile can be re-installed if you change handsets mid-trip, keep the APN details for manual entry, install it as the data line in a dual SIM setup, and treat latency as part of the product: after the fair-use threshold the drop is in speed rather than in bytes, so messaging keeps working while video calls do not. Refunds are generally limited to a plan that never activated, which is why testing the profile before you depend on it matters.

Fair usage policy (FUP) limits: Most unlimited plans give you a certain amount of high-speed data per day (typically 2-5GB). Once you exceed that, your speed drops to 128-512 kbps — enough for messaging and maps, but not for video streaming or video calls. The plan is still technically “unlimited” — you can keep using data — but the experience changes dramatically.

Hotspot limits: Some unlimited plans restrict connection sharing. Holafly caps tethering at 1GB per day. Others allow unlimited tethering but count it against your fair usage cap.

Network throttling: Even on truly unlimited plans, sustained heavy use (many GBs per day for multiple days) can trigger throttling. This is rare for typical travel usage but common for people trying to use an eSIM as a home internet replacement.

These limits are not hidden technology constraints — they are written into each provider’s terms. Holafly’s terms set the 1GB/day tethering limit as an explicit fair-use condition on its USA plan pages, and Ubigi’s terms describe its ~60GB soft cap the same way in its USA plan terms. Always read the fair-use section before you buy. To see a full provider-by-provider breakdown, read the USA eSIM provider comparison.

The fine print: common unlimited plan restrictions decoded

Every unlimited plan has restrictions buried in its terms of service. Here’s what to look for when evaluating a plan:

Daily high-speed caps: The most common restriction on travel eSIM unlimited plans. Providers allocate a set amount of high-speed data per day (often 2-5GB, though some are higher). After you exceed this amount, speeds are reduced for the remainder of the day. The cap resets at midnight. On a 7-day plan with a 3GB daily cap, you get 21GB of high-speed data total — not unlimited.

Monthly high-speed caps: Common on US carrier prepaid unlimited plans. Tello gives you 50GB at full speed before throttling. Visible gives you truly unlimited data but caps tethering at 5Mbps. Ubigi’s unlimited plan has approximately a 60GB total soft cap.

Video streaming restrictions: Some plans restrict video streaming quality to 480p or 720p, even when you have full-speed data available. This is common on US carrier prepaid plans (Tello, Visible) but less common on international travel eSIMs.

Deprioritization: During network congestion, some plans are “deprioritized” — your data traffic is slowed down to prioritize traffic from the carrier’s direct customers. This is common on MVNO plans (Tello, Visible, Google Fi) and some travel eSIMs. Deprioritization doesn’t cap your data but can make speeds unusable in crowded areas like stadiums, airports, and convention centers.

Zero-rating: Some carriers exempt certain data types from your usage cap. For example, T-Mobile’s “Binge On” and AT&T’s “Stream Saver” don’t count certain streaming services toward your data limit. These programs change frequently and are rarely available on travel eSIMs.

Tethering speed tests: what to expect in real-world conditions

Lab conditions and real-world tethering performance often differ. Here’s what travelers report for each provider:

Holafly (1GB/day tethering cap, AT&T): At the start of each day, tethering speeds match the phone’s data speed. After 1GB of tethering usage, hotspot is blocked entirely (not slowed — blocked). You cannot reconnect your laptop until the next calendar day. This is the strictest hotspot policy of any major provider.

Ubigi (unlimited tethering, T-Mobile): Hotspot speeds match phone data speeds. There is no separate tethering cap. The ~60GB total soft cap applies to combined phone + tethering usage. Hotspot speeds are generally strong on highways and more limited in rural areas.

Tello (tethering uses plan data, T-Mobile): Hotspot is treated the same as on-phone data. At full speed (before the 50GB cap), hotspot typically performs well depending on location and congestion. After the 50GB cap, hotspot slows along with on-phone data — enough for email but not video or large file transfers.

Visible (unlimited tethering, 5Mbps cap, Verizon): Despite the 5Mbps cap, Visible hotspot is surprisingly usable. At 5Mbps, you can: browse websites, send emails with attachments, use Google Maps, make VoIP calls (WhatsApp, Skype), and stream music. You cannot: stream HD video, download large files quickly, or join high-quality video calls. The 5Mbps cap is consistent regardless of your phone’s signal strength.

Google Fi (full-speed tethering, T-Mobile): No restrictions or speed caps. Hotspot performance matches phone data speeds. This is the best tethering option among unlimited plans, but it comes at the highest price ($65/month).

Common Visible setup issues and workarounds

Travelers who try Visible often encounter specific setup hurdles:

Issue 1: No US billing address. If you don’t have a US address you can use, consider: using a friend’s US address, using a hotel address (some hotels accept mail for guests), or using a virtual mailbox service. Never use a randomly generated address — Visible may verify your address against credit databases.

Issue 2: eSIM activation without a working phone. The Visible app requires an internet connection to activate the eSIM. If you land in the US without any data connection, you won’t be able to activate Visible. Solution: install the Visible eSIM and start activation while on WiFi at the airport, before leaving the arrivals area. Most US airports have free WiFi.

Issue 3: Account verification. Visible may require identity verification with a US government-issued ID or passport. Some international passports are accepted, but the process can take 24-48 hours. Plan ahead if using Visible as your primary connection.

Issue 4: Port-out and number retention. Visible numbers cannot be easily ported to international carriers. If you get a US number through Visible for a long stay, you may lose it when you leave. Plan to port the number to Google Voice before your departure if you want to keep it.

USA eSIM unlimited data: weather, geography and enforcement

Weather and geography affect unlimited data speeds more than many travelers realize.

Heavy rain and snow can reduce cellular speeds on all unlimited plans. Rain attenuates cellular signals at higher frequencies like 5G C-band and mmWave — speeds can drop by 20-40% during storms regardless of your provider. Mountain valleys, dense forests, and urban canyons between tall buildings also reduce signal strength. These are physical limitations of cellular technology, not provider throttling. Switching from 5G to 4G LTE provides more stable connectivity during adverse weather conditions.

When unlimited plans get you flagged: Providers monitor for excessive usage, even on “unlimited” plans. Using more than 100GB in a billing cycle on a travel unlimited plan may trigger a fair use review. Activities that consume this much data in a short period include: downloading large files for work, streaming 4K video for hours daily, or running a tethering connection as a home internet replacement. For typical travel usage — maps, social media, messaging, occasional streaming — no unlimited plan will flag your account.

Stricter fair usage policies: As more travelers use eSIMs as primary connections for extended stays, providers are implementing stricter FUP limits. The days of truly unlimited data at budget prices are ending. Expect more 20-40GB soft caps on plans currently advertised as “unlimited.”

Integration with US carrier prepaid brands: The line between “travel eSIM” and “US carrier prepaid” continues to blur. More US carriers are offering eSIM plans that can be purchased by international visitors without a US address. Regulatory changes: The FCC is considering rules that would require US carriers to offer short-term prepaid eSIM plans that can be activated by international visitors without a US address or credit check. If enacted, this would make US carrier unlimited plans (Visible, Tello, Mint) significantly more accessible to travelers.

USA eSIM unlimited data myths worth checking

Myth 1: “Unlimited” means you can use as much data as you want at full speed.

Reality: Every “unlimited” plan has limits. Some have daily high-speed caps, some have monthly caps, and some throttle video streaming or tethering. The only truly unlimited plans (Holafly, Visible) either restrict tethering usage or cap tethering speeds. Unlimited plans are best understood as “high-allowance plans” rather than truly unlimited.

Myth 2: Unlimited plans are always cheaper than fixed-data plans.

Reality: For most travelers, fixed-data plans are cheaper. A 10GB fixed plan at $12 covers a week of moderate use. An unlimited plan at $25-27.50 only saves money if you consistently exceed 10GB in a week. For light users (under 3GB/week), fixed-data plans at $5-10 are significantly cheaper.

Myth 3: Unlimited plans have the best coverage.

Reality: Coverage depends on the carrier network, not the plan type. A fixed-data plan on AT&T (Holafly’s network) has the same coverage as an unlimited plan on AT&T. A fixed-data plan on Verizon has better rural coverage than an unlimited plan on T-Mobile. Choose your plan based on the carrier, not whether it’s unlimited.

Myth 4: You can use an unlimited eSIM as a home internet replacement.

Reality: US carriers actively discourage using mobile plans as home internet. Sustained usage over 100GB/month on an “unlimited” travel plan will trigger a fair use review, and your account may be terminated. Home internet requires a dedicated fixed wireless or broadband plan.

Myth 5: Unlimited plans don’t require data management.

Reality: Even on truly unlimited plans, managing your data matters. Video calling, HD streaming, and tethering usage can congest the network for everyone. Most providers expect reasonable usage and may throttle users who consume significantly more than average, even without a stated cap.

Unlimited Data During a Major Event

A match day, a stadium festival or a packed convention congests every carrier at the same time, and deprioritized unlimited plans slow first — which is the opposite of what the “unlimited” label implies.

The counterintuitive part: an unlimited plan with a low fair-use cap often behaves worse than a fixed-data plan on a network that still has capacity. Once you cross the cap — 1GB/day of tethering, or 50GB total — your traffic is pushed to the back of the queue no matter how much headroom the tower has left, while a fixed-data plan that has not hit its allowance keeps full priority. So for a single match day or keynote, a plan with room left under its cap, or a multi-network USA eSIM that can move to a less-loaded carrier, is the safer bet than an “unlimited” label with a hidden ceiling.
