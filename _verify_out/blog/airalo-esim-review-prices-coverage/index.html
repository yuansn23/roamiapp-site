What Is the Airalo eSIM and How Does It Work?

Airalo’s Business Model

An Airalo eSIM doesn’t come from Airalo’s own cell towers. The business model is simple: partner with over 700 local operators worldwide (SoftBank and KDDI (au) in Japan, T-Mobile in the US, Türk Telekom in Turkey, AIS in Thailand), buy data in bulk from them, and resell it to travelers in small, trip-sized packages through its app. The technology underneath is the GSMA’s eSIM specification, the standard that lets one phone hold several operator profiles without a physical card.

Think of the Airalo eSIM as the Expedia of mobile data – it doesn’t build hotels, but it brings together accommodation options from around the world in one app for easy comparison and booking.

This multi-operator auto-switching ensures you get the strongest signal wherever you are. You can check out Roami’s full coverage plans to see if they suit your destination.

Three Scenarios Where Airalo Is a Good Fit

Scenario 1: Short 1-2 week trips You don’t want to queue 40 minutes at the airport for a SIM card, research local operator pricing, or struggle with language barriers at the counter. “5 minutes in the app” is Airalo’s core value proposition.

Scenario 2: Multi-country itineraries (Europe or Asia) One Eurolink plan covers 42 European countries; one Asialink plan covers 18 Asian countries. No more swapping SIMs at every border – one eSIM does it all. True round-the-world trips get their own product: Discover Global, Airalo’s global eSIM covering 170 countries and networks on a single profile (sold both as data-only and with calls and texts).

Scenario 3: Light data users Navigation, WhatsApp, Instagram, occasional short videos – 1GB to 5GB is plenty for most short trips. Airalo eSIM entry-level plans ($4.50/1GB) are cheap enough that you won’t bother comparing prices.

Three Scenarios Where Airalo Is NOT a Good Fit

Scenario 1: You need to receive SMS verification codes The vast majority of Airalo eSIM plans are data-only – no phone number included. If your bank app, Google account, or WhatsApp needs SMS two-factor authentication, Airalo will leave you trapped in a “can’t receive code → can’t log in → can’t complete transaction” loop. Before you leave, make sure to switch all critical accounts to authenticator apps (Google Authenticator, 1Password, etc.).

⚠️ The only exception: Airalo’s Discover+ global plan includes voice calling and SMS, but this is a rare exception – most plans have no number. Double-check before buying.

Scenario 2: Long-term stays over 1 month in one country Local SIM cards are 16% to 50% cheaper than the Airalo eSIM. In Japan, Docomo’s 5GB travel SIM is around $7, while Airalo’s equivalent is $11 – about 57% more expensive. In Thailand, AIS’s 10GB airport plan is around $5, while Airalo’s 10GB is around $11 – 120% more. The longer you stay, the worse Airalo’s value becomes.

Scenario 3: Heavy video conferencing or streaming users The Airalo eSIM’s “unlimited” plans throttle to ~1 Mbps after 3GB/day. At 1 Mbps, you can send WeChat messages and browse websites, but video calls and streaming are impossible. If you need two hours of Zoom meetings daily, skip Airalo entirely.

Airalo eSIM Installation, Activation and Troubleshooting

Standard Installation Process (about 5 minutes)

  1. Download the Airalo App (iOS or Android)
  2. Search destination → Select plan → Complete payment
  3. Install on WiFi: Tap “Install eSIM” in the app → Follow prompts → Wait for activation (a few minutes)
  4. After arrival: Settings → Cellular → Enable “Data Roaming” → Select the supported network

iOS users: Installation is automatic. Android users: Some devices may need to scan a QR code (newer models from 2020+ can install directly; older models may need another device to display the QR code). For the step-by-step version on iPhone, see Apple’s eSIM setup guide.

Three Critical Warnings (Please Read)

  1. ⚠️ You MUST install before departure. Installation requires an internet connection. If you wait until after landing and there’s no WiFi – you won’t be able to activate the eSIM.
  2. ⚠️ Turn off data roaming on your primary line after installation – this avoids a several-hundred-dollar roaming bill from your home carrier.
  3. ⚠️ Check device compatibility before you go. Some phones sold in China (and Hong Kong models with dual physical SIM slots) don’t support eSIM. Our eSIM compatible devices list covers every iPhone, Samsung, and Google Pixel generation. This is the #1 reason refund requests are denied.

Three-Step Troubleshooting for Installation Failure

If you see “Unable to Complete Cellular Plan Change” or similar errors:

Step 1: Check your environment

  • Turn off VPN
  • Ensure your current network connection is stable
  • Delete old eSIM profiles you’re no longer using (too many profiles can block new installations)
  • Update your phone to the latest OS version

Step 2: Verify if installation actually succeeded

  • iOS: Settings → General → About → Scroll to “ICCID”
  • If you see an ICCID number, the eSIM is already installed successfully

Step 3: If still no signal after arrival, try in this order

  1. Settings → Cellular → Select Airalo line → Enable “Data Roaming”
  2. Toggle Airplane Mode on, wait 30 seconds, turn off
  3. Manually select network (network list can be found in your plan details)
  4. Restart phone
  5. Reset network settings (last resort)

⚠️ Never delete an installed eSIM profile. Most eSIMs can only be installed once. Delete it and you’ll have to buy a new one.

The Truth About “Unlimited” Plan Throttling

Every “unlimited” eSIM plan on the market comes with a Fair Use Policy (FUP) – after exceeding a daily threshold, speeds are reduced.

Brand Daily throttle threshold Speed after throttle
Airalo 3GB/day ~1 Mbps
Holafly None – genuinely uncapped Full speed (but hotspot/tethering capped at 1GB/day)
Saily 5GB/day 1 Mbps

Each provider’s own published fair-use terms tell the same story: Saily’s unlimited plans throttle to 1 Mbps after 5GB a day, while Holafly states it applies no daily high-speed cap – the trade-off being a 1GB/day tethering cap (per Holafly’s official plan terms).

What the Airalo eSIM’s “unlimited” actually means: Full speed for the first 3GB, then throttled to ~1 Mbps. At 1 Mbps, you can use WeChat and browse the web, but video calls and streaming are completely impossible.

📌 Remember: “Unlimited” does not mean “full speed throughout” – it means “won’t cut off, but will slow down.”

Rule 1: 30-day application window You must submit a refund request within 30 days of the issue occurring.

Rule 2: 14-day statutory right of withdrawal As a consumer, you have the right to withdraw from a purchase within 14 days without giving a reason. However, this right expires on the earlier of: 14 days after purchase, or the day the eSIM is activated.

Rule 3: Refund method depends on the reason

  • Airmoney: Instant
  • Back to card: Usually 7-10 business days (up to 30 days, depending on your bank)

How to Request a Refund

  1. Open Airalo App → “My eSIMs” → Select the plan
  2. In “Plan History,” look for the “Request Refund” button
  3. Select refund reason
  4. Follow prompts to complete

⚠️ Note: Only eligible orders show the refund button. If it’s not visible, the order doesn’t qualify.

One Important Exception

If you’ve requested automatic refunds multiple times, future refund requests may be escalated to human support for review. Frequent refunds can trigger fraud prevention mechanisms and extend processing times.

Airalo eSIM FAQs: Answers In Depth

Q1: Does Airalo eSIM support 5G?

A: Partially. 5G availability depends on whether the local partner operator in your destination has deployed 5G. Not all countries have 5G coverage. How to check: In the Airalo App, after selecting a destination, the plan details page clearly shows the network type (4G/LTE or 5G). If your destination’s partner only offers 4G, you won’t get 5G even if your phone supports it.

Q2: Can I use hotspot/tethering?

A: Most plans allow hotspot sharing. However, on “unlimited” plans, after throttling, hotspot speed also drops to ~1 Mbps – at 1 Mbps, even loading regular web pages on a laptop will be painful. If you frequently need to work on a laptop, choose a large-volume pay-per-GB plan instead of an “unlimited” one.

Q3: Can I get a refund if I bought the wrong plan?

A: Yes, but with strict conditions:

  • Uninstalled eSIM: can be refunded within 30 days
  • “Bought by mistake” or “plan changed” → Airmoney only (in-app currency) , no cash
