Spain as a Starting Point for Multi-Country European Travel

Spain’s geography, transport connections, and EU membership make it a natural starting point for multi-country European trips. Its high-speed rail network connects to France via Barcelona-Perpignan, ferry services link mainland Spain to Morocco and the Balearic Islands, and budget airlines connect Spanish airports to virtually every European capital. For pricing on multi-country plans, see our Spain eSIM price comparison guide. For provider comparisons, see our Spain eSIM provider ranking. Understanding the connectivity implications of your route is essential to choosing the right eSIM.

Three factors make Spain particularly suited as a multi-country travel hub for eSIM planning:

1. EU roaming regulations apply from Spain. Since Spain is a member of the European Union, any eSIM purchased for Spain that includes EU roaming will continue to work seamlessly in Portugal, France, Italy, Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, and all 27 EU member states. Under EU “Roam Like at Home” regulations, mobile providers must offer roaming at domestic rates across the EU. However, this only applies if your eSIM provider specifically includes EU roaming as part of the plan — not all Spain eSIM plans do. Some budget Spain-only plans restrict data to Spanish networks only. Additionally, most Spain eSIMs that do include EU roaming apply “fair use” limits on how much data can be used in other EU countries. Providers set their own thresholds — some require a share of your data to be consumed in Spain before roaming is throttled, and the exact split varies widely between plans.

2. Non-EU destinations require separate coverage. Morocco, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway, and Gibraltar are not in the EU and are not covered by EU roaming agreements. Travelers visiting any of these destinations from Spain need either a separate eSIM for those specific countries or a global/regional plan that includes them. This is often overlooked by travelers who assume that being in Europe means seamless connectivity everywhere. For a Spain Morocco eSIM combination, you will need two separate plans or a global plan covering North Africa.

3. Spain’s transport infrastructure enables multi-country travel. Renfe’s AVE high-speed trains connect Madrid and Barcelona to French cities with through-services to Marseille, Lyon, and Paris. Ferry services from Algeciras and Tarifa reach Morocco in 35-60 minutes. Budget carriers like Ryanair, Vueling, Iberia Express, and easyJet connect over 20 Spanish airports to more than 200 European destinations. Your eSIM needs to work across all transit zones, including high-speed rail corridors that cross international borders and ferry routes where coverage drops mid-water.

For a Spain multi-country eSIM trip, the fundamental decision is whether to buy a single regional eSIM covering multiple countries in one data pool, or to stack individual single-country eSIMs for each destination and switch between them manually at each border. Each approach has distinct cost, convenience, and coverage trade-offs that we examine in detail throughout this guide.

The growth of multi-country travel from Spain reflects broader trends in European tourism. The expansion of high-speed rail connections, the proliferation of budget airlines operating from Spanish airports, and the increasing popularity of combined Iberian-Mediterranean itineraries have all contributed to more travelers crossing borders during a single trip. The EU’s elimination of mobile roaming charges in 2017 made multi-country connectivity more practical, but the specific implementation varies significantly between eSIM providers, creating confusion for travelers who assume all plans work the same way across borders.

Understanding the distinction between a Spain eSIM that “includes EU roaming” and a true regional Europe plan is critical. Many Spain eSIMs advertise EU roaming as a feature but apply significant restrictions — fair use data caps, reduced roaming speeds, limited partner networks, or additional per-GB costs for data used outside Spain. A true regional plan treats all countries equally within a single data pool, without distinguishing between “home” and “roaming” usage. The price difference between these two types of plans is often small, but the practical difference in multi-country usability is substantial.

Typical Multi-Country Itineraries from Spain

Understanding the most common routing patterns helps in choosing the right connectivity solution. Here are the five most frequent multi-country routes starting from Spain, with detailed connectivity implications for each.

Route 1: Best Spain Portugal eSIM Options for Western Iberia

The most common multi-country route from Spain. Travelers often combine Spain and Portugal in a single trip, typically flying into Madrid or Barcelona and traveling westward into Portugal by train, car, or bus. For this route, a Spain Portugal eSIM with EU roaming is usually sufficient.

  • Typical duration: 10-18 days
  • Border crossing: Multiple possible — Madrid to Lisbon (A6 highway or train), Seville to Faro/Algarve, Salamanca to Porto, or Merida to Evora
  • Coverage considerations: EU roaming applies seamlessly between both countries. All Spanish eSIMs with EU roaming will work in Portugal on local networks (MEO, NOS, Vodafone Portugal). No plan changes or manual switching needed.
  • Best eSIM approach: Spain eSIM with EU roaming included. No need for a separate Portugal plan. A regional Europe plan also works.
  • Border crossing behavior: At the Spain-Portugal border, your phone may briefly lose signal while it switches from a Spanish network to its Portuguese partner. The handoff is usually automatic and seamless.

Route 2: Spain France Italy eSIM for Mediterranean Rail Travel

This classic route follows the Mediterranean coast from Barcelona through the French Riviera (Nice, Cannes, Marseille) to Italy (Genoa, Cinque Terre, Florence, Rome). Travelers typically use rail — Renfe/TGV from Barcelona to Marseille, then regional trains along the coast.

  • Typical duration: 14-21 days
  • Border crossings: Spain-France at Portbou/Cerbère or La Jonquera/Le Perthus; France-Italy at Ventimiglia
  • Coverage considerations: EU roaming covers all three countries seamlessly. French networks (Orange France, SFR, Bouygues Telecom, Free Mobile) and Italian networks (TIM, Vodafone Italia, WindTre, Iliad Italia) are automatically available to Spanish eSIMs with EU roaming.
  • Connectivity on trains: The Mediterranean rail corridor has coverage in most sections but notable gaps in tunnels — the Perthus tunnel on the Spain-France border (high-speed rail) and the succession of tunnels between Monaco and Genoa on the French-Italian Riviera route. Download maps and media for these sections.
  • Specific issue: Some Spain eSIMs may not have partner agreements with Free Mobile (France) or Iliad Italia — if your eSIM connects to these networks, speeds may be slower. Multi-network eSIMs that can switch between carriers in each country avoid this.

Route 3: Cross-Border eSIM Europe for Atlantic Coast Route

Starting from northern Spain (Bilbao, San Sebastian, Santander, Santiago de Compostela), travelers head through the French Atlantic coast to Paris, then onward to Belgium and the Netherlands.

  • Typical duration: 14-21 days
  • Border crossing: Spain-France at Irun/Hendaye (basque coast) or along the AP-8/E-70
  • Coverage considerations: All EU, continuous along major rail and road routes. However, the rural sections of southwestern France (between Bordeaux and the Spanish border) have notably thinner coverage from all French carriers. Orange France has the widest rural coverage in this corridor.
  • Key caution: The French Atlantic coast between Bayonne and Bordeaux has sections where only Orange France provides reliable signal. If your Spain eSIM uses SFR or Bouygues as its French partner, expect coverage gaps in this section.

Route 4: Spain Morocco eSIM Combo for Two-Continent Travel

This increasingly popular route combines mainland Spain with a short ferry crossing to Morocco. The most common pattern is based in southern Spain (Tarifa, Algeciras, Malaga, or Seville) with 2-5 days in Tangier, Chefchaouen, or as far south as Marrakech.

  • Typical duration: 10-14 days total (including 3-5 nights in Morocco)
  • Border crossing: Ferry from Tarifa to Tangier City (about an hour) or Algeciras to Tangier Med (roughly 90 minutes)
  • Coverage implications: Morocco is NOT in the EU. A Spanish eSIM with EU roaming will NOT work in Morocco. You need a separate Morocco eSIM, a global plan that includes North Africa, or a local SIM purchased at Tangier port or airport.
  • Ferry connectivity: Most ferry services lose mobile signal a few kilometres off the Spanish coast and only regain it as they approach Morocco. Expect a stretch with no connectivity in the middle of the Strait of Gibraltar. Download ferry tickets, maps of Tangier, and hotel information before boarding.
  • Best approach: Separate Spain and Morocco eSIMs, or a global multi-region plan covering both. Regional Europe plans do not include Morocco.

Route 5: Europe Travel eSIM Spain for Alpine and UK Routes

A less common but growing route combining a Mediterranean Spain stay with Alpine Switzerland or UK city visits. This route has the most complicated connectivity profile.

  • Typical duration: 14-18 days
  • Coverage complications: Switzerland is not in the EU (EFTA member with separate roaming agreements) and the UK left the EU. A Spanish eSIM with standard EU roaming covers neither country.
  • Gibraltar visits: As a British Overseas Territory, Gibraltar has its own mobile market (Gibtelecom, Gibtel). Spanish networks typically do not cover Gibraltar, and you may inadvertently connect to Gibtelecom when near the border, potentially incurring unexpected charges if your plan does not include Gibraltar.
  • Best approach: Global eSIM covering all three regions plus the UK/CH specific allowances on some Europe plans.

Understanding fair use limits in practice

Many single-country eSIMs that include EU roaming apply “fair use” limits on how much data can be used outside the home country. Thresholds vary widely: some providers require roughly half the plan’s total data to be used in the country of purchase (Spain), while others set the share higher or lower.

For example, a 30 GB Spain eSIM whose limit is set at half its data effectively provides 15 GB for roaming across France, Italy, and Portugal. If you exceed this, speeds may be throttled to 2G/3G-era speeds or additional charges may apply. This is a critical consideration for travelers who will spend more than half their trip outside Spain. In a Madrid-Paris-Rome itinerary where you spend 4 days in Spain and 10 days in France/Italy, a Spain eSIM with fair use limits will likely throttle your data before you reach Italy.

Regional Europe eSIMs do not have this limitation since they are designed for multi-country use from the start, with no single “home” country.

Cross-Border Connectivity: Spain to Portugal, France and Italy

When you cross a land border in Europe with an eSIM, several technical processes happen behind the scenes. Understanding these helps you troubleshoot connectivity issues and set realistic expectations.

What happens at a land border crossing

  1. Your device detects the foreign network signal as you approach the border.
  2. The phone registers on the new country’s network, requiring a Location Area Update (LAU) with the home network via the visited network.
  3. This process usually takes under a minute but can stretch to a few minutes, during which you may have no data connectivity.
  4. If your eSIM profile includes roaming in the destination country, it automatically registers on a local partner network.
  5. A Spanish eSIM with EU roaming will automatically register on one of its provider’s local partner networks (e.g., Vodafone Spain connects to Vodafone Portugal and Orange Spain to Orange France, while many providers hand off to MEO or NOS in Portugal regardless of their Spanish network).

Spain-Portugal border crossings

  • Main road crossings: Badajoz-Elvas (A6 highway), Tui-Valenca do Minho (A3 highway, A-55/EN13), Vilar Formoso-Fuentes de Onoro (A62/BE-5), Rosal de la Frontera-Castro Marim (A-49/A22)
  • Rail crossings: The Madrid-Lisbon rail line (via Badajoz) and the Porto-Vigo/Santiago rail line (via Valenca)
  • Coverage handoff: Continuous along major highways with no significant coverage gaps between the two countries. The border area between Badajoz and Elvas has overlapping coverage from both countries’ networks.
  • Portuguese partner networks: MEO, NOS, and Vodafone Portugal
  • Best eSIM performance: Plans that roam onto MEO or NOS generally perform well across Portugal. Note that MEO belongs to Altice Portugal, not to Movistar.

Spain-France border crossings

  • Main road crossings: AP-7/E15 along the Mediterranean coast (La Jonquera/Le Perthus tunnel), AP-8/E5 at Irun/Hendaye (Atlantic coast), N-330 at Somport/Candanchu (central Pyrenees tunnel), N-134 at Portalet (central Pyrenees)
  • Rail crossings: High-speed rail via Perthus tunnel (Barcelona-Girona-Figueres-Perpignan), conventional rail via Irun/Hendaye, Canfranc crossing (currently freight only)
  • Coverage handoff: The Perthus tunnel (used by both high-speed rail and the AP-7 highway) and the roughly 8 km Somport tunnel have no coverage inside. Plan for brief disconnections.
  • French partner networks: Orange France, SFR, Bouygues Telecom, and Free Mobile (agreements vary by eSIM provider)
  • Best eSIM performance: Orange France provides the widest coverage, particularly along the Mediterranean coast and rural southwestern France. SFR and Bouygues are strong in urban areas but have gaps in rural border zones.

Spain-Italy (no land border)

  • There is no land border between Spain and Italy. Travelers cross by air (1-2 hour flight) or sea (ferry from Barcelona to Rome/Civitavecchia, 20+ hours or Barcelona to Genoa/Portofino, 12+ hours)
  • Coverage during flight: No cellular signal. Wi-Fi available on some airlines (paid). Plan for 2-4 hours without connectivity for a Barcelona-Rome flight.
  • Coverage during ferry: Signal drops a few kilometres from each coast. Some modern ferries on the Barcelona-Civitavecchia and Barcelona-Genoa routes have onboard Wi-Fi (usually paid and slow). The Balearia and Grimaldi Lines ferries offer Wi-Fi packages.
  • Italian partner networks: TIM (Telecom Italia) partners with Movistar Spain. Vodafone Italia partners with Vodafone Spain. WindTre and Iliad Italia are also available.

Andorra and Gibraltar: Outside the EU Roaming Zone

Two micro-territories on Spain’s borders sit outside the EU’s “Roam Like at Home” zone, and both catch travelers out. Andorra is a co-principality between France and Spain but is not an EU or EEA member, so a Spanish eSIM with EU roaming can still incur charges or lose data there. Gibraltar left the EU with the UK, and the same caution applies. If your itinerary includes either — a Pyrenees drive via Andorra, or a day trip to Gibraltar — check whether your plan lists them as covered destinations, or plan to rely on the local networks for a day.

And if you want real-world reports before committing, Reddit threads on Spain–Portugal trips are the fastest way to see how a specific plan’s roaming actually behaves at the border.

North Africa Note: Using eSIM Beyond Europe

A significant subset of Spain travelers also visit Morocco, and increasingly Tunisia and Algeria. These destinations require special consideration because they operate outside EU regulatory frameworks and have different network infrastructure.

Morocco connectivity essentials

  • Morocco has three mobile networks: Maroc Telecom (state-connected), Orange Morocco (subsidiary of Orange Group), and inwi (owned by Maroc Telecom Group)
  • 4G coverage is good in cities (Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier, Fes, Agadir) and thins quickly in rural areas
  • 5G is available in Casablanca, Rabat, and Marrakech but limited elsewhere
  • Rural coverage is significantly thinner than Spain, particularly in the Atlas Mountains, desert regions, and smaller towns
  • A Spain eSIM with EU roaming does NOT work in Morocco
  • You need either a separate Morocco eSIM, a global plan covering North Africa, or a local SIM purchased in Tangier, Casablanca, or Marrakech

Ferry crossing connectivity

The Tarifa-Tangier Ville fast ferry (about an hour) and the Algeciras-Tangier Med crossing (roughly 90 minutes to two hours) spend a stretch in the middle of the Strait of Gibraltar with no cellular signal from either country’s networks. Spanish coverage fades a few kilometres offshore, and Moroccan networks only appear near the Moroccan coast. The FRS and Trasmediterranea ferries do not offer complimentary Wi-Fi — some have paid Wi-Fi with limited bandwidth.

Practical recommendation: Download all maps, ferry tickets, accommodation confirmation, and contact information before boarding the ferry in Spain. Morocco’s immigration process at Tangier Med can require data access for online forms or communication with riads/hotels.

Choosing Between Regional and Stacked Spain eSIM Plans

The decision between a regional Europe plan and stacked single-country plans comes down to your specific itinerary, technical comfort level, and budget priorities.

When to choose a regional Europe plan

  • You are visiting 3 or more European countries
  • You are crossing borders by train (high-speed rail) and want seamless, automatic connectivity during border crossings
  • You prefer one installation and automatic network switching without manual intervention
  • You do not want to track and manage multiple eSIM profiles and expiration dates
  • You have limited time between connections and cannot afford coverage gaps at critical moments
  • Your itinerary includes countries where single-country eSIMs would be more expensive per GB

When to choose stacked single-country plans

  • You are primarily staying in Spain with only 1-2 days in another country
  • You want the absolute cheapest possible data for each country
  • You are technically comfortable manually switching eSIM profiles at each border
  • You need country-specific phone numbers for local services in each destination
  • One of your destinations is not covered by regional Europe plans (e.g., Morocco, UK for some providers)

Data pool management across countries

An important practical consideration for multi-country travel is how data pool allowances are consumed across different countries. Some regional plans deduct data from the same pool regardless of which country you are in, while others have separate allowances for “home” vs “roaming” countries.

  • True regional data pool: All data usage counts against a single allowance, regardless of country. You use 5 GB in Spain, 3 GB in France, and 4 GB in Italy — that is 12 GB from your 30 GB pool. Simple and transparent.
  • Split allowance: The plan has a “Spain allowance” and a separate “EU roaming allowance.” You might have 20 GB for Spain and 10 GB for other EU countries. Once you use the 10 GB roaming pool, data in France or Italy stops working even if you have remaining Spain data.
  • Daily roaming allowance: Some budget plans offer a fixed amount of data per day for roaming (e.g., 500 MB per day outside Spain) rather than drawing from the main pool. This is the most restrictive and least suitable for multi-country travel.

Always check which model your potential eSIM uses. True regional data pools are the most flexible and transparent for multi-country itineraries.

A note on 5G roaming availability

5G roaming across European borders is not yet universal. While 5G within Spain is widely available from all carriers, 5G roaming from a Spanish eSIM into other EU countries depends on specific inter-carrier roaming agreements. As of 2026, 5G roaming is available from many Spanish eSIM providers in France, Portugal, and Italy, but not from all of them; the rest fall back to 4G/LTE when roaming. This matters mainly for heavy data users — 4G speeds are sufficient for most browsing, messaging, and standard-definition video streaming, with the main limitations being large file downloads and ultra-HD video streaming.

Practical tips for seamless multi-country eSIM use

Beyond choosing the right plan, following these practical tips ensures smooth connectivity across borders:

  1. Install your eSIM before leaving home. Install the profile while connected to your home Wi-Fi. This avoids needing internet access at the airport or on the train to download a profile. Make sure you screenshot or save the QR code or activation code in a secure place in case you need to reinstall during your trip.

  2. Label your eSIM profiles clearly. If you have multiple eSIMs (home + Spain + Europe), label them clearly in your phone settings. Use names like “Home Number,” “Spain Data,” “Europe Travel” so you can identify them instantly when switching between lines.

  3. Download offline maps for each country. Before crossing each border, download offline Google Maps for the countries you will visit. This covers navigation during brief coverage gaps at border crossings and in tunnels on train routes.

  4. Keep a screenshot of your plan details. Take a screenshot of your plan’s coverage countries, data allowance, and validity period. When you cross into a new country and connectivity takes a moment to establish, you can refer to your screenshot to verify the plan should work there.

  5. Restart your phone at each border crossing. A full restart (not just airplane mode) at the border often helps the phone register on the new country’s network faster than waiting for automatic handoff. Do this when you arrive at a train station or after crossing by car.

  6. Carry a cheap backup plan. For non-EU destinations and emergency backup, have a small eSIM plan (1-3 GB) from a global provider installed and ready. It costs very little but provides connectivity if your primary plan has unexpected issues.

  7. Track data usage across countries. Some phone settings apps show data usage by country or region. Check periodically to ensure you are not exceeding any fair use limits for roaming data.

  8. Test connectivity on arrival in each new country. When you arrive in a new country, quickly check that your eSIM has connected by loading a webpage or sending a message. If it has not connected within 2 minutes, try restarting or manually selecting a network. Catching issues early at the border is easier than discovering problems when you need connectivity in a more remote location.

Activation and installation sequence for multi-country travelers

For multi-country itineraries, the order in which you set up your eSIM(s) matters. Follow this sequence:

Scenario A: One regional Europe plan covers the entire trip

  1. Install the regional eSIM profile at home, on Wi-Fi, before departure.
  2. The eSIM automatically connects in each country as you cross borders.
  3. No manual switching needed throughout the trip.
  4. Monitor total data usage against the plan’s single data pool.
  5. If you run low, top up the single plan — the top-up applies to all countries.

Scenario B: Stacked single-country eSIMs for each destination

  1. Install the first country’s eSIM before departure (e.g., Spain).
  2. On your last day in Spain, install the second country’s eSIM profile (e.g., France) while you still have internet access on the Spain eSIM.
  3. When you cross the border into France, go to Settings > Cellular > Default Data Line and switch to the France eSIM.
  4. Repeat this process at each border crossing.
  5. Keep a note of which eSIM profile is active for which country to avoid confusion.

Scenario C: Regional plan + separate non-EU eSIM

  1. Install the regional Europe eSIM before departure.
  2. Install the non-EU eSIM (e.g., Morocco, UK) at the same time, but keep it disabled until needed.
  3. When you travel to the non-EU country, switch the default data line to the non-EU eSIM.
  4. When returning to Spain or another EU country, switch back to the regional plan.
  5. Label both profiles clearly in your phone settings with country or region names.

How to handle the ferry crossing to Morocco (Scenario C example)

  • Before boarding the ferry in Tarifa or Algeciras, switch your phone to the Morocco eSIM.
  • Download offline maps of Tangier and your Moroccan destinations while on the Spain eSIM or ferry Wi-Fi.
  • During the crossing, expect a stretch in the middle of the Strait with no signal.
  • Upon arrival in Tangier port, the Morocco eSIM should connect within a few minutes to Maroc Telecom or Orange Morocco.
  • If it does not connect, restart the phone or manually select “Maroc Telecom” in network settings.
  • When returning to Spain, switch back to your Spain or Europe eSIM for seamless reconnection.

Frequently Asked Questions About Spain Multi-Country eSIM

What is the best eSIM for Spain and Portugal?

For a Spain-Portugal trip, the best eSIM is a Spain eSIM with EU roaming or a regional Europe plan. If you spend most of your time in Spain, a single Spain eSIM with EU roaming is most cost-effective. If you split your time evenly between both countries, a regional Europe plan with no fair use limits is safer. For a Spain Portugal eSIM specifically, look for plans that roam onto MEO or NOS for the smoothest handoff.

Does a Spain eSIM work in France?

Yes, a Spain eSIM with EU roaming works in France. When you cross the border, your eSIM automatically connects to a French partner network (Orange France, SFR, Bouygues Telecom, or Free Mobile). However, check the fair use limit — if you exceed the roaming allowance, your speeds may be throttled. For extended stays in France, a regional Europe plan is better.

What is the best eSIM for Spain and Morocco?

The best eSIM for Spain and Morocco is two separate eSIMs — one for Spain (or Europe) and one specifically for Morocco. EU roaming does NOT cover Morocco. Choose a Morocco eSIM from Maroc Telecom (widest coverage) or Orange Morocco (best city speeds). Alternatively, use a global plan that covers both regions. For a Spain Morocco eSIM combo, install both profiles before departure and switch at the ferry terminal.

Can I use a Spain eSIM in the UK?

Not automatically. The UK is not in the EU, so a Spain eSIM with standard EU roaming does NOT work in the UK. You need a separate UK eSIM, a global plan that includes the UK, or a regional Europe plan that specifically includes the UK (some do, some do not). Always verify UK coverage before purchasing.

How do I switch eSIMs at a border crossing?

On iPhone: Settings > Cellular > Default Data Line > select the new eSIM. On Android: Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > Mobile Data > select the new eSIM. For best results, switch 5-10 minutes before crossing the border or immediately after crossing. Restart your phone after switching to force network registration. Multi-network eSIMs handle this automatically without manual switching.

Does a Spain eSIM work in Switzerland?

No, unless your eSIM specifically includes Switzerland. Switzerland is not in the EU and is not covered by EU roaming regulations. Some regional Europe eSIMs include Switzerland (EFTA coverage) while others do not. Verify Switzerland is explicitly listed in the country coverage before purchasing.

What is a cross-border eSIM for Spain?

A cross-border eSIM for Spain is an eSIM that maintains connectivity across international borders. This can be a Spain eSIM with EU roaming (works in EU countries), a regional Europe plan (works in 30-40+ European countries), or a global plan (works worldwide). The key feature is automatic network switching at borders without requiring manual profile changes.

How much data do I need for a multi-country Europe trip from Spain?

For a 14-day trip visiting Spain, France, and Italy, plan for 15-25 GB total (1.5-2 GB per day for moderate use, 3-4 GB per day for heavy use). A 20-30 GB regional Europe plan provides comfortable coverage. For longer trips (21+ days), consider 30-50 GB plans. Always add a 20-30% buffer.

Is a regional Europe eSIM better than multiple single-country eSIMs?

For 3+ countries, yes. Regional Europe eSIMs offer automatic border switching, no fair use limits, and a single installation. For 2 countries with most time in Spain, a Spain eSIM with EU roaming may be cheaper. For Spain + non-EU destinations (Morocco, UK), two separate eSIMs are required.

What happens to my eSIM at the Spain-France border on the AVE train?

At the Perthus tunnel on the Madrid-Barcelona-France AVE line, coverage drops while the train is inside the tunnel and reconnects automatically on the French side within moments of exiting. Multi-network eSIMs reconnect slightly faster than single-network eSIMs due to automatic carrier selection.
