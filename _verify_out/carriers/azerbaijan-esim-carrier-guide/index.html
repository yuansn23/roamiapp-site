Most “which Azerbaijan eSIM works here” pages assume the only question is price. It isn’t. In Azerbaijan the first surprise is registration: bring a phone from abroad, put a local SIM in it, and the device itself must be registered in the national IMEI database within 30 days or it gets blocked on every Azerbaijani network. A travel eSIM that issues no local number sidesteps that entirely. So the real question this guide answers is which carrier should your Azerbaijan eSIM ride on — and do you even want a local SIM at all? The three networks are not equal: Azercell reaches the Caucasus foothills, Bakcell is the cheap urban pick, and Nar is the budget alternative.

Not sure if your handset is eSIM-ready in the first place? The eSIM compatibility checker settles that in under a minute, and the explainer on how eSIM activation works covers what happens when a profile downloads. After that, the floor belongs to the networks themselves.

The short answer: Choosing an Azerbaijan eSIM comes down to range against price. Driving the full Caucasus loop or heading to Gobustan and Sheki? Azercell has the widest footprint (it claims 99.8% population coverage). Staying in Baku and want the cheapest prepaid? Bakcell sells an 11 GB tourist line for about 15 AZN with eSIM activation on its website. Watching every manat? Nar undercuts both in the cities. All three sell prepaid to foreigners with a passport — but a local SIM triggers the 30-day IMEI rule, while Roami’s free network test rides Azercell and Bakcell with no registration.

What is Azerbaijan’s 30-day IMEI registration rule?

Azerbaijan runs a national IMEI database (imei.az / my.gov.az). The trap is specific:

  • The clock starts the first time a local SIM connects in your phone. You then have 30 days to register the handset and pay a state fee (roughly 30–100 AZN depending on the phone’s value).
  • If you don’t, the phone is blocked for all Azerbaijani SIMs — not just the one you bought.
  • The trigger is a local SIM, not your visit length. A foreign SIM in roaming does not start the clock. Neither does a data-only travel eSIM that issues no Azerbaijani number.
  • Registration is at ASAN service centres or online with BankID; you need the passport and the IMEI (dial *#06#).

For trips under 30 days on a single visit, this rarely bites. For longer stays, dual-SIM users, or anyone importing devices, it’s a real cost. An Azerbaijan eSIM that issues no local number is the clean way to stay online without touching the IMEI process.

Rural coverage in Azerbaijan: Azercell vs Bakcell

Azercell is the former state monopoly, and the reference point those coverage maps and travel profiles are built around. It claims 99.8% population coverage and is the only one of the three that travellers consistently report holding a signal on the mountain roads to Sheki, Qabala, Quba and Gabala. It also operates in the Nakhchivan exclave, which is Azerbaijani territory separated from the mainland.

How a visitor buys Azercell:

Crossing borders with an Azerbaijan eSIM: land entry is closed

This is the single most misunderstood fact about Azerbaijan among Caucasus travellers, and it changes the connectivity you actually need.

  • Azerbaijan has kept its land borders closed to passenger entry since March 2020, under a “special quarantine regime” repeatedly extended — most recently through 1 October 2026, with officials now citing security rather than the original pandemic justification.
  • Air travel is unaffected. You fly into Baku, Ganja or Nakhchivan normally.
  • Exiting overland is allowed. Foreigners can leave Azerbaijan into Georgia (Red Bridge crossing) or Russia (Samur crossing) even though entry by road is banned. A common Caucasus loop is therefore fly into Baku, leave overland to Tbilisi — but you must buy the Georgia leg’s connectivity separately.
  • A limited passenger rail service to Georgia resumed in May 2026, a specific humanitarian exception, not a general reopening.
  • The northern border with Russia, the western border with Armenia, and crossings to Turkey and Iran remain closed to tourist entry. Near those borders, your phone can grab a foreign tower and roam — turn off automatic network selection.

Practical effect on your eSIM: because you cannot drive in, your Azerbaijan eSIM only needs to cover the country itself (plus Nakhchivan if you fly there). For the onward Georgia leg, an eSIM for Georgia or a regional Caucasus plan is the right second purchase — your Azerbaijan plan will not roam into Tbilisi.

What mobile speeds and data prices to expect in Azerbaijan

Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index for August 2026 puts Azerbaijan 47th worldwide for mobile download, at a median of 99.13 Mbps and 17 ms latency. The global median that month was 109.05 Mbps, so Azerbaijan sits just below the typical market.

On price, Cable.co.uk values 1 GB of Azerbaijani mobile data at about USD 1.76 — 147th of 237 markets against a USD 2.59 global average, i.e. cheap by world standards.

For market context, DataReportal reports 9.23 million internet users in Azerbaijan (89.0% penetration) and 12.2 million mobile connections — about 118% of the population — at the start of 2025.

Azerbaijan has no published per-carrier Speedtest Connectivity Report, so operator-level speeds are left qualitative here rather than invented. The ranking above — Azercell widest, Bakcell strongest in cities, Nar cheapest — rests on operator coverage claims and independent travel testing.

Troubleshooting your Azerbaijan eSIM

Most eSIM faults are not country-specific. A QR that scans to “already used”, a profile frozen mid-download, a handset that hides the new line — work through the step-by-step activation guide and the wider eSIM activation troubleshooting guide for those. What follows is only the short set of failures you will meet here, on Azerbaijani networks.

Before you fly: setting an Azerbaijani line up properly

Two habits stop most problems before they start. Install the profile on your home Wi-Fi, not at GYD — the arrivals-hall network is heaviest exactly when your flight lands. And settle up front whether you are buying a local SIM (passport at the counter, plus the IMEI fee past 30 days) or a travel eSIM (neither). Either way, run the standard order once: add the eSIM, scan, name the line, give it mobile data, switch roaming on. A local line can only be registered with the passport holder present, so there is no way to buy one on someone else’s behalf.

Will my phone work with an Azerbaijan eSIM?

This is the 30-day IMEI rule firing, not an outage. If you fitted an Azerbaijani SIM and never entered the phone in the national database, the block lands on every local network at once — Azercell, Bakcell and Nar alike, including the line you bought. Register at an ASAN service centre or through my.gov.az with the passport and the IMEI (*#06#), pay the state fee, and service returns. A travel eSIM that issues no Azerbaijani number never starts that clock, which is why long-stay visitors move to one.

Can your phone run on Azerbaijan networks?

Along the northern approach to Russia and the western line with Armenia, the strongest mast is frequently across the border, and your Azerbaijan plan bills it as international roaming at harsh rates. Switch automatic network selection off and pin Azercell, Bakcell or Nar by hand before you get close. The same guard applies on the Caspian coast north of Baku, where a neighbouring signal bleeds across the water. If bars vanish entirely just past a frontier, you are simply outside your plan’s footprint — the onward Georgia leg is handled with a separate eSIM further up this page.

APN values for Azercell, Bakcell and Nar Mobile eSIMs

Local prepaid lines are the usual culprit. Bakcell expects internet.bakcell.com, Nar expects nar.az, and neither always lands automatically on a freshly registered handset. Re-enter the APN from the table above, reboot, and confirm mobile data is pointed at the eSIM line rather than your home SIM. Azercell users can instead request settings with a blank SMS to 9595. A travel eSIM arrives with its own APN and almost never needs this.

Why does Nar or Bakcell data stop loading?

Bakcell renews rather than throttles: once a TravelSIM bundle is spent, pay-as-you-go data runs at 0.49 AZN/MB, which drains a balance fast. Nar’s split “TouristSIM” packs ring-fence part of the allowance for social apps, so the everything-else bucket is smaller than the headline figure suggests. Check the operator app or the *777# menu before blaming the network.

An Azercell counter eSIM that will not activate

Azercell and Nar hand most visitor profiles over the shop counter, and a handset whose eSIM slot already holds a profile will reject a second one. Delete the stale line in the SIM manager first, then add the new code. If a direct Bakcell purchase stalls, remember the QR is single-use — ask the seller for a re-issued code rather than scanning the same one twice.

Azercell, by its own coverage claims (99.8% population) and by consistent traveller testing in Sheki, Qabala and the Caspian resorts. Nar is the weakest once you leave the cities.

What are the registration rules in Azerbaijan?

Only if you use a local SIM for more than 30 days. The clock starts on first local-SIM connection; register via ASAN or my.gov.az and pay a fee, or the phone is blocked on all local networks. A travel eSIM that issues no AZ number does not start the clock.

Does my Azerbaijan eSIM work in Nakhchivan?

Yes — Nakhchivan is Azerbaijani territory and Azercell operates there, so a Bakcell/Azercell travel eSIM and Azercell local SIM both work. Just remember you reach the exclave by air or via Turkey/Iran land, not by road from Baku.
