Phone requirements for a United States eSIM

Three things decide whether your phone works on a US carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Which 5G bands do US carriers use?

US carriers build their 5G on different bands, and the differences decide whether you see fast 5G or fall back to LTE. T-Mobile leans on n71 (600 MHz) for rural reach and n41 (2.5 GHz) for mid-band speed. Verizon uses n77 (C-band) for mid-band and n260/n261 (mmWave) in dense downtowns and stadiums. AT&T uses n5 (850 MHz) low-band and n77 mid-band. Plenty of international handsets ship without n77, which is the band AT&T and Verizon depend on for mid-band speed.

You can skip the band tables entirely. The dependable check is to compare the exact model number against the eSIM device checker. For the mechanics ahead of the purchase, see what happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile.

Will United States carriers work with your phone?

A locked phone is the most common reason a US eSIM install simply fails, and the rules here are cleaner than many visitors expect.

Under the CTIA Consumer Code, US carriers must unlock devices, but the timing differs by plan type. Postpaid phones unlock once the device financing is paid off and the account is in good standing. Prepaid phones unlock no later than one year after activation. Carriers unlock “upon request” rather than automatically in most cases. The one exception travelers used to rely on — Verizon’s 60-day automatic unlock — was waived by the FCC in January 2026, so a Verizon phone activated after that ruling may stay locked to Verizon’s network for up to a year like other prepaid devices. That rollback matters if you buy a phone in the US and expect to roam its eSIM elsewhere quickly.

Where: iPhone, Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock. When the field reads “No SIM restrictions”, the device is free and clear. A “SIM locked” reading stops any third-party profile from installing until the lock is cleared.

Fix it: call the carrier that sold you the phone and ask for the unlock code. Paid-off postpaid devices are normally cleared within a couple of business days, and there is no fee. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone from a US carrier on a payment plan, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. The FCC publishes a plain-language guide to unlocking your mobile phone that explains the commitments carriers made under the CTIA code.

How much do US eSIM plans cost?

Four routes exist, and they differ more in paperwork than in price: T-Mobile’s tourist eSIM, AT&T prepaid or Cricket, Verizon prepaid or Visible, and the T-Mobile budget brands Metro and Mint. Each route, step by step:

How to buy T-Mobile’s tourist eSIM

T-Mobile is the only national carrier with a prepaid eSIM product built specifically for short-term visitors.

T-Mobile tourist eSIM, step by step:

  1. Download the T-Mobile Prepaid eSIM app and choose a plan. The US Pass tourist tiers give 30 days of unlimited talk, text and data starting at $50, plus taxes and fees.
  2. Activate inside the app. T-Mobile states no ID required and no paperwork — you pay with a domestic or international card from select countries.
  3. You must be in the United States for the plan to activate, and you need Wi-Fi to install the profile. Plans start the moment you buy them, so purchase on the day you intend to use service.
  4. Once active, you get hotspot data, Wi-Fi calling, scam-blocking and unlimited talk, text and data across the US, Canada and Mexico on T-Mobile’s network.

T-Mobile markets this directly at international travelers, and the app checks eSIM compatibility before you pay. Regular T-Mobile prepaid lines also support eSIM, but the tourist route is the one designed for someone who has not landed yet and has no US billing address.

How to buy AT&T or Cricket as a visitor

AT&T sells its own prepaid eSIM, and it also owns Cricket Wireless, which is the lower-friction visitor path.

For an AT&T prepaid eSIM, you sign up through AT&T’s prepaid pages or the myAT&T app, pick a plan, and install the profile by QR code or in-app activation. A credit check is not required for prepaid, but AT&T’s checkout can reject some international cards, so budget for a payment-method hiccup.

Cricket is simpler for most visitors:

  • Cricket runs on AT&T’s network and offers eSIM activation with no credit check.
  • Entry pricing is $30 per month for 5 GB, with unlimited tiers at $55 and above.
  • A physical SIM carries a one-time fee in a Cricket store, but the eSIM route is digital.
  • Cricket covers the US, Canada and Mexico on select plans, which is useful if your trip crosses a border.

If you only want AT&T coverage as a visitor and do not need an AT&T-branded account, Cricket gets you the same towers with less paperwork.

How to buy Verizon or Visible as a visitor

Verizon sells a prepaid eSIM through verizon.com and the My Verizon app, delivered by QR code email for compatible devices. Postpaid Verizon plans want a US credit check and a US address, so visitors normally land on prepaid or on Verizon’s budget brand Visible.

Visible is the easy Verizon route:

  • Visible is an online-only Verizon brand — there are no stores, so everything happens in the app.
  • Plans start at $25 per month for unlimited data, hotspot at 5 Mbps, and SD video, or $45 per month (Visible+) for Ultra Wideband 5G and 10 Mbps hotspot.
  • eSIM activation is supported, and there is no contract.
  • The trade-off is support: with no store, you rely on chat, text or social channels.

Visible gives you Verizon’s network and the widest geographic coverage in the country, at a price a tourist can buy for one month and walk away from.

US eSIM APN settings: what to enter and when

Only a handful of values matter here, and you will need them only if automatic configuration fails. Below: the values for the three national carriers, when manual entry is genuinely required, and the exact menu paths.

You will only need these if you bought a SIM or eSIM directly from a US carrier or its prepaid brand. Travel eSIM profiles bring their own US APN and should be left as they are.

Carrier APN Username Password

Budget brands inherit their parent’s network values: Visible uses Verizon’s vzwinternet, Cricket uses ndo, Metro by T-Mobile uses fast.metropcs.com, and Mint Mobile uses wholesale. Leave username and password empty unless the activation instructions that came with your profile state otherwise.

When does a US eSIM need manual APN entry?

T-Mobile and the MVNOs usually provision the APN for you, so a manual entry is a fallback rather than a step in the setup. It is worth doing when:

  • A device too dated to pick up carrier settings automatically
  • A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
  • A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
  • Almost never on a travel eSIM — that is the point of a managed profile

Where to enter APN settings on your phone

  • iPhone: Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → enter the APN
  • Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → add a new APN

After saving, restart the phone. If data is still dead, confirm the active data line is the US eSIM rather than your home SIM.

Three fixes when your United States eSIM does not work

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our eSIM troubleshooting resource. The patterns below are the US-specific ones.

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Make sure no lock is present: About -> Carrier Lock shows “No SIM restrictions”
  2. Look at the QR: legible, and never scanned before
  3. For T-Mobile’s tourist eSIM, confirm you are physically in the US, since activation is location-checked

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status
  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → select T-Mobile, Verizon or AT&T manually instead of automatic
  3. Full network settings reset, then another reboot

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Put the APN back in precisely as the table lists it (common on AT&T and Verizon prepaid)
  2. Verify the US eSIM is the active data line and not your home SIM.
  3. Check you have not exhausted your data allowance — budget brands deprioritize rather than cutting off

United States eSIM carrier questions from real travellers

Can international visitors buy a T-Mobile eSIM?

Yes, and T-Mobile is the easiest national route. Its Prepaid tourist eSIM and US Pass plans are built for short-term visitors: no ID, no US address, activation in the T-Mobile Prepaid eSIM app, with 30 days of unlimited talk, text and data from $50. The one rule is that activation happens while you are in the United States, so install it on arrival Wi-Fi rather than at home.

Does a Verizon eSIM require a US address or SSN?

For a Verizon prepaid eSIM, no SSN is required, and sign-up is online. A full postpaid Verizon plan is a different story — that route wants a US credit check and a US billing address. Most visitors are better served by Verizon prepaid eSIM or by Visible, Verizon’s online-only brand, which sells unlimited data from $25 per month with eSIM activation and no store visit.

Can I get an AT&T prepaid eSIM without a US credit check?

Yes. AT&T prepaid eSIM needs no credit check, though AT&T’s own checkout can reject some foreign cards. The smoother visitor path is usually Cricket Wireless, which AT&T owns: Cricket runs on AT&T’s network, supports eSIM with no credit check, and starts at $30 per month for 5 GB. You get AT&T coverage without an AT&T account.

Rural coverage in United States: T-Mobile vs Verizon

Verizon, on the evidence. It recorded the Best Mobile Coverage in the United States with a Coverage Score of 30.0% on Ookla’s 2H 2025 report — the share of the country’s geography it reaches, which is the closest thing to a rural-reliability measure. T-Mobile is faster where it has signal, and AT&T sits between them, but for long drives through the Midwest and interstates, Verizon holds the longest stretches.

What are the cheapest US eSIM options?

Visible, at $25 per month for unlimited data on Verizon’s network, or Mint Mobile at a $15 per month intro rate on T-Mobile’s network if you pay for several months upfront. Cricket starts at $30 per month for 5 GB on AT&T. All three are prepaid with no contract, so you can stop after one month. Watch for deprioritization during congestion rather than a hard data cut-off.

Which phones have problems with US travel eSIMs?

It needs to be unlocked and eSIM-capable, and it helps if it supports the right bands — T-Mobile’s n71 and n41, or AT&T and Verizon’s n77 mid-band. Check both in one pass with the eSIM compatibility page, which covers EID support and the band question. If you want the install sequence explained first, read the eSIM activation explainer.

How do I get my locked phone unlocked in the United States?

Ask the carrier it is locked to. Under the CTIA Consumer Code, postpaid phones unlock once device financing is paid and the account is in good standing; prepaid phones unlock within a year of activation; carriers unlock on request. Verizon’s old 60-day automatic unlock was waived by the FCC in January 2026, so newer Verizon phones may stay locked longer. Paid-off devices are usually cleared within a couple of business days. Look for the lock line at Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock.

Should I buy from a US carrier or use a travel eSIM?

Direct means one network, carrier-issued provisioning, and often a US number and unlimited domestic calling in exchange for more paperwork. A travel eSIM means instant delivery, no paperwork, automatic switching between T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T, and a fixed upfront price. Short trip: travel eSIM. Months in the US, or you need a local number: direct.

Do United States carriers offer tourist specific eSIM plans?

T-Mobile does — it markets a tourist eSIM and US Pass plans for international visitors, with no ID, from $50 for 30 days of unlimited service. AT&T and Verizon route visitors to prepaid tiers and their brands Cricket and Visible rather than a labeled “tourist” product, but those prepaid eSIMs work the same way for a short stay.

Can I keep two eSIMs active at once?

Yes, and it is a sensible setup. Keep the home SIM for calls and text verification, and let the US eSIM carry the data. Set the US profile as the data line in Settings and leave voice on your home SIM. Watch two things: switch roaming off on the home line to avoid surprise charges, and remember that most data-only profiles cannot receive texts, so verification codes go to your home number.

How does eSIM activation work on US carriers?

Nothing gets swapped or mailed: the eSIM is soldered into the handset and a US carrier simply pushes your profile onto it over the air. From then on the line behaves like any ordinary SIM, complete with the carrier’s APN and roaming rules. The delivery chain and the one-scan QR limit are set out in eSIM activation explained.
