A Belarus eSIM rewards a different first question than the speed leagues ask. Most eSIM guides rank countries by speed; Belarus is better understood through price and paperwork. Local data is exceptionally cheap — Cable.co.uk’s 237-market index puts 1 GB at about USD 0.67, 66th worldwide — yet the median mobile download was just 18.55 Mbps at end-2025, per DataReportal. We keep this Belarus page updated as carrier policies shift, because the worst travel tech advice is a guide nobody has touched in two years.

The network is not the problem. Every SIM sold inside the country is registered to a passport, and the cards in your wallet may not work at the till. In Belarus the paperwork is the product, and roughly 11.4 million mobile connections serve that market.

If the basics are still open questions — does my phone support eSIM, and what actually happens during activation — the eSIM device checker and eSIM activation mechanics answer both. This page narrows the question to one thing: which carrier is worth paying for.

If you read nothing else: A1 Belarus and MTS run the two widest 4G networks and both cover every regional capital; life:) is the smaller third network and the usual budget pick. All three sell prepaid plans from roughly 30–40 BYN per month, but all three require passport registration in person, and foreign Visa and Mastercard have largely been unusable since 2022 — so budget for cash. A travel Belarus eSIM that roams onto A1, MTS or life:) costs around USD 12–27 for 10 GB and needs no local registration. New customers can try a free eSIM first.

The Belarus eSIM carriers: A1, MTS and life:)

A1 Belarus vs MTS Belarus vs life:) for visitors

Belarus has exactly three mobile operators, each under a different flag. MTS Belarus is majority-owned by Beltelecom, the state telecoms group, with Russia’s MTS holding the rest. A1 Belarus — still widely called velcom — belongs to Austria’s A1 Group. life:) is a Turkcell business. All three are licensed by the Ministry of Communications, and since 2025 all three ride on 5G infrastructure built by beCloud.

The Belarus carrier lineup

At Minsk National Airport, all three operators staff counters in Terminal 2 arrivals. Traveller reports through 2025 describe a consistent pattern: hand over your passport, pay in cash or with a local card, and leave with a working tourist plan in minutes. The bundles are unlimited-data packages with a large calling allowance — around 40 BYN for A1 and MTS, about 30 BYN for life:)’s “Like Home” plan with 100 GB and 700 minutes.

Two caveats. Activation is generally prompt, but because the SIM is tied to your passport, fixing problems later means returning to a branded store with the same ID. And some sources describe stricter handling — a local address, or a wait before the line goes live — so do not build a same-hour connection into a tight itinerary unless you installed a travel eSIM before flying.

Which network has the best 5G in Belarus?

Guides claiming A1 leads Belarusian 5G with 200 Mbps medians describe a trial, not a rollout. A1 switched on one 3500 MHz (n78) base station in Minsk for three days around the 9 May 2025 parade, and beCloud — the designated single 5G builder — ran testing in central Minsk that December.

From 1 January 2026, life:) became the first to give subscribers commercial 5G access on beCloud’s network, with more than 1,000 base stations in Minsk, Brest, Vitebsk, Mahiliou and Homiel. At launch it worked only on some Android handsets, and the national target runs to 2034. For a 2026 visitor: expect 4G LTE on any Belarus eSIM sold today, enjoy 5G in central districts if your phone supports it, and treat any “nationwide 5G” claim as marketing.

Paying for your eSIM: cards, cash and vouchers

Belarus eSIM top-ups: which payment routes work for a visitor

Payment, not price, is what stops people. Visa, Mastercard and American Express cards issued outside Belarus largely stopped working in 2022, when Belarusian banks lost access to the international payment system. The U.S. State Department’s advisory says it plainly: some vendors cannot accept Western cards, and ATMs may be out of service or levy high fees.

Traveller reports from late 2025 are more optimistic — several describe foreign cards working at ATMs and contactless terminals in Minsk — so the honest summary is a range that depends on which bank operates the terminal.

Belarus top-up vouchers: why they still need a shop visit

For mobile services the failure mode is simple: you cannot reliably top up a local prepaid Belarus eSIM with a foreign card. What works is the paper route. Retail vouchers are sold at shops and kiosks nationwide, and the scratch code is redeemed through the operator’s published SMS or call command, or entered in the operator’s app.

ЕРИП kiosks — the national payment system’s self-service terminals, common in shops and metro stations — take cash for a long list of payees. Neither route works from home. Cash in USD or EUR, exchanged at a bank or bureau on arrival, is the dependable answer, and it is why a travel Belarus eSIM bought online has become the default for short stays.

Registering your eSIM or SIM: the ten-day rule

What does registering a Belarus SIM involve?

Belarusian law requires each SIM card to be registered to an identity document, and separately requires visitors to register their stay. Both bite. The vendor scans your passport, records your details and activates the line on the spot; some operators also ask for a local address. Tourists report five to forty minutes, depending on the queue and the clerk.

The stay-registration rule is the one visitors miss, because it is a migration obligation rather than a telecom one.

The consequence is asymmetry. A physical SIM bought at a counter gives you a local +375 number and the country’s famously cheap data, but binds you to a shop visit, a passport and cash. A travel eSIM bought online gives you none of that — the profile is issued outside Belarus, nothing is registered locally, and it simply roams onto A1, MTS or life:) on arrival. For trips under two weeks, the travel Belarus eSIM is now the default.

Is there paperwork for SIMs in Belarus?

Belarus runs a free, real-time online registration service for foreign visitors, and it is the least painful version of the process. The service code on the portal is 200.12.14.1.

  1. Create or open a personal account on the Central Electronic Services Portal (E-Pasluga) with an email address and password, or with a Google or Apple sign-in.
  2. Find the registration service under visas and travel, using the code above.
  3. Fill in the electronic form exactly as your travel document reads: surname, given names, date of birth, sex, nationality, and the series and number of the passport you entered on.
  4. State your entry purpose correctly — tourism, business, a private visit — and give the address where you are actually staying.
  5. Attach the required documents, including your health insurance policy details and your Belarusian visa information where applicable.
  6. Send the application and read the reply. The system decides in real time and posts the result to your personal account, including your registration number and the registered period.
  7. Save the confirmation and keep it with your passport until you leave.

Two limits. The portal is not open to foreigners who entered from Russian territory — there is no border control there, so they must register in person. And the online route is free, while the in-person route carries a fee paid through ЕРИП kiosks or at a state bank, where foreign cards are not accepted.

Your eSIM compatibility: bands, locks and phones

Does your phone pass the eSIM check for Belarus?

All three operators built 4G on the same European band set: LTE band 3 (1800 MHz), band 7 (2600 MHz) and band 20 (800 MHz), with GSM 900/1800 and 3G on 2100 MHz in the background. A1’s trials and life:)’s commercial 5G run on n78 at 3500 MHz. Band 20 does the rural work — it travels far and penetrates buildings — while bands 3 and 7 carry urban capacity. Any phone sold in Europe, and most global flagships, attaches without thought.

Three checks before you spend money. First, carrier lock: a phone locked to a home operator will refuse any third-party profile, so confirm Settings shows “No SIM restrictions”. Second, eSIM support itself: dial *#06# and look for an EID, or check your model against the handset compatibility checker. Third, the China-market caveat — iPhones sold in mainland China have eSIM hardware disabled, and no profile will install.

Belarus eSIM coverage: Minsk versus everywhere else

Belarus mobile speeds: what the cities and regions measure

National numbers undersell the cities and oversell the countryside. DataReportal’s reading of Ookla data puts the median mobile download at 18.55 Mbps for end-2025 — up 54.8 percent in a year — while Ookla’s city rankings place Minsk near 36.8 Mbps and Gomel near 39.4 Mbps.

The capitals therefore run at roughly double the national median, and the gap between Minsk’s boulevards and the southern forests is the biggest variable in your experience of a Belarus eSIM.

Russia roaming is the exception on a Belarus eSIM

The one border where your Belarusian eSIM keeps working on ordinary terms is the eastern one. Russia and Belarus permanently abolished roaming charges between them from 1 March 2025, under a Union State resolution agreed in Minsk in December 2024. The guarantee is at least 300 minutes of voice — 150 outgoing, 150 incoming — plus 5 GB of data a month at home-level tariffs, with extra data around 4 Belarusian rubles per gigabyte. Incoming Union State calls had been free since April 2022.

Two practical notes. The arrangement covers subscribers of the two countries’ operators, so it does not help a visitor holding a European or American SIM — for those, both countries are simply expensive roaming. And an EU plan bought for a Baltic leg will not carry into Belarus, because roam-like-at-home applies to EU residents travelling within the EU and EEA. Your options across the frontier are a separate profile per country, or a regional plan that names Belarus explicitly.

Activating your Belarus eSIM and fixing common failures

The universal install sequence — add eSIM, scan the QR code, label the line, set it as your data line, enable roaming — is walked through in the activation guide. Install at home on Wi-Fi; Minsk airport’s arrivals Wi-Fi is exactly the wrong place to be fighting a profile download.

A. Installed, but no signal at arrival. The profile needs roaming enabled for the eSIM line — Belarus sits outside the EU frameworks your phone may assume. Toggle roaming on, then set manual network selection and pick A1, MTS or life:) explicitly.

B. Signal bars, but no internet. Check the APN against the table above if you bought direct, and confirm the eSIM rather than your home SIM is the active data line. Then check your allowance: travel plans stop when data runs out, while some local plans throttle, so a crawling connection can mean an exhausted package rather than a broken one.

C. The local SIM stops working mid-trip. Registration-linked lines can be suspended over identity mismatches, and prepaid credit can lapse silently when a package expires. There is no app-based fix: take your passport back to a branded store. This is the failure mode a travel eSIM avoids by design.

D. Speeds collapse in the regions. A day’s driving between regional towns explains this: it is geography, not a fault. Your better move is walking toward a town or an open main road, not tweaking the handset.

What travelers ask about eSIMs in Belarus

Does MTS Belarus sell eSIMs to tourists?

Not as a remote product. MTS supports eSIM on its network, and its airport and city counters can set up tourist plans, but every purchase involves passport registration in person. The travel eSIM market exists because none of the three sells no-registration eSIMs to foreigners online.

What data allowance fits a Belarus trip?

Mostly no, with exceptions. Foreign-issued Visa and Mastercard have largely been unusable since 2022 under sanctions on Belarusian banks, though some 2025 travellers report ATM and contactless successes in Minsk. Plan on cash for any local purchase or top-up, and pay for a Belarus eSIM before departure.

Cheapest data plans in Belarus

Very. At about USD 0.67 per gigabyte, Belarus ranks 66th of 237 markets in Cable.co.uk’s index — cheaper than Germany at USD 2.14, and far below the USD 2.59 global average. The catch is access, not price: getting and paying for a local SIM is the hard part.

City vs countryside coverage in Belarus

MTS is the most frequently cited choice beyond the regional capitals, with A1 a close second on the main corridors; sources disagree on which is wider. life:) is weakest outside cities. All three cover the six regional capitals, and the Polesie forests and Belovezhskaya Pushcha have dead zones on every network.

Where 5G works in Belarus

Yes, but narrowly. life:) opened commercial 5G to its subscribers on 1 January 2026 over beCloud’s network, with 1,000-plus stations in five cities; A1 trialled 5G in Minsk in May 2025 on n78. Coverage is limited to central districts and select Android models, and the national rollout runs to 2034.

Do I have to register my stay in Belarus?

If you are staying longer than ten days, yes. Registration happens within ten days of arrival at a Citizenship and Migration Department, or free through E-Pasluga, and hotels usually arrange it for guests. Visitors who entered from Russia must do it in person, and citizens of Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Ukraine and the UAE get 30 days instead of ten.

Can I use my Belarus eSIM in Russia?

If it is a Belarusian operator’s SIM, effectively yes: the two countries abolished roaming charges on 1 March 2025, guaranteeing at least 300 voice minutes and 5 GB a month on home-level terms. A travel eSIM bought outside Belarus is a different contract — check whether your provider’s plan names Russia before assuming it carries.

IMEI and EID checks for a Belarus eSIM

Your phone will almost certainly work, if it is unlocked and eSIM-capable. The LTE bands — 3, 7 and 20 — are standard European ones, and 5G uses n78 at 3500 MHz. Check the model against the compatibility page and confirm there is no carrier lock first.

Do I need to register a travel eSIM in Belarus?

No. The SIM-registration requirement applies to SIMs sold inside Belarus by local operators. A travel eSIM profile is issued and paid for outside the country, so nothing is registered locally.

Belarus data plans explained

You can use both at once, and it is the sensible setup. Keep your home SIM live for calls and bank codes, point mobile data at the eSIM line, and switch off roaming on the home line so background traffic cannot bill you. Tethering a laptop to the eSIM’s hotspot works like it does on any other data line.

What’s the going rate for data in Belarus?

Reported prices through 2025 cluster around 30–40 BYN (roughly USD 9–12) for a month of unlimited data with calling minutes, depending on operator and plan version. Prices differ between counters and change often, so treat those figures as a range — and remember payment is cash or a local card, not your foreign Visa.

How well does a Belarus eSIM work underground and indoors?

Better than the national average suggests, on both counts. Minsk metro platforms have continuous coverage on all three networks, with the usual short drop between stations. Indoors, band 20 at 800 MHz carries most of the load: it penetrates walls well but runs slower than bands 3 and 7. In older masonry buildings, expect a Belarus eSIM to hold at low speed rather than disappear.
