Montenegro has quietly become one of Europe’s most generous tourist-data markets: 500 GB for €15 over 15 days from any of its three operators, with 5G from all of them and eSIMs you can buy online before you even land. It also has a trap that catches visitors who buy a kiosk SIM without registering it — and it is not in the EU, so European travelers who assume their home plan roams here at no cost get a painful surprise. This guide covers the three carriers with their actual tourist plans, the Western Balkans roaming that turns one eSIM into a six-country pass, and the mountains-vs-coast coverage split that decides your choice. The sticking points below are the ones that trip up first-time Montenegro eSIM users most often.

Quick answer: Straight to the point: eSIM compatibility is the safe default for coverage across Montenegro, with the local networks usually cheaper in and near the main cities. The tables further down carry the proof — and the eSIM compatibility quiz answers the handset question before you commit.

Unsure about your phone? The eSIM device checker is the one-minute prerequisite.

Fast take: m:tel has the best rural and mountain coverage; Crnogorski Telekom (T-Mobile Montenegro) has the widest overall map and is the safest pick for Durmitor and the north; One (the former Telenor) is strong on the coast with 5G in Budva, Kotor and Podgorica. All three sell tourist eSIMs online — m:tel via a 2–3 minute ID-plus-selfie check — with 500 GB/15 days at €15, 1 TB/30 days at €20 and unlimited tiers around €25. Registration with passport or ID is mandatory by law. Montenegro is not in the EU: EU roam-like-at-home does not apply, but local tourist packs include Western Balkans roaming, and Crnogorski Telekom’s plans uniquely add Croatia.

Which Montenegro carrier has the best rural coverage?

m:tel (a Telekom Srbija group company) is the operator locals trust off the pavement — best-in-market coverage through rural villages, national parks and the mountain interior. Its tourist line is the market’s anchor: Turist 15 at €15 for 500 GB over 15 days including 8–10 GB of Western Balkans roaming (Serbia, Bosnia, North Macedonia, Albania, Kosovo), Turist 20 at €20 for 1 TB over 30 days, and a Super Turist unlimited plan around €25–30 for 45 days. The eSIM is bought directly online: identity verification with ID and a selfie takes about 2–3 minutes, and the profile activates immediately.

The regulator is the Agency for Electronic Communications (EKIP), which licenses all three operators.

Crnogorski Telekom eSIM coverage

Crnogorski Telekom (T-Mobile Montenegro, part of Croatian Telekom/Deutsche Telekom) holds the country’s widest overall coverage and is the safest choice in the mountains — Durmitor’s approaches and the north generally — plus well-developed 5G in Podgorica and along the coast. Its tourist plans mirror m:tel’s structure (500 GB/€15, 1 TB/€20, unlimited/€25), with one genuine differentiator: its roaming includes Croatia, which no other local operator matches. Doing Dubrovnik plus the Bay of Kotor in one trip? That single feature can save you a second eSIM.

Strongest 5G in Montenegro: which carrier?

One — rebranded from Telenor in 2022 — is the third peer, with excellent 5G in Podgorica, Budva and Kotor, the same 500 GB/1 TB/unlimited tourist ladder, Western Balkans roaming included, and HBO Max bundled free for three months on tourist packages. Its rural and mountain coverage is a step behind m:tel and Telekom. A niche gem: the “One for Two” package gives two travelers 500 GB each for roughly €22 — the cheapest couples’ connectivity in the Balkans.

Where travelers buy Montenegro eSIMs

Yes — and Montenegro is one of the few markets where the carriers have built a genuine online tourist product. All three sell directly to visitors through their own websites, with identity verification handled remotely. m:tel’s check — ID plus a selfie — takes two to three minutes, and the profile is delivered as a QR code that installs on your phone before you fly.

The alternative, buying in person, works too — with a caveat. Official outlets and stores register your passport or ID electronically on the spot. Kiosks and newsstands are a different story entirely, covered in the next section.

Do I need to register my SIM in Montenegro?

This is the most valuable piece of practical information in this guide: in Montenegro, a SIM bought at a kiosk, newsstand or petrol station must be registered before it goes into your phone. Insert an unregistered SIM into a handset and the operator’s system invalidates it — you will need to take the SIM to a store with your passport to sort it out. Registration is mandatory by law at official outlets, where staff handle it electronically on the spot; m:tel additionally requires a selfie with your ID even for its eSIM check.

Will my EU plan roam free in Montenegro?

Montenegro is not an EU member. Consequences, in order of pain:

  1. Your EU plan roams here at international rates — often several euros per megabyte on some home carriers. EU residents: check before you rely on it.

  2. Local tourist packs include Western Balkans roaming — Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, North Macedonia, Albania and Kosovo — which makes one Montenegro eSIM the connectivity for a six-country drive.

  3. Crnogorski Telekom adds Croatia to that list, uniquely.

What Montenegro’s non-EU roaming means for three traveler types

  • EU residents: your home plan is the trap, not the solution. A €15 local tourist eSIM will usually cost less than a single day of your carrier’s non-EU roaming.

  • UK, US, Canadian and Australian visitors: you were never covered by roam-like-at-home, so nothing changes — buy the local pack or a travel eSIM as you would anywhere.

  • Regional road-trippers: a Montenegrin pack with Western Balkans roaming is arguably the single best connectivity purchase in the region. One purchase, six countries.

Price context: Cable.co.uk’s worldwide survey prices Montenegrin mobile data at roughly USD 0.32 per GB — top-25 cheapest of 237 markets — and DataReportal’s Digital 2025 data counts 1.43 million mobile connections, 224% of the population. Ookla’s Global Index shows fixed medians around 91 Mbps — a fast, cheap, competitive little market.

How Montenegro’s geography shapes your eSIM coverage decision

Montenegro compresses a coastline, a limestone massif and deep canyons into a country you can drive across in half a day — and the network map tightens in exactly the places people go for the scenery.

  • The Bay of Kotor is a signal trap. The mountains rise straight from the water, and the town’s own walls and narrow streets add shadowing. You will have signal on the water and lose it behind the next headland.

  • Kotor’s cruise-ship days congest the coastal cells. The old town fills when a ship docks, and data slows with it. Not a fault — a scheduling fact you can plan around.

  • Durmitor’s passes are the test. The town of Žabljak is covered; the high passes on the way are not. Offline maps are the correct preparation, not a bigger data plan.

  • National parks are the rural differentiator. This is where m:tel’s advantage is visible, and where the cheapest-carrier impulse costs you.

Airport notes: both Podgorica (TGD) and Tivat (TIV) have operator desks or kiosks during arrival hours, though sources disagree on how reliable the Tivat counter is for late flights — one more reason a pre-flight Montenegro eSIM is the low-risk route.

The four cases where an APN matters

Montenegro eSIM tourist profiles bought online arrive pre-configured, so these are the exceptions rather than the rule:

  • You bought a carrier line rather than a managed travel eSIM

  • A profile installed from a manual activation code instead of a QR scan

  • An older handset that did not pull carrier settings automatically

  • You crossed into a Western Balkans country and the line did not re-register

Where the APN field hides on your phone

On iPhone the field hides at Settings → Cellular → [the eSIM line] → Cellular Data Network; on Android it is under Settings → Network & Internet → Access Point Names. Save, restart, then confirm the Montenegrin line — m:tel, Telekom or One — is the one selected for data.

Walkthrough: activating a Montenegro eSIM

  1. Order the eSIM online and install at home. It activates on first contact with a local tower — data live at Tivat or Podgorica arrivals without touching a counter.

  2. Never insert an unregistered kiosk SIM. It gets invalidated by the system; a store visit with your passport fixes it, but the eSIM route avoids the whole problem.

  3. Manual network selection helps in the mountains. Forcing the phone onto Telekom or m:tel can attach where automatic search finds nothing.

  4. Crossing into Croatia or Albania resets the picture. Toggle airplane mode to force a fresh scan, and remember only Telekom’s plans include Croatia.

  5. Signal but no data: data-line setting, roaming toggle, then APN — the full symptom walkthrough is in our eSIM activation fixes guide.

A Montenegro eSIM that will not work: four fixes

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. One gate first: Carrier Lock in Settings -> General -> About saying “No SIM restrictions”

  2. Check nobody has scanned the code before – QRs are usually single-use

  3. Ask whether support can re-send the profile

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Power the eSIM line down and back up

  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose a carrier manually

  3. Reboot once, and if it persists, wipe network settings

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Cross-check the APN against the carrier table earlier in this section

  2. Make the eSIM the data line

  3. Roaming has to be on for the eSIM line specifically

  4. Check remaining data – the allowance might be spent

D. A locally bought line died within minutes

  1. This is the unregistered-SIM trap

  2. Take the SIM and your passport to an official operator store

  3. Have the line registered and restored

  4. Consider switching to an online tourist eSIM instead of repeating the cycle
