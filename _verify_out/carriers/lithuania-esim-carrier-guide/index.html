A Lithuania eSIM is easy in the cities and subtle at the border, and this guide explains the Latvia-and-Poland crossing rule almost nobody covers. Lithuania quietly runs one of the better mobile markets in Eastern Europe: three competitive national operators, 5G across the major cities, and data priced well below the global average. It is also a country shaped by its borders — most visitors arrive for Vilnius but end up crossing into Latvia or Poland at some point, and what happens to your data connection at that border depends on a distinction almost nobody explains. This guide starts with the economics, then works through the operators, the practicalities of buying and paying, the airports, the coast, and the border-crossing logic. How much does mobile data cost in Lithuania?

Start with the number that shapes everything else. Cable.co.uk’s worldwide data pricing survey — 5,600-plus plans across 237 markets — prices 1 GB of Lithuanian mobile data at about USD 1.26, 117th cheapest in the world. The global average sits at USD 2.59, so Lithuania lands comfortably in the cheaper half, and the regional context is even more telling: neighboring Latvia averages USD 3.21 (the only Baltic state above the global mean), while Estonia manages USD 1.15. Lithuania sits in the middle of a bargain basement.

For a traveler, cheap local data translates into generous prepaid packs: operators compete by stuffing tens of gigabytes into entry-level bundles rather than metering every megabyte the way Western European carriers do. It also means a Lithuania eSIM is priced against a genuinely low-cost baseline — you are not paying a premium to subsidize an expensive domestic market, the way you would in Canada or the United States.

The network behind those prices is solid. Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index measured a median mobile download of 123.17 Mbps in August 2026 — 36th worldwide — at 19 ms latency, with fixed broadband at 187.94 Mbps. DataReportal’s Digital 2025: Lithuania counts 3.90 million active mobile connections at the start of 2025, about 137 percent of the population, and 2.55 million internet users (89.5 percent penetration). In a country of 2.85 million people, that is a market where mobile connectivity is the default, not a luxury.

Telia, Bitė and Tele2 eSIM options compared

Lithuania’s market is a clean three-way race, regulated by the Communications Regulatory Authority of the Republic of Lithuania (RRT). Each operator has a distinct personality, and each sells eSIM service directly — unusual for a market this size and good news for anyone provisioning a line locally.

Operator Character Best for
Telia Lietuva Largest operator; the widest rural and highway reach of the three Road trips, the Curonian Spit, anywhere outside the big four cities
Bitė Aggressive 5G rollout and the fastest urban medians Vilnius and Kaunas stays, video calls, streaming
Tele2 Value-focused, with dependable 4G in every sizeable town Budget trips and light data users

Telia’s footprint is the practical default for anyone leaving the cities — coverage holds along the A1 highway corridor between Vilnius, Kaunas and Klaipėda better than any rival’s. Bitė is the speed pick downtown: its 5G deployment in Vilnius consistently posts the city’s top medians, which matters if your trip is an urban business week rather than a grand tour. Tele2 rounds out the market with the kind of pricing that makes a 5 GB pack nearly an impulse purchase.

The market also has a flanker layer worth knowing about. The operators sell prepaid through consumer sub-brands — Ežys under Telia and Pildyk under Tele2 — which dominate the convenience-store racks at Narvesen kiosks and supermarkets. For a visitor, they change nothing about the registration requirement; they simply tell you which rack to look at if you do end up buying locally.

Prepaid is not a niche here either. The regulator counted roughly 1.23 million prepaid cards in use in Lithuania in a recent quarter — close to a quarter of all active SIMs in the country. Visitors are walking into a market where the prepaid shelf is the mainstream shelf, not a dusty corner for tourists.

A travel profile usually does not make you choose a network at all. It attaches to whichever of the three the provider has partnered with, and a well-built one re-attaches automatically as conditions change. Before you buy any Lithuania eSIM, confirm your handset is unlocked and eSIM-capable — that is the whole prerequisite.

Carriers compared in Lithuania

Each operator runs its own prepaid brand, and each brand has its own pricing personality:

  • Ežys is Telia’s prepaid line, and it inherits Telia’s rural strength. Its packs are the most expensive of the three at the top end, and the most reliable on the coast and in the forests.

  • Labas is Bitė’s prepaid line, and it is where the most aggressive promotions live — occasional packs that look almost like pricing errors.

  • Pildyk is Tele2’s prepaid line, the one most often stacked behind supermarket tills, with a clear value positioning and a promotional starter pack that undercuts everyone.

You do not need to memorise any of this to buy one — but it explains why two kiosks on the same street quote very different prices for what sounds like the same product.

What buying at a Lithuanian counter involves

  1. Bring your passport. Registration is mandatory for every new prepaid line, so there is no anonymous purchase, at a kiosk or anywhere else.

  2. Choose an operator store over a convenience rack if you want help. Operator kiosks at the airport and in city centres can register you on the spot; a supermarket till can scan a document but cannot troubleshoot what comes next.

  3. Complete the identity step. Under the rules that took effect in 2025, operators must record your name, personal identification number, address and identity document details. International customers may be able to identify by uploading a photo of their document or by completing the process in a store, depending on the operator.

  4. Pay the activation charge, typically a few euros on top of the pack, and confirm the pack has actually been applied before you walk away.

  5. Insert the SIM or install the eSIM profile, then set it as your mobile-data line.

  6. Diarise the expiry. Note whether the pack renews automatically and at what price, then top up by app, voucher or operator store before the window closes.

Before a Vilnius counter: what Telia, Tele2 or Bitė will want

  • Passport — the identity document for registration; a Lithuanian address is required for subscriptions, not for prepaid packs

  • A card that works locally — euro contactless is accepted nearly everywhere, including on public transport

  • Your IMEI and EID — dial *#06#; the EID tells you whether the handset can hold a profile at all

  • A phone that is not carrier-locked — the most common reason an eSIM install quietly fails

  • A little time — the counter process is quick, but the walk from arrivals to a working line is longer than ten minutes at Vilnius on a busy evening

Registering and topping up a SIM in Lithuania

Lithuania uses the euro, and daily payment life is card-forward: contactless works in supermarkets, cafés, pharmacies and on public transport, and cash sits quietly in wallets as a backup rather than a necessity. Anyone arriving with a local SIM will find reloading straightforward by local standards — operator apps handle top-ups cleanly, and vouchers remain stocked at kiosks, grocery chains and gas stations.

The friction point is the same one already mentioned: registration. Buying any locally issued SIM means a passport scan at the counter, and the staff member doing the scanning is usually not equipped to debug an installation that fails. If you buy at a Narvesen or a supermarket rather than an operator kiosk, expect the process to be quick but completely self-service once you walk away.

Two small quirks complete the picture. Prepaid packs in Lithuania do not require a local address — unlike monthly subscriptions, which are built around resident documentation — so the passport really is the whole procedure. And because the market is competitive, promotional pricing churns constantly; whatever pack price you read online is a snapshot, and the shelf price at the kiosk may already differ. A Lithuania eSIM sidesteps the churn entirely, since the price you pay at checkout is the price that exists.

Will my Lithuanian SIM work elsewhere in the EU?

Lithuania joined the EU in 2004, and the eurozone in 2015, which simplifies travel in more ways than one. The roaming consequence: any plan issued by a Lithuanian operator carries “roam like at home” rights across the EU and the wider European Economic Area. A Telia or Tele2 SIM bought in Vilnius keeps working in Riga, Warsaw and Berlin at no surcharge, subject only to a fair-use allowance that operators may apply abroad.

Three caveats worth writing down:

  • Fair-use limits are real. Weeks of foreign use on a cheap domestic pack can trigger the regulated surcharge once the allowance empties. Short trips never come close.

  • The rules do not travel. They attach to the subscription, not to the country. A Lithuania eSIM bought online from a provider in another country is not a Lithuanian subscription and gains nothing from the regime — its rights are exactly the country list printed on the plan.

  • Registration still applies at home. EU law sets no common prepaid-registry rule, and Lithuania requires identity verification for locally purchased SIMs, so the passport step at the kiosk is not optional. Lithuania’s parliament adopted that requirement in December 2023 with effect from 2025, as LRT reported when the law passed; existing prepaid users were not forced to re-register, only new purchases.

If the mechanics behind all this are unfamiliar — what a profile actually is, why it is governed by its country list, how activation differs from installation — the explainer on what happens during eSIM activation covers the whole chain.

One consequence of the currency switch is easy to overlook. Because Lithuania uses the euro and prices in euros, there is no exchange-rate arithmetic to do when you compare a local prepaid pack against a travel plan — the only variable that differs between them is what each one covers. That makes Lithuania one of the easier markets in which to see the trade-off plainly: you are choosing between the cheapest possible domestic gigabytes and a plan that works the moment you cross into Latvia.

How much data do I need for Lithuania?

Lithuania’s sightseeing geography splits into three clusters, and connectivity behaves differently in each.

Vilnius. The baroque Old Town, the self-declared republic of Užupis and the castle complex at Trakai forty minutes out all sit inside dense coverage — this is Bitė and Telia 5G territory, with median speeds well above 100 Mbps in the center. A city weekend on a Lithuania eSIM rarely burns through 5 GB.

Kaunas and the A1 corridor. The interwar capital, halfway between Vilnius and the coast, makes the natural stop en route. Its modernist center and the confluence park where the Nemunas meets the Neris sit inside solid coverage from all three operators, and the highway between the two cities holds signal almost continuously — one of the better-connected intercity corridors in the region.

Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit. The port city is the gateway to the country’s headline landscape: a ferry across the lagoon to Smiltynė, then a fifty-kilometer spit of dunes, pine forest and fishing villages ending at Nida, close to the Russian border. Towns along the spit — Juodkrantė, Pervalka, Preila, Nida — have dependable service on Telia and Bitė. The drops come between them: expect brief signal gaps on the open dune stretches and deep in the forest tracks, and download your hiking maps before leaving Klaipėda. Telia’s rural reach gives it the edge out here, which is why spit-heavy itineraries should weight the network question more heavily than city-only trips do.

Šiauliai and the Hill of Crosses. The hill itself — tens of thousands of crosses on a former fortress site, one of northern Lithuania’s defining sights — sits about twelve kilometers north of Šiauliai and is reachable by bus plus a walk. Coverage along the approach is adequate on every network, but the site is rural enough that loading maps and history notes in advance beats relying on the signal at the gate.

Two data disciplines apply everywhere in the country: offline maps for the national parks, and a generous allowance if you stream — cheap local data has made Lithuanians heavy users, and hotel Wi-Fi has not always kept pace with the demand.

If you are flying into either, the argument for provisioning a Lithuania eSIM at home is stronger, not weaker, than it is for Vilnius.

What happens to my data at the Latvian or Polish border?

The Baltic capitals chain together so naturally that most itineraries cross at least one border. Vilnius to Riga is roughly four hours by express bus, and the Warsaw route runs south through Poland by train or long-haul coach. What happens to your connection at each border depends entirely on what kind of plan you are carrying.

A locally bought Lithuanian SIM roams. EU rules carry it into Latvia and Poland at no extra cost, within the fair-use allowance. Riga sightseeing on a Vilnius-bought pack is a non-event.

A Lithuania-only travel eSIM stops. The moment the bus crosses the border, a plan scoped to Lithuania alone has no rights on Latvian or Polish networks, and your data goes dark without ceremony. This catches more travelers than anything else on this page, because the plan worked perfectly for days beforehand.

A multi-country plan keeps working. A travel eSIM whose country list covers the Baltics and Poland — or a single Europe eSIM covering the whole run — transitions at the border without user action. If your trip strings together Vilnius, Riga and Tallinn, compare the country coverage on Latvia eSIM plans and Estonia eSIM plans against simply buying one Baltic-wide or European profile, which usually wins on both price and hassle.

One non-EU note: Lithuania borders Kaliningrad and Belarus, neither of which appears in any mainstream plan’s country list, and neither of which belongs on a casual itinerary. A multi-country European plan will not extend there, and no adjustment to your settings changes that — treat those borders as the hard edge of your coverage map.

A second warning is worth adding for anyone driving or riding near those frontiers. Phones near the Belarusian and Kaliningrad borders sometimes latch onto a stronger foreign network while still physically inside Lithuania, which looks like a roaming charge or a dead line depending on your plan. If your signal disappears near Šalčininkai, Druskininkai or the southern border zone, check the network name your phone has selected before assuming the eSIM has failed. Switching network selection from automatic to manual, then picking Telia, Bitė or Tele2 explicitly, resolves it in seconds.

Do calls and hotspot work on a data-only eSIM?

Most travel eSIMs are data-only, which reshapes how you handle the things a phone traditionally does. Calls run through internet apps — WhatsApp, FaceTime, Telegram, Teams — and on Lithuania’s network quality they behave like ordinary calls. Video meetings from a Vilnius café are unremarkable, in the best sense.

Tethering deserves a check rather than an assumption: many travel plans permit the phone to act as a hotspot, but some restrict it, so read the plan terms if you intend to work from a laptop. Keep your home SIM in the tray regardless — it remains your channel for SMS verification codes, because a data-only line cannot receive texts. And note that hotel Wi-Fi in the older Old Town buildings can be inconsistent, which is one more reason the mobile data line, not the router, should be treated as the primary connection.

A locally bought prepaid pack behaves differently on calls. The Ežys, Labas and Pildyk packs bundle domestic minutes, and incoming calls inside the EU are free under roam like at home, so a kiosk-bought line can genuinely replace a hotel phone for restaurant bookings. What it cannot do is give you a number your bank recognises — for that, keep your home line active.

How do I install my Lithuania eSIM before I fly?

Ten minutes of preparation removes every kiosk variable — passport registration, opening hours, activation fees, late-night vending machines. The sequence:

  1. Verify the handset. Run your exact model through the eSIM compatibility tool; unlocked plus eSIM-capable is the full requirement.

  2. Buy the plan. Delivery arrives by email, usually within minutes of checkout. Screenshot the QR code and park a copy in cloud storage — codes are single-use.

  3. Install on Wi-Fi. Add the profile in Settings before you fly, and label the line clearly.

  4. Prioritize the lines. Set the travel profile as the data line, and leave your home SIM in place for calls and verification texts.

  5. Enable data roaming on that line only. The travel line needs the roaming toggle; your home line should have it switched off.

If activation stalls — a code that will not scan, a profile that downloads but never registers — the eSIM activation fixes guide lists the failure patterns in the order they occur. Most resolve before boarding.

When your Lithuania eSIM will not connect

Four in-country failure patterns cover nearly everything:

  • Bars but no data. The eSIM is attached but not selected. Confirm the travel line holds the “mobile data” designation, then check its roaming toggle — both reset occasionally after OS updates.

  • A dead line at the Latvian border. If your connection vanished the moment the coach crossed into Latvia, the plan’s country list is the explanation, not the network. A Lithuania-only profile has no rights abroad; check the plan’s coverage list before you assume a fault.

  • Locked onto a foreign network near the eastern frontier. Phones near Belarus or Kaliningrad sometimes select a stronger neighbouring network while still inside Lithuania, which can stall data or trigger roaming charges. Switch network selection to manual and pick Telia, Bitė or Tele2 explicitly.

  • Slower than advertised after a few days. Many plans throttle rather than cut off when the allowance runs dry. Check usage in the provider app; Lithuanian networks themselves rarely throttle outside genuine congestion.

Lithuania eSIM FAQ for short stays and longer trips

Does 5G reach beyond Vilnius in Lithuania?

Yes in the main cities — Kaunas, Klaipėda and Šiauliai all have urban 5G from at least one operator, with Bitė and Telia carrying the widest deployment. Smaller towns run fast 4G, which for maps, messaging and most streaming is functionally indistinguishable.

Does a travel eSIM need registration in Lithuania?

No. Registration applies to SIMs bought from Lithuanian operators. A travel eSIM purchased online before travel is provisioned under the provider’s own process — no passport, no kiosk, no waiting.

Data prices in Lithuania, explained

The advertised prices range from a promotional starter pack around €1.65 for 33 GB up to roughly €17–19 for an unlimited monthly tier on Ežys or Pildyk. Mid-range Ežys and Pildyk packs of about 10 GB sit near €11.50, while Bitė and Tele2 standalone 10 GB prepaid packs come in lower, at around €6–7.

Does the Curonian Spit have coverage?

In the villages and at the ferry terminal, yes — Telia and Bitė both hold service through Juodkrantė and Nida. On the open dunes and forest trails expect intermittent gaps regardless of network. Download offline maps in Klaipėda.

Will a Lithuania eSIM work in Latvia or Estonia?

Only if its country list says so. A Lithuania-only profile ends at the border; a Baltic-listed or Europe-wide plan covers all three capitals on one purchase. For a multi-capital loop, the broader plan is almost always the better buy.

Can I buy a Lithuanian eSIM at Vilnius Airport?

Yes. The post office in baggage reclaim runs around the clock, and Telia, Bitė and Tele2 staff kiosks from roughly early morning until ten at night, with vending machines filling the gaps. Every route requires a passport scan and adds a small activation charge, and the packs typically come in 5 GB to unlimited tiers for about €8.50 to €16.

Using a Lithuania eSIM abroad

A Lithuania-issued line does work abroad. EU roam like at home covers the whole European Economic Area, including Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein, within a fair-use allowance. A travel profile bought abroad gains nothing from those rules — only its own country list matters.

Is hotspot allowed on Lithuania eSIMs?

Usually, but it is a plan-by-plan term rather than a network rule. Check the hotspot policy before counting on it for work, and if it matters to your trip, confirm it before purchase rather than after landing.

Is the Hill of Crosses covered?

Coverage along the approach from Šiauliai is adequate on all three networks, and the site itself usually holds a usable signal on the main viewing area. It is rural enough that loading maps and any audio guide in advance is the sensible habit, particularly if you are arriving by bus and walking the last stretch.

Lithuania eSIM, picked for your itinerary

Telia, if you are buying locally — its rural and highway reach is the widest of the three, and the Curonian Spit is exactly the kind of landscape where that matters. If you are running a travel eSIM, the network question mostly disappears, because a managed profile reattaches to whichever partner network is strongest where you stand.

Should I keep my home SIM alongside the travel eSIM?

Recommended, actually. Your physical SIM stays active for calls and SMS codes while the travel line carries data. Just switch off data roaming on the home line so background traffic cannot bill at international rates, and remember that data-only travel profiles cannot receive SMS — bank codes arrive on your regular number.

How to install a Lithuania eSIM

At home, on Wi-Fi, before you fly. Installing in Vilnius means competing with airport Wi-Fi and troubleshooting on a phone that may still be finding its feet after a flight. Test the flow on a free trial first if you have never done it.
