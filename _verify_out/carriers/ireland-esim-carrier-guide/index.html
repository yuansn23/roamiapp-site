Will my phone work with an Irish eSIM?

Three things decide whether your phone works on an Irish carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Is your phone eSIM-capable for Ireland?

Irish carriers build their 4G on specific bands and their 5G on a narrow slice. The rural backbone is Band 20 at 800 MHz, with newer low-band help from Band 28 at 700 MHz for 5G. Vodafone and Eir hold the heaviest Band 20 investment along secondary and regional roads, which is exactly why they reach the western coast better than raw speed numbers suggest. Cities and larger towns add Band 3 at 1800 MHz and Band 1 at 2100 MHz for capacity, and 5G sits almost entirely on n78 at 3500 MHz, a band whose signal rarely travels more than two or three kilometres from a mast.

Band Frequency What it does for your trip
Band 20 (LTE) 800 MHz The rural backbone; carries signal around coastal headlands and through western valleys
Band 28 (5G) 700 MHz Newer low-band 5G that reaches further than n78 in the countryside
Band 3 (LTE) 1800 MHz City and town capacity
Band 1 (LTE) 2100 MHz Capacity in suburban corridors
Band 7 (LTE) 2600 MHz Extra city capacity
n78 (5G) 3500 MHz Fast 5G in cities only; signal rarely reaches 3 km from a mast

Nobody has to study band charts for a trip to Ireland. The safe route is matching your exact model number, not the shop label, against eSIM device checker. The behind-the-scenes version of that handshake lives in what happens during eSIM activation, if you want it before you buy.

Does your handset do eSIM in Ireland?

A locked phone is the most common reason an Ireland eSIM install simply fails. Ireland has no national lock rule of the kind Canada or Japan enforces, so the lock status depends entirely on whichever operator originally sold the handset.

Path: Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock on iPhone. “No SIM restrictions” on screen means nothing stands in your way. “SIM locked” on the screen blocks every third-party profile until it is lifted.

Fix it: only the original seller can lift the lock, so start there rather than with a local shop. Handsets out of contract are typically released within a day and without a fee. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly.

How to buy a tourist eSIM from Three, Vodafone or Eir

Four routes exist, and they differ more in paperwork than in price: Three’s visitor eSIM, Vodafone’s and Eir’s tourist plans, and the budget brands that ride their networks. Each route, step by step:

Where Ireland eSIMs are sold

Three is the network you want for speed, and it sells a visitor product built for exactly this. Its “Visit Ireland” eSIM comes in two flavours: 5GB over 7 days for 15 euro, or 25GB over 28 days for 25 euro. Both activate instantly online with no physical SIM and no store visit.

Three Visit Ireland, step by step:

  1. Open the Three Ireland tourist page and choose a Visit Ireland eSIM plan.
  2. Pay by card or PayPal; there is no credit check and no Irish address.
  3. On compatible devices the eSIM activates automatically during setup, with no QR code needed. If your device does not support automatic activation, Three emails a QR code to scan.
  4. Top up to activate the allowance; the plan runs from that moment.

One catch that trips people up: the Visit Ireland allowance is for use only in the Republic of Ireland, per Three’s own terms. If your trip crosses into Northern Ireland, this plan will not cover you there. For ordinary Republic-only travel it is excellent; for an all-island trip, read the Northern Ireland section below.

Local SIM vs travel eSIM: Ireland edition

Vodafone was the first Irish operator to support eSIM, and its tourist path is clean. The “Data Unlimited 5G” prepay plan gives unlimited 5G data plus 100 any-network minutes and texts for a 20 euro top-up every 28 days, activated online using eSIM before or after you arrive.

Vodafone tourist eSIM, step by step:

  1. Order the Pay as you Go tourist eSIM on the Vodafone Ireland site.
  2. Set up the eSIM; compatible phones activate automatically, others get a QR code.
  3. Top up 20 euro to activate the plan when you land.
  4. New My Vodafone accounts also get 10 euro in free credit after registration.

Vodafone posted the highest Consistency Score in Ookla’s H2 2025 data at 83.5, so it is a steady choice, but note it had the lowest 5G Availability at 58.7 percent, meaning you spend more time on 4G than on Vodafone 5G.

Ireland eSIM data plans and rates

Eir owns its own network and markets itself on 5G coverage, and it sells a straightforward tourist eSIM with unlimited 5G data and unlimited calls and texts for 20 euro per 28 days, activated online using your phone’s IMEI before you land.

Eir tourist eSIM, step by step:

  1. Choose the Eir tourist eSIM on the Eir site or in the app.
  2. Enter your IMEI; the profile is prepared before travel.
  3. Install the eSIM on Wi-Fi at home, then it connects on arrival.
  4. No store visit, no identity document, no contract.

Eir leads Ookla’s H2 2025 5G Availability at 82.6 percent, the highest of the three, which is why it is a strong rural and small-town pick even though its Speed Score of 53.36 trails Three.

What changes when you cross into Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland is part of the United Kingdom, so it uses UK networks, EE, Vodafone UK, O2 and Three UK, not Irish ones. Since Brexit, the EU “Roam Like at Home” rule no longer covers Northern Ireland, and that creates the single most common coverage confusion visitors face on the island.

Vodafone Ireland pay as you go Usually yes — home data allowance applies in the UK Confirm on your specific plan
Eir prepay Yes — Roam Like Home in the UK, then about 1.99 euro per 100MB per day after the allowance Check before relying on it
A UK and Ireland travel eSIM Yes — one plan covers both sides of the border Cleanest choice for an all-island trip

Most Irish operators voluntarily keep Republic-to-Northern-Ireland roaming free on their main plans, but a prepaid tourist plan like Three’s Visit Ireland is explicitly Republic-only, and Three pay-as-you-go charges per megabyte in the UK without a roam pass. The clean answer for a Dublin-plus-Belfast trip is a regional plan that treats both jurisdictions as one, so your phone never notices the open border. One local quirk worth knowing: calls from the Republic to Northern Irish landlines can be dialled with the 048 prefix to be charged at domestic rates.

APN settings for Ireland carriers

Four fixes when your Ireland eSIM fails

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our eSIM activation error guide. Ireland has its own set of four patterns, listed below.

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Open About settings and confirm Carrier Lock displays “No SIM restrictions”
  2. Guard the QR code from scratches and re-scans, since most expire after a single use
  3. One call to the operator can confirm whether the profile is waiting in a pending state

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status
  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → select Three, Vodafone or Eir manually instead of automatic
  3. Reset network settings and start the phone again

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Compare each field with the table above before saving
  2. Double-check that the eSIM, not your everyday SIM, holds the data-line role
  3. Check you have not exhausted your allowance; some unlimited plans throttle after a fair-use cap

D. You crossed into Northern Ireland and lost service

  1. If you are on a Republic-only tourist plan such as Three Visit Ireland, this is expected; open a UK-and-Ireland plan or a local UK eSIM
  2. If you are on Vodafone or Eir pay as you go, confirm roaming is enabled and the plan includes UK use
  3. On a travel eSIM, confirm the plan region covers the UK, not just the Republic
  4. As a last resort, remove the profile and reinstall from a fresh QR code

The Ireland eSIM questions travellers ask most

Can visitors buy a prepaid eSIM from Three Ireland?

Yes, and it is the simplest of the three. Three’s Visit Ireland eSIM gives 5GB over 7 days for 15 euro or 25GB over 28 days for 25 euro, activates instantly online with no physical SIM, no store visit and no identity document. The one limit is that the allowance is for the Republic of Ireland only, so it will not cover Northern Ireland.

Does Vodafone Ireland sell a tourist eSIM?

Yes. Vodafone’s Data Unlimited 5G prepay plan gives unlimited 5G data plus 100 any-network minutes and texts for a 20 euro top-up every 28 days, and it activates as an eSIM online before or after you arrive. Vodafone was the first Irish operator to support eSIM, and it posted the highest Consistency Score in Ookla’s H2 2025 data, so it is a steady choice where raw 5G speed matters less than a reliable connection.

Is Eir a good eSIM choice for a short visit?

For a short visit Eir is a strong pick if your route leaves the cities. It led Ookla’s H2 2025 5G Availability at 82.6 percent, the highest of the three, and its tourist eSIM gives unlimited 5G data and unlimited calls and texts for 20 euro per 28 days, activated online from your IMEI. Its Speed Score of 53.36 trails Three, so central Dublin will feel faster on Three, but small towns and the west favour Eir’s reach.

Which budget brand has the best eSIM for travellers?

Among the cheap brands, GoMo and Lycamobile both issue eSIMs, while 48 and Tesco Mobile ship a plastic SIM only. GoMo is Eir’s app-only brand at 12.99 euro per month for unlimited data, best for a stay over a month; Lycamobile is Eir-based at 12.99 euro per 28 days and suits callers wanting cheap minutes. If your phone has no SIM tray, avoid 48 and Tesco Mobile and use GoMo, Lycamobile or a travel eSIM instead.

Will a photocopy of my passport work in Ireland?

No. Ireland has no mandatory SIM registration, unlike Germany, Japan or many other countries, so you can buy a prepaid SIM or eSIM from a vending machine, a supermarket or an app with no identity document at all. Some network stores may ask for photo ID as a shop policy for an in-store activation, and online checkouts want a card and an email, but the law does not require it. Travel blogs that say every provider demands a passport are describing other countries, not Ireland.

Will my Ireland eSIM work in Northern Ireland?

It depends on the plan. Northern Ireland uses UK networks, and since Brexit the EU roaming rule does not cover it. Three’s Visit Ireland tourist eSIM is explicitly Republic-only, so it stops at the border; Vodafone and Eir pay-as-you-go plans usually include UK use but check the terms; and a UK-and-Ireland travel eSIM covers both sides under one plan. For a Dublin-plus-Belfast trip, a regional plan is the clean choice.

Ireland: the carrier lineup

Vodafone and Three reach furthest along the western seaboard, because both invest heavily in the Band 20 low band that carries signal around coastal headlands and through valleys. Eir is strong in Kerry and West Cork on its legacy backhaul. No network fully covers the remote stretches, so pair a Vodafone or Three eSIM with offline maps rather than trusting live signal on the bog roads and mountain passes.

Which network is best in Ireland?

The Aran Islands have reasonable 4G, with Three the strongest of the three, but the ferry crossings between them and the mainland lose signal, and the smaller islands thin out away from the villages. Connemara’s problem is its blanket bog and granite: masts sit far apart along the N59, so the moment you turn onto a single-track coastal loop the signal fades or vanishes. Download offline maps before you go, because navigation in those gaps depends on them.

How do I check my phone supports an Ireland eSIM?

Check both lock status and eSIM support in one pass with the eSIM compatibility page, which covers EID support and the Band 20 question for rural reach. If you want the install theory first, read how an eSIM profile installs. Most iPhone XS and newer, Pixel 3 and newer, and Galaxy S20 and newer handsets qualify.

What if my Ireland eSIM will not connect?

Work the Ireland-specific patterns above in order, lock status, network selection, APN and data line, then the Northern Ireland check, and if it still fails the wider error catalogue in activation error troubleshooting guide covers the rest. Support replies faster when the EID, order number and an error screenshot are already attached.

Which Ireland carrier should you pick: Three vs Vodafone?

Yes. A quality travel eSIM on Irish networks connects to whichever of Three, Vodafone or Eir is strongest at your location and moves between them as you travel, which is exactly what you want on the Wild Atlantic Way where no single carrier covers every stretch. Direct carrier eSIMs stay on one network for their whole life.

What is an eSIM and how does it behave inside your handset?

Under the hood, an eSIM is a chip fixed inside your handset that can hold several carrier profiles in place of one plastic card. In practice the network ships a profile, the handset stores it on the internal chip, and the line works like any other.
