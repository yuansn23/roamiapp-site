North Macedonia has some of the cheapest mobile data in Europe and one genuinely confusing quirk: whether Skopje Airport even has a carrier kiosk is a matter of dispute between sources. That uncertainty, plus mandatory passport registration and a Western Balkans roaming landscape where the three operators differ sharply, makes this a country where ten minutes of pre-trip reading saves a wasted airport hour. This North Macedonia eSIM guide covers the A1 vs Telekom vs MTEL decision with real plan structures, the Lake Ohrid and Matka Canyon coverage reality, and the roam-like-at-home pact among the six Western Balkans countries that most articles never mention.

If your phone’s eSIM credentials are unverified, the compatibility page is step one.

The short answer: Makedonski Telekom has the widest nationwide footprint — the only operator whose prepaid plans include meaningful Western Balkans roaming (10 GB regional on its 30-day unlimited plan, ~1,399 MKD/€26). A1 Macedonia delivers the fastest urban speeds in Skopje, Ohrid and Bitola, with good motorway coverage but reportedly no Balkans roaming. MTEL — the newcomer relaunched from Lycamobile in late 2024, running on A1’s network — is the budget option that loses signal outside towns. Registration with a passport is mandatory. Local eSIM sales to visitors are unclear at best, so the dependable route is a North Macedonia eSIM bought online before you fly.

Makedonski Telekom: the footprint leader with the regional pass

Deutsche Telekom’s Macedonian arm holds the country’s widest 4G coverage — end-to-end along the A1/A2 motorways, through rural stretches, and around Lake Ohrid’s far shore. Its prepaid structure is the tourist argument: unlimited data and calls for 7 days at around 499 MKD (~€10), or a 30-day unlimited plan at about 1,399 MKD (~€26) that includes 10 GB of roaming across the Western Balkans — Serbia, Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro, Bosnia. For any itinerary that treats Macedonia as one stop on a regional drive, that roaming inclusion is the single most consequential feature in this market.

The regulator is the Agency for Electronic Communications (AEC), which licenses all operators.

A1 Macedonia: the fast, city-focused carrier

A1 (formerly VIP) is the fast, city-focused alternative: strong 5G presence in Skopje’s center, quick uploads in Ohrid and Bitola, solid motorway coverage. The catches: sources report its prepaid includes no Balkans roaming at all, and it thins near Tetovo on the A2 and around Demir Kapija on the E75. For a single-country city trip, A1 is the value pick; for a regional one, it is not.

MTEL: the budget option with big limitations

Lycamobile’s Macedonian operation was sold and relaunched as MTEL in late 2024, running as an MVNO-style service on A1’s network with aggressive pricing — its mSTART SIM costs about 700 MKD with 5 GB per month plus unlimited calls for three months. Two honest limitations: it reportedly loses signal outside urban areas (it rides A1’s rural map at lower priority), and its Balkans roaming allowance is just 1 GB. It is a budget-city option, not a road-trip one.

How does SIM registration work in North Macedonia?

Registration: every SIM in North Macedonia must be registered to a passport — the clerk scans it at the point of sale, sometimes takes a photo, and activation takes 10–15 minutes. Physical presence is mandatory.

The airport problem: sources genuinely conflict on whether Skopje International keeps carrier kiosks in arrivals — some travelers report A1 and Telekom stands open roughly 09:00–21:00; others find nothing, and those that exist reportedly close early. The city center is 25 minutes away, where A1 and Telekom flagship stores cluster around Macedonia Square and the malls, and MTEL SIMs sell at newsagents and Makpetrol stations. Do not build your plan around buying at the airport — the dependable routes are city stores or, cleaner still, an eSIM bought online before you fly.

Can you buy a SIM at Skopje Airport?

A guide that tells you confidently where to buy a SIM at Skopje Airport would be making it up. What is verifiable:

  • Operator presence at the airport is not consistently documented, and reports from travelers differ by date and terminal.

  • City stores in Skopje, Ohrid and Bitola are certain, staffed and cheap.

  • Every route requires the same passport registration.

  • A travel eSIM removes the question entirely.

Will my eSIM work across the Western Balkans?

Here is the context that reorders this market: the six Western Balkans economies (Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia) operate their own roam-like-at-home agreement, cutting regional roaming charges among them. What that means practically:

  1. A North Macedonian plan roams cheaply across the region — if and only if its allowance includes roaming data. Telekom’s 10 GB regional bundle is the benchmark; A1 reportedly includes none; MTEL just 1 GB.

  2. North Macedonia is not in the EU, so EU roam-like-at-home does not apply here — an EU SIM pays capped-but-real roaming charges.

  3. The pact lowers prices; it does not eliminate limits. Cheap roaming is not the same as unlimited roaming, and a plan built for a domestic user will still run out of regional data on a two-week drive.

For a Balkans loop, choose your operator by its roaming allowance, not its headline price — that is the decision that saves a second eSIM. Our Montenegro eSIM guide covers the same question from the other direction, and Montenegro’s operators bundle Western Balkans roaming across the board, which is worth comparing before you commit.

Price context: Cable.co.uk’s worldwide survey prices 1 GB at roughly USD 0.86 — 86th of 237 markets, well under half the global average — and Ookla’s Global Index has ranked the country as high as 15th worldwide for mobile speed (median ~165 Mbps at 18 ms latency) in recent editions. DataReportal’s Digital 2025 report counts 2.65 million mobile connections. Small country, serious networks.

The right North Macedonia eSIM for each trip

  • The motorways are covered by everyone; the side roads are not. North Macedonia’s geography is a set of valleys and gorges, and signal follows the transport corridors.

  • The lakes are better covered than the mountains. Ohrid and Dojran are tourist infrastructure; Pelister and Mavrovo’s parklands are not.

  • The northwest is the clearest operator difference. Around Tetovo and the Albanian-majority west, Telekom holds and A1 drops — a detail that matters if your route runs toward Ohrid via the western valleys rather than the motorway.

  • Matka Canyon is a signal canyon in both senses. The approach road and the canyon mouth have service; go 400 metres up the gorge and none of the three carriers exist.

Does your phone band-match North Macedonia networks?

Two dials and one settings menu settle whether your handset can hold a Macedonian profile.

  • Dial *#06#. An EID listed in About means the handset accepts eSIM profiles.

  • On iPhone the check is Carrier Lock under Settings → General → About, and you want to see “No SIM restrictions”.

  • Android: SIM Manager is under Settings -> Connections.

When do locally bought lines need APN details?

Rare on a managed profile — these are the Macedonian cases where the settings arrive blank.

When a North Macedonia APN must be entered manually

The iPhone path is Settings → Cellular → [the Telekom or A1 line] → Cellular Data Network; on Android it is Settings → Network & Internet → Access Point Names. What to try when the eSIM fails to install or connect

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Check About -> Carrier Lock: “No SIM restrictions” expected

  2. Confirm no prior scan – the codes die after first use

  3. The provider can confirm whether the profile is pending

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Power the eSIM line down and back up

  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose Telekom or A1 manually

  3. Restart the phone, then a network-settings reset if needed

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Recompare the APN with the row your carrier has in the table

  2. Make sure mobile data is pointed at the eSIM line

  3. Turn on roaming for the line – phone-level is not enough

  4. Check the data balance has not run out

D. Works in Skopje, dead in the west or the parks

  1. You have moved onto the weaker carrier’s map — Telekom is the fix

  2. Try manual network selection

  3. Remember Matka’s gorge and Pelister’s high country are dead on all three networks

Your questions about eSIMs and carriers in North Macedonia

Can I buy a local eSIM as a tourist?

Local prepaid eSIM sales to visitors are unclear at best — A1 and Telekom do not reliably advertise visitor eSIMs, and the airport kiosk situation is disputed. The dependable route is a travel eSIM bought online before you fly, or a registered physical SIM from a city store.

Do you need a passport to buy a SIM in North Macedonia?

Yes — passport registration is mandatory, completed at the point of sale with a document scan and sometimes a photo, taking 10–15 minutes. Online-bought travel eSIMs bypass the counter – the provider registers them in advance.

Short trip vs long stay in North Macedonia

Makedonski Telekom, and it is not close — its 30-day plan includes 10 GB of roaming across the six Western Balkans countries. A1’s prepaid reportedly includes no regional roaming at all.

Is Skopje Airport a good place to buy a SIM?

Unreliable. Sources disagree on whether carrier kiosks exist in arrivals, and those reported close early evenings. The city stores around Macedonia Square are cheaper and certain — or buy an eSIM before flying and skip the question.

Does 5G reach beyond Skopje in North Macedonia?

Yes in the cities — A1 has a strong 5G presence in central Skopje and Telekom is rolling out alongside it. Rural Macedonia remains 4G, with the coverage gaps mapped above.

Will my EU plan work in North Macedonia?

It will roam, but at capped regional rates — North Macedonia is not in the EU’s roam-like-at-home zone, and the Western Balkans agreement that lowers regional charges is a separate arrangement. For anything beyond a day, a local plan or a North Macedonia eSIM is cheaper.

Will my eSIM work at Lake Ohrid?

Yes — and on Telekom or a multi-network profile it holds around most of the lake, including the quieter far shore. A1 is strong in Ohrid town and thins from there.

How many GB should you load for North Macedonia?

3–5 GB covers maps, messaging, social and video calls comfortably. At USD ~0.86 per gigabyte, data here is among Europe’s cheapest — but bandwidth in the parks, not price, is the constraint.

Is North Macedonia’s mobile network actually fast?

Yes, unusually so for its size. Ookla’s Global Index has ranked it as high as 15th worldwide for mobile speed, with medians around 165 Mbps and latency near 18 ms. The practical limit is coverage, not throughput.

Can you buy a SIM anonymously in North Macedonia?

No. Registration is mandatory for every line, and there is no anonymous prepaid tier. This is the main reason visitors prefer a travel eSIM, where the compliance work happens before your profile is issued.

Should I choose MTEL to save money?

Only if your trip is urban and short. MTEL is the cheapest option and rides A1’s network, but it loses signal outside towns and carries just 1 GB of regional roaming. On a road trip, the saving is not worth the gaps.

What happens to your data plan at the border?

Your line either stops working or charges you roaming rates depending on your plan, and a data-only travel eSIM may not follow you at all. Check before you drive, not at the border — the Border Police car park is a poor place to start reading terms and conditions.

Should I buy a Macedonian SIM or use a travel eSIM?

For a single-country city trip, a travel eSIM. For a regional drive, compare carefully: Makedonski Telekom’s 30-day plan with 10 GB of Western Balkans roaming is the one local product that can beat a regional travel plan on both price and coverage.

Is data cheap in North Macedonia?

Yes — around USD 0.86 per gigabyte, well under half the global average, in a market that has ranked among the world’s fastest for mobile speed. The constraint is coverage in the parks, not price or bandwidth.

Which operator’s map is biggest in North Macedonia?

Install a travel eSIM before you fly and enable roaming. Given that the airport kiosk situation is genuinely uncertain, that is the only route with no variables in it.

Do I need a Macedonian number?

Rarely. Skopje’s hotels, restaurants and tour operators work over WhatsApp and booking apps. A local number earns its place only on a longer stay, or on a regional drive where you want a voice line as well as data.
