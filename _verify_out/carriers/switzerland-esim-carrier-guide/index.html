Will my phone work on Swiss networks?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Swiss carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Which bands does your phone need for Switzerland?

Swiss carriers build their 5G on band n78 at 3500 MHz, and their 4G on bands B1, B3, B7 and B20. Of those, B20 at 800 MHz is the one that matters most for travellers, because it is the low-band layer that carries signal into tunnels, remote valleys and the quieter mountain roads. A phone missing B20 still works in every city, but it loses the rural and alpine reach that separates Swisscom from the rest.

Band charts are not bedtime reading, and you do not need them by heart. Compare the model number, not the shop label, with phone compatibility list. If you would rather understand the machinery before relying on it on a mountain road, eSIM activation explained lays it out.

Installing your Switzerland eSIM

A locked phone is the most common reason a Swiss eSIM install simply fails, and the lock question cuts two ways here. A phone bought on a plan from Swisscom, Sunrise or Salt may be locked until the contract or the device is paid off. A phone brought from an EU or UK carrier may be locked to that carrier and will not accept a Swiss profile either. Switzerland is outside the EU roaming zone, but lock rules are set by the seller, not by the border.

Check it: on iPhone, open Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock. When the display reads “No SIM restrictions,” any travel profile will install. A “SIM locked” verdict stops any travel profile from installing, full stop.

Fix it: ask the original seller for the unlock; neither Swisscom nor Sunrise can do it for a phone they did not sell. Once financing ends, the unlock typically arrives the same day and free of charge. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone on a contract, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. The Swiss regulator OFCOM sets the identity rules quoted elsewhere on this page, and lock status is a separate matter handled by the original seller.

Where should I buy my Swiss eSIM?

Four routes exist, and they differ more in price and in what happens at the border than in the install itself: Swisscom for the best network, Sunrise for the only EU-inclusive prepaid, Salt for the cheapest entry, and a travel eSIM for everyone who crosses a border. Each route, step by step.

The Swisscom route: Prepaid Flat for tourists

Swisscom is the incumbent and the network most visitors ultimately want in the mountains. Its tourist route is the Prepaid Flat offer, which is built for exactly this.

Swisscom Prepaid Flat, step by step:

  1. Open the Swisscom Prepaid for tourists page and choose a duration — the entry tier is CHF 20 for 7 days, with CHF 40 for 15 days, CHF 65 for 30 days and CHF 150 for 90 days above it.

  2. Choose the eSIM at checkout. Swisscom installs the profile by QR code, so there is no shop visit if your phone supports eSIM.

  3. Scan the personal QR code and the profile activates as soon as it connects to the Swisscom network in Switzerland.

  4. No contract and no long-term commitment. Data is unlimited within Switzerland but shaped to about 50 Mbps download, which is plenty for maps, tickets and video calls.

The Sunrise route: the EU-inclusive visitors prepaid

Sunrise is the one national carrier that sells a tourist prepaid with the neighbouring EU countries already inside it, which makes it the natural pick for a Switzerland-plus-France-Germany-Italy-Austria loop.

Sunrise visitors prepaid, step by step:

  1. Open the Sunrise visitors prepaid page. The starter is CHF 19.90 and arrives with CHF 20 of credit.

  2. Register the prepaid eSIM online. Sunrise states a Swiss address is not required and accepts a foreign address, with identity verification done by ID upload and a short video selfie.

  3. Pick a plan. The basic Unlimited CH rate charges CHF 2.50 per 24 hours of use for unlimited data, calls and SMS in Switzerland. The border-friendly choices are Unlimited Europe 7 at CHF 59.90 and Unlimited Europe 30 at CHF 74.90, which add 60 GB of high-speed data across 46 European countries including France, Germany, Italy and Austria.

  4. The eSIM downloads instantly after registration, and the Europe allowance starts on first use.

Does Salt sell a prepaid eSIM for visitors?

Salt is the budget option and the cheapest way to get online in Switzerland, though its prepaid is the weakest in the mountains and the least border-friendly of the three.

Switzerland-specific eSIM problems and fixes

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our eSIM troubleshooting guide. Watch for these four Switzerland-specific patterns as you read on.

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Unlocked status confirmed when Carrier Lock shows No SIM restrictions

  2. Scan the code once, carefully; almost all of them expire after the first use

  3. Ask the carrier whether the OFCOM registration is still pending, since a failed ID check blocks activation

B. Installed, but no signal in the mountains

  1. Re-check the lock status

  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → pick Swisscom manually for the best alpine reach

  3. Wipe all network settings and reboot once more, and remember coverage thins above the lifts

C. Signal bars, but a surprise roaming bill after crossing the border

  1. Confirm the eSIM is the data line and your home SIM has data roaming off

  2. On Swisscom or Salt prepaid, Switzerland-only data means France Germany Italy Austria use paid roaming; switch to a Sunrise Europe plan or a travel eSIM

  3. In Geneva Basel or Ticino, force the Swiss network manually so the phone does not latch onto a French German or Italian mast

D. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Confirm each value you typed appears somewhere in the table above

  2. Verify the data line is assigned to the eSIM and not to your home card

  3. Check you have not exhausted your allowance; Salt prepaid throttles or stops once the pack is spent

Switzerland eSIM FAQ

Can visitors buy a Swisscom eSIM?

Yes. Swisscom’s Prepaid Flat tourist offer sells an eSIM online with no contract and no Swiss address, at CHF 20 for 7 days, CHF 40 for 15 days, CHF 65 for 30 days and CHF 150 for 90 days, all unlimited within Switzerland shaped to about 50 Mbps. The catch is that the allowance is Swiss-only, so France, Germany, Italy or Austria use paid roaming unless you add a travel option.

Does Sunrise sell a prepaid eSIM that includes France Germany Italy and Austria?

It does, and it is the only one of the three that bundles the neighbours into prepaid. Sunrise Unlimited Europe 7 at CHF 59.90 and Unlimited Europe 30 at CHF 74.90 give unlimited data in Switzerland plus 60 GB of high-speed data across 46 European countries, including all four borders. Swisscom and Salt prepaid leave those countries on paid roaming, so for a cross-border loop Sunrise is the carrier eSIM to buy.

Can international visitors get a Salt eSIM without a Swiss address?

Yes. Salt PrePay issues an eSIM and accepts a foreign passport through its identity check, with no Swiss address required. The starter is advertised as a free SIM with CHF 10 of credit, and data packs run from CHF 9.95 for 1 GB. Salt is the cheapest Swiss entry, but its prepaid is Switzerland-only and thinner than Swisscom above the valleys, so weigh price against alpine reach.

Which operator blankets Switzerland best?

Swisscom, on the evidence and on the ground. It leads Ookla consistency at 93.4 and is the network that reaches the high points the others miss: the Jungfraujoch platform at 3454 m, the Gornergrat above Zermatt, and the remote stretches of the Gotthard corridor. Sunrise and Salt are strong in resorts and villages but fade on the high slopes and glaciers, so download offline maps before you head up.

Does EU roaming work in Switzerland?

No. Switzerland is not in the European Union or the European Economic Area, so the EU Roam Like at Home regulation stops at the border. An EU or EEA phone plan that worked free of charge in France starts billing at the carrier’s Swiss rates the moment you cross, and many Europe-only travel plans exclude Switzerland entirely. Confirm Switzerland is named in your plan before you rely on it.

Why does my EU phone plan charge extra in Switzerland?

Because the Roam Like at Home caps apply only inside the EU and EEA, which covers the 27 EU states plus Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein. Switzerland is an EFTA member but never joined the EEA, so it sits outside that protected zone, and your home carrier may place it in a separate, pricier tariff band. Some EU operators include Switzerland voluntarily, but inclusion is a commercial perk you must verify, not a right.

Do you need ID for a Swiss prepaid SIM?

Yes, for any Swiss prepaid, physical or eSIM. OFCOM requires every carrier and every budget brand to register the SIM with an identity document, and a passport or Swiss identity card is accepted while a driving licence is not. The check is what makes a Swiss prepaid different from a no-documents travel eSIM, which handles identity at checkout instead.

Will my phone work with a Switzerland eSIM?

It needs to be unlocked and eSIM-capable, and it helps if it supports Band 20 at 800 MHz for rural and tunnel reach. One look at the compatibility page answers both the EID and the band questions. If the install step is the unclear part, read what happens during eSIM activation.

How to unlock a carrier-locked phone for Switzerland

Ask the carrier it is locked to. A phone on a Swisscom, Sunrise, Salt, or an EU or UK plan stays locked until the device or contract is settled, and the unlock is handled by that seller regardless of where you travel. Fully paid handsets are commonly released within a few hours. Check status at Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock before you fly.

Why does my EU plan work in Liechtenstein but not Switzerland?

Liechtenstein is in the EEA, so an EU or EEA plan that excluded Switzerland suddenly works at home rates the moment you cross from Austria into Liechtenstein, then flips back to international rates when you re-enter Switzerland a few miles later. The border is almost invisible but the billing is not, so keep data roaming off on your home SIM and use a Swiss or travel eSIM for the Swiss leg.

Should I buy from a Swiss carrier or use a travel eSIM?

Direct means one network, an OFCOM identity check, and often a Swiss number; in exchange you get domestic calling and, on Sunrise, EU coverage built in. A travel eSIM means instant delivery, no documents, automatic switching across Swisscom, Sunrise and Salt, and a fixed upfront price that already covers the border. Short trip: travel eSIM. Planning a long stay or need a local number? Skip the travel products and go carrier-direct.

What if my Switzerland eSIM stops working in the mountains?

Work the Switzerland-specific patterns above in order, lock status, manual network selection to Swisscom, APN and data line, then profile reinstall, and if it still fails the wider error catalogue in activation troubleshooting guide covers the rest. High in the mountains, favour Swisscom and always download offline maps before you set out, because even the best network thins on the highest glaciers.
