China Mobile Hong Kong, csl, SmarTone and 3HK compared

Who wins on speed in Hong Kong?

China Mobile Hong Kong takes the headline numbers — median download 119.24 Mbps across all technologies, 185.43 Mbps on 5G, 5G availability of 86.3%, and the lowest 5G latency in the market at 27 ms, plus the best mobile gaming score. It holds one of the largest 5G spectrum portfolios in the 3.5 GHz band, and its coverage extends further into the New Territories and the country parks than its rivals. If your itinerary includes trails, border-adjacent towns or heavy streaming, this is the network to prefer.

csl: the consistency pick

csl posts the market’s best consistency — 92.5% of samples meeting the 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up threshold, and 85.5% on 5G at the stricter 25/3 Mbps bar — with a median download of 92.73 Mbps. That combination is why it is the pick for video calls and for older Kowloon walk-ups, where its in-building investment shows. It trails China Mobile Hong Kong on raw speed and 5G availability, and it is the network most business travelers in Central end up on.

SmarTone: network stability and service

SmarTone does not win a headline metric, and it does not need to. Its selling points are network stability in the districts locals care about and a service reputation that keeps customers who could switch. For a visitor the practical effect is simple: SmarTone is a reasonable default on the outlying islands and a fine choice for a city trip, with prepaid tiers around HK$68 for 10 GB or HK$188 for unlimited data over 30 days.

3HK and SoSIM: the cheapest local data

3 Hong Kong (3HK) runs the cheapest tourist cards in the market and, through its SoSIM prepaid brand, the cheapest way to hold a month of local data — roughly HK$33 for 50 GB over 30 days with 1,500 local minutes, then reduced speed at 128 kbps after the fair-use threshold. Coverage is marginally patchier in the New Territories than CMHK’s, which rarely matters inside the city and does on a trail. Note that SoSIM is a physical or eSIM local product, so it still needs real-name registration.

SIM and eSIM registration in Hong Kong

Every SIM card issued by a local operator for use in Hong Kong, China — prepaid cards and monthly service plans alike — must complete real-name registration before activation. The requirement sits under the Telecommunications (Registration of SIM Cards) Regulation, in force since 1 September 2021, and the authority’s own guidance is blunt: new prepaid cards cannot be used until registration is completed and verified.

What that means for a visitor:

  • Tourists register with a travel document. A passport is an accepted identification document; a Hong Kong identity card is not required, and there is no residency test.

  • Verification is manual for non-residents. Registration for Hong Kong identity card holders runs through the iAM Smart app and is effectively instant. Everyone else is verified by the operator, so activation can lag by hours rather than seconds.

  • There is a cap. An individual may register up to 10 prepaid SIM cards per operator; corporate users are capped at 25. Monthly service-plan cards carry no limit.

  • Registration is periodically re-checked. Operators run sample checks and send a verification request from the sender address #SIMREG; a card whose holder does not respond can be deregistered and stop working.

The practical consequence is the one most travel blogs get wrong. Advice that says “buy a card from 7-Eleven and you are online in two minutes, no passport” describes a rule that ended in February 2023. Convenience stores still sell cards cheaply and around the clock, but the card will not activate until it is registered, and that means producing your passport and waiting for a human check.

eSIM basics for Hong Kong: bands, registration and fair use

Which bands do Hong Kong networks use?

Hong Kong, China’s networks use n1 (2100 MHz), n78 (3500 MHz) and n79 (4900 MHz) for 5G, and B1, B3, B7, B8 and B28 for 4G LTE. Most recent handsets cover those, but n79 is the one that separates a mid-range phone from a flagship and it carries a meaningful share of local 5G capacity. Check the exact model number rather than assuming.

What do Hong Kong’s registration rules demand?

Real-name registration applies to SIM cards issued by local operators. It is worth saying plainly because the advice online contradicts itself: cards bought in Hong Kong need registration, while profiles issued by an international travel eSIM provider are not local SIM cards and are not registered with a Hong Kong carrier. The trade is that a travel profile is data-only, and it has no +852 number.

What does “unlimited” really mean on Hong Kong plans?

Most unlimited local plans carry a fair-use policy. SoSIM’s 50 GB package drops to 128 kbps after its threshold, and other “unlimited” tiers throttle at a share of full speed rather than stopping. Check the threshold, not the word: a plan that throttles at 128 kbps is usable for messages and useless for maps with live traffic.

Would a Hong Kong eSIM run on my phone?

eSIM works on iPhone XS and later, Google Pixel 3 and later, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later, and most recent Android flagships. The device must also be carrier-unlocked. Verify both against the compatibility list before buying. If the mechanics are new, how an eSIM profile installs explains it in plain language.

Which operator blankets Hong Kong best?

Urban coverage is not the test. The real gaps sit inside the natural geology of the Sai Kung and Pat Sin Leng country parks, on parts of the smaller outlying islands, and in a few deep indoor spaces in older buildings. China Mobile Hong Kong has the widest footprint; csl is the most consistent in fringe zones. The Dragon’s Back trail, the MTR system including tunnels, and the Airport Express all carry usable signal on every network.

Hong Kong, China eSIM by trip shape: six days, six different networks

Central and Admiralty business stay: Hong Kong coverage

Four days of meetings and client dinners never leaves the strongest part of the map, so consistency beats outright speed: csl’s 92.5% figure is the number that keeps a video call from freezing mid-sentence. Keep the hotel’s Wi-Fi as a backup for large transfers and remember that upper-floor hotel rooms sometimes sit in a coverage shadow.

Kowloon shopping and food crawl: in-building coverage

Mong Kok’s street markets, Jordan’s noodle shops and Tsim Sha Tsui’s malls all sit in dense concrete, which is where in-building coverage decides whether a payment app loads at the till. csl performs best in older walk-ups; China Mobile Hong Kong’s raw capacity handles the street-level crush. Expect a speed dip between 6 pm and 10 pm rather than an outage.

Lantau coverage: Disneyland, Ngong Ping and Tai O

A Ngong Ping 360 ride and a Disneyland day are the two places where a mid-journey dropout is memorable, and both are on Lantau. csl is the steadier hand on the cable car and around the resort. Tai O’s stilt-house lanes lose signal indoors, which is a good moment to have offline maps loaded.

MacLehose Trail and Sai Kung hiking coverage

This is the one itinerary where the carrier choice genuinely changes the experience. China Mobile Hong Kong’s wider footprint holds a bar at more trailheads and ridges; inside the deep geology of the Sai Kung and Pat Sin Leng country parks, every network goes dark. Treat a hike as an offline activity, tell someone your route, and carry a power bank — searching for signal is the fastest way to empty a battery.

Outlying island hopping: Cheung Chau, Lamma, Peng Chau coverage

Cheung Chau, Lamma and Peng Chau are a ferry ride from the city and a step down in build-out. SmarTone is the most dependable locally, main ferry routes carry signal and the smaller piers do not. Do not plan a live upload from the middle of the harbour.

Macao day trip over the bridge: the plan that follows you

A day in Macao is the one trip shape that crosses a network boundary. The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge takes less than an hour, but Macao runs its own networks, so a Hong Kong-only plan stops working on the far side. Either buy a card with a shared Hong Kong–Macao allowance — CMHK, csl and SmarTone all sell versions — or install a Macao eSIM before you go and let the profile switch.

Do I need to change APN settings in Hong Kong?

Roami profiles arrive pre-configured, so most visitors never touch APN settings in Hong Kong. They matter in one case only: you bought a local China Mobile Hong Kong, csl, SmarTone or 3HK line at a counter, and the phone shows full signal but no data.

iPhone: Settings → Cellular → select the line → Cellular Data Network. On Android: Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → Access Point Names.

Each Hong Kong operator publishes its exact APN on its own support site, and the counter staff will usually enter it for you at purchase. If a line misbehaves later, reinstalling the profile or re-entering the printed APN resolves the overwhelming majority of “connected but offline” cases.

Four local failure modes for a Hong Kong eSIM

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are catalogued in the eSIM activation fixes guide. The four patterns below are the local ones.

A. Bars appear, but no data, on a card bought in the city. Registration is almost always the cause. A prepaid card that has not finished real-name verification shows signal and refuses data. Check for a message from the operator, and confirm the registration is complete rather than pending.

B. Perfect in Central, silent on a trail. This is coverage shape rather than a fault. Open network selection and try China Mobile Hong Kong, csl and SmarTone manually; if none of them registers, you are inside the country-park geology and no setting will help.

C. Data stops the moment you cross to Shenzhen or Macao. A plan boundary, not a fault. A Hong Kong-only profile has no coverage on the other side; switch to a shared-allowance card or a mainland or Macao eSIM before you travel.

D. A 5G icon with 4G speeds. The handset may be missing the n79 band, or the plan may cap throughput on its tier. Compare against a second device before blaming the network, then check the fair-use threshold on your plan.

Crossing to Shenzhen and Macao: same phone, new data plan

Hong Kong, China’s networks stop at the boundary on both sides of the territory — Macao to the west, the mainland to the north. Each crossing has its own character. Lo Wu and Lok Ma Chau put you on the Shenzhen metro within minutes of clearing immigration, Shenzhen Bay is the coach connection, and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge reaches Macao in under an hour. On every one of them, a Hong Kong-only card goes dark, and the automatic roaming that some cards enable can bill you without a clear prompt.

The clean setup is two profiles rather than one heroic plan. Keep the Hong Kong, China eSIM for the territory and add a mainland or Macao plan for the crossing — or buy a single card with a shared Hong Kong–Macao allowance, which CMHK, csl and SmarTone all publish. Before you leave the hotel, switch off data roaming on the Hong Kong line so that a background sync near the boundary cannot start a billed session, and download an offline map of the crossing you are using: the queue at Shenzhen Bay is exactly where you will want to look something up.

Common questions about using an eSIM in Hong Kong (11 answered)

Is registration waived for visitors in Hong Kong?

No. Registration is required for any SIM card issued by a local operator for local use — prepaid cards included — and it must be completed before the card will activate. The requirement has applied to new prepaid cards since February 2023, and cards bought in convenience stores are not exempt.

Can a tourist register a Hong Kong SIM with a passport?

Yes. A visitor without a Hong Kong identity card registers with an accepted travel document, normally a passport, and the operator uses those details. There is no residency requirement and no local address needed.

How long does registration take?

It depends on the route. Hong Kong identity card holders use the iAM Smart app and are verified almost immediately, which is the majority of local buyers. Visitors are verified manually by the operator, so a card bought over the counter may take noticeably longer before data flows — which is why an eSIM installed at home is the safer arrival-day plan.

How many prepaid SIMs can one person register?

Up to 10 prepaid SIM cards per operator per individual, with a separate allowance for each operator, and 25 for corporate users. Monthly service-plan cards carry no cap. Operators also run sample checks and will ask you to re-verify through a message from the sender address #SIMREG.

Do I need registration for an eSIM bought online?

Not in the same way. A profile issued by an international travel eSIM provider is not a SIM card issued by a local operator, so no local real-name registration applies. The trade-off is that it is data-only: no +852 number, and no local voice minutes.

Is mobile data actually cheap in Hong Kong?

Yes, with a caveat. Unlimited tiers from HK$188 over 30 days are genuine value, and SoSIM’s HK$33 package gives 50 GB over 30 days before throttling. Read the fair-use threshold: after it, speeds drop to around 128 kbps, which is fine for messages and unusable for video.

Which carrier is best on the MTR?

Every major network carries full coverage in all MTR stations and tunnels, including the Airport Express and the harbour crossings, so the subway is not a differentiator. Where carriers diverge is indoor coverage in older buildings and reach into the country parks.

Will my Hong Kong SIM work in Macao or mainland China?

Not on a Hong Kong-only plan. Macao is a separate telecommunications region and the mainland is a different market entirely; both require a roaming add-on or a separate plan. The cheapest way to cover a Macao day trip is a card with a shared Hong Kong–Macao allowance.

What is SoSIM and is it open to visitors?

SoSIM is a prepaid brand on 3HK’s network with the cheapest large local allowance — about HK$33 for 50 GB over 30 days with 1,500 minutes. It is sold to visitors like any other prepaid product, which means a passport and real-name registration, and its coverage in the New Territories is marginally patchier than China Mobile Hong Kong’s.

Will my bank codes still arrive while I use an eSIM?

Yes. Keep your home SIM in the phone for calls and verification codes and run data on the eSIM, with data roaming switched off on the home line. This matters more than usual here because a data-only travel profile cannot receive SMS, so bank and booking codes still arrive on your original number.

What if your Hong Kong eSIM still will not connect?

Work the four patterns above in order — registration status, network selection, plan boundary, then band and fair-use checks — and if it still fails, the wider error catalogue in the eSIM activation troubleshooting guide covers the rest. Have your EID, order number and a screenshot ready before contacting support.
