Ecuador’s three connectivity markets

Getting an Ecuador eSIM right starts with a fact most guides skip: Ecuador compresses three very different connectivity environments into one compact country. The mainland corridor — Quito, Guayaquil and Cuenca — runs dependable 4G on every carrier, is switching on 5G in the largest cities, and sells some of the cheapest mobile data in South America. The Galápagos Islands sit roughly 1,000 km offshore, hang off a single submarine fiber link, and only carry mobile signal in three port towns. The Amazon fringe — the Oriente — thins to 3G and dead zones within minutes of leaving towns like Tena, Puyo and Coca. Any advice about an Ecuador eSIM that ignores this split will steer you wrong.

The mainland numbers are genuinely good. DataReportal counted 15.2 million internet users in Ecuador at the start of 2025 — 83.7 percent of the population — and 18.0 million mobile connections, slightly fewer than the country’s 18.2 million people. Cable.co.uk puts the average price of mobile data at about USD 1.00 per gigabyte, one of the lower figures in South America. Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index lists median mobile downloads around 64 Mbps on its all-technology measure, with median fixed broadband above 300 Mbps.

Then there is the part most travel guides skip. The Galápagos are a special regime in every sense: entry is regulated through a transit control card, tourism is managed for conservation, and telecom buildout has deliberately stayed minimal. Island mobile service rides one submarine cable, supplemented since August 2025 by satellite capacity that state operator CNT EP resells. In Puerto Ayora or Puerto Baquerizo Moreno your phone works. At visitor sites, on day boats and on most beaches, it does not — on any network, with any eSIM.

Claro vs Movistar: coverage compared

Claro, owned by América Móvil, holds roughly 55% of the Ecuadorian market, the most towers, and the best rural and highway coverage of the three. It is the only carrier with consistent signal in places like Vilcabamba, Baños and Mindo and along the coast between Guayaquil and Salinas. Its 5G is live in parts of Quito and Guayaquil plus points near Coca and Puerto Ayora, and its prepaid catalogue is broad: a SIM costs about $3–5 with initial credit, and combos run from roughly $5 for 3 GB over 7 days to $30 for 35 GB over 30 days. Registration happens by dialling any number after inserting the chip and entering your passport details; a confirmation message follows.

Movistar/Tigo: consistency first, and the best city cover

Movistar followed Claro at 45.88 Mbps in Ookla’s 1H 2026 measurement while posting the best consistency in the country — 91.8% of samples met the 5/1 Mbps threshold. Its published combos are simple and cheap: about $3.00 for 5 GB over 7 days, $9.00 for 10 GB over 30 days and $15.50 for 19 GB over 30 days, taxes included. Note the ownership change: in November 2025 Telefónica completed the sale of its Ecuadorian business — the operator behind Movistar and the youth brand Tuenti, roughly 5 million customers and about 28 percent market share — to Luxembourg-based Millicom for around $380 million. Millicom trades as Tigo across Latin America, so Movistar and Tigo may refer to the same network depending on when you visit. Numbers, plans and chips keep working through the transition.

The flanker brand is worth knowing because it is the best-value prepaid in the market for light users. Tuenti, on the same network, sells 1 GB for $2, 3 GB for $4, 8 GB for $8, 12 GB for $10 and 20 GB for $20, zero-rates WhatsApp and Spotify, and rolls unused data into the following period rather than expiring it.

Cheapest data plans in Ecuador

CNT EP (Corporación Nacional de Telecomunicaciones) is state-owned and historically the price leader. Its prepaid chip costs about $3, data combos start near $1, and its 5G deployment has started in sectors of Quito. Where CNT trails is reach: outside cities and larger towns its footprint is noticeably thinner than Claro’s, which is why few travelers make it their primary line for a multi-region trip.

Will your phone run an Ecuador eSIM on local 4G and 5G bands?

Ecuadorian LTE sits mainly on common international bands. Movistar’s 4G runs on LTE bands 2, 4, 5 and 7; CNT uses bands 3, 7 and 28; Claro’s LTE covers the mainstream mid-bands, and its limited 5G rollout uses band n78. Any iPhone from the 8 onward, any recent Samsung or Google flagship, and most mid-range Android phones sold in the Americas or Europe cover these. A phone bought for a North American carrier will be fine; one bought for a single Asian market may not be.

Carrier locks matter more than bands. A phone locked to a home-network operator will refuse any third-party Ecuador eSIM profile, no matter how compatible the hardware is. Check before you buy: iPhone → Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock (“No SIM restrictions” is what you want); Android → Settings → Connections → look for an unlock or network-status entry. If it is locked, resolve that with the original carrier first — unlock requests for paid-off devices are usually processed quickly and free.

Run your specific model through the eSIM compatibility tool before spending money, and if the mechanics are new to you, what actually happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile explains the install in plain language. One quirk worth knowing: mainland-China iPhone variants ship with eSIM hardware disabled, and no Ecuadorian workaround exists — a physical SIM in another device is the only route for those.

How to buy an Ecuador eSIM as a foreign visitor

The direct route is in-person by design. Registration rules mean you walk into a store with a passport and walk out with a working line — there is no online tourist channel for Claro, Movistar/Tigo or CNT prepaid.

Claro, step by step:

  1. Find a Claro store — shopping centres in Quito, Guayaquil and Cuenca have several (CCI Mall, Quicentro Norte and Quicentro Sur in Quito; Mall del Sol and San Marino in Guayaquil; Mall del Río in Cuenca) — or use the airport counters at Quito’s Mariscal Sucre (UIO) or Guayaquil’s Olmedo (GYE), accepting a modest markup for the convenience.
  2. Hand over your passport for registration and choose a combo: entry options include about $1.05 for 1 GB per day, roughly $8.00 for 10 GB over 25 days, or around $10.25 for 14 GB over 30 days.
  3. Pay — cash is easiest; cards generally work at operator counters but not always at small resellers.
  4. Ask staff to install the eSIM or insert the physical SIM and load the data bundle before you leave; confirm data works on the spot.

Movistar/Tigo follows the same pattern: passport, in-store provisioning, prepaid combos from about $3.00 for 5 GB over 7 days to $15.50 for 19 GB over 30 days, taxes included. CNT sells its chip from about $3 with cheaper-per-gigabyte combos, and has at times offered a tourist-oriented chip for foreign passport holders — ask what is currently available rather than relying on a fixed product name. Pharmacies, supermarkets and corner shops sell SIMs and top-ups, but staff there rarely register lines or configure data.

SIM and eSIM registration in Ecuador

Ecuador splits its customers by document, and the line between the two is the single most useful thing to understand before you queue.

  • Prepaid runs on a passport. A visitor registers the line in a store with a passport, and that is the end of the paperwork. There is no local address requirement and no credit check.
  • Postpaid runs on a cédula. A contract plan requires the Ecuadorian national identity document plus a credit check. A foreigner can hold a cédula only with residency status, so postpaid is effectively closed to a visitor regardless of budget.
  • The registration is tied to the number, not the handset. Unlike Chile or Indonesia, Ecuador has no IMEI-registration clock on a phone you brought with you. What matters is that the person holding the line is identified.
  • Topping up is separate from registering. Buying credit at a pharmacy or supermarket does not re-register anything, but it also does not extend validity — you have to apply the top-up to a data package in the operator’s app or by dialling the right code.

The practical consequence: if you need a month of heavy data, a prepaid line on your passport is available to you, and a cheaper-postpaid-plan fantasy is not. Budget for prepaid rates and buy the largest valid pack at the start.

Buy the Galápagos-relevant plan deliberately — check that your profile roams on Claro — and download offline maps of the towns before boarding the flight from Quito or Guayaquil.

Ecuador eSIM by itinerary

Best Ecuador eSIM, sorted by trip

Quito is the best-connected city in the country, so the interest here is elevation rather than coverage. The Teleférico, the historic centre and the valleys all work on Claro and Movistar; the Cotopaxi day tour works in the park’s lower reaches and stops on the approach road. Take a pack sized for maps and photo uploads rather than streaming.

Galápagos coverage: a cruise or island stay

Book this one around a hard truth: mobile data is a town activity. Puerto Ayora, Puerto Baquerizo Moreno and Puerto Villamil work; the ferry between islands, every park visitor site and the highlands do not. If your boat advertises Wi-Fi, ask what it runs on before you count on it. The practical setup is a large allowance for evenings in port plus offline maps, books and a downloaded species guide for the days at sea.

Amazon lodge coverage: Tena, Puyo or Coca

Claro is the only carrier with coverage beyond the main Oriente towns, and even Claro falls to 3G or nothing in deep jungle. Lodges generally provide scheduled Wi-Fi or a satellite connection. Treat the phone as an evening tool, buy the largest Claro-attached bundle that fits your budget, and do not plan a work call from the river.

Avenue of the Volcanoes coverage by road

Cotopaxi, Chimborazo, Baños and the descent to the Oriente reward consistency over speed, which is Movistar’s strength on this route. Signal is reliable in the towns and the market villages and absent in the park interiors, so download the day’s route in the morning and keep a paper backup of key phone numbers.

Spondylus coast coverage: a surf and beach week

Montañita, Puerto López and Salinas are covered on Claro, and the coastal highway holds up town to town. The gap is the beaches between them — surf spots included — where there is no signal on any network. This is a trip where the hotel’s Wi-Fi matters more than a bigger data pack.

A month in Cuenca: long-stay Ecuador coverage

Cuenca is the long-stay base in Ecuador, and it is the one itinerary where a local line is worth the store visit. CNT undercuts the private carriers on per-gigabyte pricing, Claro and Movistar hold the better urban speeds, and all three work in the city proper. Register a prepaid line on your passport, top up in the app, and keep a travel eSIM as a backup for the valleys.

Why you should leave your APN alone in Ecuador

Most travelers never touch an APN in Ecuador, and that is the intended design. A travel eSIM profile ships with its own preloaded access-point settings — editing them is the classic way to break a working line. A carrier-issued eSIM or physical SIM is provisioned in store, and the clerk’s activation step normally writes the correct configuration to the phone automatically.

If a direct-purchased line shows signal but no data, do not hand-type values guessed from forums — APN strings for Ecuadorian carriers are not consistently published, and a wrong entry mimics a real fault. Instead: restart the phone once, cycle airplane mode, then ask the store that sold the line, or the operator’s own support channel (the Mi Claro, Mi Movistar or Mi CNT apps), to confirm the settings. ARCOTEL requires operators to provide customer support for exactly this class of problem.

When manual entry is genuinely needed — an older handset, or a profile that never self-configured — the menu paths are the same everywhere: iPhone: Settings → Cellular → the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network. Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → create a fresh APN profile. Enter only what the operator confirms, leave username and password blank unless stated, save, and restart.

Activating an Ecuador eSIM and fixing the usual failures

The standard install — save the QR code, add the profile on Wi-Fi, name the line, point mobile data at it, and switch roaming on — is laid out in the step-by-step eSIM activation guide. Install before you fly: Quito and Guayaquil arrival halls work, but their Wi-Fi is crowded at exactly the wrong moment. When errors appear, the catalogue in the eSIM activation error guide covers the standard ones. The four patterns below are Ecuador-specific.

A. Working in Quito, dead in the Oriente or the highlands. This is coverage, not a fault. Claro carries the widest rural map; if your profile connects to Movistar, open network selection and switch to Claro manually where it is available, and expect 3G at best between Oriente towns.

B. Strong signal bars, no internet. Confirm data roaming is enabled on the eSIM line — travel profiles roam onto local networks and need the toggle on. Then verify the eSIM, not your home SIM, is selected for mobile data in the dual-SIM settings.

C. Data stops after a day or two. Prepaid bundles are fixed-validity and fixed-volume; Ecuadorian prepaid throttles or hard-stops at the cap rather than billing overage. Check the remaining allowance in the operator app and load a top-up, or in a travel eSIM, check the app’s data meter before blaming the network.

D. Nothing in the Galápagos outside town. No profile fixes this. Within Puerto Ayora, Puerto Baquerizo Moreno and Puerto Villamil, coverage is usable on Claro or Movistar; at Tortuga Bay, on the ferry between islands and at every park visitor site, there is no signal on any network. Crossing between Ecuador and Peru or Colombia

Ecuador is bordered by two countries with their own networks and their own registration rules, and the crossings are ordinary road journeys rather than dramatic frontiers. Huaquillas reaches Tumbes and the Peruvian coast, Macará is the southern inland route, and Rumichaca on the Pan-American Highway is the main crossing into Ipiales and Colombia. On each of them the Ecuadorian line stops at the boundary post.

Two habits cover the crossing. Buy a plan that includes Peru or Colombia before you leave, or a South America regional profile that roams across the frontier and switches networks on arrival — cheaper than two local registrations and one fewer queue. And switch data roaming off on the Ecuadorian line as you approach the border, so a background sync in a queue cannot start a billed roaming session. The northern crossing at Rumichaca has the added quirk of altitude and a long walk between migration desks, which is exactly when an offline map earns its place.

Common Ecuador eSIM questions

Can I use an eSIM in the Galápagos?

Yes, with limits. Mobile coverage exists in Puerto Ayora on Santa Cruz, Puerto Baquerizo Moreno on San Cristóbal and Puerto Villamil on Isabela, and an Ecuador eSIM roaming on Claro or Movistar connects there like any local line. Outside the towns — visitor sites, boats, beaches and highlands — there is no signal on any network, so download offline maps and treat connectivity as a town-center activity.

Ecuador registration rules: how tight?

Only if you buy direct from a carrier. Ecuadorian prepaid lines are tied to ID, so Claro, Movistar/Tigo and CNT register your passport in store before activating a line. A travel eSIM is different: the profile roams on local networks under the provider’s own agreements, so no local registration happens and no Ecuadorian number is issued.

Can you buy an Ecuador eSIM from Claro?

Only for a contract plan. Prepaid registration runs on a passport and takes minutes. A postpaid plan requires the Ecuadorian national identity document and a credit check, and a foreigner can hold a cédula only with residency — which is why prepaid is the realistic route for a visitor even for a long stay.

Is eSIM available from Claro Ecuador directly?

Yes — Claro activates eSIMs for compatible devices, but provisioning happens in a physical store with your passport or cédula; there is no online tourist eSIM channel. Call ahead or check the Mi Claro app to confirm your chosen plan supports eSIM issuance, and budget time for the store visit, which experienced travelers usually schedule in Quito or Guayaquil rather than at the airport.

Which Ecuador eSIM works best in the Amazon?

One that reaches Claro’s network. Claro is the only operator with meaningful coverage beyond the main Oriente towns of Tena, Puyo and Macas, and even Claro falls to 3G or nothing in deep jungle. Lodge guests should assume satellite Wi-Fi at the property, buy the largest Claro-attached bundle that fits the budget, and keep expectations modest on river routes.

The going rate for Ecuador data

Only in early pockets. What happens to my Movistar eSIM during the Tigo rebrand?

Functionally, nothing at first. Are unlimited app bundles a thing on Ecuadorian prepaid?
