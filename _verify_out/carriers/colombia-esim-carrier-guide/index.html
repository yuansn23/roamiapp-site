Colombia’s mobile market just went through its biggest shake-up in a decade: 5G spectrum was auctioned in December 2023, commercial networks lit up in February 2024, and by the end of 2024 Claro had built 92% of the country’s 1,433 active 5G sites. That single fact reshapes the carrier question for any Colombia eSIM. Tigo and Movistar now share a combined network in many places, WOM is still mostly a 4G story, and the gap between “the fastest city network” and “the network that follows you up the mountain” is as wide as the Andes themselves. First-time Colombia eSIM setups come with predictable stumbling blocks — each section below takes on one of them.

Quick answer: Claro wins the coverage argument in Colombia, Tigo wins on cheap data — the tables below settle the details. You can also try the Colombia network setup through Roami’s test-drive eSIM before paying anything.

This page works through the decision in the order a traveller actually faces it: which carrier fits which trip, what the independent measurements say, what Colombia’s 5G rollout really looks like on the ground, and how to get connected without a store visit. Device support is quick to confirm on the compatibility checker if that is still unclear.

Bottom line: For most Colombia trips, Claro is the safe answer — fastest mobile network overall (median download 44.26 Mbps, Ookla H2 2025), the most consistent (85% of samples above 5 Mbps down), and by far the largest 5G build-out. Tigo is the 5G speed specialist (median 5G download 297.43 Mbps, best 5G latency at 39 ms) where its coverage reaches. Movistar’s headline numbers belong to its fixed-fibre empire; its mobile role is now largely a shared network with Tigo. Claro, Tigo and Movistar, measured against each other

Claro vs Tigo 5G: which is better in Colombia?

Claro leads the H2 2025 Ookla report on every “does it work everywhere” metric: median download 44.26 Mbps, upload 14.03 Mbps, 52 ms latency, and 85% of samples clearing the 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up consistency bar. The 5G story is even more lopsided. Under the coverage data cited in the OECD’s connectivity review, Claro was responsible for 92.2% of Colombia’s 1,433 active 5G sites at the end of 2024, reaching 43 municipalities while rivals measured their rollouts in dozens of sites.

For a visitor, that translates simply: in most Colombian cities your eSIM lands on Claro’s 5G by default, and on the highways and secondary towns Claro’s 4G is the one most likely to still be there.

Which carrier leads 5G in Colombia?

Where Tigo’s 5G reaches, it is the fastest consumer experience in the country — median 5G download of 297.43 Mbps, 5G upload of 34.51 Mbps, and the best 5G latency at 39 ms, with 93.2% of 5G samples meeting the 25 Mbps / 3 Mbps consistency threshold. The catch is reach: Tigo’s 5G coverage is measured in a fraction of Claro’s site count, and since the Tigo–Movistar network-sharing arrangement, the two brands increasingly draw on the same infrastructure. Treat Tigo as the premium urban 5G lane rather than a national solution.

Where does Movistar fit in Colombia?

Movistar’s headline figure — a median fixed download of 308.37 Mbps, fastest ISP in Colombia — describes its fibre empire, which matters for long-stay travellers choosing accommodation, not for the eSIM in your phone. On mobile, Movistar now operates substantially in partnership with Tigo, with a combined 5G footprint that is real in the big cities but small next to Claro’s. In practice your Colombia eSIM will meet Movistar infrastructure most often through that shared urban coverage.

Where does WOM fit in Colombia?

WOM, the market’s brash fourth entrant, won an 80 MHz block in the 2023 auction but had not deployed 5G as of the end of 2024. Its 4G network remains a genuine urban alternative — but for now, every eSIM ranking in Colombia is really a contest between Claro and the Tigo–Movistar axis.

Colombia’s mobile market has one structural fact worth knowing before any plan decision: coverage is excellent and throughput is modest. Claro, Tigo, Movistar and WOM all reach the cities, and Ookla’s 2H 2025 Colombia report (collection period July to December 2025) shows what separates them.

How to get a Colombian SIM or eSIM as a visitor

Colombia requires identity registration for SIM activation — locally that means presenting a passport at a carrier store or authorised dealer, a process travellers typically do in Bogotá or Medellín on day one or two. Some routes add a selfie or address verification on top. It works, but it costs an afternoon.

The travel eSIM route removes the counter entirely: the profile is issued and installed before departure, and activates on arrival. WhatsApp calling on Colombia networks

Colombia’s daily logistics run through two things travellers should plan around: altitude-driven transit and WhatsApp.

The transit reality. Bogotá’s TransMilenio and Medellín’s Metro — including the cable-car lines that climb into the comunas — are covered end to end on Claro and Tigo, which matters because both cities’ journey planning runs through apps with live crowding data. Ride-hailing apps work throughout the major cities and are the standard way visitors move at night.

The WhatsApp economy. Restaurants confirm bookings, apartment hosts send door codes, tour operators run whole businesses from WhatsApp groups — and none of it needs a Colombian number. A data-only eSIM with your home SIM kept alive for verification is the complete setup; keep the home line’s data roaming off so background traffic cannot bill you.

Where remote workers actually settle. Medellín’s El Poblado and Laureles co-working scene runs on the country’s fast fixed fibre, which is the practical complement to a mobile eSIM: fibre at the desk, Claro or Tigo on the move. Envigado’s 54.76 Mbps city median — the fastest in Colombia — sits exactly in that co-working corridor.

When a Colombian carrier SIM needs a manual APN

This matters only for a Colombian line bought at the carrier’s own counter. A travel eSIM supplies its own APN, and changing it breaks a working connection.

Carrier APN Username Password
Claro Colombia internet.claro.com.co blank blank
Movistar Colombia internet.movistar.com.co blank blank
Tigo Colombia internet blank blank
WOM carrier-pushed profile blank blank

Aggregator sites list several variants for Movistar and Tigo; if one is rejected, the carrier’s own settings page is the authority, not a third-party list.

  • iPhone: Settings → Cellular → the Colombia line → Cellular Data Network → APN. Nothing else on that screen needs editing.
  • Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → + → enter the APN, then save from the menu.

Save and restart. Then confirm the eSIM, not the physical SIM, is selected for mobile data — a very common cause of “the plan works but nothing loads” on Colombian dual-SIM setups.

Colombia eSIM troubleshooting, in the order you should try it

Nothing installs. Check the handset’s carrier-lock status first, then ask whether the QR code has already been consumed. A paid-off phone that was bought on contract abroad is the usual culprit.

Installs but shows no bars. Colombian automatic attach occasionally settles on a cell it cannot hold. A fifteen-count in flight mode, then a manual pick from Network Selection — Claro, Tigo, Movistar or WOM. A handset that has just crossed a departmental border needs a longer first scan than usual.

Bars, but no internet. Three causes, in order of likelihood: the eSIM is not the selected data line, data roaming is off for that line, or the APN is wrong on a locally bought profile. Check them in that sequence.

“Emergency calls only”. Two profiles do not share one radio politely. Remove the second, reboot, reset network settings, and keep a fresh-code reinstall for the end of the list.

Quick answers for eSIM users heading to Colombia

Where 5G is strongest in Colombia

Claro, by build-out: roughly 92% of the country’s active 5G sites at the end of 2024 were Claro’s, per data cited in the OECD review. Tigo is faster where its 5G exists. “Best” depends on whether you weight coverage (Claro) or peak speed in covered zones (Tigo).

Can you buy a Colombia eSIM from Claro?

Yes, with a passport and an in-person visit to a store or authorised dealer — registration is mandatory for all Colombian lines. It is a workable option for stays of a month or more. For trips under a couple of weeks, a travel eSIM installed before departure is the faster path by some distance.

Will my phone support Colombia’s bands?

Colombia asks nothing exotic of a handset: its 4G and 5G layers, 700 MHz LTE and n78 included, are carried by mainstream international models. The device compatibility checker confirms your exact model in one lookup.

Can you rely on WhatsApp calls in Colombia?

Absolutely — WhatsApp voice and video are the default calling method in Colombia and run perfectly over data. Traditional SIM calls are not included in data-only plans, but between WhatsApp and VoIP apps you will not miss them.

How much data do I need for Colombia?

Maps, ride-hailing, social media and daily video calls fit comfortably in 5–10 GB. Streaming adds up fast; hotels and cafés in the big cities have usable Wi-Fi anchored by that fast national fibre, so sync your heavy downloads to the room — and if hotspot time for a laptop enters the picture, pick the next data plan up rather than rationing.

Is Colombia’s 5G any good?

Yes, and it is competitive rather than exceptional. Tigo leads on 5G with a 297.43 Mbps median download and a 5G Speed Score of 65.27, ahead of Movistar at 280.72 Mbps. Ookla found no statistical difference between providers on overall 5G Connectivity Score, which means the field is close and your experience will depend on location rather than brand.

Which Colombia carrier should I choose?

Claro is the safest default: Best Mobile Network on a Connectivity Score of 66.80, a 44.26 Mbps all-technology median, and the fastest provider in all ten cities the report measures. Tigo is the pick if 5G throughput is what you care about, and WOM has the best mobile video experience at 75.84.

Which Colombian city is best for video calls?

Bogotá posts a 43.58 Mbps median download with 16.58 Mbps upload and — crucially — the lowest latency of any Colombian city measured, at 36.83 ms. That combination makes it the best city in the country for video calls, even though Medellín and Envigado post higher raw download numbers.

Why is Cartagena’s mobile data slower?

Cartagena’s 33.92 Mbps median and 70.11 ms latency are the weakest in the ten-city set, and the gap is about site density and backhaul rather than tourism load. Medellín and Envigado, at 50.72 and 54.76 Mbps, sit at the other end. For a resort week it makes no difference; for a work trip it does.

Do you show ID for a Colombia SIM?

Yes — a Colombian prepaid line is registered against an identity document at the point of sale, and a passport is the document a visitor uses. There is no legitimate anonymous route, which is one reason a travel eSIM is the faster option for a short trip.

APN values for Claro, Tigo and Movistar eSIMs

Claro uses internet.claro.com.co, Movistar internet.movistar.com.co, and Tigo internet, with username and password blank for all three. WOM pushes its profile automatically. You only need any of this if you bought the line from a Colombian carrier — a travel eSIM configures itself.

Does a Colombia eSIM work in Ecuador, Peru or Panama?

A single-country profile will not; it stops at the border. If the itinerary crosses into Ecuador, Peru or Panama, buy a regional Latin America plan or a separate profile per country, and check our Peru eSIM page for the southern leg.

Do I need 700 MHz support for a Colombia eSIM?

For city coverage, no — 1900 MHz and AWS carry urban traffic and nearly every handset has them. For rural, coastal and Amazonian coverage, 700 MHz (B28) matters a great deal. If your route leaves the cities, check the band before you choose a handset to travel with.

Is Colombia good enough for video calls?

In Bogotá and Medellín, yes — comfortably. Bogotá’s 36.83 ms latency makes it the best call city in the country and Medellín’s 50.72 Mbps median handles large transfers. On the Caribbean coast, at 70.11 ms latency in Cartagena, treat connectivity as good enough for email and messaging rather than meetings.

Do I need a different eSIM for the Amazon or the Pacific coast?

Not a separate profile, but a larger allowance and lower expectations. Leticia on the Amazon and the Pacific towns around Bahía Solano run on thinner infrastructure than the interior, with coverage concentrated in settlements and nothing between them. A multi-network Colombia eSIM will attach wherever a tower exists; between them, offline maps are the only plan that works.

Does tethering work on a Colombia eSIM?

Yes on the standard travel tiers, with the laptop sharing the same data pool. Working from cafés changes the arithmetic: a tethered laptop consumes several times what phone use does, so buy the tier that fits the computer rather than the handset.

Is a travel eSIM cheaper than roaming in Colombia?

Almost always, by a wide margin. Colombian prepaid is cheap by international standards, but a home carrier’s roaming rate is a multiple of even the local retail price. A travel eSIM sits between the two: more than a local SIM, far less than roaming, and available before you land.

When should you switch the Colombia profile on?
