Before you buy a Pakistan eSIM: five things that decide the outcome

Does Pakistan support your device model?

Pakistan’s 4G LTE runs on Band 3 (1800 MHz), Band 5 (850 MHz) and Band 40 (2300 MHz); 5G uses n78 (3500 MHz) and n41 (2500 MHz). Jazz and Zong have deployed 5G in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad and are still expanding. Flagship handsets sold in Europe, the Gulf and Asia normally cover all of these; a handset missing Band 40 will fall back to LTE more often than you would like.

Which documents does a Pakistan SIM take?

This is the step that catches visitors out, and it is more than a form. Pakistan’s regulator requires every connection to be biometrically verified, and foreign nationals register with a passport and valid visa instead of a CNIC. The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority caps foreign nationals at a small number of connections per passport — commonly three — and the line’s validity is generally tied to the visa period, so a tourist SIM can stop working when your permission to be in the country does. As one foreigner-registration guide puts it, expect fingerprint capture, a scanned passport, and a local contact address, usually your hotel.

3. What “unlimited” means on a Pakistani prepaid bundle

Pakistani prepaid bundles are almost all capped, and the cap is usually a byte count rather than a speed throttle. Two consequences follow. First, a “monthly” pack is a fixed volume that expires with the calendar, not a subscription you can stretch. Second, when the volume runs out the data stops rather than slowing, so a hotspotted laptop can burn a week’s allowance in an afternoon. Read the pack’s data figure, not its name, and buy a bigger pack than you think you need if you are tethering.

What changes in Pakistan’s northern valleys?

Northern Gilgit-Baltistan and the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa valleys are where the national averages stop meaning anything — see the regional table below. Zong’s consistency advantage shows up here, and SCO is the local operator in Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. Download offline maps for the Karakoram Highway regardless of which carrier you buy.

Do visitors need to register their phone in Pakistan?

Pakistan runs a separate device-approval system. An imported handset using a Pakistani SIM may need approval through the Device Identification Registration and Blocking System, and eligible visitors can normally register one personal device free for up to 120 days per visit. Find your IMEI by dialling *#06#, and note that the process needs a Pakistani number already issued in your name — a foreign number will not do.

How to buy a Pakistan eSIM or local SIM

Route one: a travel eSIM, bought before you fly.

  1. Check your handset is unlocked and eSIM-capable with the device compatibility checker — a locked phone fails at install, not at checkout.

  2. Buy the plan and install the profile over Wi-Fi at home, then label the line.

  3. Set the eSIM as the line used for mobile data and switch data roaming on.

  4. Land, switch data roaming off on your home SIM, and confirm the profile attaches before you leave the arrivals hall.

  5. Keep the QR code screenshot in cloud storage for a reinstall.

Route two: a local SIM, bought in the country.

  1. Go to an official operator point — an airport kiosk at Islamabad, Karachi or Lahore, a Jazz Experience Centre, or a branded Zong, Ufone or Telenor service centre. Street vendors and unbranded shops cannot process a passport registration and may sell you a number registered in someone else’s name.

  2. Present your original passport and visa; both will be scanned and kept on file.

  3. Give a local address — your hotel is accepted — and complete the registration form with your purpose of visit and intended stay.

  4. Complete fingerprint biometric verification against NADRA’s system. Expect this even as a tourist; it is not optional.

  5. Choose a bundle, note the expiry, and keep the verification receipt.

  6. Test data before leaving the counter, and confirm the number is registered in your passport name.

The travel eSIM route exists precisely to skip steps two to six. The local route exists because it is cheaper per gigabyte and gives you a Pakistani number for local calls. Neither is wrong; they suit different trip lengths.

How to install and activate a Pakistan eSIM

You add the profile yourself in a couple of minutes — plans and activation details are on the Pakistan eSIM page, with the platform-by-platform walkthrough in our eSIM activation guide.

iPhone: Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → Use QR Code, then label the line and set it as the line used for mobile data.

Android: Settings → Connections → SIM Manager → Add eSIM, then scan the QR and set the new profile as the preferred data SIM.

Either way, turn data roaming on for the eSIM line, leave your home SIM active for calls and verification codes, and switch data roaming off on the home line so background traffic is not billed. If the counter installed a local SIM instead, the same two settings decide whether data flows.

Getting your eSIM working: the failures that actually happen

The generic activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our eSIM activation error guide. The list below narrows to four patterns that matter in Pakistan.

A. The local SIM works, then stops a few hours later. Classic biometric-verification failure: the registration did not complete cleanly against NADRA’s records and the operator’s system blocked the line. Take the original passport, the visa and the verification receipt back to the same franchise and re-verify. Do not buy a replacement SIM — the new one will fail the same way.

B. A working imported phone shows no service on a Pakistani SIM. The device, not the SIM, is the problem: the handset’s IMEI is not approved in the PTA device system. Dial *#06# for the IMEI, confirm its status, and register the device. This is separate from SIM registration and is the single most common cause of a phone that works at home and not in Pakistan.

C. Installed, but no data on the Karakoram Highway or in a valley town. The phone is hunting for 4G or 5G that is not there. Force manual network selection and allow 3G, then accept what the valley has rather than waiting for a band that is absent. In the settlements this usually recovers a usable signal; on the passes there is nothing to recover.

D. Your travel eSIM camps on the weaker network. A roaming profile attaches to whichever partner network it is provisioned for and will drift to another one that has no data agreement. Turn off automatic selection and lock the phone to the partner network the product page names, then re-enable automatic selection only when you leave that region.

What a Jazz, Zong or Telenor helpdesk needs from you

Keep your EID (from *#06#), your order or account number, the name of the partner network your profile should attach to, a screenshot of the error, and a short list of what you have already tried. Pakistan’s verification questions turn on the registration record, so having the passport and receipt to hand shortens the conversation considerably.

Crossing out of Pakistan: India, Afghanistan, Iran and China

Pakistan’s land borders each behave differently, and each one ends your Pakistani coverage in a different way.

India — Wagah/Attari. The Attari–Wagah crossing near Lahore is the only regularly functioning land route between the two countries, and it can close at short notice when diplomatic relations sour. Border formalities run from about 30 minutes to over two hours, and the crossing is the busiest leg for third-country nationals, since visas on both sides are hard to obtain. Your Pakistani SIM stops working at the last Pakistani post; a plan covering both countries is the only thing that keeps you online across the line. The Kartarpur Corridor is a separate, pilgrimage-only facility and is not a general entry point.

Afghanistan — Torkham. The main crossing on the Khyber route, roughly four hours from Islamabad, is busy with freight and requires a visa arranged well in advance. Coverage dies at the border; there is no roaming arrangement a travel profile can lean on in the immediate border zone.

Iran — Taftan. The border post on the Quetta–Zahedan corridor is remote — around 1,520 km from Islamabad via the N-40 — and the approach has long gaps in signal on the Pakistani side. Travellers using it should treat the whole corridor as offline-first, with offline maps, cash and a printed itinerary.

China — Khunjerab Pass. At 4,693 m, this is the highest paved international crossing in the world and the northern terminus of the Karakoram Highway. It is seasonal, generally open May to September, and there is no usable signal on the pass from either side. Whatever plan you carry, plan the gap.

In every case the pattern is the same: a Pakistan-only profile dies at the border without a warning message, and a multi-country plan is the only fix. Buy for the itinerary, not just for the first country you land in.

Common questions about Pakistan eSIM (12 answered)

Can you keep your home eSIM active alongside a Pakistan eSIM?

Yes, if your phone supports dual eSIM (iPhone 13 and later, Google Pixel 7 and later, and equivalent Samsung models), you can have two profiles installed at once. Only one can carry data at a time, and you switch between them in settings. Roami supports this, which lets you keep your home eSIM active for calls while the Pakistan profile handles data.

Does data slow down at peak hours in Pakistan?

Yes, as on any network — expect slower throughput between roughly 7pm and 11pm when demand peaks. Jazz still recorded a 24.13 Mbps median download across the measurement period, and Zong’s 86.2% consistency score indicates it holds up under load. A larger data pack does not buy priority; if peak-hour performance matters, prefer the network with the better consistency in your city.

What speeds should I actually expect?

Based on 2H 2025 Speedtest Intelligence data, the fastest mobile network in Pakistan is Jazz at a 24.13 Mbps median download and 8.89 Mbps median upload, with 48 ms latency. Zong follows at a 20.5 Mbps median download with the country’s best consistency. Nationally, Pakistan’s median mobile download measured 32.01 Mbps in August 2026 in Ookla’s global index. Your own figure will move with the handset, the band and the hour.

Will WhatsApp calls work in Pakistan?

Yes. Roami’s Pakistan plans are data-only, which is exactly what VoIP services need — WhatsApp, WeChat, FaceTime, Skype and Zoom all work over the connection. Jazz’s 48 ms latency and 8.89 Mbps upload make video calls stable in the cities. Traditional circuit-switched voice calls are not supported on a data-only eSIM, which is why keeping your home SIM active for voice is worth doing.

Can you buy more than one Pakistan data package?

Yes. You can buy multiple Pakistan packages in advance, keep them in your account and activate them sequentially — a 7-day pack first, a 30-day pack after it expires. Each is activated by QR code or through the app, which suits long stays and repeat visits.

Do I have to complete biometric verification to use a Pakistan eSIM?

For a locally issued line, yes — any Jazz, Zong, Ufone or Telenor connection, physical or embedded, is registered against a passport or CNIC with fingerprint verification. A travel eSIM bought outside Pakistan connects through a roaming profile instead and is not registered locally, which is why it needs no biometric step. Do not assume every product labelled “eSIM” avoids the requirement: an operator-branded eSIM is a Pakistani line and follows the local rules.

How many SIMs can a foreigner register in Pakistan?

Fewer than a Pakistani citizen can. The limit for foreign nationals is commonly reported as three connections per passport, against five per CNIC for citizens, and the line’s validity is generally tied to your visa period rather than being indefinite. If you plan a long stay, ask at the counter how the number behaves when your visa expires.

Does your phone need IMEI registration in Pakistan?

Possibly, if its IMEI has not been approved in the device registration system. Pakistan approves imported handsets separately from SIM registration, and eligible visitors can normally register one personal device free for up to 120 days per visit. Check the IMEI at the official DIRBS portal before you travel if you intend to use a local SIM, and note that the process requires a Pakistani number issued in your name.

Is 5G available in Pakistan yet?

Yes, in the major cities. Jazz and Zong have deployed 5G on n78 (3500 MHz) and n41 (2500 MHz) in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, and coverage continues to expand. Outside those cities, assume 4G — the national median download of 32.01 Mbps is an LTE-era number, and any 5G indicator on your phone in a valley town is optimistic.

Which carrier is best for northern Pakistan?

Zong, on the consistency evidence — 86.2% of its samples cleared the 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up threshold, the best in the country, and consistency is what decides whether a map reloads on a mountain road. SCO is the regional operator in Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir and is worth asking about locally. No carrier covers the Khunjerab Pass or Fairy Meadows, so offline maps are not optional.

Can I share my Pakistan eSIM connection?

Yes. Roami’s Pakistan plans support hotspot tethering, and Pakistani prepaid bundles generally permit it. The practical limit is data volume rather than policy: a tethered laptop can consume a monthly bundle in a single afternoon of video calls, so size the pack to your tethering, not to your phone usage.

What happens to my connection when I cross into India or Iran?

It stops. A Pakistan-only profile has no coverage outside the country and usually gives no warning message — the phone simply loses service. A multi-country plan is the only way to stay online across a land border, and on the Torkham, Taftan and Khunjerab approaches there is no usable signal in the border zone regardless of what plan you hold.

Pakistan eSIM claims worth checking

Myth: every Pakistan eSIM delivers the same speed.
