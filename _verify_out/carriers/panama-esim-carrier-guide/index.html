Panama is the Central American country where the carrier question has a clear answer (+Móvil owns the widest map), the airport question has a clear answer too (a 7-day unlimited tourist SIM costs about $30 at Tocumen and $10 or less in town — the steepest airport markup in the region), and the registration rule is unambiguous: passport registration for every SIM is required by law. This guide covers the four operators — including Digicel, the fourth network most recent articles quietly drop — the islands where coverage ends, and the currency quirk that makes price comparisons here unusually easy: Panama runs on US dollars.

Quick answer: Most travellers in Panama will be happiest with the local networks; travel eSIMs make sense for tighter budgets and city-only trips. Confirm your handset on the handset compatibility checker before anything else — the step most often skipped in Panama.

+Móvil vs the rest: which is better in Panama?

+Móvil, the consumer brand of Cable & Wireless Panama, holds the widest and most reliable national coverage — 4G+ in every major city and the strongest rural map, which is what matters on the Pan-American Highway legs to David and Boquete. For a visitor, it is the default answer to “which carrier,” and the one that justifies itself the moment you leave Panama City.

The regulator is ASEP — the National Public Services Authority — which licenses all operators and, usefully, requires them to publish the bandwidth and prices they actually sell, so its site doubles as a consumer-protection resource.

Tigo and Claro eSIM: the urban peers in Panama

Movistar’s Panamanian operation was rebranded under the Tigo name, joining Claro as the two credible urban alternatives — good 4G in the capital, decent coverage along the Canal and the main highways, thinner rural maps than +Móvil. Both support eSIM on compatible devices, with rollout that has been gradual rather than complete. For a Panama City stay, either is fine; for anything else, they are the fallback networks, which is precisely what a multi-network profile uses them for.

Digicel: the fourth network most guides drop

Digicel still operates in Panama as the fourth MNO — a Caribbean-rooted group whose Panamanian network is best characterized as city-competent and country-thin. Articles that list only three operators or that build their whole comparison around Digicel’s tourist zones (as older guides did) are both slightly off: the honest read is that Digicel is a reasonable urban budget option and not a national plan.

Can you buy a Panama eSIM directly from a carrier?

Yes — but the pricing is the story, not the availability. All four operators sell prepaid lines and support eSIM on compatible devices. What varies wildly is where you buy it.

Registration is the constant across all four: presenting a valid passport to register a SIM is a legal requirement in Panama, enforced at every purchase point — airport kiosks, carrier stores, supermarkets like Riba Smith and El Rey, pharmacies and corner shops. The clerk records your passport details; activation is usually immediate.

The Tocumen airport SIM markup, and how to skip it

Tocumen International Airport is where most visitors meet Panamanian connectivity, and it is the most expensive meter in the country.

Tocumen SIM pricing arithmetic: kiosk versus city store

  • One traveler: the airport kiosk charges about $30 for a 7-day unlimited tourist SIM; the same package in town is around $10. You overpay roughly $20 for the convenience of buying at arrivals.
  • A couple: three times the lines, three times the markup — about $40 extra.
  • A family of three: buying at the airport costs around $60 more than the same three lines bought in town.

The two clean ways around it: buy from a city store on day one if your arrival is by day, or install a Panama eSIM bought online before you fly — no counter, no markup, data live at the gate.

Why the Tocumen airport SIM premium exists in Panama

Three factors combine in Panama in a way they do not in most countries:

  1. Tocumen’s kiosks are a concession, not a carrier store. The operator running them prices for transit passengers who have no alternative.
  2. The city is close. Panama City is 25–35 minutes from the airport, so the “captive audience” window is short — but the kiosks still price as if it were not.
  3. Red-eye arrivals are common. Transatlantic and US west-coast flights land after midnight, when city stores are shut and the kiosks are the only option — except they close around 00:30.

The practical rule: if your flight lands between roughly 05:30 and 00:30 and you want a local SIM, still wait for the city. If it lands outside those hours, you cannot buy at the airport at all — which settles the question in favour of a pre-installed Panama eSIM.

Panama coverage gaps and weak spots

  • The Interamericana is the coverage spine. Signal follows the Pan-American Highway from the capital through Santiago to David, and thins the moment you leave it.
  • The archipelagos are boat problems, not network problems. Bocas and San Blas both have service on their main islands; the gaps are the water in between.
  • The Darién Gap is genuinely disconnected. This is not a coverage gap you can plan around with a better carrier — it is roadless terrain where a satellite messenger is the only correct tool.
  • Panama City congestion is real. The financial district at 08:00 and 18:00 is the country’s only genuine capacity problem, and it affects every operator equally.

For market context: Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index places Panama’s median mobile download around 40 Mbps (roughly 88th globally) in recent editions — below the world average but ample for maps, WhatsApp and video calls in the cities. Cable.co.uk’s pricing survey prices 1 GB at about USD 1.63, and DataReportal’s Digital 2025: Panama counts 5.40 million mobile connections against 3.54 million internet users. And since Panama’s currency is the US dollar, every price you see is directly comparable — no mental conversion needed.

Will my phone clear Panama’s eSIM check?

Check this before you pay for anything — a carrier-locked handset cannot be unlocked from inside Panama.

  • Dial *#06#. An EID confirms the handset carries eSIM hardware.
  • iPhone: Carrier Lock under Settings → General → About — without “No SIM restrictions” the profile will not install.
  • Android: Settings → Connections → SIM Manager — greyed out means locked or unsupported.

Situations that call for hand-entered APN settings

Only a short list of situations in Panama calls for hand-entered settings.

  • A profile installed from a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
  • An older handset that did not pull carrier settings automatically
  • The line did not re-register after travelling to an archipelago

On iPhone: Settings → Cellular → [the Panamanian line] → Cellular Data Network. On Android: Settings → Network & Internet → Access Point Names. Save, restart, then verify the eSIM — not your home SIM — is the line carrying data, because with dual SIM the default often still points abroad.

Installing the profile, step by step

  1. Install before departure. The airport kiosk closes overnight (roughly 00:30–05:30 dead zone), and red-eye arrivals are common at Tocumen.
  2. San Blas and Darién silence is expected — check the region table before blaming your phone.
  3. Manual network selection helps outside the capital. Forcing the phone onto +Móvil can attach where automatic search settles on a thinner network.
  4. Bars, no data: verify the data line, the roaming switch, then the APN. Anything still broken is covered in our eSIM troubleshooting guide.

Four recovery steps when the eSIM misbehaves

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Head to Settings -> General -> About; Carrier Lock should show “No SIM restrictions”
  2. Make sure the code has not been used – eSIM QRs are generally one-shot
  3. Support can tell you if the profile is waiting to be released

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Toggle the line off, wait a beat, then on
  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose +Móvil, Tigo, Claro or Digicel manually
  3. Try a restart, then a network settings reset as step two

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Match the APN entry to the value listed for your carrier above
  2. The eSIM should be the default line for mobile data – confirm it
  3. Turn data roaming on for this specific line
  4. Check the allowance has not been used up

D. Works in Panama City, dead on the highway or an island

  1. That is the rural map, not a fault — +Móvil holds where the others thin
  2. Try manual network selection
  3. Check whether you are simply between towns on a side road

Paying in Panama: cards, cash and data

Panama runs on the US dollar, which makes budgeting simple — but the payment picture is worth knowing before you arrive:

  • Cards work widely in Panama City and in tourist areas, so you are not dependent on a local wallet the way you would be in much of East Africa.
  • Small vendors, boats and rural services often prefer cash. Keep dollars on you for the Bocas and San Blas legs.
  • Ride-hailing works. Uber operates in the capital and the metro area, and it needs a data connection to book — another argument for a Panama eSIM from touchdown.
  • Your bank may flag Panamanian transactions. Tell them before you travel, because a blocked card combined with no data is a genuinely bad afternoon.

None of this requires a local number. It requires a working connection and a little cash.

The Panama eSIM questions we hear most

What does a tourist SIM cost at Tocumen airport?

Yes — the Travel Data Global kiosks sell tourist SIMs around the clock (roughly 05:30–00:30) with passport registration on the spot, at about $30 for 7 days of unlimited data. The same package costs around $10 in the city, so frequent advice is: eSIM before you fly, or city store on day one.

Will SIM shops in Panama ask for ID? Yes — passport registration is a legal requirement at every purchase point, from airport kiosks to minimarkets. Online travel eSIMs arrive pre-registered via the provider.

Coverage compared: +Móvil vs the rest

+Móvil, consistently — the best rural and national map, with Tigo and Claro strong in the cities and Digicel city-focused. Outside the capital, +Móvil is the network that holds.

The companies behind Panama’s networks

Four: +Móvil, Tigo, Claro and Digicel. Digicel is the one most recent articles drop — it is still operating and is a legitimate urban option, but it is not a national network.

Will my eSIM work in San Blas or Bocas del Toro?

In Bocas, yes on the main island and patchy between islands. In Guna Yala, only limited coverage on the main islands — plan to be offline. Darién has no practical coverage at all.

5G compared: +Móvil vs the rest in Panama

5G expansion is under way in Panama City and other major centers; a 4G connection remains the practical norm for travelers. Judge coverage by region, not by technology label.

Do I need a local number in Panama?

Not usually — WhatsApp handles calls, messages and most business communication, and data-only eSIMs run it perfectly. A local number matters mainly for longer stays and local banking.

How many gigabytes should you buy for Panama?

5–10 GB covers maps, WhatsApp, social and video calls comfortably. Data costs around USD 1.63 per GB locally, so oversizing is cheap — but bandwidth in the archipelagos, not price, is the limiter.

Does my phone need to be unlocked for Panama?

An unlocked phone is required. A locked phone will not accept any third-party profile. Check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock (iPhone) before you buy, and ask your home carrier to unlock if needed.

Is the airport SIM really that much more expensive?

Yes — about three times the city price, which makes Panama one of the starkest airport markups anywhere. Buying in town is the same product at roughly a third of the price, with identical registration requirements.

Can I pay for a Panama eSIM in dollars?

Panama uses the US dollar as its currency, so every price is directly comparable to what you would pay at home — and there is no exchange-rate surprise on your card statement. It is one of the easier markets in the region to budget for.

Do cheaper regional plans beat single-country eSIMs?

Not on a Panama-only plan. For a Central or South American loop, price a regional plan instead of stacking single-country profiles — our Costa Rica eSIM guide and Colombia eSIM page cover the two most common onward stops.

Problem phones for Panama travel eSIMs

Prices compared: +Móvil vs the rest

In town, yes — about $10 for a week of unlimited data is genuinely good value. At the airport, no: the same product at roughly $30 is the worst-value purchase of the trip.

Will my eSIM work on a Canal transit or cruise?

On a partial transit, yes — the locks are covered. Signal follows the shore infrastructure rather than the water, so expect gaps on the longer lake sections in the middle of the isthmus.
