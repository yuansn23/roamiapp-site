Russia is the one destination where “which carrier is best” is the third question, not the first. The first two: you cannot realistically buy a local SIM as a tourist anymore, and your eSIM will be data-blocked for roughly 24 hours after it first registers on a Russian network. Both are government policy, not operator choice, and both have changed more than once since 2025 — which is why most older guides to Russian connectivity are now actively wrong. This guide lays out the current rules with dates, the MTS vs MegaFon vs Beeline vs Tele2 reality for roaming profiles, and the day-one checklist that decides whether your Russia eSIM lands you connected or leaves you stuck.

Russia SIM and eSIM rule changes in 2025–2026

Russia’s connectivity rules changed more in eighteen months than in the previous decade. These are the facts a current guide must state, with dates:

Date What changed What it means for you
1 Jan 2025 Foreigners buying local SIMs now need a SNILS number, a verified Gosuslugi account, biometric registration and IMEI binding Local SIM purchase is effectively closed to tourists
1 Jul 2025 Enforcement deadline — non-biometric foreign-registered SIMs deactivated Old “buy a local SIM at the airport” advice is dead
Early Oct 2025 24-hour “cooling-off” block on all foreign SIMs and eSIMs at first registration on a Russian network Data and SMS blocked on arrival day; voice calls still work
21 Nov 2025 Operator captcha page opens to foreigners The 24-hour block can often be cleared in ~5 minutes
Feb 2026 WhatsApp fully blocked inside Russia Install an alternative (Telegram remains throttled but reachable) before flying
10 Jul 2026 Traffic filtering now follows your physical location, not your operator A roaming eSIM no longer bypasses Russian app blocks

The official rationale for the cooling-off block is drone defense — SIMs are used in drone navigation — and implementation is inconsistent: some travelers report no block, others a block that appears hours after landing. Two re-triggers most guides omit: 72 hours of inactivity re-arms the block (idle over a weekend on hotel Wi-Fi and Monday you are blocked again — let the eSIM register briefly each day), and switching operator or region can re-trigger it.

What the 2025–2026 Russia SIM rules mean for your trip

The practical upshot is that Russia is now a destination you prepare for, not one you improvise in:

  • Nothing can be bought on arrival — not a local SIM, not a data top-up with a foreign card, often not even airport Wi-Fi access.
  • Day one is offline by policy. Build your first-day plan around that rather than fighting it.
  • The rules are recent and moving. Any guide that does not mention the October 2025 block or the July 2026 filtering change is out of date, whatever else it gets right.
  • Voice still works when data does not. That single fact rescues most arrival-day logistics: a call to your hotel or transfer is usually possible even while the data block is active.

What usually goes wrong with mobile data in Russia?

1. The 24-hour block on arrival

The moment your Russia eSIM first finds a Russian tower, the clock starts: mobile data and SMS blocked, voice calls working throughout. Since November 2025, the operator sends a verification link by SMS; open it, complete the captcha, and data typically returns within minutes — even inside the official 24-hour window. Check your message inbox the moment you land.

Practical consequence: treat day one as an offline day. Download everything before boarding:

  • Offline maps: Google Maps offline packs, or 2GIS — the Russian mapping standard, far more accurate for local addresses
  • Yandex Go — the dominant ride-hailing app; register and add a payment card at home
  • Your hotel address, transfer details and bookings as offline screenshots
  • Any messaging alternatives you plan to use

2. Public Wi-Fi that rejects foreign numbers

Most public Wi-Fi in Russia — including at Sheremetyevo (SVO), Domodedovo (DME) and Pulkovo (LED), and most metro stations — requires SMS verification via a Russian mobile number. Your foreign number does not authenticate. Exceptions: international hotel chains with email login, some branded café chains, business lounges. The workaround is the same as above: have data working (after the captcha clears) or plan your first hours around pre-downloaded content.

Three practical consequences worth planning around:

  • Do not count on the airport for anything. No SIM purchase, no reliable Wi-Fi, no card payments for a data top-up.
  • Your hotel is your first connectivity. International chains with email-based login are the dependable landing point; a screenshot of your booking plus the address gets you there.
  • Restaurants and cafés often work. Some branded chains accept other verification; treat them as opportunistic wins rather than a plan.

3. App blocks that now follow your location

Until mid-2026, a roaming eSIM’s traffic exited abroad and slipped past Russian filtering. Since 10 July 2026, filtering follows your physical location — WhatsApp, Instagram and YouTube stay blocked even on a roaming profile. Any guide still promising “your traffic exits abroad, so the blocks don’t apply” was written before that date. What still works: Telegram (throttled), Russian apps, and most of the open web. If WhatsApp is your primary messaging channel, set up an alternative before departure.

Which mobile networks operate in Russia?

In most countries this section would decide your purchase. In Russia it decides almost nothing, for three reasons:

  1. You are not choosing a carrier — your provider’s roaming agreement does. You cannot walk into an MTS store and ask for their network.
  2. All four cover the tourist circuit well. Moscow, St. Petersburg, the Golden Ring, Kazan, Yekaterinburg and the Trans-Siberian cities are covered by all of them in practice.
  3. Switching networks can re-arm the 24-hour block. Stability beats optimization: once your profile attaches and the captcha clears, leave it alone.

What this means in practice: a multi-network Russia eSIM profile that can attach to more than one of the four is the resilient choice. 5G is effectively not a factor: public 5G rollout has been stalled by spectrum disputes for years, and 4G/LTE is the working layer everywhere.

What a Russian local SIM actually requires in 2026

For completeness, because guides still recommend it: since January 2025 a Russian prepaid line requires a SNILS (individual insurance account number), a verified Gosuslugi government-services account, biometric registration and IMEI binding. None of that is available to a short-stay visitor, and enforcement from July 2025 deactivated foreign-registered lines that did not comply. This is not a matter of finding the right shop — it is a closed channel.

How to confirm your phone’s eSIM and lock status

There is no store inside Russia that can fix a locked handset, so settle this at home.

  • Dial *#06#. The presence of an EID confirms eSIM capability.
  • iPhone: Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock must read “No SIM restrictions”. Once you are inside Russia there is no fixing this.
  • Android: the path is Settings -> Connections -> SIM Manager.

Do this at home, not in Russia.

When does a Russia eSIM need manual APN entry?

This concerns manually provisioned profiles only — and it is never a cure for the 24-hour block. Manual entry applies when:

  • You are on a manually provisioned profile rather than a managed travel eSIM
  • A profile installed from a manual activation code
  • An older handset that did not pull carrier settings automatically
  • Your profile re-attached after the block lifted and data did not resume

Where to enter Russian APN settings on your phone

Settings → Cellular → [the Russian line] → Cellular Data Network on iPhone, or Settings → Network & Internet → Access Point Names on Android. Save, restart, confirm the line carries data — and remember that editing an APN cannot lift a policy block, only clear a configuration error.

Five quirks of using a Russia eSIM

  1. Install before boarding. The profile attaches when it first meets a Russian tower — that is when the 24-hour clock starts, so time it to your arrival.
  2. Check for the verification SMS the moment you land. The captcha link is your five-minute escape from the block; it arrives as soon as the phone registers.
  3. Keep the eSIM registering daily. 72 hours idle re-arms the block — a brief daily connection on the mobile network keeps it clear.
  4. Avoid switching networks once connected. Moving between operators or regions can re-trigger the block.
  5. If data never comes back after the captcha, work the standard sequence — data line, roaming toggle, network selection — then the full troubleshooting guide.

Russia’s SIM and eSIM rules explained

The terminology in this guide is not optional background — each term is a specific gate you either pass or do not:

Term What it means for a visitor
SNILS A Russian individual insurance account number. Required to buy a local SIM since January 2025 — and unavailable to short-stay visitors.
Gosuslugi The government services portal. A verified account is part of the same local-SIM requirement.
Biometric registration In-person fingerprint and photo capture. Another local-SIM gate with no tourist workaround.
IMEI binding Tying the line to a specific handset’s hardware ID. Change phones and the line can stop working.
Cooling-off period The roughly 24-hour data and SMS block applied to foreign SIMs and eSIMs at first registration. Voice calls continue.
Captcha verification The operator page that can clear the cooling-off block in minutes; the link arrives by SMS when you land.
Location-based filtering Since July 2026, app blocking follows where you physically are rather than which network carries you.

Read together, the list explains the whole guide: four of these gates apply only to local SIMs, which is why the travel eSIM is the route that stays open — and three apply to every foreign line, which is why day one is offline.

Reader FAQ: eSIMs and carriers in Russia

Can you buy a local SIM in Russia?

Practically, no. Since January 2025, buying a local SIM requires a SNILS number, a verified Gosuslugi account, biometric registration and IMEI binding — a process built for residents. Enforcement from July 2025 deactivated non-compliant foreign-registered SIMs. The Russia eSIM bought before you fly is the working alternative.

What is Russia’s 24-hour arrival block?

Government policy, in force since early October 2025: every foreign SIM and eSIM is data- and SMS-blocked for roughly 24 hours after first registering on a Russian network, officially as an anti-drone measure. Voice calls keep working, and since November 2025 the operator’s captcha page can clear the block in minutes — look for the verification SMS as soon as you land.

Does a travel eSIM bypass Russian app blocks?

No longer. Since 10 July 2026, filtering follows your physical location rather than your operator: WhatsApp, Instagram and YouTube stay blocked even on a roaming profile. Telegram remains throttled but reachable; Russian apps work normally.

Which Russian network will your eSIM use?

Whichever network your profile’s host agreement covers — MTS, MegaFon, Beeline or Tele2 all host inbound roaming, and all four cover the main tourist geography well. Multi-network profiles that can attach to more than one are the resilient choice, but note that switching networks can re-trigger the arrival block.

Does airport Wi-Fi work for foreigners?

Usually not — most Russian public Wi-Fi, including at SVO, DME and LED, requires SMS verification with a Russian mobile number. International hotel chains and some branded cafés are the reliable exceptions. Plan your arrival hours around pre-downloaded content.

Will my eSIM work on the Trans-Siberian Railway?

Along the route, as a station-by-station service: strong in the cities it passes, silent on the long stretches between. Download offline maps and entertainment before departure, and expect connectivity at the major stops rather than en route.

Does 5G work in Russia?

Not for practical purposes — public 5G rollout has been stalled for years by spectrum disputes, and 4G/LTE is the working layer in every city. Ignore any guide promising 5G speeds in Moscow.

Is it safe to use an eSIM in Russia for banking apps?

The connection itself is standard encrypted mobile data. The operational risks are different: some banking and messaging apps behave differently from inside Russia, and payments with foreign cards do not process — so handle any banking before departure and keep the trip’s transactions on cash or a card that works locally.

What happens if I leave Russia and come back?

The registration state resets in important ways: returning after a border crossing starts the arrival process again, so plan a second offline window on re-entry. If your itinerary is Moscow–Helsinki–St. Petersburg by train, treat both entries as separate connectivity events.

How to top up a Russia eSIM

Not with a foreign card, and often not through the provider’s own website either. Buy a plan sized for your whole trip before you leave, and treat any top-up as something to arrange from outside the country.

Do I need a VPN?

Popular VPN services are widely blocked or degraded inside Russia, and since July 2026 the filtering follows your physical location rather than the network you are on — so a VPN is not the reliable workaround that older guides suggested. Plan your messaging and cloud storage around alternatives you have already installed.

Are Russia travel eSIM plans data-only?

Standard travel eSIM plans are data-only, which is why voice calls are described separately throughout this guide: your home SIM in the physical slot handles calls and SMS verification if your carrier supports roaming there. Check your home carrier’s Russia terms before you fly — some have suspended roaming there entirely.

How many GB do you need in Russia?

10–20 GB is a sensible range for maps, messaging and photo uploads, with laptop hotspot use pushing you toward the upper end — particularly since day one is offline and hotel Wi-Fi is unreliable for foreigners. Local data is cheap, but you are buying at travel-eSIM prices, not local ones.

Will Russia’s rules change before my trip?

They probably will — this page states the rules as of its last update. Re-check the block and filtering regime within a few weeks of departure, and treat any guide that does not date its claims as untrustworthy on this topic.

Can I use a physical travel SIM instead of an eSIM in Russia?

If your phone has no eSIM hardware, a physical travel SIM bought at home is the alternative — the rules that close local SIM purchase to foreigners do not apply to a foreign travel SIM you bring with you. The arrival block and the filtering rules still do.

What should I do if my flight lands after midnight?

Plan for the block rather than around it: your hotel address as a screenshot, a card that works locally for the taxi, and no expectation of data until you reach Wi-Fi and can complete the captcha. Late landings are the worst case for a policy that assumes connectivity.
