A Uruguay eSIM comes with one absolute exception worth knowing before you book: Cabo Polonio has no signal from any carrier — this guide covers that, and the passport rule at official outlets. Uruguay is South America’s quiet 5G overachiever: the state carrier Antel was an early regional adopter, blankets Montevideo and the resort coast, and holds the interior in a way no rival matches. But the country also has one of the region’s starkest coverage exceptions — Cabo Polonio, a village with no electricity grid and no signal from any carrier — and a registration rule that shapes your arrival: every SIM, foreigner included, must be tied to a passport at an official outlet. This guide covers the Antel vs Movistar vs Claro decision, the Punta del Este January congestion effect, and the Carrasco airport kiosk that closes too early for late flights. The sections on coverage and registration below are honest about the Uruguay failures our support desk fields weekly.

Quick answer: The carrier decision in Uruguay matters less than buying before you fly: pick Antel for the widest map or Claro for budget data, and get the profile installed at home. First eSIM? Download and install are both covered in the activation guide.

The 30-second version: Antel is Uruguay’s coverage leader — the widest 4G map, genuine 5G in Montevideo and Punta del Este, and the only network that holds across Ruta 1 to Colonia and into the interior. Movistar is the strong urban-and-coast alternative that thins inland; Claro is the budget third. Physical SIMs require passport registration at official outlets (the Carrasco airport Antel kiosk is convenient but not 24/7), so the friction-free route is an eSIM bought online before you fly. And if Cabo Polonio is on your itinerary: download offline maps, because no eSIM choice changes that map.

Antel vs Claro: which is better in Uruguay?

Antel — Uruguay’s state-owned carrier — is not just the domestic leader; it is one of Latin America’s strongest mobile networks, an early 5G adopter with genuine coverage in Montevideo, Pocitos and Punta del Este’s main avenues, and the widest rural footprint by a distance. Traveler reports converge on the same story: on Ruta 1 toward Colonia, on the coastal roads east, and in the interior towns, Antel holds while the rivals thin. Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index backs the infrastructure story — fixed medians around 208 Mbps (top-40 globally) in recent editions — and Cable.co.uk’s survey prices local data at roughly USD 0.28 per GB, among the world’s cheapest 20 markets.

The regulator is URSEC, the communications services regulatory unit.

Registration is the one hoop: every SIM in Uruguay is tied to an identity document by law — the clerk scans or photographs your passport at an official outlet. Foreigners can register a limited number of lines per passport, and registration happens only at official shops, not at repair stalls or random resellers. For top-ups afterward, the ubiquitous Abitab and Redpagos kiosks reload credit in cash in under a minute.

Movistar eSIM: the Uruguay urban-coast alternative

Movistar (Telefónica) is the credible number two: very good in Montevideo and along the coastal strip, competitive tourist bundles, growing 5G. Its documented weakness is the interior — traveler accounts of losing signal inland while a companion on Antel keeps streaming are consistent enough to be a pattern. City and resort trip: fine. Country-wide trip: the backup network.

Claro: the budget option in Uruguay

Claro (América Móvil) is the smallest network and prices accordingly — decent urban coverage, cheap packs, noticeably thinner rural reach than either rival. The honest verdict from locals and long-stayers: the few dollars saved are not worth the dead zones unless you never leave the pavement.

Can you buy a Uruguay eSIM from Antel?

Yes for physical SIMs, with paperwork; less clear for carrier eSIMs. Uruguay’s operators sell prepaid lines to foreigners legally and cheaply, but every purchase requires in-person passport registration at an official outlet. That is a fixed cost in time rather than money — the process takes about ten minutes, and the clerk photographs your document.

Why Cabo Polonio has no signal at all

Cabo Polonio is not a coverage gap in the ordinary sense — it is a village with no electricity grid, accessible only by 4x4 or on foot across the dunes, where the nearest confirmed signal is roughly 7 km away at the Valizas turnoff. Three practical consequences:

  1. You cannot book or rebook anything from there. Confirm your posada before you travel.

  2. Download offline maps in Montevideo, not in Rocha — the coast road has gaps before you even reach the village.

  3. Tell someone your plan. This is the correct use case for an offline itinerary and, if you are travelling solo, a check-in arrangement.

What Uruguay coverage maps do not show

  • The coast and the capital are covered by everyone. The operator differences only register once you head inland or north.

  • January changes the physics. Punta del Este’s population multiplies, and cells built for a small town serve a seasonal city. Speeds fall; coverage does not disappear.

  • The Brazilian border is a roaming trap. Around Rivera and Chuy, phones frequently attach to Brazilian networks. If your plan does not include Brazil, switch to manual network selection.

Market context: DataReportal’s Digital 2025: Uruguay counts 7.18 million mobile connections — 212% of the population — against 3.15 million internet users. That saturation figure is the statistical signature of a small, wealthy, thoroughly connected market where the networks are built for residents first and visitors inherit a good deal.

The one-minute eSIM readiness test

Run it before you leave home — Carrasco arrivals has no repair kiosk.

  • Android: Settings -> Connections -> SIM Manager is the place.

Troubleshooting a Uruguay eSIM in four steps

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Carrier Lock (Settings -> General -> About) must read “No SIM restrictions” before anything else matters

  2. Make sure the QR is unclaimed; most work exactly once

  3. Have support resend the profile if it is stuck

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Toggle the line off, wait a beat, then on

  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose Antel manually

  3. Reboot, and escalate to a network reset only if the fault stays

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Re-key the APN from the table without shortcuts

  2. Assign mobile data to the eSIM

  3. Line-level roaming needs to be on, not just the handset switch

  4. The usage page will show if the allowance is finished

D. Worked all week, dead at the coast or the border

  1. Cabo Polonio has no signal at all — check the region table first

  2. At the Brazilian border, force manual network selection

  3. In January at Punta del Este, congestion rather than coverage is the cause

Is ID registration required for SIMs in Uruguay?

Which carrier is best in Uruguay?

Antel, on every measure that matters to a traveler: the widest rural map, the strongest 5G, the Ruta 1 corridor, and the interior. Movistar is the urban-coast alternative; Claro is the budget option with the thinnest map.

Will my eSIM work in Cabo Polonio?

No — and neither will anything else. The village has no cell signal from any carrier and no electricity grid; the nearest coverage is around 7 km away. Book accommodation in advance and download offline maps.

Antel vs Claro 5G: which is better in Uruguay?

Yes — Antel’s 5G covers central Montevideo, Pocitos and Punta del Este’s main avenues, with Movistar and Claro expanding selectively. Rural Uruguay is a 4G experience, and it is a good one.

Does my eSIM work on the Buenos Aires ferry?

Once you cross into Uruguayan waters, yes — the profile attaches to a local network mid-crossing if data roaming is enabled. Argentine networks serve the Buenos Aires side; mid-River Plate is the switch zone.

What’s the right GB count for Uruguay?

5–10 GB covers maps, messaging, social and video calls if you use accommodation Wi-Fi for uploads. Local data is among the world’s cheapest — around USD 0.28 per GB — so oversizing costs little.

Can I share my Uruguay eSIM connection?

Yes — hotspot and tethering work on most plans, both local and travel eSIM. Watch the fair-use terms if you plan to run a laptop all day on a resort connection in January.

Cheapest data in Uruguay, compared

Because the market is small, wealthy and state-led: Antel is owned by the government, competition is real, and 212% mobile-connection penetration means operators compete on volume rather than scarcity. It is one of the few countries where a traveler’s per-gigabyte cost genuinely rivals what locals pay.

Is Punta del Este usable in January?

Yes, with a caveat — it is the country’s annual stress test. The peninsula’s population multiplies for the summer season, and cells built for a much smaller town are shared among everyone. Expect lower speeds at peak hours rather than no service.

Do I need a local number for a long stay?

For stays past two weeks, and especially if you are opening a local account or dealing with Uruguayan businesses, yes — buy an Antel line with your passport and top up for cash at Abitab. For a holiday, a data-only eSIM covers everything.

Will my Uruguay eSIM work in Argentina or Brazil?

Not on a Uruguay-only plan. For a Río de la Plata or Southern Cone loop, price a regional plan instead of stacking profiles — our Argentina eSIM guide and Brazil eSIM page cover the two most common onward stops.

Is Antel really that much better than Movistar and Claro?

For a city holiday, no — all three are good in Montevideo and along the resort coast. For anything inland, on Ruta 1, or in the interior towns, yes: Antel holds where the others thin, and that is the trip type where the difference shows.

Can I use a Uruguayan SIM in Argentina?

It will roam, but Uruguay is not in the EU and there is no equivalent free-roaming arrangement across the Río de la Plata. Expect charges, and price a regional plan if your itinerary crosses the river more than once.

How Uruguay data plans break down

Local numbers in Uruguay: do you need one?

Only if you are staying long enough to deal with local services, open a local account, or be reachable by a Uruguayan business. For a holiday, WhatsApp over data covers everything.
