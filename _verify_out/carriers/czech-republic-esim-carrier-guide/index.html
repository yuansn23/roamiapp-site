Does it matter which Czech network I choose?

Vodafone vs T-Mobile vs O2 for visitors

Three network operators own the towers, and they are unusually evenly matched: T-Mobile CZ is the largest by subscribers (around 40 percent share), O2 CZ is the incumbent former state operator with the biggest total connection base, and Vodafone CZ — the old Oskar network — rounds out the trio. Regulated by the Czech Telecommunication Office (ČTÚ), all three run mature 4G and city-scale 5G, and their prepaid pricing differs by only a couple of hundred crowns at entry level.

Czech Republic eSIM coverage: Prague versus the rest

Prague coverage: what the Czech capital actually delivers

Every network is excellent in the capital. Ookla’s measurement work credits O2 with the fastest 5G medians specifically in Prague, the metro runs signal end to end, and hotel-and-café congestion is the only real enemy — the Old Town square kilometre at midday tests every network’s capacity, not its coverage. A multi-network Czech Republic eSIM simply attaches to whichever operator is strongest at your position, which is the ideal setup for a city break.

One Prague-specific caveat: the metro’s deepest central sections and the stone cellars under the Old Town are the two places where bars drop on every network, and neither is a coverage problem you can configure your way out of. Surface streets recover within metres.

The airport is similarly frictionless: Vodafone staffs shops at Václav Havel Airport’s terminals, and travellers report tourist SIMs around 599 CZK for 10 GB — convenient, though roughly twice the per-gigabyte cost of the city-street starters.

Brno, Ostrava and regional city coverage in Czechia

Brno, Ostrava, Plzeň and Olomouc all sit inside dense 4G and active 5G footprints on all three networks, and the motorway and rail corridors connecting them to Prague stay usable at speed. This is the part of the map where carrier choice genuinely stops mattering: independent research summarised for 2026 newcomers describes T-Mobile as having the best overall 4G/5G coverage nationwide, while conceding that O2 posts the fastest 5G in Prague and Vodafone the widest 5G spread — three different wins, one competitive market.

The practical consequence is that a single-network profile is not obviously penalised inside these cities, but it is penalised on the way between them — a locked profile can sit on a weaker carrier while a better one is broadcasting from the same hill.

How do I buy a SIM or eSIM from a Czech carrier?

Is a passport required for a Czech Republic SIM?

Czechia is the easy case. A physical prepaid SIM requires no registration at all — no passport, no address, buy at a newsstand and call to activate. The moment you want an eSIM from a local operator, identification enters the picture: operator eSIM sales involve SIM-registration compliance, which in practice means a passport at a branded store or an online flow that verifies you. Travel eSIMs sold internationally sidestep this entirely, which is why they dominate the short-stay market.

Each operator’s self-serve route works like this:

Does my phone need special bands for Czechia?

Czech eSIM bands, carrier locks and the two checks that matter

The three networks share a common European band foundation — LTE bands 1, 3, 7, 8 and 20, with band 20 (800 MHz) providing rural reach — and build 5G primarily on n78 at 3500 MHz. O2 additionally operates a 3700 MHz TDD block, and Vodafone deploys 5G on n3 (1800 MHz) as well, which extends mid-band 5G into more places. Practically every European and international flagship from the last several years handles all of it, and unlike some markets there is no exotic band a visitor is likely to miss.

The usual two checks still apply. Confirm the phone is not carrier-locked — Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock should read “No SIM restrictions” — and confirm eSIM support via the EID shown when you dial *#06# or directly through the eSIM compatibility tool. Mainland-China iPhones have eSIM disabled in hardware and will not install any profile. A locked phone is the single most common reason an eSIM fails to install, so settle it before purchase.

Does Vodafone sell a tourist eSIM at Prague airport?

What do Czech Republic’s registration rules demand?

A physical prepaid SIM requires no registration and no ID. An eSIM bought from a Czech operator does involve identification under SIM-registration rules, so bring your passport to a store or complete the online verification. Travel eSIMs bought internationally skip the requirement entirely.

Will a Czech Republic eSIM work in Slovakia or Germany?

Yes. As an EU member, Czechia’s roam-like-at-home framework lets a Czech prepaid allowance work across the EU and EEA at no extra cost, within fair-use limits. For a Prague-plus-Vienna or Prague-plus-Berlin trip, one Czech SIM or a multi-country travel eSIM covers the whole route.

Does your phone pass the eSIM check for Czech Republic?

If it is unlocked and eSIM-capable, yes. Czech networks use standard European LTE bands (1, 3, 7, 8, 20) and 5G on n78 at 3500 MHz, which virtually all international flagships support. Verify your model on the compatibility list before buying.

Do Czech Republic eSIMs include a local phone number?
