Getting a South Korea eSIM right means understanding all three layers, not just the speed table — and deciding early, because South Korea’s own data market is one of the priciest in the developed world.

South Korea carriers compared: SK Telecom, KT and LG U+

SK Telecom: the overall leader

SKT’s H1 2024 numbers top the general table: a Speed Score of 236.24, median download of 205.10 Mbps, upload of 19.53 Mbps, and the best consistency in the country at 89.6% of samples above 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up. Its 5G median (513.74 Mbps) trails LG U+’s peak, but the combination of speed and consistency is what a real day in Seoul feels like — trains, crowds, and a network that does not wobble. SKT also runs the largest retail footprint, which matters if you end up needing a physical counter.

How good is 5G in South Korea, and on which network?

LG U+’s 5G is the fastest consumer experience in Korea: a median 5G download of 546.83 Mbps, 5G upload of 44.28 Mbps, a 5G Speed Score of 456.32, and the best 5G gaming experience (Game Score 92.56). Its overall median (127.99 Mbps) sits below SKT’s because more of its traffic rides 4G — but where its 5G reaches, in Gangnam as much as Daejeon (the fastest city median in the country at 204.73 Mbps), nothing in Korea downloads faster.

KT: the balanced all-rounder

KT posts a median 5G download of 526.41 Mbps and an overall median of 109.33 Mbps — a hair behind the leaders on raw numbers, ahead of neither and behind nobody in practice. Its distinguishing trait for travellers is logistics: the strongest airport presence at Incheon and Gimpo, deep coverage through the transit corridors, and tourist products (including T-money-integrated SIMs) built around the arrival experience. For a first-time visitor, KT is the network you are most likely to meet first.

Does South Korea 5G mmWave matter?

Korea licensed 28 GHz mmWave at 5G launch and later revoked the operators’ licences after build-out obligations went unmet — so today’s 5G runs on the 3.5 GHz band (n78), and no mainstream handset is missing out on anything that matters. If a guide still promises mmWave in Seoul, it is out of date.

South Korea’s three networks are so good that the ranking is close to a rounding question — until you look at 5G, where one operator separates decisively. Ookla’s South Korea report for 1H 2024 (collection period January to June 2024) makes SK Telecom the fastest provider overall and hands LG U+ the 5G crown.

SIM registration and ID rules in South Korea

Korea’s identity rules divide the market in two, and visitors sit on the easy side. Residents register with an Alien Registration Card (ARC); tourists buy carrier SIMs and eSIMs with a passport alone — the official Seoul tourism guide walks through the options and price ranges. The carriers’ tourist products are genuinely good: SKT’s Traveler SIM, KT’s prepaid eSIM line (purchasable online at a discount, extendable in-store) and LG U+’s data SIMs all offer unlimited-style data over fixed windows, with KT selling some plans bundled with a local 010 number for app verification. Every one of them requires a carrier-unlocked handset, as does any travel profile.

The trade-offs are worth knowing before you choose:

  • Data-only eSIM (travel provider or carrier): no Korean number, no in-person step, installed before departure. The standard tourist setup.
  • Carrier tourist SIM/eSIM with a 010 number: costs more, may need a counter visit, and buys you app verification that some Korean services expect — notably Kakao Taxi and certain delivery apps.
  • Pocket Wi-Fi: unlimited data for groups, picked up and returned at airport counters. Still a reasonable option for families with several devices.

Data-only South Korea eSIM profiles cover the tourist mainstream: Naver Map and KakaoMap (both better than Google Maps in Korea, a quirk worth knowing before you land), Papago for translation, and the T-money app for transit top-ups all run fine on data alone. The full install walk-through is in our activation how-to guide.

How to install a South Korea eSIM before you land at Incheon

The profile arrives by email within minutes of checkout and installs in Settings; the Korea eSIM plans page has the current plans, and the general failure catalogue lives in the troubleshooting guide.

Korea adds one technical quirk worth knowing in advance: a handful of profiles — particularly MVNO-riding plans — need an APN entered manually. The standard values are lte.sktelecom.com for SKT, lte.ktfwing.com for KT and internet.lguplus.co.kr for LG U+, left blank on username and password; most travel eSIMs ship preconfigured and never ask. Beyond that, the drill is universal: install on Wi-Fi at home, set the eSIM as your data line, enable data roaming for it, and keep the home SIM active for SMS verification. Incheon’s arrivals halls are busy enough that you do not want to be troubleshooting there.

One cultural-connectivity note: Korea’s Wi-Fi is ubiquitous in cafés and the metro, but the apps that matter (Naver Map, KakaoMap, Papago, delivery platforms) assume you are always online — the eSIM covers exactly that gap between hotspots.

Pocket Wi-Fi vs eSIM in South Korea: which is better?

South Korea is one of the few destinations where the old advice — rent a pocket Wi-Fi egg at Incheon — still has defenders, so it is worth comparing honestly.

Where pocket Wi-Fi still wins: groups. One unlimited rental covers three to five devices, the cost splits, and there is nothing to install on anyone’s phone. Airport pickup and return are simple.

Where the eSIM wins now: everyone else. A profile costs less per day, never needs collection or return queues, survives on the phone you already carry (no second device to charge), and keeps your home number live for verification — the exact thing a pocket Wi-Fi cannot do. With Korea’s 5G riding n78, a modern handset on an eSIM gets the same network experience the egg does, minus the hardware.

The hybrid pattern: families increasingly do both — one pocket Wi-Fi for shared tablets and laptops, eSIMs on each adult phone for navigation and messaging. Since a data-only Korean profile needs no counter visit, adding it to the arrival plan costs nothing but a few minutes at home.

Korean apps that need mobile data

South Korea’s connectivity is excellent and its app ecosystem is idiosyncratic, and the mismatch is what actually frustrates visitors. Three downloads before you fly: Naver Map or KakaoMap for navigation, because Google Maps has limited functionality in Korea; Papago for translation, which handles Korean better than general-purpose tools; and KakaoTalk, which is how guesthouses, restaurants and tour operators take bookings. A T-money transit card and its companion app let you check your balance on the move once you have data.

None of this is a network problem, and all of it is the difference between a Korean trip that runs smoothly and one spent asking for directions.

Troubleshooting a Korean eSIM, step by step

Install fails. Carrier lock is the first suspect — a handset bought on contract abroad is the usual cause — then a QR code that has already been consumed.

Installs, no bars. Fifteen counted seconds in flight mode, then a manual pick — SK Telecom, KT or LG U+ in that order. Korean networks register within seconds, so a no-service state lasting a minute points at the handset rather than the network.

Connected but no throughput. Verify the eSIM is the data line and roaming is enabled for it. On a locally bought KT line, check whether the manual APN string differs from the default before assuming a fault.

Emergency calls only. The cause is usually a stale registration: kill every other active profile, restart, reset network settings, and reinstall only if that fails.

What travellers ask about South Korea carrier eSIMs

Do tourists need a Korean ID to buy a SIM?

No. Tourist SIMs and eSIMs — from the carriers themselves or from travel providers — work with passport verification only. The ARC requirement applies to residents signing standard local contracts, not to visitor products.

How different are SK Telecom, KT and LG U+?

All three are outstanding; the practical differences are location-dependent. SKT leads overall and in consistency, LG U+ owns the 5G peak and gaming, KT is the balanced pick with the best airport logistics. A multi-network profile removes the choice altogether, latching onto whichever network is strongest at that moment.

Would 10 GB see you through South Korea?

For most, yes — the apps install and run on data alone. The nuance: a few Korean services verify accounts with local SMS codes, and taxi-hailing in particular behaves best with a Korean 010 number. If your week depends on those flows, consider a carrier tourist plan bundled with a local number; otherwise the data-only setup covers the standard itinerary.

Do I need a mmWave phone for South Korea?

Not for practical purposes. The 28 GHz licences were revoked after build-out deadlines were missed, so Korean 5G runs on the 3.5 GHz band — which, at 546.83 Mbps on LG U+, delivers speeds no mmWave-dependent feature would improve for a traveller.

Can I use my South Korea eSIM in Japan or Taiwan, China?

Inside Korea and nowhere else. The Japan eSIM page and Taiwan, China eSIM guide cover the neighbouring legs, and a regional East Asia profile covers the whole route in one purchase.

How much data do you need in South Korea?

10 GB is comfortable for maps, translation, transit apps and daily streaming, with café and metro Wi-Fi absorbing the rest. Heavy video callers and hotspot users should carry 20 GB — but note Korea’s data is expensive locally, so matching your data plan to real usage matters more here than in most destinations.

Which South Korea carrier is fastest?

SK Telecom on everyday use — a 205.10 Mbps median download against LG U+’s 127.99 Mbps and KT’s 109.33 Mbps, with the best consistency at 89.6% and the fastest provider ranking in eight of the nine measured cities. On 5G the three are effectively level, with LG U+ marginally ahead at a 546.83 Mbps median.

Is SK Telecom, KT or LG U+ best for a tourist eSIM?

SK Telecom for coverage and consistency, particularly between Incheon and central Seoul. LG U+ if 5G throughput is the priority — it holds the fastest 5G median, the fastest 5G upload and the lowest 5G latency. KT is the balanced option with the strongest airport presence, but it is also the network that most often needs a manual APN entry.

Why is Seoul slower than Daejeon?

User density. Daejeon records a 204.73 Mbps median against Seoul’s 184.48 Mbps, because the capital’s cells carry far more traffic. Seoul still posts 90.2% consistency, so the gap is invisible in normal use — the same pattern appears in Hungary and the Netherlands.

Do I need a Korean phone number?

Only if something specifically requires it, such as booking apps that verify by SMS. Data-only tourist profiles work fine without one, and tourists do not need an ARC or Korean ID to buy a carrier eSIM — a passport is enough. Some KT plans bundle a 010 number at a higher price if you need one.

What are the APN settings for South Korea carriers?

SK Telecom uses lte.sktelecom.com, KT lte.ktfwing.com and LG U+ internet.lguplus.co.kr, all with blank credentials and MCC 450 (MNC 05, 08 and 06 respectively). SK Telecom configures automatically on most handsets, while KT is the one that most often needs the manual entry.

Do I need a 5G phone in South Korea?

Not to work, and not to be fast. A 5G handset unlocks the 513–546 Mbps 5G medians on 3.5 GHz, but a 4G-only phone still records around 180 Mbps in Seoul, which is more than any travel task needs. The band that matters is n78; the eSIM compatibility page confirms whether your model has it.

Where is mobile data cheapest in South Korea?

Published tourist products start around ₩20,000 for a five-day KT 5G travel SIM with 2 GB per day and 20 minutes of voice, ₩25,000 for a seven-day SKT prepaid line with 5 GB, and ₩28,000 for ten days of unlimited use with a 1 GB daily high-speed allowance. At ₩45,000 the Discover Seoul Pass bundle adds a tourist pass to 15 days of 1.5 GB daily data.

Is mobile data slow on Jeju Island?

Modestly. Jeju-do records a 103.75 Mbps median against Gangwon-do’s 173.35 Mbps, and it is the only province where Ookla found no statistically faster provider. At 103 Mbps it remains far ahead of most countries — this is a relative gap inside an excellent market.

Can you tether a South Korea eSIM?

Permitted on nearly all travel profiles here, billed against the same allowance. Korean bandwidth is rarely the limiting factor; for long video calls, hotel fibre is still the better connection because of the 90 ms mobile latency, and a KT or SKT plan with a local number helps with apps that verify by SMS.

Does a South Korea eSIM work in Japan?

Only on a multi-country plan. Korea and Japan are separate profiles in almost every travel eSIM catalogue, and a Korea-only plan stops working the moment you land in Tokyo. Our Japan eSIM guide covers the crossing.

How much data for a Seoul break or a KTX itinerary?

Four to six gigabytes covers a five-day Seoul break, with subway and café Wi-Fi filling the gaps and hotel Wi-Fi handling video. Add several gigabytes for a multi-city KTX itinerary, where navigation and booking apps run all day.

Is a Korean eSIM better than pocket Wi-Fi?

For one to two travellers, an eSIM is simpler: nothing to collect, return, charge or lose, and no deposit. Pocket Wi-Fi still makes sense for a family or a group sharing one connection across several devices, and it can be picked up and dropped at the airport — but it never gives you a number and it is one more thing to keep charged.

Is Incheon airport worse for mobile data than Seoul?

Slightly, and interestingly so: Incheon records a 142.39 Mbps median against Seoul’s 184.48 Mbps, making it the slowest city in the Korean report and the only one where LG U+ beats SK Telecom.
