Telkomsel, XL and Indosat plans compared

Telkomsel: performance and coverage

Telkomsel leads in all the headline metrics: Speed Score 42.93, median download 32.11 Mbps, median upload 13.86 Mbps and 47 ms latency, plus the highest 5G availability in the country at 3.5% of users on 5G most of the time. Its 5G median download is 74.09 Mbps and its 5G consistency 80.7%. The number that should decide your Indonesia eSIM purchase, though, is not any of those — it is Telkomsel’s geographic coverage score of 8.6 out of 10, against 5.5 for Indosat and 4.8 for XL, which is what separates a working phone in Labuan Bajo or Bukit Lawang from a paperweight.

XL: consistency and video streaming

XL takes the best 5G video streaming score (88.67) and the highest overall network consistency at 87.2%, with a median download of 27.66 Mbps. In southern Bali it is competitive with Telkomsel and often ahead on reliability and time spent on 5G, per Opensignal’s April–June 2026 measurements. Its tourist eSIM — Xtra Combo Flex M, around Rp 46,000 for 14 GB over 30 days with five minutes of calls — is one of the cheapest operator-issued tourist products in the market, and online registration asks for passport details, email and your phone’s IMEI.

Indosat (IM3): cheaper data plans

Indosat, sold as IM3 Ooredoo, posts a median download of 26.7 Mbps and sits third on speed while undercutting both rivals on price: local listings show 72 GB for about Rp 110,000 a month, tax included, and its Travel On tourist range starts near Rp 100,000 for 30 GB. It is a strong everyday choice in Jakarta, Bandung and Surabaya. It is not the carrier to take to Raja Ampat.

Smartfren and by.U: budget plans

Two names appear in every price comparison and suit a narrow case each. Smartfren’s Tourist Starter Pack lists Rp 100,000 for 14 days and 30 GB, but read the split: 15 GB is anytime data and 15 GB is midnight-only, so it is not the 30 GB it appears to be. by.U, Telkomsel’s all-digital brand, sells small bundles from around Rp 15,000 for 7 GB with a validity period that has been advertised at 14, 20 and 30 days in different campaigns — convenient for residents, unpredictable for a two-week visitor.

SIM and eSIM registration in Indonesia

Indonesia has required imported handsets to be known to its networks since 2020, and an unregistered phone can behave normally for a while and then stop connecting. Three things are worth separating, because travelers routinely confuse them:

  • Registering the SIM is not registering the phone. They are two separate procedures. A registered prepaid line on an unregistered handset will still be cut off.

  • Tourists get a temporary route. Customs guidance allows visitors staying up to 90 days to have the device registered through the mobile operator’s own channel — which is exactly what an official airport counter or a GraPARI shop does when it asks for your IMEI alongside your passport. It adds two to five minutes.

  • Longer stays need the formal route. Beyond 90 days the customs framework applies, with an allowance of about USD 500 on the declared handset value and import duty above it.

None of this applies if you keep your home SIM on international roaming and use a travel eSIM for data — which is why an eSIM switch on a foreign handset bypasses the issue instead of solving it.

A second registration layer landed in 2026. Permenkomdigi No. 7 of 2026, in force from 19 January 2026, overhauled subscriber registration: from 1 July 2026 every new subscriber passes a biometric facial recognition check at the point of registration, and each identity is capped at three prepaid numbers per operator. Foreigners register with a passport, a KITAS or a KITAP. Telkomsel applies its own ceiling of three SIM cards and three eSIMs per passport on its tourist products. Two practical consequences: bring the passport you entered on, and do not expect to stockpile cards.

Indonesia eSIM fundamentals: bands, biometrics and bundle windows

Which bands do Indonesian networks use?

Indonesian 5G runs mainly on n40 (2300 MHz) and n78 (3500 MHz), while 4G LTE sits on B1 (2100 MHz), B3 (1800 MHz), B5 (850 MHz), B8 (900 MHz) and B40 (2300 MHz). Most recent international handsets cover those, but n40 is the band many Western models lack, so a phone that shows 5G in Europe may show 4G here. Check the model number against the phone compatibility list rather than the marketing name.

Identity checks, biometrics and the three-number cap

Buying a local line means a registration, and since July 2026 that registration includes a biometric check. A passport is accepted for foreign visitors; a KITAS or KITAP is accepted for longer stays. The cap is three prepaid numbers per operator per identity, and Telkomsel applies the same ceiling to its tourist products. Cards sold by a random convenience store are often distributed inactive and only switch on after a verified identity check.

Fair-use thresholds and what happens after them

Most prepaid plans carry a fair-use policy, and after the threshold speeds drop to something between 256 kbps and 1 Mbps — enough for messaging, not for maps with live traffic. Note the shape of the allowance too: Smartfren’s “30 GB” is half anytime and half midnight-only, and by.U’s validity period changes between campaigns. Check both the threshold and the window before you buy.

When to buy an Indonesian prepaid pack

The clock on an Indonesian prepaid package starts when the line first attaches to a local network, not when you pay for it. Some plans let you choose a start date. If you buy a card at the airport and do not insert it until the hotel two hours later, the countdown begins at the hotel. Buy the pack you will use on the day you will arrive.

Which phones support an Indonesian eSIM?

eSIM works on iPhone XS and later, Pixel 3 and later, Galaxy S20 and later, and most recent Android flagships — but the handset must also be carrier-unlocked, or no third-party profile will install regardless of hardware support. Indonesia eSIM by trip shape: seven islands, seven different networks

Bali coverage: a southern beach stay

Seminyak, Canggu and Kuta sit in the strongest part of the island’s map, where Telkomsel has the best average download and XL matches it on consistency. Beach clubs and villas are thick-walled, so expect to lose a bar indoors. Buy a generous allowance: at USD 0.28 a gigabyte there is no reason to ration, and the weather app is the most important thing on your phone during the wet season.

Ubud and the northern hills: Bali coverage inland

Ubud itself is fine on every network; the trouble starts in the rice-field villas and the volcano-side guesthouses, where Telkomsel is the carrier most likely to hold signal. If your work depends on video calls, book accommodation that advertises fibre rather than relying on mobile data.

Jakarta and the commuter belt: 5G and congestion

Jakarta has the country’s best 5G and its worst congestion. Telkomsel’s 47 ms latency is the figure that matters for calls in Sudirman, while XL often shows a stronger indoor signal in malls because of denser tower placement. Do not schedule a video call for 7 am on a Monday.

Yogyakarta and the temple circuit coverage

Borobudur at sunrise and Prambanan in the afternoon are covered on all networks, and Telkomsel’s wide coverage keeps ticketing apps and ride-hailing responsive in between. The pinch point is people rather than infrastructure: at sunrise the crowd on the temple terraces is competing for the same cells.

Lombok and the Gili islands coverage

Mataram and the Lombok mainland are covered, and the transfer to the Gilis works until the boat is out of sight of land. On the islands themselves signal is patchy and often depends on which side of the island you booked. Treat the crossing as offline time and download what you need before boarding.

Komodo, Flores and Labuan Bajo coverage

Labuan Bajo and the main Flores towns have usable 4G, and the dive-boat routes mostly do not. If you are diving Komodo, plan uploads for the evening; the marine park itself is one of the largest genuinely disconnected stretches on a standard Indonesian itinerary.

Raja Ampat and Papua: Indonesia’s remotest coverage

The most remote trip on this page. Sorong and Waisai have signal, and beyond them the practical internet is whatever your homestay or liveaboard provides. Take a satellite messenger if you are heading far from the towns, and treat mobile data as a bonus rather than a safety net.

What to do when your eSIM misbehaves in Indonesia

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are catalogued in the eSIM error troubleshooting guide. The four patterns below are the Indonesian ones.

A. A local SIM shows bars, then stops after a day. The classic IMEI case. An unregistered foreign handset gets a temporary grace period on a local network and then loses access. Complete the operator-based IMEI registration with your passport and entry document, or move your data to a travel eSIM, which is not subject to the rule.

B. Signal is there but mobile data never connects. Almost always the APN. Confirm the correct value for the operator that issued the line, save it, restart, and give the phone a minute to reattach. This is the single most common silent failure on Indonesian prepaid.

C. Data works in Denpasar and dies on the crossing to Lombok or Flores. Coverage, not a fault. Open network selection and try each operator manually, then accept that the sea crossing is offline time and lean on offline maps.

D. The package expires far earlier than expected. Validity starts at first network attachment, not at purchase, and promotional bundles carry their own window. Check the remaining balance and expiry in the operator app — MyTelkomsel, myXL or myIM3 — rather than assuming the network has failed.

Crossing to Singapore and Malaysia: same phone, new data plan

Indonesia’s borders are short hops and complete network changes. Batam and Bintan are a ferry ride from Singapore, Johor Bahru is a causeway crossing from Singapore and a short flight from Sumatra’s cities, and Kuching and Kota Kinabalu in Malaysian Borneo sit close enough to Kalimantan for a combined itinerary. On every one of them the local Indonesian plan stops at the boundary.

Two habits cover it. Buy a plan that includes Singapore or Malaysia before you leave — a Southeast Asia regional profile switches networks on arrival without a second registration — and switch data roaming off on the Indonesian line as you approach the ferry or the border post, so a background sync cannot start a billed session at roaming rates. If you are crossing more than once, the regional plan is cheaper than two local top-ups and saves a second passport registration.

Common questions about an Indonesia eSIM (12 answered)

Where does each Indonesia network shine?

That reach shows up in Komodo, Gunung Leuser and Bromo as well as in Jakarta, Bali, Surabaya and Yogyakarta. XL is competitive in southern Bali and Bandung on consistency rather than reach.

Is Indonesia’s mobile network good for gaming?

Telkomsel recorded the best 5G gaming experience in the country (score 79.55) with an average latency of 47 ms across all technologies, which is adequate for Mobile Legends, PUBG Mobile and similar titles. Peak-hour congestion in Greater Jakarta is the variable to watch rather than the network itself.

Should you install an Indonesia eSIM before you fly?

Yes, and you should. The profile installs from a QR code on home Wi-Fi and stays dormant until it first attaches to an Indonesian network, so the validity clock does not start while you are still abroad. Confirm the device is unlocked and eSIM-capable first.

When does the validity period of my Indonesia eSIM start?

At first connection to a local network in Indonesia, not at purchase. Some plans allow a chosen start date. Buy for the day you arrive rather than the day you book, and check the terms so a package does not expire in your inbox.

Does a foreign phone need IMEI registration in Indonesia?

If the handset was bought abroad, yes — for access to local networks. Visitors staying up to 90 days can arrange it through the mobile operator when they buy the SIM, which is what the airport counters and GraPARI shops do with your passport and entry document. It takes a few minutes and costs nothing. Beyond 90 days the customs procedure applies, with an allowance on the declared handset value.

Does SIM registration also cover the device IMEI in Indonesia?

No, and this is the mistake that leaves people offline. The two registrations are independent: a properly registered prepaid line on an unregistered handset will still be cut off. If you plan to rely on a local SIM, do both in the same store visit.

How many SIMs can you register in Indonesia?

Three per operator under Permenkomdigi No. 7 of 2026, which also introduced a biometric facial recognition check for new subscribers from 1 July 2026. On top of that, Telkomsel caps tourist products at three SIM cards and three eSIMs per passport.

Is 5G widely available in Indonesia?

Not yet. Telkomsel leads with only 3.5% of users on 5G most of the time. 4G LTE remains the network you will actually use, especially outside Jakarta and southern Bali, and a good 4G connection is comfortable for maps, calls, streaming and hotspot work.

Why is the airport so much more expensive than a store in town?

Because airport counters are resellers. The same Telkomsel 25 GB national plan that lists at Rp 150,000 was recorded at Rp 400,000 at a Denpasar airport counter in 2026. Buy the minimum at the airport if you need to be online immediately, then complete the purchase at an official GraPARI, Gerai IM3 or XL Center in town.

Will my home number keep working alongside an Indonesian eSIM?

Yes — keep your home SIM in the phone for calls and verification codes and run the data on the eSIM. WhatsApp is the default communication tool for drivers, homestays and tour operators across Indonesia, so nothing breaks when data moves to the eSIM; just switch data roaming off on the home line so background traffic does not trigger international charges.

Do I need a local number to use Gojek or Grab?

It helps. Ride-hailing, food delivery and some payment apps are built around a +62 number, and drivers often call rather than message. A data-only travel eSIM will run the apps, but a locally registered card is the smoother setup if you plan to live on Gojek for a month.

Where do I start when an Indonesian eSIM will not connect?

Work the four patterns above in order — IMEI status, APN, network selection, then package validity — and if it still fails, the wider error catalogue in the troubleshooting guide covers the rest. Have your IMEI, order number and a screenshot ready before contacting support.

Common eSIM myths in Indonesia

❌ Myth: You must register your phone’s IMEI at the airport.

✅ Fact: You need the IMEI registered, but not necessarily at the airport: the operator-based route works at any official GraPARI or XL Center within your first 90 days, and it is free. Travelers using a data eSIM alongside a home SIM on roaming skip it altogether.

❌ Myth: A locked phone will work fine on an Indonesian eSIM.

✅ Fact: It will not. A carrier lock rejects third-party eSIM profiles whatever the hardware supports, so the lock has to be cleared with the original carrier before you buy anything.

❌ Myth: All Indonesian eSIMs use the same network at the same speed.

✅ Fact: Providers partner with different operators. Telkomsel leads on speed and reach, XL on consistency and video, and Indosat on price in Java’s cities. In a country this size the network you land on matters more than the brand on the checkout page.
