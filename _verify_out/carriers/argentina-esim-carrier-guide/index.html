SIM registration in Argentina: what the DNI rules mean for visitors

For most of the last decade, Argentina ran one of the stricter SIM-registration regimes in South America.

Every mobile line had to be registered against a DNI, which residents carry and visitors do not.

An eSIM bought before departure never enters that regime at all.

What ENACOM actually regulates in Argentina

ENACOM, the Ente Nacional de Comunicaciones, is the federal body that licenses telecom operators, allocates radio spectrum and writes the registration rules carriers must follow.

The nominatividad policy is its work. As of March 2025, operators have to capture DNI details when activating any new line, so anonymous prepaid use is not permitted.

That matters to a visitor for one blunt reason: the rule deciding what you must show at a carrier counter belongs to the regulator, not to the brand on the door.

How does SIM registration work in Argentina?

In practice, yes. Argentina’s official tourism portal and consistent traveler reports say all three operators will register a prepaid line for a foreign visitor against a passport, with staff handling the paperwork in store.

The line normally activates within minutes.

Two caveats matter. The regulatory framework itself is DNI-centered, and travelers occasionally report that passport-only activation involves extra questions or store-level discretion.

Treat a smooth experience as likely but not guaranteed.

The process is also anchored to physical stores. Per the tourism portal, even Claro’s own digital SIM has to be collected in person with a passport rather than bought online.

Movistar’s activation instructions for its embedded SIM require you to be physically in Argentina when it provisions.

Add the airport wrinkle. Ezeiza has historically offered a single carrier stand (Personal) in the arrivals hall, with the other operators’ stores a taxi ride into the city.

Carrier shops in El Calafate or Ushuaia keep shorter hours than you would like after a 5 a.m. flight.

For anyone arriving with an eSIM profile already live in the phone, none of this machinery applies.

How strong is Personal for a visitor?

Personal swept the report. Beyond raw speed, it posted the best 5G video streaming score (92.15), the best gaming score, and the highest consistency at 88.3%, the share of tests meeting a minimum usable-quality threshold.

Its latency was the lowest in the market at 37 ms, and it was the fastest provider in most of the country’s major cities, which is why it took the overall award.

The weakness is subtle. Personal’s lead is a city-speed lead: where its signal exists it is the quickest network by measurement, but the report does not hand it a rural reach advantage over Claro or Movistar.

Matching an Argentina eSIM to your trip

Claro sits a clear second on speed at 39.79 Mbps, with a strong 5G median of 279.62 Mbps of its own.

Its real argument is national reach. Claro carries the reputation, and the subscriber base to match, for holding signal deep into Patagonia and the smaller provinces, exactly where a road trip spends its miles.

Argentina’s competition authority put Claro’s share of mobile subscribers at roughly 41.8%, ahead of Personal on 33.8% and Movistar on 24.4%.

The trade-off: on a city speed test Claro trails Personal, and that reach comes at a slightly slower median.

Which network suits your Argentina itinerary?

Movistar does not win the measured speed races. Its footprint, however, covers parts of the interior where the other two thin out, and its fixed-fiber arm remains one of the fastest in the country.

For a traveler, the value is as a fallback: a network that may hold a bar or two where your primary attach has nothing.

For an eSIM, that fallback is often the difference between a loaded map and a blank screen.

The weakness is momentum. Movistar is the brand being absorbed, so expect its consumer identity to blur rather than sharpen.

One market-structure note worth knowing: Telecom Argentina moved to acquire Telefónica’s Argentine mobile business, the company behind the Movistar brand, and Argentina’s competition authority cleared the deal in June 2026 with conditions attached.

Roughly six million mobile customers, plus the spectrum needed to serve them, must transfer to an independent operator.

The brands keep running separately for now, but a three-brand market is consolidating, which makes a travel eSIM that can move between networks a more resilient choice, not less.

Will my data be fast outside Buenos Aires?

National medians hide the single most important fact about Argentine connectivity: the map is wildly uneven, and your eSIM inherits whichever patch of it you happen to be standing on.

Ookla’s regional table from the same report makes the point in hard numbers. Buenos Aires province-level capital measured 113.51 Mbps while Misiones province, the Iguazú gateway, measured 27.57 Mbps.

Plan around your itinerary, not the country average.

Argentina coverage: Buenos Aires, La Plata and the Pampas corridor

The capital is the fastest city in the country at 113.51 Mbps, with Personal the fastest provider there, and La Plata close behind at 104.34 Mbps.

Coverage along the central highway corridor is dependable on all three networks.

The one dead zone every visitor discovers the hard way: the Subte metro has no usable signal on any carrier, so download your map before descending.

Patagonia coverage: Bariloche, El Calafate and El Chaltén

This is where plans are won and lost. Río Negro (Bariloche) measured 32.04 Mbps, Santa Cruz (El Calafate and the road to El Chaltén) 30.88 Mbps with median latency above 85 ms, and Tierra del Fuego (Ushuaia) 41.95 Mbps with a striking 90.94 ms of latency.

Coverage clings to the towns and the main routes and thins to nothing on secondary roads.

The famous Ruta 40 stretches between fuel stops can be silent for hours.

Here Claro’s nationwide rural footprint and Movistar’s interior reach matter more than Personal’s city speed advantage. An eSIM that can hop networks is worth more than a fast one locked to a single operator.

Iguazú and the slower northeast: Argentina coverage off the corridor

Misiones is one of the slowest provinces in the country at 27.57 Mbps, and neighboring Formosa is slowest of all at 26.52 Mbps.

The falls themselves are covered well enough for uploads at the visitor circuits, but expect 3G-grade speeds in the surrounding towns rather than the Buenos Aires experience.

Plan your eSIM around the airport and the town, not the viewing walkways.

Mendoza coverage: the wine valleys and the Andean passes

The city measured a respectable 85.24 Mbps, third-fastest in the country, and Personal was the fastest provider in Mendoza province (43.98 Mbps).

Head into the high-altitude wine valleys or toward the Chilean border pass and coverage drops quickly.

The cross-Andean drive has long dead stretches on every network.

Choosing your Argentina eSIM data plan

Argentina’s money situation is its own travel topic, and it directly affects how you buy connectivity.

The peso has been volatile for years, prices are adjusted frequently, and the rate you get for foreign currency varies dramatically depending on how you exchange it.

Three consequences matter for a traveler:

  • Card acceptance is patchy at the point of sale for small purchases. Kiosks and smaller carrier outlets can be cash-first, and international cards sometimes fail or trigger declined transactions at Argentine terminals. Airport counters are commonly card-only, at tourist-inflated prices.

  • Local prepaid pricing is cheap by global standards. Cable.co.uk’s worldwide survey of 237 markets puts 1 GB of mobile data in Argentina at about USD 1.11 — 104th cheapest, well below the global average of USD 2.59.

  • Every peso price you read online ages fast. Carrier tariffs adjust repeatedly through the year, so any specific figure quoted in an article, including ours, is a snapshot rather than a quote.

The cheapest plans in Argentina

Carrier tariffs in Argentina track inflation and the exchange rate, so peso-denominated plans are re-priced far more often than prepaid offers in stable-currency markets.

That is why this page quotes measurement benchmarks instead of standing tariff sheets, and why an Argentina eSIM priced in your own currency at checkout is easier to budget.

Sometimes, and that “sometimes” is the problem. A large carrier store in Buenos Aires will usually process an international card, while a kiosk selling top-ups may not.

A declined transaction at a terminal can be hard to diagnose while a queue forms behind you.

If you do buy locally, worth it for long stays, bring physical USD cash to exchange and expect the store to handle registration with your passport.

This is the quiet argument for a prepaid travel eSIM. You pay one fixed price in your home currency, before departure, with no top-ups, no exchange-rate roulette mid-trip, and no queue at a carrier store on day two.

For the country-level numbers behind that comparison: Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index put Argentina’s median mobile download at 67.79 Mbps in August 2026, ranking 68th worldwide with 26 ms latency.

DataReportal counted 41.2 million internet users in Argentina at the start of 2025, a 90.1% penetration rate that makes it one of the most connected markets in Latin America on paper.

How much data do I need for Argentina?

Sizing an Argentina eSIM is the question almost nobody asks before checkout and everybody asks on day four.

The honest answer depends on two habits: whether you stream video, and whether your camera roll syncs over mobile data.

Argentina for short trips vs long stays

Most of what a visitor’s phone does in Argentina is light: maps, messaging, ride-hailing, translation and the occasional photo post.

A modest eSIM allowance carries that comfortably for a week.

The variables that change the answer are video, backup and tethering. Streaming an hour a day, letting full-resolution photos upload to the cloud, or feeding a laptop through your phone’s hotspot is what turns a comfortable allowance into a tight one.

Size the plan around your evening habits, not your daytime ones, because hotel Wi-Fi covers most daytime browsing.

Buying an Argentina eSIM

A 5 GB eSIM tier is generous for a week of navigation, messaging and light social use, and thin for a month of daily video or constant uploading.

Warning signs arrive before the allowance runs out. Your phone’s data counter climbs faster than your itinerary, and uploads that used to finish in seconds start to crawl.

Track the balance in your phone’s settings rather than waiting for a notification, because an exhausted allowance and a network failure look identical on screen.

How do I set up the APN on my Argentine eSIM?

Access point names rarely need attention, and they are worth checking early when a data line will not load.

Most eSIM profiles never need this, but the section explains what the setting does and where the values live.

What an APN (access point name) actually does

An APN is the name your phone hands to the network to say which data gateway it should use.

Bars with no internet is the classic symptom of an APN that is missing, wrong, or attached to the wrong line.

A wrong APN can make a perfectly installed eSIM look broken.

Argentina: local SIM against travel eSIM

An eSIM bought online arrives pre-configured, and Argentine operators push settings over the air to handsets that accept them.

Manual entry is the exception, not the rule.

You may need it when an older Android handset does not pick up carrier settings, when a profile was installed with a manual activation code rather than a QR scan, or when a locally bought chip did not auto-configure.

The screens involved are walked through in our fix-it guide for activation problems.

Do not copy an APN string from a forum. Look the value up on the operator’s own support pages, or in the activation instructions that arrived with your eSIM, and enter that.

APN configuration in Argentina

On iPhone, open Settings, tap Cellular, select the travel line, then open Cellular Data Network and fill in the APN field.

Save, then restart the phone.

If the Cellular Data Network option is missing entirely, your eSIM is almost certainly configured already, which means this is not your problem.

When to type in an Argentina APN by hand

On Android, the path runs Settings, then Connections or Network and Internet, then Mobile networks, then Access point names.

Add a new entry, save it, and select it as active.

Menu wording varies by manufacturer, so search your settings for “APN” if that path does not match your phone.

Installing your Argentina eSIM before the plane lands

The install takes about five minutes and should happen at home, not in the arrivals hall.

The short version:

  1. Confirm your handset is unlocked and supports embedded SIMs — run it through the phone compatibility list if you are unsure.

  2. Buy the plan; the QR code or app-based install arrives immediately.

  3. Install the profile on Wi-Fi, label the line, and leave it switched off (or on, per the instructions) until you land.

  4. On arrival: select the line for mobile data and enable data roaming. The profile attaches to a local network automatically.

Switch the travel line on and give it a minute to register. The first attach after a flight can take longer than the fifth.

Then confirm three things: the travel line is selected for mobile data, data roaming is enabled on that line, and your home SIM’s roaming is switched off.

Fixing an Argentine eSIM that will not connect

Then try manual network selection: pick Personal, Claro or Movistar from the carrier list rather than relying on automatic search, which can be slow to settle in low-density areas.

If that fails, reset network settings and reboot before changing anything else.

Bars with no internet usually means the wrong line is carrying data. On dual-SIM phones, home-number campaigns and data traffic quietly switch lines more often than people realize.

Questions travelers ask about Argentina eSIM plans

Do you need to show a passport to buy a SIM in Argentina?

Yes, in practice — all three operators register prepaid lines for foreign visitors against a passport, and staff complete the registration in store. But the regulatory framework is built around the DNI, so a few travelers report extra questions or discretion at store level, and the purchase itself is in-person. For short trips, a pre-departure data plan remains the lower-friction route.

Which network is best for a Patagonia road trip?

None is uniformly best, which is the honest answer. Claro has the strongest rural reputation nationally, Movistar reaches parts of the interior the others skip, and Personal leads on measured speed wherever its signal exists. Across Santa Cruz, Río Negro and Tierra del Fuego, Ookla measured medians of roughly 30–42 Mbps with high latency, so expect usable data in towns and long silences between them.

Who runs the best 5G in Argentina?

In Buenos Aires and a handful of other big cities, absolutely — Personal’s median 5G download of 518.8 Mbps is world-class. But 5G coverage is urban, and your travel eSIM will spend most of its time on 4G outside the metros. The 55.48 Mbps all-technology national figure is the more realistic planning number.

Using your Argentina eSIM in other countries

Your Argentina eSIM works abroad. Data roaming toggles on the travel line itself, and with a prepaid plan there are no roaming charges from your home carrier to worry about. Roaming being off is the single most common reason a correctly installed eSIM shows no connection.

Understanding Argentina data plans

You can run both lines on any dual-SIM phone. Keep your physical SIM active for calls and SMS verification codes, point mobile data at the travel line, and disable data roaming on the home SIM so it does not bill you for background traffic. Data-only eSIM profiles usually cannot receive SMS, so your bank codes will still arrive on the home number.

What’s the going rate for data in Argentina?

Cable.co.uk’s 237-market survey averages 1 GB of Argentine mobile data at about USD 1.11, far below the USD 2.59 global average, so a local chip is cheap once you hold it in your hand. An Argentina eSIM costs more per gigabyte but bundles in the delivery: no store trip, no registration line, and no top-ups bought in a currency you are still learning to count.

Which Argentina carrier has the best rural coverage?

In the towns, yes — Ushuaia measured a 41.95 Mbps median in Ookla’s report, and El Calafate sits on covered networks as well. The eSIM does not fail in Patagonia; the coverage does, on the secondary roads between destinations. Set the expectation correctly: connected in town, dark on the long Ruta 40 stretches, and offline maps loaded before you need them.

Can one eSIM cover Argentina plus a side trip to Chile or Uruguay?

Not automatically. A single-country Argentina profile stops registering at the border, so an Iguazú add-on, a lake-district crossing into Chile, or a Colonia day-trip from Buenos Aires needs either a second eSIM or a single regional plan. Check your itinerary honestly: two separate country profiles cost little more than one and behave far better than a plan that was never meant to cross a border.

Argentina eSIM installation steps

The purchase and install take minutes, and the profile sits dormant until it meets an Argentine network. What takes time is registration, not installation: after you land, the line can need a minute or two to attach the first time.

When your Argentina data runs dry

Nothing breaks. The data line simply stops passing traffic, which looks like a network fault if you are not watching the balance. Check your allowance in the phone’s settings first, then decide whether to buy an add-on or lean on Wi-Fi until the next town.

Will your handset take an eSIM in Argentina?

Unlocked, eSIM-capable hardware is the baseline. Check both in one pass by matching your handset against the eSIM device checker before you buy, and remember that a carrier-locked phone will refuse any third-party eSIM until the original carrier unlocks it.
