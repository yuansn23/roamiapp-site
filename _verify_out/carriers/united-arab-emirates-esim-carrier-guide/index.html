A UAE eSIM is the rare travel purchase where the underlying network is not the question — the menu is. The UAE has been the fastest mobile market on earth for months: Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index put the country 1st in the world for median mobile download speed in August 2026, at 675.51 Mbps with 18 ms latency, and 2nd for fixed broadband at 382.40 Mbps. Against a worldwide mobile median of 109.05 Mbps, that is not a competitive market, it is a different category. The catch sits on the price line: Cable.co.uk prices 1 GB of UAE mobile data at roughly USD 4.61, 211th of 237 markets. Most visitors search for a Dubai eSIM rather than a country-wide product, but the airport counters sell the same two networks under either name.

So the real question here is not which network is fastest — both of them are. It is which product, from which regulated seller, at which point in your journey. The UAE sells connectivity the way it sells everything else: as a polished product menu with clear tiers, a regulator (the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority, TDRA) behind every number, and two national brands — du and e& (formerly Etisalat) — competing on bundles rather than on infrastructure quality.

One prerequisite worth ten seconds of your time: confirm your handset is eSIM-capable via the eSIM device checker, and skim how an eSIM profile installs if the process is new to you.

If you read nothing else: The UAE has two licensed carriers, e& (Etisalat) and du, both TDRA-regulated, plus Virgin Mobile as an MVNO riding du’s network. Both carriers sell official tourist products: e&’s Visitor Line starts at AED 48.99 for 4 GB plus 30 flexi minutes (28 days, before VAT), and du’s tourist bundles start at AED 49, with both offering a free 10 GB / 24-hour tourist eSIM at airport arrivals. Buy at the airport counter or arrival-hall QR code if you want a local number immediately; buy online before you fly if you want data live the moment the plane doors open.

How the UAE eSIM market works: TDRA, du and e&

Understanding the retail structure takes one paragraph, because the UAE is a two-utility market. The TDRA licenses exactly two mobile network operators: e& UAE, the consumer arm of the group rebranded from Etisalat in 2022 (the old etisalat.ae domain now tells visitors that “Etisalat is now e&”), and du (Emirates Integrated Telecommunications Company). In August 2026 the TDRA board renewed both operators’ licences for a further twenty years, and in the same month granted Starlink a licence to offer satellite internet.

Everything else on the shelf is built on those two networks:

UAE eSIM brands explained

Two structural facts follow. First, any UAE eSIM you buy — from a carrier, an airport counter, or a travel platform — ultimately runs on either e& or du; there is no third network. Second, registration is non-negotiable: TDRA rules require every SIM to be registered against a passport or Emirates ID, tourist products are restricted to visitors aged 18 and over, and each passport is limited to a small number of active lines.

e& vs du tourist plans: what actually differs

Three things are worth stating plainly. e& publishes its prices before VAT while du tends to quote them after, so a like-for-like comparison adds about a twentieth to every e& figure. e&’s calling allowances climb much higher as you pay more, which matters if you plan to make ordinary phone calls rather than app calls. And at the entry tier the two are within a dirham of each other once tax is normalized — so the decision usually comes down to which network history suits your itinerary, not which menu is cheaper.

What the free arrival eSIM really gives you

Both carriers hand over 10 GB valid for 24 hours, and it is genuinely useful: enough to book a ride, message your hotel and load a map. What it is not is a plan. When the day ends, the line does not renew itself — a paid pack has to be bought through the app, a counter or a store. Travelers who assume the free data simply continues are the single most common source of confusion in UAE connectivity, so treat the arrival eSIM as a bridge to your real plan rather than the plan itself.

Flexi minutes — included in both carriers’ tourist packs — work for local and international calls, which matters more than it sounds: ride-hailing drivers, hotels and restaurant bookings all consume voice minutes. Whichever channel you choose, a carrier line is live within minutes of checkout.

What documents does a United Arab Emirates SIM purchase require?

The dividing line is the plan, not the network. Tourist packs are built for passports; resident plans, migrations and most account changes are built around the Emirates ID. TDRA’s own numbers draw the boundary too: visitors may hold up to two SIMs per provider and residents up to five, and the count is per operator rather than per person.

Two practical consequences follow. If your passport already holds two active UAE lines from a previous trip, a third will be refused until you deactivate one at a store. And if you decide to stay on, you migrate the visitor line to a resident plan by presenting your new Emirates ID at any operator outlet.

Which phones support a United Arab Emirates eSIM

Spectrum is where the two-utility structure shows up in hardware terms. The TDRA split the country’s core 5G band deliberately: of the n78 (3.5 GHz) range, e& holds 300 MHz (3300–3600 MHz) and du holds 200 MHz (3600–3800 MHz), and both hold mid-band n41 spectrum, with millimetre-wave allocations (24.25–27.5 GHz) layered on top since 2020. In practice, phones sold in Europe, Asia and the Middle East cover everything that matters; a handset missing n78 simply runs on LTE. Any eSIM from either carrier installs the same way on the same device list.

UAE eSIM: three checks before you fly

  • Unlocked. A carrier-locked handset will install the profile and then refuse to register.

  • eSIM-capable. Dial *#06# and look for an EID before you spend anything.

  • Data line chosen. Set the UAE eSIM as the mobile-data line, or your home SIM will keep the traffic.

The lock check matters more in the Gulf than most regions because many UAE- and region-sold handsets arrive operator-financed and locked. Run all three before you leave, and a profile installed at home will register the moment the aircraft doors open.

Internet calling rules for UAE eSIM users

One regulatory fact shapes more visitor complaints in the UAE than any other, so it belongs stated plainly: voice and video calls made through general internet-calling apps are restricted in the UAE. WhatsApp, FaceTime and Skype calls do not connect on local networks, by TDRA-supervised policy; text messaging and data for browsing, social media, maps and streaming all work normally.

What replaces WhatsApp calls in the UAE?

What the market did in response is worth knowing, because it turns a limitation into a product feature — and no UAE eSIM changes it, because the restriction is network-level, not device-level. The carriers sell data for licensed internet-calling apps: e&’s Visitor Line premium and unlimited packs include promotional unlimited data for GoChat Messenger, and du’s tourist plans support licensed calling apps such as BOTIM. Practically, your family can install the same licensed app, or you rely on ordinary phone calls — the flexi minutes in every tourist pack exist partly for this reason. Plan around it before you fly rather than discovering it at the arrivals gate.

Practical notes: ride-hailing, parking and hotel Wi-Fi with a UAE eSIM

A United Arab Emirates eSIM is not just about browsing. Several everyday services in the emirates assume you can receive a call or a text, and a data-only travel profile will not.

UAE apps that need a live local number

Ride-hailing and delivery work best when the driver or courier can reach you. Careem, which runs the Hala Taxi fleet, plus Uber, Bolt and the Dubai Taxi Corporation app, all let you register with a foreign card — but a driver who needs to find you will phone the number on the booking. That is the strongest argument for a carrier tourist pack with a real UAE number over a data-only profile.

Two habits that keep a UAE eSIM working

  • Keep the data line pinned to the eSIM. Most “no internet” reports in the emirates are a phone quietly using the home SIM instead of the UAE eSIM.

  • Leave data roaming on. Local lines and managed travel profiles often need the roaming toggle enabled even inside the country.

A simple rule covers all of it: keep the eSIM as your data line and your home number alive for authentication codes, and check the parking method before you drive, because the SMS route is the one that quietly expects a local SIM.

Do you need APN settings on a UAE eSIM?

Both carriers’ tourist products — and any travel eSIM running on their networks — configure their data settings automatically when the profile installs, and neither operator publishes manual APN strings for its eSIM service that we can verify. If a locally purchased line fails to carry data, do not invent settings: use the operator’s own support channels (the e& UAE app or the Visitor Line support pages; the du app or du support), where the current values live.

What to check instead of the APN on a UAE profile

Because neither TDRA-licensed operator publishes a manual APN for its eSIM service, the honest manual step is not the APN at all — it is making sure the tourist line, and not your home SIM, is the one carrying data. On iPhone that is Settings → Cellular → your eSIM line; on Android it is Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks. Confirm the eSIM owns the traffic, and leave the APN fields untouched on a managed profile.

How to activate your UAE eSIM step by step

The install itself is the universal sequence — Settings → Add eSIM → scan the QR code → label the line → set it as the data line → enable data roaming if the plan requires it — and our activation guide walks it in detail, with the activation troubleshooting guide covering the general error catalogue.

How to set up a UAE eSIM in six steps

  1. Install the profile on home Wi-Fi before you fly

  2. Label the line so you can tell it apart from your home SIM

  3. Point mobile data at the new line, not your home SIM

  4. Enable data roaming for that line

  5. Land, then wait up to two minutes for registration

  6. Run one speed test — if it fails, check the APN and the data-line setting before anything else

Four failure patterns specific to a UAE eSIM

A. The free 24-hour eSIM expired and “nothing works”. It worked exactly as designed: the free tourist eSIM carries 10 GB for one day. A paid pack does not attach itself — buy one through the du or e& app, at a counter, or online, and the same line continues.

B. Installed, but the line never activates. The most common UAE-specific cause is incomplete identity verification: TDRA registration requires a passport (and entry stamp) match, sometimes with a selfie, before the line goes live. Re-open the activation flow or the carrier app and finish the KYC step; at a store, staff complete it for you.

C. “Two SIMs per passport” refusal. If your passport already holds two active UAE lines — common with repeat visitors — a third will be refused. Deactivate an old line at a carrier store before buying new.

D. Five bars in Dubai, no service at the border. A UAE tourist pack is UAE-only; at the Saudi or Omani border it drops to roaming, if it works at all. Either accept pay-per-use roaming from the tourist line or carry a separate regional eSIM for the crossing.

Your UAE eSIM questions, answered

Do UAE tourist eSIMs include calls?

Yes — both carriers’ paid tourist packs bundle flexi minutes usable on local and international calls: 30 minutes on the entry tiers, up to 525 on e&’s Visitor Line Premium+. Data-only travel eSIMs from third-party platforms usually do not include a UAE number, so if you need to receive local calls or OTPs, buy the carrier product.

Do WhatsApp calls work on a UAE eSIM?

WhatsApp voice and video calls are restricted on UAE networks, as are FaceTime and Skype calls — a regulatory restriction, not a network defect. Messaging, browsing and social media work normally. The licensed internet-calling apps the carriers support, such as GoChat Messenger and BOTIM, are the intended replacement for calls.

Is there really a free tourist eSIM at Dubai airport?

Yes. Both du and e& hand visitors a free 10 GB eSIM valid for 24 hours, self-activated by scanning a QR code in the arrivals hall after immigration. It is designed to get you to your hotel; a paid pack takes over from there, and the free eSIM must be activated within 30 days of issue.

Do you need to show a passport to buy a SIM in the UAE?

Yes — TDRA rules require every SIM, physical or embedded, to be registered against a passport or Emirates ID. Tourist products are limited to visitors aged 18 and over, and each passport can hold a maximum of two active SIMs per provider. Online purchases collect the same details during checkout.

du or e& for a Dubai holiday: which is better?

For a Dubai-centric trip the practical difference is small — both run excellent 5G in the city, and either eSIM serves the typical itinerary. du’s bundles include VAT and skew slightly cheaper at the entry tier; e&’s published menu is broader at the top end and includes GoChat calling data on premium packs. Abu Dhabi and long-route itineraries lean e&, on network history.

Cheapest United Arab Emirates eSIM plans

Through the official tourist products: AED 98.99 before VAT for 10 GB plus 60 flexi minutes on e&’s Visitor Line Advance, with du’s nearest tier around AED 99–139 depending on the current offer. Data-only travel eSIMs are often cheaper still; the USD 4.61 per-gigabyte national average reflects resident pricing rather than tourist packs.

What happens when your UAE tourist eSIM expires after 28 days?

Both carriers’ tourist packs are valid for 28 days and can be repurchased at the same price; the line itself stays valid as long as your visa, up to 90 days. If you switch to UAE residency, you migrate the line to a resident plan at a store with your Emirates ID.

Will a UAE eSIM work in Abu Dhabi and the other emirates?

In Abu Dhabi city, yes, comprehensively. On the long western routes — Liwa and beyond — e& covers the main ways and du the major highways, but between settlements no network promises service. Download offline maps before leaving the last petrol station.

Can you install a UAE eSIM before you land?

Yes — that is the main argument for buying online. Install the profile on home Wi-Fi, and it registers on landing. The only caveat: carrier tourist products that require in-person passport checks complete registration differently online, so read the checkout flow; data-only travel eSIMs install without any registration at all.

Which UAE eSIM is best for a stopover under 12 hours?

Take the free arrival eSIM from whichever carrier has the shorter queue and skip the paid menu entirely — 10 GB for a day covers a transit run to a hotel and back. If you will leave the airport, add a small paid pack only if you expect to use data past the first 24 hours.

Do you need a UAE phone number on your eSIM?

A carrier tourist pack does, and it is the reason to choose one over a data-only profile: food couriers and ride-hailing drivers call the number on the order. A data-only travel eSIM usually cannot receive calls or SMS, so you would be relying on in-app chat alone.

Can I run a UAE eSIM and my home SIM at the same time?

Yes, and it is the usual setup: your home line stays active for bank codes while the UAE eSIM carries data. If you plan to tether a laptop, check that your pack allows hotspot use before you rely on it. Two cautions. Switch off data roaming on the home SIM so background traffic is not billed at home rates, and remember that a data-only eSIM cannot receive SMS, so one-time codes still land on your own number.
