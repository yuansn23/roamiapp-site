Romania is Europe’s data-price anomaly. Locals pay about USD 0.54 per gigabyte — 50th of 237 markets, roughly a fifth of the price of data in Germany — and fixed broadband runs at 276.18 Mbps nationally, 15th in the world. Mobile speeds are more ordinary: Ookla’s August 2026 index puts the country 56th at 82.57 Mbps. For a Romania eSIM, the interesting tension is exactly there: world-class cheap data, world-class fibre, and a mobile landscape where Orange leads on speed, availability and consistency while DIGI — the discount giant that made Romanian data famously cheap — owns 5G consistency and gaming. Set aside a few minutes, no more — that is all any Romania step below has ever taken our test users.

Quick answer: Pick Orange for the widest footprint, DIGI for the lowest cost per gigabyte, and install your eSIM before departure either way. Speeds, prices and registration rules later in this Romania guide explain the fine print behind that split.

This guide works through that landscape for travellers: what each carrier is actually good at, how the EU roaming rules apply, where the coverage map betrays you (the Transfăgărășan and the Danube Delta, mostly), and what to have ready before you install. Confirm your handset against our eSIM compatibility table when you are ready.

The verdict up front: Orange is the default pick — fastest mobile network (median 86.91 Mbps, 156.76 Mbps on 5G), best 5G availability (50.9%), best consistency (92.6%), and the best rural reach. DIGI is the value challenger: cheapest data culture in Europe, 5G consistency of 88.1%, and the country’s best 5G gaming experience. Install a Romania eSIM before departure, and expect city performance to be excellent, mountain passes to be patchy, and the delta to be offline — for everyone.

Romania eSIM carrier speeds: how fast is each network?

How fast is Orange in Romania?

Orange Romania’s H2 2025 report card is close to a clean sweep: median download 86.91 Mbps overall and 156.76 Mbps on 5G, upload of 18.57 Mbps (27.62 on 5G), the country’s highest 5G availability at 50.9%, and the best consistency at 92.6% of samples clearing 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up. Add the best 5G video streaming score (85.99) and regulator ANCOM’s picture of Orange holding the largest spectrum portfolio, and the summary is simple: the fastest network is also the one most likely to still have signal when you leave the city limits. Iași’s 155.1 Mbps city median — the fastest in Romania — sits on Orange’s eastern network.

How good is DIGI’s 5G?

DIGI (RCS&RDS) built its reputation by collapsing Romanian mobile prices, and its 5G network is no longer a punchline: a median 5G download of 126.22 Mbps, the country’s best 5G consistency at 88.1% of samples above 25 Mbps down / 3 Mbps up, and the best 5G gaming experience in the H2 2025 report. Its overall median (64.75 Mbps) trails Orange’s, and its footprint concentrates on cities and suburbs — Timișoara is its showcase, where 333.25 Mbps fixed broadband sets the pace. For travellers, DIGI is the value lane: strong where it is strong, and clearly second on rural reach.

What about Vodafone and Telekom Romania?

Both remain licensed national operators, and both appear in multi-network eSIM plans as fallback lanes. Neither won a category in the H2 2025 Ookla report, so this guide — like most traveller itineraries — treats the market as an Orange-versus-DIGI race with two understudies.

EU roaming rules for a Romania eSIM

EU membership cuts both ways in Romania. On the plus side, a SIM or eSIM from another member state works here at domestic rates under “roam like at home” — the European Commission’s roaming page sets out the rights and the fair-use data limits. Second, it matters as a warning: Romania’s famously cheap local data does not travel — an EU plan’s roaming allowance is capped by fair-use formulas, so heavy users arriving on EU profiles still hit ceilings.

For visitors with no EU plan, a travel profile is the predictable answer: installed before departure, attached to Orange or DIGI on landing, with the home SIM kept in place for verification. Data-only profiles include no Romanian number — fine for tourists, since ride-hailing, reservations and messaging all work over data with your existing number. Identity registration applies to locally bought SIMs (passport at the carrier store), which is the in-person step the eSIM route skips.

How to set up and activate a Romania eSIM

There is no counter and no clerk: pay online, receive the profile by email, add it in Settings, and permit roaming on the eSIM line. The whole thing is a five-minute job on home Wi-Fi, and doing it early avoids hunting for a SIM at Henri Coandă after a long flight. You can follow the activation how-to for either platform, and the failure list covers what to do when it does not work.

Two Romania-specific notes for road trippers. First, the Transfăgărășan and Transalpina highways cross genuine coverage voids — spectacular ones — so download offline maps and accommodation details in Sibiu or Brașov before the climb, not at the summit. Second, if your phone parks on a weak carrier in the mountains, select Orange manually from the network list; automatic selection does not always pick the strongest available network in terrain. Four eSIM failure patterns in Romania

A. Installed, but no bars in Bucharest. Choose the network by hand — Orange, Vodafone or DIGI — under Settings → Cellular → Network Selection. Romanian networks sometimes decline a foreign profile’s first automatic attach, and a manual pick sidesteps the wait.

B. Signal, no data. Nine times in ten this is the APN row above, or the data line still pointing at the home SIM. Check both before resetting anything.

C. Data works in the city, dies in the Carpathians. Expected behaviour in the thinner counties (Harghita, Covasna, Giurgiu). Switch to the strongest available network manually and drop to 3G/2G only if the handset insists on holding a weak 5G cell.

D. Cross-border failure in Bulgaria or Hungary. A Romania-only profile stops at the border. If your route continues to the Black Sea coast or through the Balkans, check the Bulgaria eSIM page or a regional Europe profile before you leave.

Romania eSIM on trains, buses and the road to the Danube Delta

Romanian travel splits into two connectivity regimes, and planning for both is what keeps a Romania eSIM useful.

The rail and bus network. The Bucharest–Brașov line, the westward routes to Timișoara and Cluj, and the intercity bus network all hold usable coverage on Orange for most of their length — long enough for streaming on the plain, with expected dropouts where the line climbs into the Carpathians. Tickets for the rail operator and the long-distance bus platforms are bought in apps and validated by QR, which makes live data part of the boarding process.

The Delta and the back roads. Everything east of Tulcea behaves differently: signal follows the main channels and the towns, guesthouses have Wi-Fi that stops at the property line, and boat transfers into the reed beds are offline by nature. The correct pattern is the same one the salt flats teach in Bolivia — book and download in the last city, treat the boat as airplane mode, and let Orange’s consistency handle the drive to Tulcea.

Cash, cards and connectivity. Romanian cities are card-friendly, but rural fuel stations, market stalls and guesthouses in the Delta still lean on cash — download maps and carry lei, and the eSIM’s job stays simple: navigation, messaging and the occasional video call from a mountain valley.

  1. Carrier lock. On iPhone, Settings → General → About carries a Carrier Lock entry that must read “No SIM restrictions”. EU-sold phones are locked only if bought on a subsidised contract.

Where mobile coverage gets weak in Romania

The Danube Delta. Tulcea, Sulina, Sfântu Gheorghe and Murighiol all have usable coverage, and Tulcea County posts a 55.75 Mbps median — respectable for a wetland region. The gap is on the water: channels between villages lose signal for long stretches, on every network. Download an offline map of the delta at Tulcea and treat the boat crossings as offline time.

High Carpathian passes. Transfăgărășan and Transalpina hold signal through most of their length, with the failures concentrated in narrow, forested valleys. Above roughly 1,800 m, treat the phone as unreliable for emergency calls and carry a paper map on serious hikes. Snow-closed sections in winter remove the question entirely.

The catch is procedural rather than financial: a local line means registration and a shop visit, which is where the travel eSIM premium goes.

Orange, on the strength of its consistency in the delta’s towns and along the main channels. Signal exists in Tulcea, Sulina and the larger villages and thins out on the smaller waterways, where no carrier reaches. Does a Romania eSIM work in Bulgaria, Hungary or Ukraine?

Only inside Romania’s borders. Border-hoppers should either carry a profile per leg (our Ukraine eSIM guide covers the eastern neighbour) or use a regional plan. EU roaming rights only apply to profiles issued inside the EU bloc, not to travel eSIMs generally.

Which is better in Romania: Orange or DIGI?

Orange wins on speed, availability, consistency and rural reach; DIGI wins on price culture, 5G consistency in its cities and gaming performance. For a visitor on the move, Orange is the safer default — the moment your route leaves city centres, its network is the one still standing.

Where 5G works in Romania

Orange leads at 50.9% 5G availability, concentrated in the major cities; DIGI’s 5G is strong in its urban strongholds. Rural Romania — including the Carpathian resorts — runs on 4G, which at Orange’s 92.6% consistency is perfectly dependable.
