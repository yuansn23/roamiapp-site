Iraq sells some of the cheapest mobile data on earth and makes you work harder than most countries to use it. DataReportal counted 48.1 million mobile connections at the start of 2025, 103 percent of the population, on top of 38.0 million internet users. The catch is structural: Iraq is really two mobile markets under two regulators, and the best Iraq eSIM for your trip depends on which side of the internal border you spend your days — federal Iraq or the Kurdistan Region. We have set up enough of these plans to know which steps people get wrong, and the activation guide below calls those out explicitly. Your eSIM basics: two licensing regimes, one country

Every misunderstanding about an Iraq eSIM starts here. The federal regulator — the Communications and Media Commission (CMC), established in 2004 and headquartered in Baghdad — licenses the three national mobile operators: Zain Iraq, Asiacell and Korek Telecom. The Kurdistan Regional Government runs its own oversight through the Ministry of Transport and Communications in Erbil, and its own ecosystem has grown alongside the federal trio: Fastlink and Newroz Telecom operate inside the Kurdistan Region, while Korek, though federally licensed, is Erbil-based and functions as the region’s home operator.

Iraq counts 26 international crossings — 20 under federal control and six under the Kurdistan Region, including four land crossings and two airports. That administrative split shows up in telecoms too: the big operators’ signals span both regimes, but regulatory enforcement does not apply evenly, and that is where visitors get caught.

The Korek dispute is the clearest demonstration of why the split matters. In June 2026 the CMC declared Korek’s operating licence expired, and in August it moved to shut the operator’s switches, exchanges and sales offices in Baghdad; reporting through September 2026 put the number of governorates where Korek offices had closed at eight, with no outage recorded. A Baghdad court then issued a stay suspending that decision, and the KRG’s transport minister said publicly that Korek would keep operating inside the Kurdistan Region regardless. So: do not build an Iraq eSIM plan around Korek service in federal Iraq, and treat Korek inside Kurdistan as strong but legally unsettled.

How the three Iraqi operators compare

The pattern is consistent. Asiacell and Zain Iraq compete for the federal centre and south, with Asiacell ahead on network experience and Zain Iraq on subscriber depth. Korek owns the northern cities outright, and Fastlink is a regional third option with no coverage outside Kurdistan.

Three quality signals are worth knowing. Asiacell has publicly claimed an Ookla award as Iraq’s fastest 4G network. Independent measurement published for January 2026 put Asiacell first on average download speed at 38.0 Mbps, against 25.8 Mbps for Zain Iraq and 14.6 Mbps for Korek. And Zain Group’s own results put Zain Iraq at 20.4 million customers in Q3 2025, up five percent year on year.

Most international travel eSIMs sold for Iraq ride Asiacell and Korek as roaming partners, with a smaller group riding Zain Iraq. If a product page names its networks, read it — that choice, not the brand on the box, decides whether your Iraq eSIM works in Erbil.

Is your phone ready for your eSIM? Bands, locks and setup

An Iraq eSIM connects to Iraqi 4G, which runs on LTE bands common across Europe, the Middle East and Asia — 1800 MHz (Band 3) among them — so virtually any unlocked international handset attaches without drama. There is no commercial 5G network in operation yet: federal authorities coordinated 5G planning with the Kurdistan Region in August 2026, but the eSIM you buy today means 4G LTE, full stop.

Iraq eSIM compatibility: the three checks that decide it

  • Carrier lock. A phone locked to your home carrier will not accept any third-party profile. On iPhone, check Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock; “No SIM restrictions” is what you want.
  • eSIM support. Dial *#06# — an EID on screen means the phone can hold a profile. If you are unsure about your exact model, the compatibility page settles it by model number, not marketing name.
  • Regional quirks. iPhones sold in mainland China have the eSIM hardware disabled, and some carrier-purchased Samsungs grey out the eSIM menu until the original carrier unlocks the feature.

Iraq eSIM dual-SIM behaviour worth knowing

Keep your home SIM live for bank codes and calls, point mobile data at the eSIM, and switch data roaming off on the home line so background traffic does not bill against it. Data-only travel profiles cannot receive SMS, so nothing time-critical should depend on your Iraqi line.

Registering an Iraq eSIM: CMC rules for foreign visitors

Registration comes first, everywhere. Iraqi law requires every mobile line, SIM or eSIM, to be registered against an identity document, and the rule applies to foreigners exactly as it does to residents. At an official operator store, staff scan your passport, record your details in the registration portal, and at Zain Iraq the process includes a photograph and fingerprint. Expect 15 to 30 minutes.

Identity checks at Iraq SIM counters

  1. Choose an official operator store or a staffed airport counter — Baghdad International or Erbil International both have them.
  2. Hand over your original passport with the physical visa sticker or entry stamp already inside it.
  3. Give a local address: hotel, guesthouse or host address. Nothing is posted to it.
  4. Let staff record your details in the operator’s registration portal; at Zain Iraq expect a photograph and fingerprint too.
  5. Receive the eSIM QR code, printed or on screen, and scan it while the counter staff are still with you.
  6. Buy the first bundle with cash, then confirm data works with Wi-Fi switched off before you leave.

Buying routes compared: Asiacell, Zain and Korek

Buying an Asiacell eSIM as a visitor

Asiacell advertises “Buy SIM or eSIM Online” on its own site, with prepaid packages starting from 3,000 IQD and delivery inside Iraq — but as a foreign visitor without an Iraqi address, your realistic path is a main Asiacell shop, where staff issue an eSIM QR code for a new line after taking your passport details. Bundles and top-ups are managed in the Asiacell app afterwards.

Buying a Zain Iraq eSIM in store

Zain Iraq’s new-line page describes the flow directly: choose a bundle, visit the nearest sales centre, and pick eSIM or physical SIM — the eSIM is bought in store, then installed by scanning the QR code on the spot. Its prepaid KAFOO bundles combine credit, SMS and social-media data, and the digital sub-brand Oodi sells app-managed plans from 15,000 IQD for 5 GB a month.

Korek, Fastlink and Kurdistan-only trips

Korek’s Erbil home market remains its stronghold, but with its federal licence formally disputed and enforcement running through 2026, a visitor should not make Korek the primary line. If your trip is Kurdistan-only, ask at Korek stores in Erbil about current eSIM issuance; if the answer is uncertain, an Asiacell line covers Sulaymaniyah while remaining federally licensed.

Asiacell vs Zain: which Iraq carrier is faster?

Inside the Kurdistan Region, Fastlink issues SIMs to visitors against a valid passport plus a temporary local address — no biometric capture is required for foreigners, unlike the resident path. Its promotional bundles reach large allowances at low prices, and the trade-off is coverage confined to the region.

Iraq internet curbs and rhythms worth planning around

During national school exams, Iraq has scheduled nationwide internet curbs — dozens of outages were documented across the 2025 exam season — so a dead connection between roughly 6 and 8 in the morning during exam season is the state doing exactly what it announced.

During Arba’een, the pilgrimage concentrating millions on the Najaf–Karbala corridor, networks saturate; authorities reported over 277 million calls along that corridor during the 2025 observance alone.

And in the Kurdistan highlands — around Rawanduz, Barzan and the Turkish border — expect genuine dead zones, with the Mesopotamian marshes and the desert west of the Euphrates much the same.

Iraq border rules: the two entry rules that decide your visa

The visa you are granted depends on where you enter. Coming in over Ibrahim Khalil from Türkiye gives you a Kurdistan Region visa, and travelling on to Baghdad means paying for a second, federal visa. Entering from Jordan or Kuwait gives a federal visa valid across the country.

What are the APN settings for Asiacell, Zain and Korek?

Here is the honest version: there is no reliably published, operator-confirmed APN table for Iraq that can be verified the way it can for Canada or Germany, and guessing APN strings causes more failures than it fixes. What is actually true:

  • Travel eSIM profiles configure themselves. The profile carries its own APN invisibly. If data fails on a travel eSIM in Iraq, the APN is almost never the cause — check the roaming toggle and the selected data line first.
  • Carrier-issued lines auto-provision. When Asiacell, Zain Iraq or Fastlink staff install your eSIM or SIM in store, the line and its settings activate on their network side. If anything is missing, the store that issued the line is the correct source for the exact values.
  • Manual entry paths, if a technician ever gives you values: on iPhone, Settings → Cellular → your eSIM line → Cellular Data Network; on Android, Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names. Restart after saving.

Activating your Iraq eSIM and fixing what goes wrong

The generic install sequence — Add eSIM, scan the QR code, label the line, set it as your data line, enable data roaming — is documented step by step in our eSIM activation guide. Install before departure, on home Wi-Fi.

The four failure patterns that are specifically Iraqi:

No network appears — or the wrong one

Open network selection, switch from automatic to manual, and pick a network you can actually see — Asiacell (418-05) or Zain Iraq (418-20 / 418-30). If only Korek appears on a federal-network profile in Baghdad, the licensing dispute is the likely cause; force a different network and move on.

Connected, but no data flows

Confirm data roaming is ON for the eSIM line and that the eSIM, not your home SIM, is set as the mobile-data line. On a dual-SIM phone, also disable data roaming on your home line so background traffic does not bill against it.

Works in Baghdad, dies in Erbil — or the reverse

This is the two-regime country doing what it does. A single-network profile rides one operator’s footprint; if your itinerary crosses the internal border, either add a second profile or use a dual-network Iraq eSIM from the start. Manual reselection usually restores service when the phone clings to a fading operator.

Data dies mid-trip

Three ordinary causes, in order of likelihood: your allowance ran out and a top-up is needed; an exam-season national outage is in its scheduled window; or the phone’s registration lapsed — terminal for street-bought SIMs, and fixed at the issuing store for properly registered ones.

Common Iraq connectivity questions

Does Asiacell sell eSIMs to tourists?

Yes, with a condition. Asiacell issues eSIM QR codes at its main shops for new lines, and Iraqi identity registration — passport for visitors — applies exactly as it does to physical SIMs. Tourists who want to skip the shop buy a travel eSIM riding the Asiacell network, installed before the flight.

Can you buy an Iraq eSIM from Asiacell?

No. Zain Iraq’s eSIM is purchased in a store after ID registration; the operator’s own new-line page walks customers through exactly that flow. Visitors abroad who want Zain Iraq coverage need a store visit after landing or a travel eSIM that specifically names it as a partner.

Is Korek eSIM a safe choice in 2026?

Inside the Kurdistan Region, Korek remains the dominant network and the KRG has committed to keeping it operating. But its federal licence is disputed, the CMC moved to shut its Baghdad facilities during 2026, and a court stay is the only thing holding enforcement off. For a visitor, Korek should be a fallback, never the primary.

Signing up: Iraq SIM registration

If the eSIM comes from an Iraqi operator, yes — passport registration, a photograph and often a fingerprint, done in person at an official store. Travel eSIMs bought abroad need no registration, because they operate under the provider’s roaming agreements rather than an Iraqi subscription.

Getting hold of an Iraq eSIM

Baghdad International and Erbil International both have carrier counters in arrivals, and Erbil’s include Korek and Zain Iraq. Those counters sell physical SIMs and complete the registration on the spot. They do not sell travel eSIMs, and airport queues after a long flight are a poor place to install one.

Will one eSIM work in both federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region?

Physically, yes — every major operator’s signal reaches across the internal border. Practically, strength flips: Asiacell and Zain Iraq lead in the federal cities, Korek leads in Erbil and Duhok. A dual-network travel eSIM handles the transitions automatically.

Asiacell vs Zain 5G: which is better in Iraq?

No commercial 5G network operates in Iraq as of late 2026. The federal government announced in August 2026 that it is coordinating a synchronised rollout with the Kurdistan Region, and the KRG had taken earlier exploratory steps — but every Iraq eSIM sold today connects to 4G LTE.

What’s the right GB count for Iraq?

Cable.co.uk’s comparison puts 1 GB at about USD 1.07 — 97th of 237 markets, well below the USD 2.59 global average. Local prepaid bundles are cheaper still once registered: Asiacell tiers start at 3,000 IQD, and travel eSIMs run roughly USD 5–8 per gigabyte on short validity.

Is my phone eSIM-compatible in Iraq?

Iraqi 4G runs on widely supported LTE bands including 1800 MHz, so ordinary European, Asian and Middle Eastern handsets attach normally, and there is no 5G band question yet. The checks that matter are carrier lock and eSIM support.

Why did my internet stop during the morning?

If it is national school exam season, Iraq deliberately curtails connectivity during exam hours to prevent cheating — the practice is officially announced and applies to every operator. Outages in the 2025 season numbered in the dozens between May and September. No settings change on your phone will bypass it.

Asiacell vs Zain: which is better in Iraq?

Korek has historically been the region’s benchmark, strongest in Erbil, Duhok and Zakho, with Fastlink also active under KRG oversight. Asiacell is the strongest federally licensed alternative and dominates Sulaymaniyah, its home city.
