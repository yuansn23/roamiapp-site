The Netherlands is the most connected country most people visit without noticing. Cities are dense, networks are dense, and Ookla’s H1 2025 report shows a genuine three-way race: Odido posting the fastest medians in the country (216.3 Mbps all-technology, 272.74 Mbps on 5G), KPN Mobile winning on 5G consistency (88.6% of samples above 25 Mbps down), and Vodafone holding the broad consumer middle. A Netherlands eSIM rides all of that — the real decisions are which network your plan attaches to and what you expect from the places where Dutch flatness ends: the Wadden Islands, the forests of the Veluwe, the trains between them. Nothing anonymous made it into this guide; the closing source list covers every Netherlands figure.

This guide covers the carrier comparison, the EU roaming angle for visitors, the arrival logistics at Schiphol, and the honest coverage truth about a country that is easier to connect than almost anywhere — except in the few places it is not. Whether your specific model supports eSIM is answered on the compatibility lookup tool.

If you read nothing else: Odido is the speed pick — fastest medians in the Netherlands by a wide margin, ideal for heavy data users and hotspots. KPN Mobile is the consistency pick, especially outside the big cities and in the national parks. Vodafone is the all-rounder with solid urban coverage everywhere you will actually be. Any of the three is excellent by European standards; a multi-network Netherlands eSIM simply attaches to whichever wins in each spot.

Odido, KPN and Vodafone: who leads, and where

Odido, the fastest network in the country

Odido’s H1 2025 numbers lead every headline metric: median download of 216.3 Mbps across all technologies (against KPN’s 152.08 Mbps and Vodafone’s 104.7 Mbps), 272.74 Mbps on 5G, 28.38 Mbps 5G upload, and an overall Speedtest Connectivity Score of 84.34. Formerly T-Mobile Netherlands and now merged with fibre operator T-Mobile/Tele2’s infrastructure under the Odido brand, it built its lead on dense urban capacity — and it shows in the city table, where Eindhoven’s 220.42 Mbps median is the fastest populous-city reading in the country. For raw speed, nothing else in the Netherlands is close.

KPN Mobile: the most consistent network

KPN’s story is the one that matters outside the cities: the best 5G consistency in the market, with 88.6% of samples meeting or exceeding 25 Mbps down / 3 Mbps up. Its median 5G download (182.84 Mbps) and overall median (152.08 Mbps) trail Odido’s peaks, but KPN — the incumbent, with the deepest rural obligations enforced by the regulator ACM, the Autoriteit Consument en Markt — is the network that behaves the same in the Veluwe as it does in Amsterdam. For itineraries that leave the Randstad, KPN’s floor is the highest.

Vodafone: reliable coverage across the Netherlands

Vodafone’s Netherlands network posts a 104.7 Mbps overall median and 114.99 Mbps on 5G — the slowest of the three on paper, and still faster than most of Europe’s urban reality. Its strength is breadth: the tourist Netherlands — museum districts, train stations, Schiphol, the coastal towns — is thoroughly covered, and the network never asks you to think about it. As a pick in a multi-network profile, it is the dependable third lane.

Dutch speeds are high enough to make carrier choice look academic — until the detail says otherwise. In Ookla’s 1H 2025 Netherlands report, drawn from January to June 2025, Odido takes both Best Mobile Network and Best 5G Network.

Crossing the border with a Netherlands eSIM

The Netherlands is an EU member, so the “roam like at home” framework applies: a SIM or eSIM issued in another EU country works here at domestic rates, within the fair-use data caps your home plan sets — the European Commission’s roaming page documents the rights and limits. Travellers from outside the EU (UK included, post-Brexit) have no automatic rights, and home-carrier roaming in Western Europe is reliably expensive.

For everyone else the straightforward route is a travel profile bought and installed before departure: it attaches to Odido, KPN or Vodafone when you land, and the home number stays in the tray for verification. Data-only profiles do not include a Dutch phone number — irrelevant for most tourists, since Dutch daily life (NS journey planners, iDEAL payments, restaurant booking) works through apps that run fine on data. One local quirk: many Dutch apps assume a European payment method, but connectivity itself is never the barrier.

Dutch trains, bikes and the apps that use your eSIM data

The Netherlands runs on apps more thoroughly than almost any destination, and they define what your eSIM actually does.

The rail layer. NS journey planning and e-tickets run on data, and intercity coverage on Odido, KPN and Vodafone holds through most of the Randstad — brief dropouts occur in tunnels and a few northern rural stretches, so download tickets before long rides. Contactless card payments (OVpay) work directly at station gates, which is worth knowing if your phone’s payment card is sorted before arrival.

The bike layer. OV-fiets rental bikes are unlocked with the same transit card, and navigation happens on Google Maps or the Dutch cycling apps — both data-hungry, both fine on any of the three networks. Cycling with live navigation through Amsterdam is genuinely one of the better tests of an urban network, and all three pass it.

Museums and booking. The big institutions (Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum, Anne Frank House) run timed-entry tickets that genuinely sell out in high season — booking over Wi-Fi the night before and carrying the QR offline is the pattern that never fails.

None of this needs a Dutch phone number; it needs reliable data, which is the one thing the Dutch networks never shortchange you on.

Why manual APN entry is rare in the Netherlands

Dutch operators push their settings automatically, and a travel eSIM supplies its own profile, so manual APN entry is genuinely rare here. If you do need it after buying a local line, the pattern is the same across the market: an APN field containing the operator’s own string with username and password left blank, and the carrier’s support page as the authority for the exact value rather than a third-party list.

  • iPhone: Settings → Cellular → the Netherlands eSIM → Cellular Data Network → APN.
  • Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → + → enter the APN and save.

The reason it is worth knowing the menu path at all is the symptom it fixes: bars and no data on a locally bought prepaid line is almost always an APN or data-line problem, not a network fault. On a travel eSIM the equivalent symptom is nearly always a data-line or roaming toggle.

Dutch eSIM faults and how to clear them

The install refuses to start. Work through the handset’s carrier lock first, then ask whether the QR code has been used before; these are single-use, so a fresh code from the provider is the remedy.

Installs without bars. Force the radio off for fifteen seconds, then select Odido, KPN or Vodafone by hand in Network Selection. Arrivals from Germany or Belgium routinely need this to force a fresh attach onto a Dutch network.

Data stalls although the bars are there. Check three things: the eSIM is the mobile-data line, roaming is permitted for it, and the APN matches the carrier on a local prepaid card. If you crossed in from Belgium or Germany, one Airplane Mode cycle before investigating further.

Emergency calls only. Switch off any other active profile, restart, then reset network settings; a fresh installation sits at the bottom of the list.

Anything past these belongs in the full eSIM troubleshooting guide, while the activation sequence covers the install itself.

Common Dutch eSIM questions, answered

Odido vs KPN Mobile: which is better in Netherlands?

Whichever is strongest where you are — which is the argument for a multi-network profile. On single-network plans: Odido for speed in the cities, KPN for consistency outside them, Vodafone for dependable middle-ground coverage. The differences between them inside Amsterdam or Rotterdam are smaller than the marketing suggests.

Is 5G available everywhere in the Netherlands?

Across the cities and intercity corridors, effectively yes — the Netherlands ranked 18th globally in August 2026. The honest exceptions are the smaller Wadden Islands, some nature reserves, and a handful of rural pockets in Friesland and Drenthe, where 4G remains the workhorse.

Can you tether from a Netherlands eSIM?

Yes, and it is worth doing: hotel Wi-Fi in the cities is often metered or slow, and a 5G hotspot from an Odido-riding profile is frequently the faster option for laptop work.

Do I need a Dutch phone number?

Almost never. Transit, payments and bookings run through apps that work on data alone, and verification codes arrive on your home number over the physical SIM card. A Dutch number only matters for long stays and local contracts; for a normal visit, one data plan on the eSIM — with a mid-trip top-up if the itinerary grows — does everything.

Will my EU SIM work in the Netherlands?

If it was issued in another EU country, yes — EU roaming rules apply, with fair-use data limits defined by your home plan. UK, US and other non-EU profiles have no such rights, which is why a dedicated Netherlands eSIM is the predictable option.

Why won’t my Netherlands eSIM connect?

Confirm the eSIM is selected as the data line and data roaming is enabled for it, then toggle airplane mode once. If the problem persists after a restart, work through the activation troubleshooting guide — APN and profile-state issues are the usual remaining causes.

Netherlands mobile speeds by carrier

Odido, on every headline measure: a 216.3 Mbps median download across all technologies, a 272.74 Mbps median 5G download, the best video score at 81.31 and the best gaming experience at 92.76. It was also the fastest provider in all ten measured cities and all twelve provinces.

Is KPN better than Odido in the Netherlands?

On two measures, yes: KPN has the best 5G consistency in the market at 88.6%, and it is the top-rated Dutch network by its own users at 3.39 out of 5. Odido wins on speed. Since Ookla found no statistical difference in overall consistency between the three, the choice matters less than the spread suggests.

How fast is mobile data in Amsterdam?

Amsterdam records a 186.4 Mbps median download with 24.07 Mbps upload and 30.54 ms latency. It is not the fastest Dutch city — Eindhoven leads at 220.42 Mbps — but the capital’s latency is as good as anywhere in the country, which is what makes it feel quick.

Why is Vodafone slower than Odido in the Netherlands?

Ookla’s 1H 2025 report records Vodafone at a 104.7 Mbps median download against Odido’s 216.3 Mbps, and 114.99 Mbps on 5G against 272.74 Mbps. Both are still comfortably fast; the gap reflects spectrum holdings and site density rather than anything a normal user would notice on a map or a message.

Do I need 5G in the Netherlands?

No. The slowest Dutch province, Drenthe, still records 133.13 Mbps on 4G-inclusive measures, which is faster than most countries’ 5G. 5G adds headroom for large transfers rather than changing how the network feels for ordinary travel use.

APN values for Odido and KPN Mobile eSIMs

Dutch operators push settings automatically and a travel eSIM carries its own, so manual entry is rarely needed. If you bought a local prepaid line, use the carrier’s own support page for the exact value rather than a third-party list — the menu path is Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Network on iPhone.

Will a Netherlands eSIM cover Belgium and Germany?

Not a Netherlands-only profile — but a regional European plan will, and that is the version worth buying if your itinerary crosses a border. On a single-country eSIM the connection drops when the train leaves the Dutch network; a multi-country tier keeps the same profile alive through Brussels or Aachen. If your trip stays inside the Netherlands, there is no reason to pay for the extra coverage.

Can I work remotely on Dutch mobile data?

Unusually good. A 33 ms national latency, 95%-plus consistency in most provinces and 180 Mbps-plus medians mean a mobile connection is a credible office substitute, and Dutch hotel and café Wi-Fi is fast enough to use for bulk transfers instead.

Does 10 GB last for Netherlands?

Budget five to eight gigabytes for a train-and-city week: maps, messaging and photo back-ups dominate, while hotel Wi-Fi takes the streaming load. Add a few gigabytes for a cycling tour, where navigation runs continuously all day.

Can you buy a Netherlands eSIM from Odido?

Yes — Schiphol arrivals has carrier counters and shops selling prepaid SIMs, usually with English-speaking staff and short queues. Getting an eSIM from Dutch carriers is less predictable for visitors than the physical route, which is one reason to install a travel profile before you fly.

A look inside Netherlands data plans

The Netherlands sits in the middle of the European pack per gigabyte — cheaper than Belgium and Germany in Cable.co.uk’s pricing study, more expensive than Poland or Romania. Local prepaid bundles are the value route; a travel eSIM is the one that works on landing.

Do Netherlands SIMs require documents?

Less rigidly than in many EU markets — prepaid SIMs are widely available, though some operators ask for identification at activation. It is not the biometric-style registration you meet in Hungary or Kuwait, but it is still a shop visit rather than an instant purchase.

Choosing your Netherlands data allowance

Five to eight gigabytes for a week is the honest range. Trams, museums and cafés all offer Wi-Fi, so the eSIM carries maps, tickets, translation and the evening’s uploads rather than everything. Add a few gigabytes if you plan to work from cafés, because Dutch networks are fast enough that a video call will happily consume an hour of allowance without you noticing.

Are the Wadden Islands well covered?

The islands themselves have urban-grade coverage in their main villages, comparable to the mainland’s 144.49 Mbps Friesland median. The exception is the crossing: ferry journeys between Den Helder and Texel, or Harlingen and Terschelling, run without usable signal for an hour or more, so book accommodation and transport before you board.

Which Dutch city has the fastest mobile data?

Eindhoven, at a 220.42 Mbps median, ahead of Rotterdam at 210.87 and Haarlem at 210.03 Mbps. Amsterdam is seventh in the ten-city table at 186.4 Mbps — density costs it peak speed, though its 30.54 ms latency is as good as anywhere in the country.

Should I buy my Netherlands eSIM before I land?

Yes, for the same reason it is worth buying anywhere in the EU: it arrives installed and works on landing, and Schiphol is not the place to spend your first hour shopping for a SIM. The Dutch retail route is easy, but it is still a queue you do not need on a short break.

Does Dutch mobile data work well on trains?

Better than the rail Wi-Fi does. Intercity services between Amsterdam, Utrecht, Rotterdam and The Hague run through provinces with a minimum 143 Mbps median and 95%-plus consistency, while onboard Wi-Fi is shared and inconsistent in tunnels. Use the eSIM for anything that matters.

Is island mobile data good enough for video calls?

In the main villages, yes — Texel, Terschelling and Schiermonnikoog carry mainland-grade coverage, so a call from a guesthouse works. What does not work is calling from the water: the crossings from Den Helder and Harlingen run for an hour or more without usable signal, which is worth knowing before you schedule a meeting on the ferry.

Is data throttled on Dutch networks?

Rarely on the network side, occasionally on the plan side. Dutch operators publish no throttling on ordinary data traffic, but a travel profile’s fair-use terms may apply a cap to hotspot use specifically. For a city break that is academic; if you intend to tether a laptop for a working week, read the tier’s terms before buying rather than after.

When should I install my Netherlands eSIM?

Before. The profile lies dormant until it registers on a Dutch network, so loading it at home costs nothing and spares you a Schiphol arrival spent hunting for Wi-Fi and a QR code. Validity starts on that first registration, not on the day you paid.

Can I use one plan across the Netherlands and Belgium?

Yes, with an EU-roaming plan: the Netherlands is inside the roam-like-at-home framework, so allowances cross borders without surcharge, fair-use limits aside. See the Belgium coverage page for the southern neighbour.

Should I keep my home SIM active alongside the eSIM?

Yes, exactly as at home: calls and verification codes on the physical SIM, Dutch data on the eSIM. Dutch networks are fast enough that the practical risk is the opposite of what travellers expect — you will use more data than planned, not less, because the connection is good enough to stop thinking about it.
