Is your phone compatible with a Thailand eSIM?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Thai carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Do international phones support Thai network bands?

Thai carriers build their 5G on specific bands — notably n28 (700 MHz) for rural reach, n41 (2600 MHz) and n78 (3500 MHz) for city capacity, plus n1 and n3 in metro areas. Plenty of international handsets, including several iPhone regional variants, ship without the full Thai mid-band stack. Those phones still work: they fall back to 4G once you leave dense urban areas, which is fine downtown and frustrating on a quiet island.

Band charts rarely help a traveler in practice. The dependable step is to match the model number, not the marketing name, against the device compatibility list. If you would rather understand the mechanics first, what happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile has the detail.

Technology Bands used in Thailand
4G LTE B1 (2100 MHz), B3 (1800 MHz), B5 (850 MHz), B8 (900 MHz), B20 (800 MHz), B28 (700 MHz), B40 (2300 MHz)
5G NR n1 (2100 MHz), n3 (1800 MHz), n28 (700 MHz), n40 (2300 MHz), n41 (2600 MHz), n78 (3500 MHz), n79 (4500 MHz)

A phone with n28 and n41 covers the Thai mid-band stack that carries most 5G capacity; missing those bands means more fallback to 4G.

What if your phone is carrier-locked?

A locked phone is the most common reason a Thailand eSIM install simply fails, and Thailand has no rule forcing carriers to unlock devices for you.

Thailand does not require carriers to unlock a phone after a set period, so the unlock depends entirely on the policies of your home carrier. If the device is paid off, most home carriers will clear it within hours, often for free; if it is still on a payment plan, the lock stays until the balance is settled.

Check it: on iPhone: Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock. When it reads “No SIM restrictions”, you can install straight away. A “SIM locked” result blocks any outside profile until the operator unlocks the phone.

Fix it: approach the operator that locked the handset and ask them to release it. Only then try installing your Thailand eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone from a carrier you no longer use, assume it may be locked and check before you fly.

How to buy a Thailand eSIM and what it costs

Three Thailand eSIM routes exist, and they differ more in paperwork than in price: AIS direct, TrueMove H direct, and dtac inside the True Group. Each route, step by step:

How to buy an AIS tourist eSIM

AIS is the largest network by coverage and the most awarded on Ookla’s 1H 2025 report. International visitors can buy a tourist eSIM two ways.

AIS eSIM, step by step:

  1. Buy online from the AIS tourist page or a reseller and choose eSIM at checkout, or pick up at an AIS shop or airport counter.
  2. If buying online, scan the QR code sent by email and label the line “Thailand.”
  3. If buying in store, hand over your passport and complete the live facial scan; staff activate the profile on the spot.
  4. Set the eSIM as your mobile data line and turn on data roaming.

AIS tourist tiers run from an 8-day 15 GB package around 299 THB up to 30-day packages near 899 to 1199 THB, with unlimited social-app tiers in between. Check your model before buying, and keep the AIS app for top-ups.

Does TrueMove H sell a tourist eSIM

TrueMove H is the consumer brand inside True Corporation, and it sells tourist eSIMs through the same online and in-store paths as AIS.

TrueMove H’s edge is 5G availability: Ookla measured the highest 5G Availability in Thailand at 74.2% for TrueMove H in 1H 2025. Its tourist tiers mirror AIS pricing, with an 8-day 15 GB package around 299 to 449 THB and 30-day unlimited packages near 1199 THB. The in-store rule is identical: passport plus facial scan, then instant activation.

Does dtac still sell its own tourist eSIM?

Yes. dtac merged with True in March 2023 to form True Corporation, and dtac still operates as its own consumer brand with its own tourist eSIM and SIM products. The two networks finished integrating their towers into a single “One Network” in October 2025, so a dtac eSIM now also benefits from the combined infrastructure.

dtac’s tourist tiers are usually the cheapest entry: an 8-day 15 GB package can start around 299 THB, a 15-day 30 GB package around 599 THB, and a 30-day package near 1199 THB. Coverage is strong in cities and decent on the main islands, but AIS still leads in the rural north and on the smaller Gulf islands, so weigh price against where you are headed.

What to prepare before buying a Thailand eSIM

  • Passport — required by NBTC for any in-store SIM or eSIM registration; foreign visitors must show the original document
  • A Thai address or hotel name — requested on some carrier registration forms, even for tourists

Thailand eSIM APN settings: do you need to enter them manually?

Tourist SIMs usually configure themselves, so treat the APN screen as a troubleshooting tool rather than a setup step. This section gives you every carrier value, the rare cases that need manual entry, and the tap-by-tap route.

Cases where a Thailand eSIM needs a manual APN

AIS, TrueMove H and dtac tourist profiles normally arrive preconfigured, so manual entry is a troubleshooting move rather than a setup step. Reach for it when:

  • An older handset that does not pick up carrier settings automatically
  • A profile installed via manual activation code rather than a QR scan
  • A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
  • Almost never on a travel eSIM — that is the point of a managed profile

How to enter a Thailand APN manually

  • iPhone: Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → enter the APN
  • Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → add a new APN

After saving, restart the phone. If data still will not load, check that the active data line is the Thai profile and not your home SIM.

Thailand eSIM troubleshooting in four steps

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our eSIM error troubleshooting guide. Four patterns are worth naming upfront, because they keep resurfacing in Thailand.

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Look in Settings -> General -> About: Carrier Lock has to show “No SIM restrictions”
  2. Check the QR code is smudge-free and unused, because most can be scanned only once
  3. Ask the carrier if the profile is still sitting in a pending state

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status
  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → select AIS, TrueMove H or dtac manually instead of automatic, especially near the Mekong border where Laos towers appear
  3. Wipe the network settings, then restart

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Check the APN once more against your carrier row above
  2. Point the data line at the Thai eSIM instead of your home SIM.
  3. Check you have not exhausted your data allowance — tourist plans throttle after a fair-use cap rather than stopping

D. “SOS” or “Emergency calls only” on an island

  1. The eSIM must be toggled on in the SIM manager
  2. Disable or delete any other active SIM or eSIM profile to avoid conflicts
  3. Reboot and reset network settings
  4. Only as a final step, delete the profile and reinstall from a new QR code

Common questions about Thailand carrier eSIMs

AIS eSIM plans for visitors

AIS sells tourist eSIMs online and at AIS shops and airport counters. Online eSIMs arrive by QR code with no in-person step; in-store SIMs require your passport and a live facial scan under NBTC rules. AIS tourist tiers start around 299 THB for 8 days and 15 GB.

What does registering a Thailand SIM involve?

For an in-store SIM or eSIM, NBTC requires passport registration plus a live facial scan for every new line. If you buy a TrueMove H eSIM online before you fly, the registration is handled by the seller and you skip the counter queue.

Is dtac still a separate network from True?

Yes. dtac merged with True in March 2023 to form True Corporation, but dtac still sells its own tourist SIM and eSIM products under the dtac brand. The two networks completed tower integration into a single One Network in October 2025, so dtac eSIMs now ride the combined infrastructure.

Who claims the widest coverage in Thailand?

AIS has the strongest reputation on the smaller Gulf islands such as Koh Tao and the quieter parts of Koh Samui and Koh Phi Phi. TrueMove H and dtac cover the main resort strips well but thin out as you move between islands or onto boat crossings.

Do you need a Thai number for your eSIM?

No. eSIMs for travelers are usually data-only and do not require a local number. You can use WhatsApp, LINE, FaceTime and similar apps for calls and messages. You only need a Thai number if a specific bank app, hotel booking or delivery service demands one.

Can I use a travel eSIM and my home SIM at the same time?

Yes, and it is a sensible setup. The usual split works well in Thailand: home number on the physical SIM, data on the local profile. Point the data setting at the Thai profile and leave your home line for calls. Turn roaming off on the home line so background traffic never reaches your bill. If a laptop is coming too, check the plan’s hotspot terms before counting on tethering.

How do I get my locked phone working in Thailand?

Ask the carrier it is locked to for an unlock before you travel; Thailand has no rule forcing a local unlock. Your handset shows it under Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. A phone that reads “SIM locked” will not install any Thai eSIM until the lock is cleared by your home carrier.

Cheapest Thailand eSIM plans for budget travelers

dtac usually wins on entry price, with an 8-day 15 GB tourist package around 299 THB, while AIS and TrueMove H start near the same figure and climb to around 1199 THB for 30-day unlimited tiers. Budget brands and 7-Eleven blank SIMs start lower but need a package added on top.

Why did my eSIM stop working on a boat crossing?

Boat crossings between islands move you out of tower range, and your phone may latch onto a weak neighbor signal or drop to no service. This is expected on the quieter Gulf and Andaman routes. Download offline maps before you sail and re-select your Thai carrier manually once you land.

Can one eSIM cover Thailand and its neighbors?

A Thailand eSIM is valid only inside Thailand. Crossing into Laos, Cambodia or Malaysia needs a separate plan or a regional eSIM. Multi-country eSIMs cover regional travel, which is easier than buying at each border.

What the eSIM chip does when you land in Thailand

The eSIM chip sits inside your handset permanently and stores whatever profile you load onto it. Once the profile reaches the chip, the line behaves like any other connection on the phone. The delivery chain behind it, including why a QR code can only be used once, is covered in how an eSIM profile installs.

What if my Thailand eSIM will not activate?

Work the four Thailand-specific patterns above in order — lock status, network selection, APN and data line, then profile reinstall — and if it still fails, the wider error catalogue in troubleshooting guide covers the rest. Have the EID, order number and an error screenshot ready for support.
