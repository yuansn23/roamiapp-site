A Hungary eSIM comes with unusually good speed numbers for the price, and this guide walks the three national networks so you know which one fits your route. Hungary is the rare European destination where the speed numbers are almost embarrassing — in a good way. In Ookla’s H1 2025 connectivity report, Yettel recorded a median 5G download of 369.08 Mbps, among the fastest readings in the region, while Magyar Telekom countered with the country’s highest 5G availability and the best video score. Three carriers, three genuinely different strengths, and a traveller-facing question that mostly comes down to where between Budapest and Lake Balaton your days are spent. Yettel, Magyar Telekom and One: what the 1H 2025 measurements found

Yettel vs Magyar Telekom: which Hungary carrier is faster?

Yettel’s H1 2025 numbers lead the report on both speed tiers: median 5G download of 369.08 Mbps with 37.18 Mbps up, a 4G median of 68.40 Mbps, and the best consistency in the country at 94.6% of samples clearing 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up. In plain terms: the fastest network is also the one that holds up most reliably outside the capital — which is why it appears at the top of both the city and the countryside rows above. Yettel is majority-owned by PPF Group and runs the former Telenor Hungary network, with a long-established rural footprint to show for it.

Magyar Telekom eSIM performance

Magyar Telekom, the country’s Deutsche Telekom arm, wins the metrics that describe how often you are on 5G at all: 50.8% 5G availability, the highest in Hungary, plus the best mobile video experience score at 91.23. Its median 5G download (101.82 Mbps) trails Yettel’s headline number, but availability and video quality are what you feel during an actual streaming session or a video call from a moving train. For remote workers spending a month in Budapest or Debrecen, Telekom’s profile fits the workload best.

One eSIM performance and value

One (backed by 4iG) is Hungary’s third network and its Ookla story is latency: the best 5G gaming experience in the country, with a median 5G download of 66.84 Mbps in the report period. It is the network locals pick for price-sensitive plans, and for a traveller it is a perfectly good third choice — genuinely useful in towns like Szeged and Pécs, thinner between them.

EU roaming rules, and what they change for travel eSIM users

Hungary is an EU member, and that matters for who needs what. A SIM or eSIM issued in another member state roams here at domestic rates under the “roam like at home” regime — the European Commission’s roaming page sets out the rights and the fair-use limits. Travellers from the UK, the US and elsewhere enjoy no such automatic rights, and home-carrier roaming in Europe is where bills get ugly.

That is the niche the travel eSIM fills: a Hungarian or regional European profile, bought and installed before departure, that attaches to Yettel, Telekom or One on arrival at local rates. Two practical notes: data-only travel eSIMs do not come with a Hungarian phone number (Hungarian SMS verification is rarely needed by tourists), and the EU’s roaming rights do not extend to a non-EU provider’s plan — check whether your provider’s regional plan covers only the EU bloc or includes your neighbours as well.

How much data do I need for Hungary?

Hungary’s tourist infrastructure runs through a handful of apps, and they set the real data budget.

Getting around. Budapest’s BKK tickets and passes are sold in the official app with QR validation at the gate — a live connection at the barrier is part of the routine. Inter-city rail to Vienna, Bratislava and Debrecen is covered well on all three networks, so mobile tickets and the MAV app behave on the move.

Thermal baths and museums. The big baths (Széchenyi, Rudas) and the main museums all push advance online booking with time slots, and walk-up capacity in high season is not something to bet an afternoon on. Booking over the hotel Wi-Fi and presenting the QR from your phone’s offline copy is the safe pattern.

Day trips. The Danube Bend (Szentendre, Visegrád, Esztergom) and Lake Balaton are the classic escapes, and coverage along both routes is solid — Yettel’s consistency numbers were earned largely on exactly these corridors. Between towns, brief 4G blips are the worst of it.

One quirk worth knowing: Hungarian carrier networks do not fall back to each other, so a single-network plan can sit on a weak signal in a valley while a stronger tower is visible across it. That is precisely the failure mode a multi-network eSIM for Hungary removes.

Fixing a Hungary eSIM that will not connect

Install fails. Carrier lock is the first suspect, then a QR code that has already been used. A handset bought on contract in another EU country is frequently the problem.

Installs, no bars. Put the phone into flight mode for fifteen seconds, then choose by hand from Yettel, Magyar Telekom or One. One cause is worth ruling out early: a handset that speaks only 3G shows this exact symptom in Hungary, and no setting will repair it.

Signal but no throughput. Three things to verify in order — that the eSIM rather than the home line is carrying mobile data, that roaming is switched on for that line, and, on a locally bought tariff, that the APN matches the operator’s.

Emergency calls only. Start by removing the second active profile, then restart. Reset network settings only if that changes nothing, and reinstall as a very last step.

Common Hungary eSIM questions, answered

Does your device pass Hungary’s eSIM screening?

Almost certainly, if it is unlocked and eSIM-capable. Hungary’s 4G bands (800/1800/2600 MHz) and 5G (n78 primary, n1 for coverage) are supported by mainstream international handsets. One lookup on the compatibility page confirms your exact model.

Yettel vs Magyar Telekom 5G: which is better in Hungary?

Very. Yettel’s 369.08 Mbps median 5G download in H1 2025 is among the fastest readings in Central Europe, and Magyar Telekom’s 50.8% 5G availability means you spend real time on 5G in the cities rather than seeing the icon once a day. Outside Budapest and the regional cities, 4G carries the load — and at 68.40 Mbps (Yettel’s 4G median), that is no hardship.

Does tethering work on a Hungary eSIM?

Yes. Tethering works on the travel eSIM plans, which matters for the laptop-on-the-train crowd — intercity rail between Budapest, Vienna and Bratislava has good coverage on all three networks.

Which handsets can load Hungary eSIMs?

Tourists almost never do. Ride-hailing, reservations and messaging all work over data with your existing number. A local number only becomes relevant for domestic SMS verification or a stay of many months, at which point a registered local plan — usually a shop-activated prepaid SIM card — makes sense anyway. For shorter trips, a travel data plan with a mid-trip top-up covers everything a visitor needs.

Does my EU SIM work in Hungary without extra charges?

Yes if the plan is EU-issued, within whatever fair-use ceiling your home tariff sets. Non-EU profiles (UK, US, and most travel eSIMs) have no automatic rights; their pricing is set by the plan itself, which is why a purpose-built Hungary eSIM usually wins on both price and predictability.

Hungary carrier speed comparison

Yettel, decisively. Its median 5G download measured 369.08 Mbps against Magyar Telekom’s 101.82 Mbps and One’s 66.84 Mbps, and it recorded the best consistency in the country — 94.6% across all technologies and 96.2% on 5G — and was the fastest provider in all ten cities measured.

Is Magyar Telekom a good alternative to Yettel in Hungary?

Yes, and on one metric it beats Yettel outright: 5G availability of 50.8%, meaning half its users spend most of their time on 5G. It also has the best mobile video experience in Hungary at 91.23. You give up peak speed — 101.82 Mbps median 5G against 369.08 — in exchange for reach.

Why is Budapest’s mobile download slower than Szombathely’s?

Szombathely recorded the fastest city median in Hungary at 130.9 Mbps against Budapest’s 88.94 Mbps, which looks counter-intuitive until you account for user density: the capital’s cells carry far more traffic. Budapest compensates with the lowest latency of any measured city, 30.65 ms, which is what makes it feel responsive.

Is 3G still available in Hungary?

No. Hungary switched its 3G networks off in November 2023. A handset that relies on 3G for voice fallback will not make calls, and a device that cannot hold 4G will not register usefully at all. Check your phone supports 4G LTE or 5G before you travel.

Is identification needed for Hungary SIMs?

Yes — prepaid lines are registered under your name and identity number, for which a passport or an EU national ID is accepted. That makes a local SIM a shop visit rather than a newsstand purchase, and it is the main reason a travel eSIM is the faster option for a short break.

Can I rely on mobile data alone in Hungary?

I already have an EU SIM — do I still need a Hungary profile?

Will my home number still ring while Hungarian data runs?

Yes. The physical SIM takes the calls and the verification texts, the eSIM takes the data, and the pairing is unremarkable in Hungary because both are mainstream configurations on modern handsets. Switch data roaming off on your home line unless your tariff includes it — that is the setting that decides your bill, not the eSIM.

Yettel contract or a prepaid card from a Budapest shop?

Neither is worth your holiday time. Hungary registers prepaid SIMs against an identity, so a shop visit is unavoidable, and a Yettel or Telekom contract is simply not open to a visitor.
