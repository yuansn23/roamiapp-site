Will my phone work with a Spain eSIM?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Spanish carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Which bands do Spanish networks use?

Spanish carriers build 4G on bands B1, B3, B7, B8, B20 (800 MHz) and B28a (700 MHz), and 5G on n1, n3, n7, n28a (700 MHz), n78 (3.5 GHz) and n258 (26 GHz). Most international handsets sold in Europe, Asia and the Americas already support these, so compatibility is rarely the problem it is in, say, Canada. The band that actually separates city from countryside is B20 (800 MHz) for 4G and n28a (700 MHz) for 5G: phones missing them still work in Madrid and Barcelona but lose the low-frequency reach that fills rural valleys.

No one needs to memorise these band numbers. The most dependable step is matching the exact model number to the eSIM compatibility list. Prefer the technical background first? What happens when a phone loads an eSIM profile is the place to start.

Will a SIM lock block your Spain eSIM?

A locked phone is the most common reason a Spain eSIM install simply fails. Unlike some markets, most phones sold in Spain and the European Union arrive unlocked, because EU retail practice defaults to open devices. The phones that trip visitors up are contract handsets locked to a carrier in the United States, Canada, Japan or the Middle East.

Check it: iPhone, go to Settings, General, About, Carrier Lock. A “No SIM restrictions” line means no carrier is holding the device. Should it read “SIM locked”, no third-party profile installs until the device is freed.

Fix it: request the unlock from whichever operator locked the device. Once the lock is removed, only then try installing your eSIM. If you bought a used or grey-market phone, assume it may be locked and check before you fly.

Where travelers buy Spain eSIMs

Movistar is Spain’s largest operator and the coverage leader, owned by Telefónica. Visitors buy a prepaid eSIM through the Mi Movistar app or a Movistar store.

Movistar eSIM, step by step:

  1. Download the Mi Movistar app and choose a prepaid plan. Entry prepaid runs around 10 EUR for 40 GB, with higher tiers near 20 EUR for 50 GB.

  2. Choose eSIM at checkout. A new main-line eSIM is free; swapping a physical SIM to eSIM can carry a one-time fee unless you use iPhone Quick Transfer.

  3. Verify identity with your passport. Spanish law requires the registration before the profile is released.

  4. Receive the eSIM by QR code or install directly in the app, then turn on data roaming when you arrive.

Movistar publishes eSIM support for iPhone XS and later, Google Pixel 3 and later, and Samsung Galaxy S20 and later. Check your model before buying.

How to buy an Orange (MásOrange) prepaid eSIM

Orange is now the consumer face of MásOrange, the group formed when Orange Spain merged with MásMóvil in 2024. Visitors can buy an Orange prepaid eSIM through the My Orange app, where the Prepago 10 plan costs around 10 EUR for 60 GB plus a 20 GB bonus, with unlimited national calls and 28-day validity. EU roaming on that plan reaches up to 26 GB.

The MásOrange family also includes Yoigo, Jazztel, Simyo, Pepephone, Lebara and Lycamobile, so the same group covers almost every price point. If you want Orange’s network without Orange’s branding, Simyo is the cheapest route, while Pepephone accepts international cards more readily. All of them still require passport or Spanish ID registration.

How to buy a Vodafone Spain prepaid eSIM

Vodafone Spain was sold by Vodafone Group to Zegona Communications, but the network and the prepaid eSIM process are unchanged. Visitors sign up through the Vodafone prepaid portal or a store.

Vodafone’s prepaid tiers are generous on data: Prepago S is around 10 EUR for 90 GB and Prepago M is around 15 EUR for 270 GB, each with no contract. After you verify your passport, Vodafone emails a QR code and a six-digit confirmation code; scan the code in your phone’s settings and enter the code to download the profile. Vodafone also supports iPhone Quick Transfer on iOS 17.4 and later, which converts a SIM to eSIM without a QR code.

Does Yoigo sell a prepaid eSIM for visitors?

Yoigo sells a prepaid eSIM and is worth a close look for budget travelers. It operates its own urban network and uses a national roaming agreement with Movistar for rural and mountainous zones, so Yoigo prepaid users effectively inherit Movistar’s footprint outside cities.

Yoigo prepaid tiers undercut the three big carriers: around 7 EUR for 7 GB, 12 EUR for 15 GB, 15 EUR for 25 GB, 20 EUR for 50 GB and 30 EUR for 100 GB, all on 30-day validity. Registration is available online with video ID verification, a foreign passport is accepted, and the QR code typically arrives within an hour during business hours. Yoigo stores are limited, roughly 150 mostly in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and Seville, so most visitors register online.

What to prepare before buying a Spanish carrier eSIM

When do I need to enter the APN manually?

  • An older handset that does not pick up carrier settings automatically

  • A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan

  • A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run

  • Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile

How do I enter the APN on my phone?

  • iPhone: Settings, Cellular, tap the eSIM line, Cellular Data Network, enter the APN

After saving, restart the phone. If nothing loads after that, make sure the data line is set to the Spanish eSIM and not the home SIM.

Spain eSIM failure: four fixes

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our complete eSIM troubleshooting guide. Watch for these four Spain-specific patterns as you read on.

A. Registration rejected at checkout

  1. Confirm your passport is clear and not expired

  2. Try a hotel or Airbnb address if a Spanish address is requested

  3. Switch to a carrier or budget brand that accepts international cards, such as Pepephone or O2

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status

  2. Settings, Cellular, Network Selection, select Movistar, Orange, Vodafone or Yoigo manually instead of automatic

  3. Network settings reset, power-cycle, then test again

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Key the APN in again, character for character, from the table

  2. Check that data is set to the Spanish eSIM line and not your home SIM.

  3. Check you have not exhausted your data allowance, budget prepaid plans throttle hard after the cap

D. Works in the city, dies in the mountains

  1. Accept that rural Spain is genuinely patchy; download offline maps before the Pyrenees or the meseta

  2. On a single-carrier plan, manual network selection may recover a bar where automatic found none

  3. A multi-network travel eSIM re-attaches to the strongest of the four without your input

What do visitors ask about Spain eSIM carriers?

Can international visitors buy a prepaid eSIM from Movistar?

Yes. Movistar sells a prepaid eSIM through the Mi Movistar app or a store, with entry tiers around 10 EUR for 40 GB. The catch is Spanish registration law: you must verify a passport before the profile is released, and a physical-to-eSIM swap can carry a one-time fee unless you use iPhone Quick Transfer.

Does Orange still exist after the MásMóvil merger?

Yes. Orange Spain merged with MásMóvil in 2024 to form MásOrange, but the Orange brand remains the main consumer face of the group, and Orange prepaid eSIMs are still sold under the Orange name through the My Orange app. The same group also runs Yoigo, Jazztel, Simyo, Pepephone, Lebara and Lycamobile.

Does a Spain eSIM work elsewhere?

Yes, by law. Under the EU Roam Like at Home rules (Regulation EU 2022/612, in force through 2032), a Spanish prepaid eSIM works across the EU and EEA at no extra cost within fair-use limits. For example, Orange’s Prepago 10 allows up to 26 GB of roaming out of its allowance. The United Kingdom is no longer covered automatically after Brexit, and Andorra and Gibraltar are outside the EU, so they may roam at a charge.

Is passport registration required for a Spain eSIM?

For a Spanish carrier or budget brand, yes. Every Spanish SIM, whether physical or eSIM, must be tied to a verified identity under national law, and a passport is the document tourists use. International travel eSIMs register under the provider’s home country instead, so they typically need no Spanish ID at activation. That registration waiver is one of the main reasons visitors choose a travel eSIM over a local one.

Which Spain carrier should you pick: Movistar vs Orange?

Movistar, on the evidence. It led Ookla’s 1H 2025 Spain report on 5G availability at 78.7% and on Consistency at 88.8%, the share of tests meeting a 5 Mbps down and 1 Mbps up threshold, which is the closest thing to a rural reliability measure. Yoigo borrows Movistar’s rural network through a roaming agreement, so Yoigo prepaid users get similar interior reach.

Do budget brands in Spain run their own networks?

No, they are brands that rent capacity from the four owners. Lowi runs on Vodafone, O2 on Movistar, and Simyo, Pepephone, Lebara and Lycamobile on the MásOrange group. Their coverage matches the parent network, but their registration rules can be stricter, and several ask for a Spanish ID and a Spanish card that a short-stay visitor may not have.

Which Spain carrier gives the most data for the money?

Yoigo, on a per-gigabyte basis. Its prepaid tiers run about 7 EUR for 7 GB up to 20 EUR for 50 GB, and it inherits Movistar’s rural roaming. Among the big three, Vodafone’s Prepago S at around 10 EUR for 90 GB is the most data for the money, while Orange’s Prepago 10 at around 10 EUR for 60 GB plus a bonus is the strongest all-round tourist tier.

Will my phone support Spain’s 5G bands?

If your phone supports European bands it will. Spain’s 5G uses n1, n3, n7, n28a (700 MHz), n78 (3.5 GHz) and n258 (26 GHz). The band that matters most outside cities is n28a, the low 700 MHz band that fills rural and indoor gaps; phones missing it still work in cities but lose the deepest coverage. Check your model on the compatibility list before you fly.

Should I keep my home SIM alongside the eSIM?

Yes, and it is a sensible setup. Run calls and verification on your home line and Spanish data on the eSIM. Assign data to the Spanish eSIM line and leave the home line for voice. If you plan to run a laptop on hotspot, check the plan’s tethering terms first. Two warnings apply: turn off roaming on the home line to avoid background charges, and expect verification texts to keep arriving on your home number rather than the data profile.

What happens on the eSIM chip once a Spanish profile loads

The swap you would once perform at a Movistar or Orange counter is now a download, because Spain uses the same embedded-SIM standard as everywhere else. The carrier pushes a profile onto the handset’s built-in chip, and the resulting line then behaves like any ordinary SIM in the phone. The transfer itself, including why a QR code survives only one scan, is explained in the eSIM activation explainer.

What to do when Spain eSIM activation fails
