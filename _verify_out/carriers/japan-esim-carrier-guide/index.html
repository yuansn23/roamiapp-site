Will my phone work on Japanese networks?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Japanese carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Does my model qualify for Japan eSIM service?

Japanese carriers build their 5G on specific bands, and the split matters more than in most countries. Rakuten Mobile, au and SoftBank all share the global mid-band n77, so almost any foreign 5G phone connects there. NTT Docomo adds its own n79 layer and leans on LTE Band 19 at 800 MHz for low-band coverage, and many North American and European handsets ship without those two. Those phones still work on Docomo, but they fall back to Bands 1 and 3 and lose signal in rural areas, mountain tunnels and the Shinkansen.

The Japanese band list looks intimidating and is mostly not worth learning. The safe bet is to match your exact model, the model number rather than the shop name, against the eSIM compatibility list. Want the theory before the practice? The explainer on what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile covers the load sequence.

Docomo vs au: which is better in Japan?

A locked phone is the most common reason a Japan eSIM install simply fails, and Japan has unusually clear rules here.

Under guidelines from the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications that took effect in September 2019, Japanese carriers must offer SIM-lock removal, and the process is free when done through the carrier’s online or phone channel. Phones sold since May 2015 are unlockable once any mandatory lock-in period, commonly around 100 to 180 days, has passed. MIC names eSIM on its own travel advice as a way to avoid roaming charges.

How to check: iPhone → Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock. Where the field says “No SIM restrictions”, the phone is free to take a profile. If the field shows “SIM locked”, no outside profile will load until the handset is freed.

Fix it: the operator that sold the handset is the one to ask for an unlock. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and there is no fee through the carrier’s self-serve channel. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a phone from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. Full detail: MIC — travelling with your device.

What Japan data plans cost and where to buy them

Four routes exist, and they differ more in paperwork than in price: au through povo for a true tourist eSIM, Docomo and SoftBank through resident brands, and Rakuten as a resident contract. Each route, step by step.

Sales channels for Japan eSIMs

Docomo’s own consumer eSIM is for domestic users: it wants a Japanese address and a residence card, and it rides on plans like ahamo, Gigaho or Gigalight. International visitors are pointed at the Docomo network through budget brands instead.

Reaching Docomo as a visitor, step by step:

  1. The cleanest tourist route is IIJmio Japan Travel SIM, which runs on Docomo’s 4G and 3G network. From 25 November 2025 it is sold at FamilyMart multifunction copiers nationwide.
  2. Pick a plan on the copier, pay at the register, and receive a slip with an entry code.
  3. Register on the dedicated website, enter the code, and download the eSIM profile over Wi-Fi.
  4. No residence card, no Japanese credit card, and the plans are data-only with no contract.

Docomo explicitly serves visitors this way rather than through its own app, because its own eSIM assumes a resident identity. Check your model before buying.

Does SoftBank sell a tourist eSIM for visitors?

SoftBank does not sell a direct prepaid eSIM to short-stay visitors. Its online budget brand LINEMO requires a residence card and a Japanese payment method, and its eSIM tiers are limited to permanent or special permanent residents. SoftBank’s own prepaid history is physical SIM and its Free Wi-Fi Passport, not a tourist eSIM.

For SoftBank network data as a visitor, the practical paths are a travel eSIM that rides on SoftBank, or a budget brand that leases its capacity. SoftBank remains worth targeting because Ookla rated it best for video and gaming in 2H 2024, which matters if you stream or play on the move.

What Japan eSIM plans cost

Rakuten Mobile is the fastest and most consistent network in Ookla’s 2H 2024 Japan data, but it is built for residents. Its eKYC wants a residence card or special permanent resident certificate, a Japanese address, and a Japanese-issued credit card or bank account. Overseas cards are rejected.

Rakuten’s SAIKYO Plan is genuinely cheap, about ¥1,078/mo up to 3 GB, ¥2,178/mo from 3 to 20 GB, and ¥3,278/mo above 20 GB with an unlimited tier, but the eligibility bar is the point. An ordinary tourist on a 90-day stay cannot clear it, so Rakuten reaches visitors through travel eSIM brands that lease its network rather than through Rakuten itself.

APN configuration in Japan

Most Japanese profiles configure themselves; these are the narrow cases that do not.

  • An aging handset that doesn’t collect carrier settings automatically
  • A profile installed with a manual activation code instead of a QR scan
  • A carrier-issued prepaid eSIM where automatic configuration did not run
  • Almost never on a travel eSIM, which is the point of a managed profile

Japan: when the APN won’t auto-load

  • iPhone: open Settings → Cellular → tap the eSIM line → Cellular Data Network → key in the APN
  • Android: Settings → Connections → Mobile Networks → Access Point Names → add a new APN

After saving, restart the phone. When data still refuses to work, confirm the Japanese profile owns the data line before touching other settings.

Four remedies for a stalling Japan eSIM

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our eSIM troubleshooting resource. Japan has its own set of four patterns, listed below.

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Carrier Lock showing No SIM restrictions means the phone is unlocked
  2. Check the QR code is clean and unscanned, most are single-use
  3. Ask whether the profile is queued on the carrier side

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status
  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → pick Docomo, au, SoftBank or Rakuten manually instead of automatic
  3. Do a network reset followed by a full restart

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Re-read the APN row for your carrier in the table
  2. Make sure the Japan eSIM is the line carrying data rather than your home SIM.
  3. Check you have not hit a fair-usage throttle; unlimited travel plans often drop to a floor after a daily cap

D. Dead zones on the train or in the mountains

  1. If you are on Docomo, confirm your phone supports Band 19; without it you fall back to Bands 1 and 3
  2. Switch to SoftBank or au, which use globally aligned low bands, or use a multi-network eSIM
  3. Download offline maps before the Shinkansen or a trailhead

Japan eSIM FAQ

Can I buy a tourist eSIM directly from NTT Docomo?

Not as a walk-up visitor. Docomo’s own eSIM rides on resident plans like ahamo and needs a Japanese address and residence card. Visitors reach Docomo’s network through IIJmio Japan Travel SIM at FamilyMart, or through a travel eSIM, both of which use Docomo capacity without a contract.

Does au sell a prepaid eSIM that visitors can buy?

Yes, and it is the only national carrier that does. au’s povo brand sells a tourist eSIM at Lawson from 1 July 2025, with plans from ¥2,200 for 3 GB over 7 days to ¥4,500 for unlimited over 7 days, paid in cash with no residence card. It connects to au’s network in about three minutes.

Can international visitors get a SoftBank eSIM?

Not directly. SoftBank’s LINEMO brand needs a residence card and a Japanese payment method, and its eSIM tiers are limited to permanent or special permanent residents. Visitors get SoftBank data through a travel eSIM that leases its network, which is where SoftBank’s best-in-class video and gaming scores are still useful.

Is Rakuten Mobile a good eSIM choice for a short visit?

Rakuten is the fastest and most consistent network in Ookla’s 2H 2024 data, but its own eSIM needs a residence card, a Japanese address and a Japanese card, so a short-stay tourist cannot sign up. Rakuten reaches visitors through travel eSIM brands that ride its network instead.

Docomo vs au: coverage compared

NTT Docomo, on the evidence. It led Ookla’s 5G availability at 43% and wins on breadth of coverage, especially in Hokkaido, Tohoku and the mountains, because its low-band layer uses Band 19. The catch is that many foreign phones lack Band 19, so they see Docomo’s coverage only on Bands 1 and 3. A multi-network eSIM avoids that trap.

Best-coverage network in Japan

Docomo routes its foundational coverage through Band 19 at 800 MHz and its 5G through n79 at 4.5 GHz, neither of which ships in most North American or European phones. Without those bands your device clings to Bands 1 and 3, which are urban layers, so tunnels, rural roads and ski resorts drop out. SoftBank is the most foreign-phone-friendly network because it uses the global Band 8 and n77.

The Japan SIM registration process

From 1 April 2026, yes, for almost everyone. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications requires all data SIM and eSIM buyers, tourists and residents alike, to show identification: a passport for tourists, a residence card for residents. The rule applies in stores and online, so travel eSIM checkouts now ask you to upload a passport photo before the profile downloads.

Should I buy from a Japanese carrier or use a travel eSIM?

Direct means one network, carrier-issued provisioning, and often a residence card or Japanese payment method; in exchange you get a real local number and monthly billing. A travel eSIM means instant delivery, no paperwork, automatic switching across Docomo, au, SoftBank and Rakuten, and a fixed upfront price. Short trip: travel eSIM. Living in Japan: direct.

Dealing with a locked phone in Japan

Ask the carrier it is locked to. Under MIC guidelines effective September 2019, carriers must offer SIM-unlock removal free through their online or phone channel, and phones sold since May 2015 are unlockable once any mandatory lock-in period, around 100 to 180 days, has passed. Unlock requests on paid-off handsets usually clear in hours. iPhone owners can read the lock state in Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock.

Checking IMEI and EID for Japan

It needs to be unlocked and eSIM-capable, and it helps if it supports the bands each carrier uses.
