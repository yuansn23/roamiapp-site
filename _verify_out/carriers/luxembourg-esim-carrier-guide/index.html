A Luxembourg eSIM may matter less than you think — the country is 82 km long, and most “which carrier” guides assume a place big enough that the answer varies by region. The honest opening line is: for many visitors, the carrier barely matters — and for EU arrivals it may not matter at all, because Roam Like at Home lets your home SIM work here at domestic rates. What follows explains when carrier choice is real (business travellers in Kirchberg, cross-border workers, non-EU visitors) and when it is not, plus the one rule that does affect everyone: Luxembourg’s prepaid-SIM registration law.

Quick answer: Tango if you are moving around Luxembourg, the local networks if you are staying in a single city. That split is backed by the speed, price and registration details below — and the compatibility page rules out phone issues first.

The one-paragraph answer: If you are arriving from another EU country, just use your existing SIM — Luxembourg is inside the Roam Like at Home zone and there is no surcharge. If you are from outside the EU, POST Luxembourg is the safe default (largest 5G footprint, own infrastructure), Tango rides the same POST network as a budget brand, and Orange is the price challenger. How Luxembourg’s three networks fit together

Luxembourg packs three mobile networks into a postage stamp: POST Luxembourg (the former LuxGSM, and the only operator with its own full infrastructure), Tango (a POST subsidiary that runs as an MVNO on POST’s network), and Orange Luxembourg (the independent third network). Because Tango and POST share towers, the “choice” between them is mostly about price, not coverage. That leaves three practical scenarios:

  • EU/EEA visitor: your home SIM already works here. Don’t buy anything. Carrier choice is irrelevant.
  • Non-EU visitor, short stay: pick POST for maximum reliability, or a travel eSIM to avoid the registration queue. Tango and Orange are valid cheaper alternatives on the same or comparable coverage.
  • Business / cross-border traveller: POST’s 5G in Kirchberg and the city matters; if you commute into Belgium, France or Germany daily, EU roaming fair-use is the thing to watch, not the local carrier.

What POST, Tango and Orange each offer

POST Luxembourg is the incumbent and the one every comparison lands on first. It holds the largest 5G spectrum allocation (the 3.6 GHz band) and covers Luxembourg City, the Kirchberg EU district, Esch-sur-Alzette and the airport on 5G, with 4G/LTE reaching over 99% of the population. It is the most expensive of the three but the most consistent.

Tango is owned by POST and uses POST’s infrastructure, so its coverage map is effectively POST’s. Its 5G is offered to prepaid customers, and its appeal is price — solid 4G for messaging, maps and social at a lower cost. It is not a separate network; it is a cheaper ticket onto POST’s.

Orange Luxembourg is the genuine independent alternative and is typically the most aggressively priced. It runs its own network with 4G LTE and 5G in the main towns, and is the network to compare if POST’s pricing puts you off.

There is no fourth meaningful player: the Grand Duchy is one of the smallest mobile markets in Europe, which is exactly why “which carrier” is a smaller question for a Luxembourg eSIM than almost anywhere else.

What each Luxembourg carrier charges

Because the networks barely differ, the real decision for a non-EU visitor is price and convenience. POST is the incumbent with the widest 5G footprint and the only fully owned infrastructure. Its prepaid offering is the most expensive but the most consistent, and POST is the one operator whose prepaid allowance is explicitly usable across the EU/EEA at domestic rates.

How Luxembourg’s 2017 SIM registration law works

Luxembourg abolished anonymous prepaid SIMs under the law of 7 June 2017, which the regulator Institut Luxembourgeois de Régulation (ILR) oversees. Anyone buying a prepaid card with a +352 number — resident or visitor — must register it in their own name against a valid ID (passport, national ID card or residence permit), either at the point of sale or online with a photo of the document. The operator switches the card off if registration is not completed. This applies to POST, Tango and Orange alike.

Two consequences for travellers:

  • A local prepaid SIM or eSIM is not a walk-up purchase. Expect to show a passport and have it recorded, whether you buy at a POST office, a supermarket, or an Orange store.
  • A data-only travel eSIM avoids the rule. Plans that carry no Luxembourg phone number are not caught by the registration requirement, so they install by QR code before you fly with no ID scan. That is the main reason visitors use them — not coverage, but paperwork.

Note: Luxembourg Findel Airport has no dedicated SIM counter, so arriving connected means either using your EU home SIM or installing a travel eSIM on the plane. POST and Orange stores are in the city; the airport itself points you to in-town outlets.

What happens to your plan at Luxembourg’s borders

Luxembourg sits inside the EU/EEA, so Roam Like at Home (EU Regulation 2022/612, guaranteed through 2032) applies to postpaid plans and, for POST at least, to prepaid too — POST prepaid customers may use their full allowance across the EU/EEA at domestic rates. Three things visitors miss:

  • Switzerland is not covered. RLAH stops at the EU/EEA border, and Switzerland — a common day trip from Luxembourg — is outside it. A local plan will bill roaming there, or you need a separate arrangement.
  • Cross-border workers cross twice a day. The fair-use policy is calibrated for exactly this; if you live in France/Belgium/Germany and work in Luxembourg (or vice versa), keep both numbers and let each handle its own side.
  • Border bleed. In Schengen, Wasserbillig, Rodange or Troisvierges, your phone can latch onto the German, French or Belgian network from across the border. On a Luxembourg-only travel eSIM this can drop you off-network — switch network selection to manual and pick Orange, Tango or POST to stay put.

Is a local SIM worth it in Luxembourg?

For a short stay the decision is rarely about coverage — it is about registration and price. Work it through:

  • EU/EEA visitor, under two weeks: your home SIM is free under RLAH. Buying anything local is the more expensive option. Do nothing.
  • Non-EU visitor, short stay: a local prepaid runs about €14–25 for a few GB plus the time cost of the in-person 2017 registration. A travel eSIM costs a similar or slightly higher per-GB rate but installs before you fly with no ID scan and no shop hunt — the convenience is the premium you pay.
  • Non-EU visitor, month-plus stay: the local prepaid wins on price per gigabyte once you are settled, especially POST prepaid, which roams the EU/EEA at domestic rates. The registration is a one-time errand.
  • Cross-border commuter: POST prepaid’s EU roaming is the differentiator; a travel eSIM that explicitly covers Belgium, France and Germany is the alternative if you never want a +352 number.

The break-even is roughly a fortnight: shorter, take the travel eSIM; longer, take the local prepaid. Either way, because Tango rides POST’s network, the coverage you get does not change with the cheaper brand.

What data speeds and prices look like in Luxembourg

  • Ookla Speedtest Global Index (August 2026): Luxembourg ranks 33rd in the world for fixed broadband at a median 212.17 Mbps and 6 ms latency — a fair proxy for the backhaul behind local mobile sites. (Mobile eSIMs ultimately ride this fibre.)
  • Cable.co.uk per-GB pricing: roughly USD 1.70 per GB, 145th of 237 markets — more expensive than most EU neighbours, reflecting Luxembourg’s high cost base.
  • DataReportal Digital 2025: 98.9% internet penetration and mobile connections at 137% of the population — near-saturated.

Luxembourg is one of Europe’s wealthiest countries, and mobile pricing tracks that: local prepaid starts around €14–15 for 5 GB over 30 days, and unlimited runs €25–35. EU visitors sidestep this entirely by roaming.

When do I need to enter an APN in Luxembourg?

If you bought a local POST, Tango or Orange eSIM, it auto-configures the APN on install in almost all cases — leave it alone. The one time you touch APN is on a multi-network travel eSIM, where the value is supplied in your activation email (examples seen in the market include plus for a Tango-backed profile and data.esim for a multi-network one). Enter it only if data fails after install:

  • iPhone: Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Network → enter the APN from your email.
  • Android: Settings → Network & Internet → Mobile Network → Access Point Names → add the APN.

Save, reboot, and confirm the eSIM is the selected data line before changing anything else.

What to sort out before you land in Luxembourg

The law of 7 June 2017 is a counter rule rather than an airport one, and Findel has no SIM desk at all — so the list below is about landing with the right document, card and expectation for a +352 line.

  • EID — dial *#06# to see it. A travel eSIM wants the number before it ships; a local card is only issued after the registration step.
  • An unlocked handset — ask the carrier the phone is locked to, since many unlock on request; on iPhone, Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock shows “No SIM restrictions” when clear.
  • The original ID document — the 2017 law reads a genuine passport, national ID or residence permit at purchase. Copies and phone photos are refused.
  • A card your bank will not freeze abroad — Luxembourg terminals occasionally reject non-EU billing, and the sums are small enough that cash is a perfectly good answer.
  • Install any travel eSIM before you fly — Findel has no SIM shop, and in-town POST and Orange branches keep weekday hours.
  • Decide whether you need +352 at all — EU/EEA arrivals can simply roam on the home line; only non-EU visitors with a genuine need should face the registration queue.
  • Data Roaming on for the eSIM — required even inside the EU; the setup list below explains why the profile stays dark without it.
  • Maps cached for the Ardennes and the Moselle — the north and the valley road genuinely drop out; save routes while you still have Wi-Fi.

The three faults a Luxembourg eSIM runs into

A code that will not scan or a profile stuck on “activating” is not a Luxembourg story — those belong to the troubleshooting guide, with the plain install sequence in how to activate an eSIM. The failures below are the ones this particular corner of Europe causes.

Is 10 GB of data enough in Luxembourg?

For anything short of a streaming-heavy month, yes. Local prepaid tiers run from about 5 GB on a POST starter to 15 GB for a month on Orange’s Hello card, so 10 GB sits comfortably mid-range for maps, messaging and browsing, with city Wi-Fi absorbing the heavy use. The one variable is roaming: data burned outside Luxembourg on a local line falls under the EU fair-use rules rather than the headline allowance.

Your Luxembourg eSIM hits zero: next steps

Luxembourg’s prepaid market is small enough that top-up is thin. The Orange Hello card wants credit bought in €5, €10, €20 or €30 steps, each stretching the balance’s validity to three months, and a non-EU billing card is sometimes refused at the terminal doing the charging.

Load more than you expect to need before a long stay, or confirm in the operator app that a foreign card is accepted. Running out mid-trip in a market this size means finding a shop, not tapping a button.

Why your phone grabs a foreign network at the border

Along the Moselle, at Schengen, Wasserbillig or Rodange, German, French and Belgian masts are often the loudest signal available, and a handset left on automatic will take them. On a Luxembourg-only profile that means dropping off-network entirely.

Force the decision yourself: Settings → Cellular → Network Selection, switch to manual, and hold POST, Tango or Orange until you are well inside the Grand Duchy again.

If none of the three fits, work through the generic list in the activation-error guide.

Luxembourg eSIM FAQ

POST vs Tango: which is better in Luxembourg?

Usually not. The country is 82 km long, Tango runs on POST’s network, and EU visitors can roam for free. Carrier choice matters mainly for non-EU short-stay visitors (POST for reliability, Orange for price) and business travellers who need Kirchberg 5G.

Can I use my home EU SIM in Luxembourg?

Yes. Luxembourg is in the Roam Like at Home zone, so an EU/EEA SIM works at domestic rates with no surcharge. The exception is Switzerland, which is outside RLAH.

Do Luxembourg SIM purchases require ID?

A local prepaid SIM or eSIM with a +352 number, yes — under the 2017 law, against a passport or ID, in person or online. A data-only travel eSIM with no local number is exempt, which is why visitors prefer it.

Is there a SIM counter at Luxembourg airport?

No. Findel Airport has no dedicated SIM shop. Use your EU home SIM, or install a travel eSIM before you land; POST and Orange stores are in the city.

POST vs Tango 5G: which is better in Luxembourg?

POST Luxembourg holds the largest 5G spectrum and the widest city coverage; Orange is the comparable independent; Tango offers POST’s 5G at a lower price as a POST brand.

APN values for POST and Tango eSIMs

Local POST/Tango/Orange eSIMs auto-configure. Only a multi-network travel eSIM may need a manual APN — use the value in your activation email (e.g. plus or data.esim).

Will my eSIM work in the Ardennes or Mullerthal?

Yes on 4G in villages and on marked trails; signal thins on minor valley roads and off-trail gorges. Download offline maps before hiking.

Picking a Luxembourg eSIM by trip type

City-only, any of the three — or just your EU home SIM. Kirchberg business trips favour POST or Orange; the Ardennes, Mullerthal, Vianden and the Moselle road favour POST’s rural reach; and a Switzerland day trip needs a plan that names it, because Roam Like at Home stops at the border. Can a travel eSIM make calls and receive SMS?

Most data-only travel eSIMs cannot — they carry data only. Keep your home SIM active for calls and verification texts, and run data on the eSIM. If you need a Luxembourg number, only a registered local prepaid gives you one.

Is there SIM registration in Luxembourg?

Yes. The registration law targets SIMs and eSIMs that carry a +352 number. A data-only travel eSIM that connects to POST/Tango/Orange without issuing a local number is outside that requirement, so it installs by QR code with no passport scan.

Which network has the best 5G in Luxembourg?

No. 5G is broad in Luxembourg City, Kirchberg, Esch-sur-Alzette and the airport, but it thins to 4G in the Ardennes, the Moselle valley and the north. For most visitors 4G is fast enough for maps, calls and streaming; pick a plan on coverage where you are going, not on 5G alone.

Can I use a travel eSIM and my home SIM together?

Yes. Keep your home number on the physical SIM for calls and SMS, and run data on the eSIM — just switch Data Roaming off on the home line so you are not billed for background traffic while abroad.

What happens to my eSIM after I leave?

A travel eSIM simply stops being useful once its data or validity runs out; delete the profile or let it lapse. A local prepaid registered under the 2017 law keeps its +352 number only while topped up — POST/Tango/Orange credit validity is typically three months per top-up, after which the line is recycled.

Adding data to a Luxembourg eSIM

Most travel eSIMs are prepaid and fixed — when the data or days run out you buy a new plan rather than top up. Some multi-network profiles (the HelloRoam-style ones) do allow in-app top-ups; check the activation email or the provider’s app before you rely on it for a longer stay.

Is public Wi-Fi reliable in Luxembourg?

In Luxembourg City, the airport, trains and major museums, yes — free Wi-Fi is common and decent. Elsewhere, especially in the Ardennes and along the Moselle, do not count on it. Treat Wi-Fi as a backup and keep the eSIM for maps, translation and ride-hailing.

Which carrier should you pick: POST vs Tango?

POST for reliability and the widest reach, Tango for the same towers at a lower price, Orange when price leads and you stay in the towns. EU arrivals can skip all three and roam on their home line. The trip table above is the tiebreaker when the itinerary leaves the city.
