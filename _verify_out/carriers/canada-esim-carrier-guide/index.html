Will my phone work on Canadian networks?

Three things decide whether your phone works with a Canada eSIM: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Which phones are eSIM-locked out of Canada?

Canadian carriers build their 5G on specific bands — notably n71 (600 MHz) for rural reach, plus n66 and n78 in cities. Plenty of international handsets, including several iPhone regional variants, ship without n71. Those phones still work: they fall back to LTE once you leave dense urban areas, which is fine downtown and frustrating in the mountains.

You do not need to learn the band tables. The reliable move is to check your exact model, model number not marketing name, against the compatibility lookup tool. If you want the underlying mechanics first, what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile explains the process.

Unlocking your phone before your Canada trip

A locked handset is the single most common reason a Canadian eSIM install fails — and Canada is unusually generous about clearing it.

Under the CRTC’s Wireless Code, phones sold by Canadian service providers must be unlocked, and if a device is locked to a provider’s network, that provider must “unlock the device, or give the customer the means to unlock the device, upon request, at no charge.” That requirement — Rule F.1.(ii) — took effect December 1, 2017. The CRTC also names eSIM directly on its own travel advice as a way to avoid roaming charges.

Where to look: Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock on iPhone. If it reads “No SIM restrictions,” you are clear. If it reads “SIM locked,” no third-party eSIM will install until that changes.

Fix it: contact the carrier the phone is locked to and request an unlock. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and there is no fee. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone before December 2017, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. Full detail: CRTC — travelling with your device.

Manual APN values for Canada

Automatic configuration handles Canadian carrier profiles in almost every case; this list covers the exceptions.

Four steps to fix a Canada eSIM

General activation errors — a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers — are covered in our eSIM troubleshooting guide. The four failure patterns below are specific to Canada.

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock: “No SIM restrictions” is the only acceptable reading
  2. Confirm the code is still fresh – a used QR never installs
  3. Check with the carrier if the profile is still pending

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status
  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → select Bell, Rogers or TELUS manually instead of automatic
  3. Wipe the network settings, then restart

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Look the APN up again in the carrier table above
  2. Point mobile data at the eSIM line, not your home SIM
  3. Check you have not exhausted your data allowance — Lucky Mobile and similar prepaid brands throttle to 128 Kbps rather than stopping

D. “SOS” or “Emergency calls only”

  1. Check the SIM manager shows the eSIM as enabled
  2. Disable or delete any other active SIM or eSIM profile
  3. Reboot and reset network settings
  4. As a last resort, remove the profile and reinstall from a fresh QR code

Can I keep my home number active alongside a Canadian eSIM?
