What Myanmar’s internet restrictions mean for your trip

Four dated facts frame everything else on this page:

  • Mid-2024: authorities blocked Signal and the major VPNs, closing the most common workarounds travellers used to reach blocked services.
  • January 2025: Myanmar’s Cybersecurity Law codified censorship mandates, restricting VPN providers and imposing local data-retention requirements.
  • Throughout 2025: civil-society tracking at the Myanmar Internet Project logged scores of localised internet cuts across townships in most of the country’s states and regions — often with no notice and no schedule.
  • Ongoing: UN reporting documents network cuts clustering around conflict-affected areas with little notice — the late-2025 background paper has the detail.

The practical translation for a traveller: your eSIM is a local data connection on a monitored, occasionally interrupted network. Plan around outages rather than pretending they will not happen.

All prices below are in Myanmar kyat (MMK), sourced from Myanmar Tech Press’s monthly operator price tracking, which records the operators’ own published rates (≈ 2,100 MMK to US$1 on the parallel market).

MPT plans for the rural and temple routes

Best for: Maximum reach — rural trunk roads, temple regions, and anywhere past the city limits.

Market position: MPT is the country’s original operator, with a legacy footprint that follows the trunk roads and the temple zones where newer entrants thin out. If your itinerary spends more time outside Yangon and Mandalay than in them, MPT is the network you want attached.

Airport counters also sell cheap tourist starter packs combining a small data bucket with calling credit — handy for the arrival day, with larger packs to follow.

The visitor’s buying process, step by step:

  1. Go to an MPT counter at Yangon (RGN) or Mandalay airport, or any MPT shop.
  2. Present your passport and visa/entry stamp — registration is done on the spot.
  3. Choose a data pack; pay in MMK (cash or card at official counters).
  4. Insert the SIM or scan the eSIM QR; the profile registers against your passport.
  5. Top up via USSD *682# or the MPT app when the pack runs out.

Quirks: MPT is the only network worth betting on in Bagan and Inle; its 4G is solid in towns and thins to 3G on rural B-roads.

Will my home SIM’s roaming work in Myanmar?

Most visitors arrive on the official e-visa (processing typically three working days; visa-on-arrival has been suspended for many nationalities). The practical connectivity note: your home SIM’s roaming will not work in Myanmar — international roaming onto local networks ceased after 2021 — so do not count on landing with data. At Yangon International the MPT, ATOM and U9 counters sit in the arrivals halls and register a SIM against your passport in minutes, which is the most reliable on-the-ground route. A travel eSIM installed before you fly is the lower-friction alternative: it activates on the local network the moment you land and skips the passport-upload queue, though it still inherits the national blocking and shutdown risk above. Either way, arrive with a plan already chosen — the arrival hall Wi-Fi is congested and a shutdown can blank the network without warning.

How fast is a Myanmar eSIM, and what does data cost?

We quote only what is sourced, and we do not invent per-carrier mobile speeds.

  • Fixed broadband proxy: Ookla’s Speedtest Global Index places Myanmar’s median fixed download at 24.64 Mbps, 138th globally, with 35 ms latency (global median 129.68 Mbps). Fixed-line is a reasonable proxy for the backhaul behind local mobile sites.
  • Per-GB price: Cable.co.uk prices 1 GB of Burmese mobile data at about USD 0.99, 89th of 237 markets — cheap by global standards.
  • Market size: DataReportal counts 33.4 million internet users (61.1% of the population) and 63.3 million mobile connections (116%) entering 2025.
  • Mobile speed: We deliberately omit a national mobile-median figure — Ookla does not publish a clean per-country mobile rank for Myanmar in the sources we could verify, and per-carrier mobile benchmarks are unreliable post-2021. Treat any single-operator “fastest” claim you see elsewhere with suspicion.

Troubleshooting a Myanmar eSIM: what actually fails here

A Myanmar eSIM that will not work is rarely a broken SIM. The install sequence — add the profile, scan, label the line, enable roaming — is covered in our eSIM activation walkthrough, and the standard fault list sits in the eSIM problem-solving guide. In this country, four very specific situations account for nearly every failure, and none of them is the phone’s fault.

Myanmar eSIM shows full bars but nothing loads: a local shutdown

This is the signature of a localised shutdown rather than a fault in your eSIM. Independent trackers log cuts township by township, and they arrive with no notice and no schedule. The tells are consistent: the signal indicator is healthy, every carrier is equally dead, nothing you change makes any difference, and service returns on its own. Do not keep toggling settings or buying top-ups — wait it out, move on, or fall back on whatever you downloaded in advance. In a border or conflict township the same reading means there is simply no network to attach to, and that is the one problem no settings screen can solve.

Does Myanmar require SIM registration?

Every Myanmar number, eSIM included, is registered in person against a passport and a visa or entry stamp. There is no foreigner self-signup with document upload the way there is in the Maldives or Thailand, so a profile that arrives without a completed registration will never attach. Airport counters at Yangon and Mandalay, operator stores and authorised kiosks are the routes — bring both documents and expect it to be finished on the spot.

Why your home SIM shows no service in Myanmar

This is not a broken handset. International roaming onto Myanmar networks stopped working after 2021, so a home SIM expecting full-strength service will read “no service” from the moment you land. A locally registered SIM or a travel eSIM activated by its provider is the only data path; no amount of settings work revives roaming here. Leave the home SIM switched on for SMS verification codes, with its data roaming firmly off.

APN values for ATOM, MPT and Mytel eSIMs

Myanmar’s operators use different APNs — mptnet on MPT, internet on ATOM and on U9, mytel on Mytel — and a hand-added profile can easily point at the wrong one, especially if a SIM has moved between operators. Compare the APN screen against the table above, then confirm the eSIM, not the home SIM, is the line carrying mobile data.

The registration counter experience

  • Passport plus visa or entry stamp. Both are checked together at registration; neither alone is accepted.
  • In person, at a counter or store. Yangon (RGN) and Mandalay (MDL) arrival counters handle MPT, ATOM and U9, and registration happens on the spot.
  • No online foreigner route. Unlike the Maldives, there is no document-upload signup — which is precisely why travel eSIMs, registered by the provider on your behalf, are the lower-friction option.
  • Kyat in hand. Kiosk top-ups are cash-first and foreign cards are refused often.
  • Tourist starter packs suit arrival day. MPT’s counter pack pairs a small data bucket with calling credit, which is handy before you have sorted a longer-validity top-up.
  • The number stays tied to you. Registration attaches the SIM to your identity for as long as it is active, with no anonymous option and no way to hand it on.

VPNs in Myanmar: what a VPN will and will not do

Restrictions on VPN use were codified in the January 2025 cybersecurity law and enforcement is real, so we do not recommend leaning on one to reach blocked services. A VPN can obscure traffic; it cannot conjure a connection where the network has been switched off.

MPT counter, ATOM store, or travel eSIM?

  • MPT airport counter: queue time after large arrivals is the usual snag, and rural B-roads thin to 3G once you leave the trunk routes.
  • ATOM store or eSIM: registration is instant, but the network is strongest on the tourist corridor and weaker well outside it.
  • Travel eSIM: no documents to upload, yet it still inherits national blocking, so a shutdown takes it offline exactly as it would a local SIM.

We are not a security adviser, but the factual picture is documented at the top of this page: Freedom House rates Myanmar 9/100 for internet freedom, the VPN ban is codified in law, and SIM data feeds surveillance systems. Civil-society monitoring at the Myanmar Internet Project publishes township-level tracking of cuts, and local contacts usually know faster than any map. Can I use my eSIM and home SIM together?

Yes. Keep your home SIM for calls/SMS (disable its data roaming to avoid charges) and run data on the eSIM. Most data-only eSIMs cannot receive SMS, so verification codes still arrive on your home number.
