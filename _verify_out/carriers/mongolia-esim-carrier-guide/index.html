A Mongolia eSIM starts with distance, not brand: how far outside Ulaanbaatar you are going decides everything, and this guide works through the Unitel versus Mobicom call with the airport detail others miss. Mongolia is the country where “which carrier is best” is the wrong first question — the right one is “how far outside Ulaanbaatar am I going, and for how many days.” The capital has fast, cheap, genuinely modern mobile internet; the Gobi, the Altai and the Khövsgöl shorelines operate on a different physics entirely, where coverage means the nearest ger camp and not the trailhead. This guide covers the Unitel vs Mobicom decision with the airport-tourist-eSIM detail other articles miss, the registration rules, and a region-by-region plan for the world’s least densely populated country. This is the Mongolia version of the talk we give friends the night before they fly, stripped of jargon.

Quick answer: Pick Unitel for the widest footprint, Mobicom for the lowest cost per gigabyte, and install your eSIM before departure either way. The rest of this guide — Mongolia speeds, prices and registration rules — fills in the detail behind that split.

If you have not checked your phone yet, the compatibility list settles that in one lookup — unlocked, eSIM-capable handsets only.

The short version: Unitel is Mongolia’s coverage leader — the network locals recommend for anything beyond the capital, with the widest rural reach. Mobicom is the strong number two in Ulaanbaatar and, since July 2025, hands arriving tourists a free 1 GB eSIM QR code at Chinggis Khaan International Airport — the easiest tourist welcome gift in Asian telecom. G-Mobile and Skytel fill niche rural roles. Registration requires a passport, so the friction-free route remains an eSIM installed before you fly. And for the Gobi or western Altai: no eSIM choice changes the map — you download offline maps and treat coverage as a town-level service.

Can you buy a Mongolia eSIM directly from Unitel or Mobicom?

Mobicom — yes, and it is the friendliest route in the market. Since July 2025 arriving tourists can collect a free 1 GB eSIM at Chinggis Khaan International Airport counters and Mobicom branches, with paid tourist top-ups available online afterwards. Passport registration is handled at the counter.

Unitel — yes, at city stores. Unitel sells prepaid lines with passport registration; a store visit in Ulaanbaatar is the practical route, and it is the right answer if you want the network with the widest rural footprint as your own line.

G-Mobile and Skytel — not a general visitor product. They matter when an itinerary names a far-western province, where neither giant has full coverage.

Mongolia coverage: what the coverage map does not tell you

Three things worth internalizing before you plan connectivity:

  • Distance is the enemy, not terrain alone. A ger camp 40 km from a provincial capital can be dark while the capital has five bars. Signal is a town service that leaks a little into the surrounding countryside.

  • Tourist camps cluster where coverage exists. The camps that host foreigners are usually the ones that chose a site with signal, or installed Starlink. That is why “the middle of nowhere” often has Wi-Fi at dinner and nothing at breakfast on the road.

  • Your battery matters more than your bars. Long days in a vehicle with the phone hunting for a network is the fastest way to a dead handset. Carry a power bank, and turn the eSIM line off in known dead zones rather than letting it search.

One modern fact changes the remote-travel calculus: Starlink service works in Mongolia and is increasingly used by tour operators and ger camps for backhaul Wi-Fi. Like in Kenya, it does not replace your Mongolia eSIM — it explains why the middle-of-nowhere camp still has Wi-Fi at dinner. And arriving via the airport, note that UBN sits roughly 50 km from the city — if you land late with nothing installed, the free Mobicom tourist eSIM at arrivals is the fastest fix.

How to set up a Mongolia eSIM

Install your Mongolia eSIM on home Wi-Fi and it finds Unitel or Mobicom the moment you clear Chinggis Khaan arrivals:

  1. Start with the phone compatibility list; unlocked and eSIM-capable is the bar.

  2. Label the new line “Mongolia data” and complete the install on home Wi-Fi.

  3. Set it as your mobile-data line and switch on data roaming for that line — travel eSIMs roam onto Unitel or Mobicom by design.

  4. Land at UBN and let the profile attach; if it does not, select the network manually.

  5. Keep your home SIM active in the physical slot for calls and verification codes.

Mobicom’s free tourist eSIM at the airport

  1. Head to the Mobicom counter in arrivals — the scheme has run since July 2025.

  2. Register with your passport on the spot.

  3. Collect the free 1 GB eSIM QR code, or a physical SIM if you prefer.

  4. Top up online if you need more; the free gigabyte is a landing buffer, not a trip allowance.

  5. Remember UBN is roughly 50 km from central Ulaanbaatar — get connected before you take the road in.

Buying Unitel or Mobicom in Ulaanbaatar

  1. Bring your passport for registration.

  2. Choose a prepaid bundle — local data is genuinely cheap.

  3. Test data at the counter before leaving.

  4. Ask which network the operator recommends for the specific aimags on your route if you are going west.

Before you buy: confirm the phone is unlocked, note your EID (*#06#), and install any pre-purchased profile before departure — the profile activates when it first meets a Mongolian tower, not when you scan the code. Data-only travel eSIMs give no Mongolian number, so messaging happens over WhatsApp or Telegram, both of which work normally on the local networks.

Three checks before you buy anything

Three steps, each answering a question that decides whether a Mongolian profile is even an option:

  • Dial *#06#. An EID means the hardware is there.

  • iPhone: Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock — you want “No SIM restrictions”.

  • Android: the SIM manager is in Settings -> Connections. Greyed out? The phone is either locked or too old.

Where to type the APN on your phone

On iPhone the field lives at Settings → Cellular → [the line] → Cellular Data Network; on Android it is at Settings → Network & Internet → Access Point Names. Restart after saving, then confirm the Mongolia profile — not your home line — is the one set for data.

Activating on Unitel, Mobicom and Gobi

  1. Manual network selection helps at the edges. Outside the towns, forcing the phone to a specific operator (Settings → Network Selection) can attach where automatic search gives up.

  2. No signal is usually geography, not configuration. Before troubleshooting, check whether you are simply outside every footprint — the region table above is the sanity check.

  3. The profile may take a minute to register after a long drive. Coming out of a dead zone, give the phone time before assuming the eSIM has failed.

  4. Signal but no data: data-line setting, roaming toggle, then APN — the full sequence is in our eSIM problem-solving guide.

Four Mongolia eSIM failure fixes

A. The eSIM will not install

  1. The handset must be lock-free; Carrier Lock under About proves it with “No SIM restrictions”

  2. The QR needs to be sharp and unused – rescans fail

  3. Ask the provider to verify the profile was really pushed

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Confirm you are inside a coverage area — check the region table

  2. Settings → Cellular → Network Selection → choose Unitel or Mobicom manually

  3. Toggle the line off and on, then reboot

C. Signal bars, but no internet

  1. Put the APN back in precisely as the table lists it

  2. Verify data is routed to the eSIM, not your home SIM

  3. Turn data roaming on for this specific line

  4. Make sure the plan data bucket is not spent

D. Worked yesterday, dead today

  1. You probably moved — recheck the coverage map for your new position

  2. Toggle airplane mode to force a fresh network scan

  3. Select the network manually rather than leaving it on automatic

How to plan Mongolia eSIM data for an overland route

Mongolia’s long-distance travel rewards a little planning in a way that city trips do not. Work through this before you leave Ulaanbaatar:

Question Why it matters
How many nights are outside a provincial capital? Each one is a potential full offline day
Does your driver carry a satellite phone or radio? Local operators often do — ask before you assume
Which operator does your tour company use? Their routes are built around one network’s coverage
Is there ger camp Wi-Fi anywhere on the route? Increasingly yes, often Starlink-backed
What is the longest stretch with no settlement? That number is your offline stretch, not your plan’s data allowance

Five rules for the overland legs

  1. Download before you leave the capital. Every leg of the route, plus bookings and any tickets, as offline screenshots.

  2. Accept that connectivity is a town-level service. Two days on the road will contain hours of no signal on any carrier.

  3. Charge at every opportunity. A phone searching for a network in a moving vehicle drains faster than one sitting on a good signal.

  4. Set a daily check-in. If someone at home expects to hear from you, tell them which days you will be dark.

  5. Switch the eSIM line off in confirmed dead zones. Letting it hunt for a network wastes battery you cannot recharge.

How many gigabytes does a week in Mongolia burn?

Mongolian phone plans are cheap, but rural bandwidth is not generous. Turn off automatic photo and video uploads for the duration of the trip and sync in towns instead. A single evening of backup-to-cloud over a congested provincial 4G cell can cost more time and data than the whole week’s maps put together.

Does a Mongolia eSIM need registration?

Will my eSIM work outside Ulaanbaatar?

In the towns — Dalanzadgad and the provincial centers — yes. Between the towns, or on a multi-day overland leg, no. Plan connectivity as a town-level service, download offline maps in Ulaanbaatar, and carry a satellite messenger for remote routes.

In Ulaanbaatar, yes — 5G has been awarded to the major operators and is expanding in the capital. Outside it, plan around 4G and honest gaps.

Who has the widest reach in Mongolia?

You will not call from open country on any carrier. Guides on organized trips carry radios; independent travelers on long overland routes should carry a satellite messenger and file a route plan. How does the airport arrival actually work at UBN?

Land, clear immigration, and either install your pre-purchased eSIM and switch on data roaming, or visit the Mobicom counter for the free tourist eSIM. Ignore any temptation to wait until Ulaanbaatar: the airport is roughly 50 km out, and you want the profile working before the drive in.

Is a travel eSIM better than a local SIM in Mongolia?

For a short trip, yes — a Mongolia eSIM bought online skips registration and works on landing. For a stay measured in months, a local Unitel or Mobicom line is cheaper per gigabyte and gives you a Mongolian number. The decision is about trip length, not network quality.

What happens if my eSIM does not work in Ulaanbaatar?

The capital has four networks and dense coverage, so a failure there is almost always configuration: check the data line, the roaming toggle, then network selection. If it works in the city and fails on the road, that is geography rather than a fault.

Unitel eSIM plans for visitors

Mobicom’s tourist product and most travel eSIM providers sell before you fly; Unitel’s prepaid lines are generally bought in person. The online route is the one that involves no local interaction at all.

Does a Mongolian number matter for anything?

Very little beyond local bureaucracy. Camps, drivers and tour operators coordinate by phone number, but they will use whatever number you give them — and WhatsApp over data covers everything after that first contact.
