MEO vs NOS: which is better in Portugal?

Portugal has four national mobile networks. The three legacy operators, MEO, NOS and Vodafone, share the market with Digi, a low-cost entrant that now runs its own infrastructure rather than renting capacity. All four sell prepaid service, and all four support eSIM on at least some plans.

Is your phone ready for a Portugal carrier eSIM?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Portuguese carrier: its bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Will my model work with Portugal eSIM service?

Portuguese carriers build their 5G on specific bands, led by n78 at 3.5 GHz for city speed, with n1 and n3 for wider coverage and n28 at 700 MHz plus n20 at 800 MHz for rural and indoor reach. On 4G the workhorses are B3 at 1800 MHz, B7 at 2600 MHz, B20 at 800 MHz and B1 at 2100 MHz. Most modern international handsets already support these, so a visitor from North America or Asia is rarely blocked outright.

No one needs the band tables by heart. Model number beats marketing name, so look yours up in eSIM compatibility page. eSIM activation mechanics unpacks the process, useful reading while you plan the Camino.

Will your phone work on Portugal networks?

A locked phone is the most common reason a Portugal eSIM install simply fails. Portugal itself has no blanket unlock mandate for visiting phones, so the lock status is set by whatever carrier sold your device at home.

Check it: iPhone to Settings, then General, then About, then Carrier Lock. The words “No SIM restrictions” mean you can skip this worry entirely. If it reads SIM locked, no Portuguese profile will install until that changes.

Fix it: approach the operator that supplied the phone; Portuguese sellers are required to unlock on request. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and many operators do it at no charge through their self-serve channel. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a phone on a plan from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. A locked handset will reject a local card or profile at the counter.

Portugal eSIM plans and prices compared

Four routes to a Portugal eSIM exist, and they differ more in paperwork than in price: MEO online, NOS and Vodafone in-store, Digi for the budget minded, and the travel eSIM that skips the counter. Each route, step by step:

Where to buy a Portugal eSIM online

MEO is the only one of the big three that lets a visitor finish everything online before flying. It is the legacy national operator, formerly Portugal Telecom, and it still holds the broadest physical tower footprint in the country.

MEO eSIM, step by step:

  1. Go to meo.pt and open the Turistas or eSIM section, then pick a tourist plan.
  2. Upload passport or ID scans when prompted and pay by card.
  3. An activation QR code arrives by email, typically within five to ten minutes.
  4. Scan it from your phone settings. Service activates within one to two minutes.

MEO’s online flow is the fastest way to land with a working Portuguese number and no airport queue, which is why it leads this guide for visitors who hate errands.

Does NOS sell eSIMs online to visitors?

NOS does not yet offer a fully online tourist eSIM purchase for new customers. Its prepaid eSIM is activated at a store or kiosk, where you present your passport and the assistant generates a QR code on screen or on paper.

For a NOS profile, the newcomer path is a visit: find a Loja NOS in a shopping centre or an arrivals-hall kiosk at Lisbon or Porto, show your passport, and scan the code within one to three minutes. NOS carries Ookla’s Best Mobile Network title for 1H 2025, so the trade-off is real convenience for a strong network.

Where can you buy a Vodafone Portugal eSIM?

Vodafone Portugal follows the same in-store pattern as NOS for visitors. Its kiosks sit in Terminal 1 at Lisbon airport, in Porto arrivals, and at Faro, and its prepaid eSIM is issued after a passport check.

Vodafone’s edge is balance: it ranked first on 5G availability at 80.3 percent and first on consistency in the Ookla mobile charts, and it is the most prominently stocked brand at airport counters, which makes it the default pickup for many arriving travelers.

What about Digi and the budget brands?

Digi is the fourth national network and the price disruptor. It sells prepaid plans at aggressive rates, but its own coverage is still building outside Lisbon and Porto, and its eSIM support is rolling out rather than universal. For a short city break it can be excellent value; for anywhere remote, it is a gamble.

The other budget brands, Lycamobile on MEO capacity, UZO and Woo, tend to target residents and can ask for a Portuguese tax number that blocks a quick tourist sign-up. Treat them as backup options rather than your first choice.

Madeira mirrors this: Funchal and the southern shore are well served, while the rugged northern roads and the high central plateau see patchier service on any carrier.

What goes wrong with Portugal eSIMs specifically?

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our complete eSIM troubleshooting guide. The patterns below are the Portugal-specific ones.

A. The MEO online QR code never arrives. Email delivery can lag five to ten minutes, and airport spam filters are aggressive. Check spam first, then re-upload your passport if the order stalled at verification.

B. NOS or Vodafone will not issue a profile remotely. These two still want you in person. If a website claims an online tourist eSIM for them, it is usually a travel brand reselling access, not the operator itself, so expect a store or kiosk step.

C. Signal bars but no internet in the Alentejo or on a mountain road. You are on a weak low-band fringe. Switch network selection to manual and pick MEO or Vodafone, which hold rural reach better than NOS in the interior.

D. Dropouts while hiking in Madeira or the Azores. The volcanic terrain blocks radio waves. Download offline maps before a levada or crater walk, because no carrier is flawless on the high plateau or the smaller islands.

E. Data dies after crossing into Spain on the Camino Portugués. The Portuguese Way of the Camino de Santiago runs north through rural Trás-os-Montes and Minho, then crosses into Galicia. On a single Portuguese carrier, rural northern stretches favor MEO or Vodafone, and once you cross the border the EU Roam Like at Home rules keep your data working with no extra charge, so a national Portuguese SIM is genuinely useful all the way to Santiago.

Buying a Portugal eSIM from the carriers

Can I buy a tourist eSIM directly from MEO?

Yes, and MEO is the easiest of the legacy operators for a visitor. Its online Turistas flow takes a passport upload and a card payment, then emails a QR code you scan before you fly, so you land with a working Portuguese number and no airport queue.

Does NOS sell a prepaid eSIM that visitors can buy?

Not fully online yet. NOS issues its prepaid eSIM at a store or airport kiosk after a passport check, which is why it ranks a step behind MEO for convenience even though Ookla named it the Best Mobile Network in Portugal for 1H 2025.

Can international visitors get a Vodafone eSIM?

Yes, at a Vodafone kiosk or store with your passport. Vodafone is the most visible brand at Lisbon, Porto and Faro airports and it led Ookla’s 1H 2025 mobile charts on 5G availability at 80.3 percent and on consistency, so the in-person step buys you a strong network.

Is Digi a good eSIM choice for a short visit?

Digi is the price disruptor and it posted the best mobile gaming score at 82.23 in Ookla’s data, but its own coverage is still building outside Lisbon and Porto and its eSIM support is rolling out. Choose it for a city stay where price matters more than reach.

Which network covers Portugal best?

MEO and Vodafone, on the field evidence. The Alentejo interior, the Serra da Estrela and remote northern valleys hold signal better on those two because of broader low-band infrastructure, while NOS, strong in cities, can fade on quiet secondary roads.

Do tourists need to register SIMs in Portugal?

Yes. Since 2021 Portuguese law requires identification at the moment any SIM or eSIM is activated, and ANACOM, the national regulator, enforces this so a store or online flow will capture your passport number. Prepaid does not require a local tax number, but postpaid contracts do.

Will my Portugal eSIM work in the Azores and Madeira?

Yes. The Azores and Madeira are Portuguese territory, so a mainland profile works there as a domestic connection with no roaming charge. Coverage is strong on the main islands and in the towns, but the smaller Azorean islands and the volcanic interiors are patchier, with MEO reaching the outermost spots best.

Will your Portugal eSIM work abroad?

Because Portugal sits inside the EU, any Portuguese SIM or eSIM uses the Roam Like at Home rules, so your allowance of calls, texts and data works across the other EU and EEA countries at domestic rates, subject to a fair-use data cap. A visitor from outside the EU gets the same benefit from a Portuguese SIM, because the rule follows the network, not the passport. Is a Portuguese carrier plan or a travel eSIM the better buy?

Direct means one network, carrier-issued provisioning, and often a local number and EU roaming; in exchange you face registration and sometimes a store visit. A travel eSIM means instant delivery, no paperwork, automatic switching between MEO, NOS, Vodafone and Digi, and a fixed upfront price. Short trip: travel eSIM. Long stay or you need a local number: direct.

How to unlock your phone for a Portugal eSIM

Ask the carrier it is locked to. Paid-off devices are usually cleared within hours through the operator’s self-serve channel at no charge. iPhone owners can read the lock state in Settings, General, About, Carrier Lock before they ever board a plane.

Which phones have problems with Portugal eSIMs?

The recurring ones are a carrier-locked phone, a handset without n28 or n20, mainland-China iPhone models with the hardware disabled, dual-SIM phones whose installed profile carries no data, and very old Androids with no eSIM menu at all. Underneath, the idea is simple: a chip already in your handset stores the carrier profile, so no physical card is needed.
