Is my phone ready for a Greek eSIM?

Three things decide whether your phone works on a Greek carrier: its frequency bands, its lock status, and a short list of device-specific quirks. All three are covered below.

Is your model approved for Greece eSIMs?

Greek carriers build their 4G on bands 3 (1800 MHz), 7 (2600 MHz) and 20 (800 MHz), and their 5G on band n78 (3500 MHz) and band n1 (2100 MHz). Almost every flagship phone sold in Europe, North America or Asia since 2019 carries those bands, so most visitors connect without thinking about it. The phone you should worry about is an older model or a budget handset that lacks band 20, because that 800 MHz layer is what carries signal into rural valleys and across open water.

Nobody expects you to memorise band charts. What counts is the model number, not the retail name, so match it against eSIM compatibility quiz. If the technical side interests you before you board the ferry, what your phone does when it loads an eSIM profile covers it in plain terms.

Who sells mobile service in Greece

A locked phone is the most common reason a Greece eSIM install simply fails, and the fix is the same everywhere. Greek carriers sell phones locked to their own network, and a handset locked to a carrier abroad will refuse any profile that is not from that carrier.

Check it: on iPhone, open Settings -> General -> About -> Carrier Lock. A “No SIM restrictions” line means you are free to install anything. A “SIM locked” result means travel profiles will refuse to install until the phone is freed.

Fix it: the lock belongs to whoever sold the phone, so the unlock request goes to them, not to Cosmote. Devices that are paid off are normally cleared within hours, and most carriers no longer charge for it. Only then try installing your eSIM.

If you bought a used phone, or a phone on a plan from a US or European carrier, assume it may be locked and check before you fly. The Greek shop staff cannot unlock a foreign handset for you.

Where to get a Greece eSIM

Cosmote is the incumbent and the market leader, and it is the network Ookla named best overall and best for 5G in the first half of 2025. Cosmote supports eSIM on eligible postpaid plans and on prepaid lines through its app and shops.

  1. Visit a Cosmote or Germanos shop, or use the Cosmote app if you already hold an eligible plan; pick a prepaid or postpaid line.

How do you get a Vodafone Greece plan?

Vodafone Greece is the solid number two. It supports eSIM on eligible plans through its app and stores, and it runs strong 5G in Athens, Thessaloniki and the bigger islands.

For a Vodafone plan, the newcomer path is a shop or kiosk visit: bring your passport or EU ID, and staff register the line on the spot. Vodafone’s own prepaid brand, CU, sells an unlimited summer bundle that is popular with visitors, and the airport desk sells a tourist pass at a premium. Vodafone posted a Speed Score of 63.65 and a Consistency Score of 92 in Ookla’s 1H 2025 data, so it is a close competitor to Cosmote in towns even if it trails on raw reach.

Does Nova sell a prepaid eSIM for visitors?

Nova is the network you may still hear called Wind, a name it dropped after the merger. It supports eSIM on eligible plans and runs the most aggressive prices, which is why budget travellers gravitate to its Free2Go prepaid brand.

Nova’s Ookla numbers are third of the three: a Speed Score of 59.76 and a 5G availability of 75.3%, with a Game Score of 81.27 that actually edges Vodafone. In practice Nova is perfectly good in cities and large resorts but historically thinner on the smaller islands and in the mountains. If your trip is Athens plus a single big island, Nova saves money without much risk; if you are stringing together the small Cyclades, spend the extra euros on Cosmote.

Four fixes when a Greece eSIM refuses to work

General activation errors, a profile that will not download, a scan that fails, an eSIM that installs but never registers, are covered in our eSIM troubleshooting resource. Four patterns come up again and again in Greece — the list below covers them.

  1. Check the phone is lock-free: Carrier Lock shows No SIM restrictions
  2. Look over the QR code before scanning; one scan is all it gets
  3. If the status looks odd, ask the operator whether your profile is parked in a pending state

B. Installed, but no signal bars

  1. Re-check the lock status

  1. This is expected, not a fault; open water and deep gorges have no mast in range

Greece eSIM questions and answers

Cosmote eSIM plans for visitors

Cosmote supports eSIM on eligible plans, and its prepaid brand What’s Up runs on the Cosmote network. In practice, visitors buying prepaid at a Cosmote or Germanos shop are often handed a physical SIM, with eSIM available on compatible devices. Either way, Greek law requires the line to be registered against your passport. If you want a Cosmote profile with no shop and no ID copy, a travel eSIM that rides Cosmote is the cleaner route.

Does Vodafone sell a tourist eSIM for visitors?

Yes, on eligible plans through the Vodafone app or a store, with passport registration. Vodafone’s prepaid brand CU sells an unlimited summer bundle that is popular with visitors, and the airport desk sells a pricier tourist pass. Vodafone posted the second-best Ookla numbers after Cosmote, so it is a strong choice in Athens, Thessaloniki and the larger islands.

Can international visitors get a Nova eSIM?

Nova supports eSIM on eligible plans and is the cheapest of the three, with Free2Go unlimited around EUR 20 for 30 days. The trade-off is reach: Nova’s Ookla 5G availability was 75.3% and its network thins out on the smaller islands and in the mountains. For a city-plus-one-big-island trip it is fine; for scattered island hopping, spend up on Cosmote.

Which carrier wins outside Athens?

Cosmote, on the evidence. It led Ookla’s 5G availability at 78.5% in the first half of 2025 and is the network most travel writers name for holding signal on the Cyclades ferry routes and in remote island harbours. Vodafone is a close second in towns; Nova is the one to avoid if your itinerary flirts with the small islands.

Do I need ID to buy a Greek SIM?

ID is required. Greek law bans anonymous prepaid lines, so every local SIM and prepaid eSIM is registered against a valid passport or EU identity card, and the seller photographs or copies the document at the point of sale. You must also be 18 or over. A travel eSIM bought before you fly usually needs only an email, which is why many visitors skip the shop entirely.

Does a Greek SIM roam free in the EU?

Inside the EU and EEA, yes, a Greek SIM roams at home rates under the roam-like-at-home rules. Turkey, Albania, North Macedonia, Serbia, Montenegro and Bosnia are outside that zone, so a plain Greek prepaid plan is not covered and roaming charges apply. On Kos and Patmos you can even connect to a Turkish mast by accident, so set the network manually to Cosmote GR near those borders.

Why do ferries lose signal mid-crossing?

Because there is no mast in the open Aegean. Cellular signal typically drops about 5 to 10 km from any coastline and does not return until you approach the next port, leaving 30 to 90 minutes offline on most crossings. Even Cosmote, the strongest network, falls to nothing a few kilometres off Santorini. The fix is preparation: download offline maps and your ferry ticket as a PDF before you board.

Why is my phone on a Turkish network?

On Kos and Patmos, which sit close to the Turkish coast, a phone set to automatic network selection can camp on a Turkish operator, a non-EU network that bills at international rates. Open Settings, Cellular, Network Selection, switch off automatic, and manually choose Cosmote GR. That keeps you on the Greek network you paid for.

Rural coverage in Greece: Cosmote vs Vodafone

Cosmote, on the evidence. Its Connectivity Score of 77.64 led the market and its 5G availability of 78.5% was the highest, which matters most on mountain roads and minor islands where the other two fade. Nova’s reach is the most limited inland and on the smaller archipelagoes.

Should I buy from a Greek carrier or use a travel eSIM?

Direct means one network, carrier-issued provisioning, and a passport registration step; in exchange you get a real Greek number and unlimited domestic calling. A travel eSIM means instant delivery, no paperwork, automatic switching across Cosmote, Vodafone and Nova, and a fixed upfront price. Short trip: travel eSIM. For trips measured in months — or if a local number matters — buy from a carrier directly.

What if my Greece eSIM stops working in the Samaria Gorge?

That is expected, not a fault. The Samaria Gorge runs 16 km through a national park with no signal from end to end, so download offline maps and the Agia Roumeli ferry schedule before you start the descent. Signal returns at the trailhead in Omalos and at the coastal exit in Agia Roumeli. The same applies to the Vikos Gorge in Zagori and to any deep mountain road.

How does an eSIM actually work?

The eSIM is the chip factory-fitted in your handset that holds carrier profiles in place of a plastic card. An operator issues the profile, your handset saves it onto that built-in chip, and from then on it acts like any SIM. The mechanics, SM-DP plus servers, activation codes, and why a QR code is single-use, are explained in eSIM activation mechanics.
